# Culturally and Linguistically Diverse (CALD) Young People

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway guides the assessment and management of young people from culturally and linguistically diverse backgrounds, including migrants, refugees, and those navigating multiple cultural identities.

---

## Background

Culture plays an important part in developing identity. Within any culture there can be a wide range of attributes, attitudes, values, and practices.

Young people from CALD backgrounds may be stressed because of:

- Identity conflict between New Zealand culture and family culture
- Adjustment to a new country and culture
- Social isolation exacerbated by racism, discrimination, or bullying
- Past experience of war or conflict
- Parents' expectations of success in a new country

Young people's families may have different expectations and attitudes to healthcare access and provision, for example:

- Wanting to address only medical symptoms, not broader information sharing or psychosocial issues
- Beliefs about illness, the meaning of symptoms, and the use of medications
- A concept of healthcare as a family issue — it may be difficult to see a young person alone

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

1. The young person's cultural background
2. Family relationships
3. Customs regarding decisions about healthcare
4. Identity — do they identify with their parents' culture, as a New Zealander, or a combination?

Your doctor will also:

- Explore any difficulties or stresses arising from cultural identity
- Establish if supports are available within the patient's family, community, and wider social group
- Assess the presenting health concern, being aware of specific health issues which may arise for different groups

### Refugee-Specific Health Issues

Refugees can arrive with unique healthcare needs including:

- Infectious diseases
- Longstanding undiagnosed chronic conditions
- Neglected injuries
- Musculoskeletal and pain issues
- Poor oral health
- Mental and social health problems, such as post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD), depression, and anxiety — these may present as physical (somatic) symptoms, and determining the underlying health concerns may require multiple health visits

Many conditions often require long-term management and support.

### Other Health Needs to Consider

Your doctor may also consider:

- Access to low-cost services
- Diet and nutrition
- Substance use
- Mental health
- Sexual health
- **Edgewalking** — a concept describing young people who belong to two or more ethnic, cultural, or spiritual worlds. Edgewalkers reject both "melting pot" conformity and the tendency to emphasise differences; they embrace their cultural complexity while engaging in mainstream society. Understanding this experience can help your doctor provide more effective, culturally responsive care.

---

## Management

Your doctor will develop a management plan — with family agreement where appropriate — that takes cultural and intergenerational issues into account.

Because cultural context can significantly affect how symptoms are interpreted, communicated, and managed, your doctor may take additional time to understand your background before recommending a course of action.
