# Calprotectin

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers faecal calprotectin — a stool test that helps distinguish irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) from inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) in people with ongoing gastrointestinal symptoms.

---

## What Is Faecal Calprotectin?

- Faecal calprotectin is a marker of gut mucosal inflammation.
- It can help differentiate between IBS and IBD (e.g., Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis) in people with chronic gastrointestinal symptoms such as abdominal discomfort, bloating, or a change in bowel habit.
- It is **not** appropriate for symptoms lasting less than 6 weeks, as acute gut disturbance can cause a falsely raised result.
- It is **not** a screening test for bowel cancer.
- A 5 g stool specimen is required, separate to any other faeces tests. Cooling is not necessary.

---

## When Your Doctor May Consider This Test

Faecal calprotectin is usually a **second-line investigation** — other tests are done first.

Your doctor will typically check the following before requesting calprotectin:

- Faecal sample for enteric pathogens (gut bugs)
- Faecal sample for ova and parasites (if relevant travel history)
- Blood tests: FBC, CRP, ferritin, tTG (coeliac), TSH (thyroid)

Calprotectin may be considered if:

- Symptoms have been present for more than 3 weeks and IBD is a concern
- There is a **family history of IBD** — in this case it may be used as a first-line test
- Symptoms suggest possible IBD rather than IBS

Your doctor will **not** request calprotectin if:

- CRP is already elevated (inflammation has been confirmed by blood test)
- You already need a colonoscopy based on your symptoms
- It would be used as a first-line test for diarrhoea, pain, rectal bleeding, or mucus

---

## Interpreting the Result

Your doctor will interpret your result in context of your symptoms and other test findings.

| Result | What it may mean |
|---|---|
| < 50 micrograms/g | Low — effectively excludes IBD in a younger patient with chronic non-specific symptoms |
| 50–200 micrograms/g | Equivocal — may need repeat testing or further investigation |
| > 200 micrograms/g | Raised — specificity for IBD is over 90% in adults |
| > 500 micrograms/g | Highly suspicious for IBD |

**Note on re-testing:** A repeat calprotectin sample will only be processed if 90 days have passed since the original test (unless the first result was equivocal or there was a sample problem).

**Note on accuracy:** Patient variation, stool consistency, and sample handling can all influence results.

---

## In People With Known IBD

If you have a known diagnosis of IBD and are currently in remission:

- A calprotectin result > 50 micrograms/g is associated with an increased risk of relapse over the next 12 months.
- Elevation can reflect mucosal inflammation even when you have no current symptoms.
- Your doctor may consider this information when monitoring your condition.

---

## In Children

Calprotectin is less reliable in children:

- In healthy young children, calprotectin levels are naturally higher.
- A raised result is less specific for IBD in children than in adults.
- It is also less reliable as a marker of treatment response in children.

Your doctor will take age and clinical context into account when interpreting results.

---

## What Happens Next

If your calprotectin result is raised, your doctor may consider referral for specialist review or colonoscopy. A normal result (< 50) in a younger patient with chronic symptoms is reassuring and helps avoid unnecessary procedures.

Always discuss your results and next steps with your doctor.
