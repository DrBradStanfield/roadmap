# Foreign Body in Ear

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of a foreign body (object) lodged in the ear canal.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Care

Contact your doctor or go to the emergency department immediately if:

- The object is a **button battery** — requires same-day removal
- There is associated **acute vertigo** (spinning sensation) and/or **nystagmus** (involuntary eye movements)

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history including:

- What the object is
- How long it has been in the ear
- Any symptoms or signs of infection (e.g., discharge)
- Any hearing loss

Physical examination typically includes:

- Identifying the object with an otoscope
- Checking **both** ears
- Looking for signs of trauma to the ear canal and eardrum (tympanic membrane)
- Assessing how cooperative the patient is before attempting removal

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following approach:

**Most ear foreign body removals can be delayed** — same-day urgency is only needed for button batteries.

Your doctor will seek ENT/otolaryngology advice if:

- The object is a **button battery** (same-day removal)
- The object has entered the **middle ear space**
- The foreign body is associated with vertigo and/or nystagmus
- Removal equipment or skills are not available
- The patient requires restraint (distress makes removal more difficult)
- An attempt at removal has been unsuccessful

**If the object is a live insect**, your doctor may first kill it by flooding the ear with oil (e.g., olive oil) before attempting removal.

**Removal techniques** your doctor may use depending on the object type:

| Technique | Suitable for | Not suitable for |
|---|---|---|
| Irrigation (syringing) | Insects, loose inorganic objects (e.g., small beads) | Batteries, vegetable matter, perforated eardrum, grommets, polystyrene |
| Alligator forceps | Insects, graspable items (e.g., paper, sponge) | Round objects |
| Right angle hook | Non-occlusive round objects | Fragile objects, objects deep in canal |
| Wax loop/curette | Wax, small round objects | — |
| Suction | Small non-occlusive objects | — |

**If signs of infection remain after removal**, your doctor may prescribe antibiotic and steroid ear drops — for example, sofradex or kenacomb (5 drops three times a day), after drying the ear canal first.
