# Colorectal Cancer Risk

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how to assess a person's risk of colorectal cancer (CRC) based on family history, a personal history of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), or previous CRC.

> **Note:** This information is for educational purposes. Risk stratification and decisions about colonoscopy or referral should always be made with your doctor.

---

## When to Follow a Different Pathway

Before assessing familial risk, your doctor will check whether a more urgent pathway applies:

- **Symptoms suggestive of CRC** (bleeding, change in bowel habit, unexplained weight loss) — these need assessment under a Colorectal Symptoms pathway, not a risk pathway.
- **Previous bowel cancer** — managed under Bowel Cancer Follow Up - Post Treatment.
- **IBD for more than 10 years** — surveillance follows the IBD pathway.
- **Known bowel polyps** — managed under the Bowel Polyp Surveillance pathway.

---

## Family History Risk Categories

Your doctor will take a detailed family history to assign a risk category. The three categories are:

### Category 1 – Slightly Above-Average Risk

- One first-degree relative diagnosed with colorectal cancer at age 55 or older.

### Category 2 – Moderately Increased Risk

Either of:
- One first-degree relative diagnosed with colorectal cancer younger than age 55, OR
- Two first-degree relatives on the same side of the family diagnosed with CRC at any age (without Category 3 features).

### Category 3 – Potentially High Risk

Any of the following:
- Two first-degree relatives, or one first-degree relative plus one or more second-degree relatives, all on the same side of the family, with CRC — and at least one of them:
  - was diagnosed at age 54 or younger, or
  - developed multiple CRCs, or
  - had an extracolonic tumour suggesting Lynch syndrome (endometrial, ovarian, stomach, small bowel, renal pelvis, pancreas, or brain cancer).
- A family history of adenomatous polyposis (FAP), Lynch syndrome, or other familial CRC syndromes.
- One first-degree relative plus two or more first- or second-degree relatives on the same side of the family with CRC at any age.
- First- or second-degree family member diagnosed with CRC and multiple adenomas.
- First-degree relative with 10 or more adenomas by age 30.

## Fitness for Investigation

Not all patients are suitable candidates for colonoscopy. Your doctor will consider whether the procedure is appropriate — particularly if you are older than 80, frail, or have significant other health conditions. In these cases, the risks of the procedure and the likelihood that findings would change management are weighed carefully.

---

## Management by Risk Category

### Category 1 – Slightly Above-Average Risk

- Participate in the National Bowel Screening Programme (NBSP) if eligible.
- Maintain healthy lifestyle choices.
- Report any new bowel symptoms to your doctor promptly.

No additional surveillance colonoscopy is recommended above what the NBSP provides.

### Category 2 – Moderately Increased Risk

- Your doctor will arrange a **colonoscopy starting at age 50**, or 10 years before the youngest age at which CRC was diagnosed in your family — whichever comes first.
- You can continue to participate in the NBSP if you have had a high-quality colonoscopy within the previous five years.
- Maintain healthy lifestyle choices and report any new bowel symptoms.

### Category 3 – Potentially High Risk

Management involves specialist input:

- Your doctor will refer you to the **New Zealand Familial Gastrointestinal Cancer Service (NZFGCS)** for a genetic risk assessment. Provide as much family history detail as possible (relationship to you, maternal or paternal side, age at diagnosis).
- A **colonoscopy** is also arranged from age 50, or 10 years before the earliest familial CRC diagnosis — whichever comes first.
- After assessment by the NZFGCS, a genetic service, gastroenterologist, or colorectal surgeon, a personalised surveillance plan is developed.
- Ongoing surveillance follows the Bowel Polyp Surveillance pathway. Once colonoscopy surveillance is no longer indicated (typically from age 60 onwards), you may return to the NBSP.
- Participate in the NBSP from eligible age until you are accepted onto a formal surveillance programme.
- Maintain healthy lifestyle choices and report any new bowel symptoms.
