# Huntington Disease

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the diagnosis and genetic testing considerations for Huntington disease (HD).

---

## About Huntington Disease

Huntington disease is an inherited condition that progressively damages the brain and nervous system. Key facts:

- It is caused by a mutation in the HTT (Huntington) gene — specifically, an abnormal expansion of a CAG repeat sequence.
- HD is **autosomal dominant**: each child of an affected person has a 50:50 chance of inheriting the mutation, regardless of sex.
- Symptoms usually begin after age 30, though onset can range widely.
- Life expectancy is approximately 15 to 20 years after symptom onset.
- Currently there is no cure — treatment is symptomatic only, though clinical trials of new therapies are ongoing.

---

## Symptoms

Huntington disease causes progressive problems across three domains:

**Movement:**
- Chorea (involuntary, jerky movements)
- Reduced coordination and balance

**Cognitive:**
- Reduced judgement and concentration
- Declining work performance

**Psychiatric/Behavioural:**
- Personality change
- Apathy and depression (often early features)

---

## Types of Genetic Testing

There are three main types of testing, each used in different circumstances:

| Test type | Who it is for |
|---|---|
| **Diagnostic testing** (via neurology clinic) | People with neurological symptoms thought to be due to HD |
| **Pre-symptomatic genetic testing** (via Genetic Health Service NZ) | People without symptoms who have a blood relative with HD |
| **Prenatal/pre-implantation diagnosis** (via Genetic Health Service NZ) | Parents who carry the HD gene expansion and are planning a pregnancy |

---

## Thinking About Genetic Testing

Deciding whether to have pre-symptomatic genetic testing is a very personal decision. Importantly:

- **80% of people at risk of HD choose not to be tested** before symptoms appear.
- A positive result cannot predict when symptoms will start, how severe they will be, or how quickly the disease will progress — even within the same family.
- Test results have significant implications for life decisions (insurance, employment, family planning).
- If HD is a new diagnosis in your family, testing of at-risk relatives may be delayed until the family has had time to adjust.

Your doctor's role is to provide information, support, and a non-judgmental space to discuss your options — not to push you toward a particular decision.

Pre-symptomatic testing through Genetic Health Service NZ (GHSNZ) is available for people aged 18 and over.

---

## Understanding DNA Results

GHSNZ will interpret your results and discuss what they mean for you. Results are based on the number of CAG repeats in the HD gene:

| Result | CAG repeats | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal (negative)** | 26 or fewer | Will not develop HD; children not at risk from this parent |
| **Unstable/mutable range** | 27–35 | Will not develop HD personally, but the repeat may expand in the next generation; ~1% of the general population fall in this range |
| **Reduced penetrance** | 36–39 | Likely to develop HD, probably later in life; children have a 1 in 2 chance of inheriting the expanded gene |
| **Positive (high risk)** | 40 or more | Will almost certainly develop HD; children have a 1 in 2 chance of inheriting the mutation |

---

## Management

- If you have **psychiatric symptoms** (e.g., depression), your doctor will treat those first before arranging genetic testing.
- If you have **neurological symptoms** suggestive of HD, your doctor will refer you to a neurologist for diagnostic assessment.
- For **pre-symptomatic testing, pre-conception counselling, or antenatal counselling**, your doctor will refer you to Genetic Health Service NZ and include your family history in the referral.
- If you are already diagnosed with HD and need **symptom management**, your doctor can refer you to the Combined HD Clinic.
