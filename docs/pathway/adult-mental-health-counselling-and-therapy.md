# Adult Mental Health Counselling and Therapy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This page covers mental health counselling and therapy options for adults. If you are in crisis or concerned about your safety, call or text **1737** (free, 24/7) or call **111**.

---

## When to See Your Doctor

Speak with your doctor if you are experiencing:

- Persistent low mood, anxiety, or worry that affects daily functioning
- Thoughts of self-harm or suicide
- Significant changes in sleep, appetite, or concentration
- Symptoms that have not improved with self-help strategies
- Uncertainty about what type of support is right for you

Your doctor can assess your symptoms, rule out medical causes, discuss treatment options (including medication if appropriate), and refer you to the right service.

---

## Types of Support

### Talking Therapies

Different types of therapy suit different presentations:

- **Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT)** — structured, evidence-based therapy for depression and anxiety; can be delivered in person or online
- **Dialectical Behaviour Therapy (DBT)** — builds skills for coping with intense emotions and difficult situations
- **Psychotherapy / counselling** — broader, often longer-term support for a range of mental health concerns
- **Peer support** — support from people with lived experience; available through several national organisations

### E-Therapy (Online Programmes)

E-therapy can be a good starting point or complement to in-person care. Your doctor should consider your ability to read, concentrate, access devices, and have private space at home before recommending e-therapy.

**Monitored programmes (clinician involved):**

- [Beating the Blues](https://beatingtheblues.co.nz/) — online CBT for depression and anxiety in adults 18+. 8 sessions (~50 min each). Funded; requires clinician registration of the patient. Practitioners receive progress reports.
- [Just a Thought](https://www.justathought.co.nz/) — free online CBT for adults 16+. Courses cover generalised anxiety, social anxiety, depression, OCD, health anxiety, panic, insomnia, and postnatal wellbeing. No referral needed.
- [Togetherall](https://togetherall.com/) — 24/7 online mental health and wellbeing community. Free for eligible New Zealand residents; register with your postcode.

**Self-directed programmes:**

- [Small Steps](https://www.smallsteps.org.nz/) — free personalised programme to build mental wellbeing skills (available in te reo Māori)
- [Aunty Dee](http://www.auntydee.co.nz/) — structured problem-solving using CBT principles; aimed at Māori rangatahi and Pacific youth (14–25 years)
- Centre for Clinical Interventions — free self-help workbooks: [Depression](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Depression), [Social Anxiety](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Social-Anxiety), [Self-Esteem](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Self-Esteem), [Assertiveness](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself/Assertiveness)

If using e-therapy, your doctor may follow up by phone within a week to check you have started, then arrange a clinical review at 2–4 weeks. If symptoms worsen or do not improve, seek further review.

## Finding a Therapist

Private therapists are available throughout New Zealand. There will usually be a fee for private therapy. Directories include:

- [New Zealand Association of Counsellors](https://nzac.org.nz/client/roster/clientRosterView.html?clientRosterId=677)
- [New Zealand College of Clinical Psychologists](https://www.nzccp.co.nz/for-the-public#find-a-psychologist)
- [New Zealand Psychological Society](https://www.psychology.org.nz/public/find-psychologist)
- [New Zealand Association of Psychotherapists](https://nzap.org.nz/member-directory#!directory/map)
- [Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists](https://www.yourhealthinmind.org/find-a-psychiatrist)
- [Talking Works](https://www.talkingworks.co.nz/listing-category/counsellors/)

**ACC-funded therapy** is available following a physical injury or sexual abuse. See [ACC counselling and therapy](https://www.acc.co.nz/im-injured/types-of-ongoing-support/counselling-therapy/).

**Employee Assistance Programmes (EAP):** If your employer provides EAP access, this offers free confidential counselling — not limited to work-related concerns.

**University students:** Many tertiary institutions provide free counselling for enrolled students.

## Specialist Support

### Eating Disorders

- [Eating Disorders Association of New Zealand (EDANZ)](https://www.ed.org.nz/) — support for individuals, families, and carers

### Gambling

- [Safer Gambling Aotearoa](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/) — free and confidential gambling-related services; online, phone, and in-person
- Gambling Helpline Aotearoa: **0800 564 655** | free text **8006** | [gamblinghelpline.co.nz](https://gamblinghelpline.co.nz/)

### Men's Services

- [Man Alive](https://www.manalive.nz/) — counselling, support groups, and family/couples counselling; includes specialist Māori programmes. Phone **0800 826 367**
- [Male Survivors Aotearoa](https://tautokotane.nz/services/) — support for male survivors of sexual abuse. Phone **0800 044 334** | text **4334**
- [Better Blokes](https://betterblokes.org.nz/) — peer support for men who have experienced childhood sexual abuse or assault. Phone **0800 111 757** | text **4202**

### Grief, Loss, and Trauma

- [Skylight Trust](https://www.skylight.org.nz/) — counselling throughout New Zealand specialising in grief, loss, and trauma

### Suicide Bereavement

- [Aoake te Rā](https://www.aoaketera.org.nz/) — free support for individuals and families bereaved by suicide; face-to-face, online, or phone
- [After a Suicide](https://afterasuicide.nz/) — information and support after a suicide loss
- [Mental Health Foundation – Suicide Bereavement Groups](https://mentalhealth.org.nz/groups?category=SuicideBereavementGroups)
