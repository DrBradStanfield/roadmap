# Contraceptive Implant Removal

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the removal of contraceptive implants — including reasons for removal, timing, the procedure itself, and what happens after. Your doctor will guide you through each step based on your individual circumstances.

---

## About Contraceptive Implants

Two types of implant may be encountered:

- **Jadelle** — the only contraceptive implant registered in New Zealand. It comprises 2 rods, each containing 75 mg of levonorgestrel (a progestogen hormone), and has a licensed contraceptive duration of **5 years**.
- **Implanon NXT** — a single rod containing 68 mg of etonogestrel, available in Australia and elsewhere. It has a contraceptive duration of **3 years**.

---

## Reasons for Removal

Your doctor will assess why you are requesting removal. Common reasons include:

- You wish to become pregnant
- The implant has reached the end of its licensed duration (5 years for Jadelle, 3 years for Implanon)
- You have developed a medical condition that means continued implant use is no longer advisable (e.g., ischaemic heart disease, breast cancer, cerebrovascular disease, stroke or TIA, liver tumours, or decompensated cirrhosis)
- Side effects (such as irregular bleeding)
- Menopause
- You are pregnant and wish to continue the pregnancy

If irregular bleeding is the reason for requesting removal, your doctor may discuss options to manage this before proceeding with removal.

---

## Timing of Removal

Your doctor will consider the timing of removal based on your situation:

### If you want to become pregnant
- Fertility returns immediately once the implant is removed.
- Your doctor may offer pre-conception advice, including folic acid supplementation.

### If you do not want to become pregnant
- Your doctor will discuss contraceptive options with you.
- If removal is because the implant has passed its licensed duration, your doctor will first check whether pregnancy can reasonably be excluded.
- A new implant can be inserted at the time of removal if desired.

### If you are considering removal because of menopause
- **Under 50 years:** If menopause is suspected, you can have the implant removed and switch to a non-hormonal method that does not cause amenorrhoea. If you then remain without periods for 2 years, you can stop contraception.
- **50 years or older:** You may continue implant use, replacing it every 5 years until age 55, when natural loss of fertility can be assumed. If you are aged 50 or older, have had no periods for at least 12 months, and wish to stop before age 55, your doctor will check a single follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) level:
  - FSH 30 units/L or fewer: continue contraception and repeat in 12 months
  - FSH more than 30 units/L: you may discontinue contraception after a further 12 months

Any heavy bleeding or changes in bleeding pattern should be promptly assessed by your doctor.

## Before the Procedure

Before removal, your doctor will:

- Discuss your reasons for removal and your plans regarding pregnancy
- Check whether the rods can be felt (palpated) under the skin
- Obtain your informed consent — for a contraceptive implant removal, written consent is recommended

Your doctor will advise you that:

- Contraceptive protection stops immediately after removal
- The procedure requires a local anaesthetic injection
- The wound may be tender for a few days and will leave a small scar
- There is a small possibility of wound infection
- If one or more rods cannot be easily felt, removal may not be possible in the usual way and you may need to see another provider

---

## The Removal Procedure

The process described below is for Jadelle (2 rods). Implanon removal is the same but involves only a single rod.

Your doctor will only attempt removal after appropriate clinical training in this procedure.

1. The rods are located by palpation.
2. A small amount of local anaesthetic (with adrenaline) is injected under the narrow ends of the implants.
3. A small incision (about 4–5 mm) is made in the skin.
4. Each rod is gently manoeuvred to the incision, grasped with forceps, and removed. Your doctor will check the length of each rod to confirm it has been fully removed (Jadelle: 43 mm per rod; Implanon: 40 mm).
5. The incision is closed with Steri-Strips and a waterproof dressing, and a bandage is applied.
6. You will be advised to remove the bandage the next day and keep the area dry for a few days.

### If removal is not straightforward

- **If one or more rods cannot be removed despite being palpable:** Your doctor will refer you to a more experienced colleague.
- **If one or more rods cannot be felt (impalpable):** Your doctor will not attempt removal and will arrange an ultrasound-guided removal. This may be covered under an ACC treatment injury claim. If the implant has expired or will expire while you are waiting, your doctor will discuss alternative contraception.

If a single Jadelle rod is left in place after a partial removal, it does not provide adequate contraception and may increase the risk of ectopic pregnancy — your doctor will discuss alternative contraception with you.

---

## After the Procedure

- You will receive a written information sheet about post-removal care.
- The procedure will be documented in your medical record. With your consent, correspondence will be sent to your registered GP.
- If you do not wish to become pregnant, your doctor will ensure you have an appropriate alternative form of contraception. Condoms will be provided if needed while another method becomes effective.
- There is no need to avoid intercourse after removal, but pregnancy may occur at any time once the implant is removed.
