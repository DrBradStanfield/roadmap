# Emergency Contraception

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers emergency contraception after unprotected sexual intercourse (UPSI) or contraceptive failure — what options are available, how they work, and what to expect.

---

## Background

Emergency contraception (EC) can be used after unprotected sexual intercourse or when a contraceptive method has failed. There are two main methods:

**Copper intrauterine device (Cu-IUD)**
- The most effective method of emergency contraception — failure rate less than 1%
- The only method that works after ovulation has already occurred
- Works by the toxic effect of copper on sperm and ova, preventing fertilisation; also prevents implantation
- Not affected by body weight, BMI, or medications
- Can be removed at the next period or left in place for ongoing long-term contraception
- Can be inserted within 5 days of the earliest estimated date of ovulation, or up to 5 days (120 hours) after the first episode of UPSI

**Oral levonorgestrel emergency contraceptive pill (LNG-EC)**
- Works primarily by delaying ovulation — not effective if ovulation has already occurred
- Effective as soon as possible after sex, ideally within 12 hours, but can be used within 72 hours
- Efficacy demonstrated up to 96 hours (unapproved indication) but diminishes with time
- No evidence of effectiveness after ovulation or more than 96 hours after UPSI
- Approximately 2.2% pregnancy rate when taken within 72 hours
- Can be repeated within the same cycle if further UPSI occurs more than 12 hours after the previous dose

Both methods are suitable after pregnancy and during breastfeeding.

---

## When Emergency Contraception Is Needed

Emergency contraception is indicated after UPSI on any day of a natural menstrual cycle, and in cases of contraceptive failure, including:

- Condom breakage or other barrier method failure
- Missed or late contraceptive pills (combined pill or progestogen-only pill) — timing rules apply
- Late depot medroxyprogesterone acetate (DMPA) injection (more than 14 weeks since last injection, or UPSI in the first 7 days after a late injection)
- Actual or suspected IUD expulsion or displacement, or UPSI with location of IUD unknown
- Expired contraceptive implant (more than 5 years for Jadelle, more than 3 years for Implanon)
- UPSI from day 21 after childbirth (unless fully breastfeeding, amenorrhoeic, and within 6 months of childbirth)
- UPSI from day 5 after abortion, miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, or uterine evacuation

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will consider:

- The timing of all episodes of UPSI in the current cycle
- The date of your last normal period and your usual cycle length, to estimate when ovulation occurred
- Whether a pregnancy test is needed (note: pregnancy testing cannot reliably exclude pregnancy if UPSI occurred fewer than 21 days ago)
- Whether there has been any non-consenting sexual activity
- Whether you have recently had gestational trophoblastic disease (GTD) — this affects Cu-IUD eligibility
- Your weight and BMI — this affects LNG-EC effectiveness
- Any medications you are taking, particularly liver enzyme-inducing drugs

Your doctor may also offer opportunistic screening for sexually transmitted infections (STIs), particularly if you have had multiple sexual contacts, a recent change in partner, or genital symptoms.

---

## Management

Your doctor will offer emergency contraception after UPSI and will discuss both options with you.

**Cu-IUD (first-choice if appropriate)**

Your doctor may recommend a copper IUD as the most effective option. The Cu-IUD is not affected by weight, BMI, or medications that reduce the effectiveness of hormonal contraception.

- If you are at high risk of STIs and have no symptoms, your doctor may consider azithromycin at the time of insertion
- If insertion is unsuccessful and you still wish to use this method, your doctor will arrange timely referral to another provider
- If you have recently had gestational trophoblastic disease with abnormal beta-hCG levels, your doctor will seek specialist advice before inserting a Cu-IUD

**Oral levonorgestrel (LNG-EC)**

Standard dose is 1.5 mg as a single dose. Your doctor may consider a double dose (3 mg) if you:

- Weigh more than 70 kg, or have a BMI greater than 26
- Are currently using, or within 28 days of finishing, liver enzyme-inducing drugs or griseofulvin

Note: The double dose of LNG-EC (3 mg) has not been approved by the manufacturer.

Liver enzyme-inducing drugs that reduce contraceptive efficacy include:
- Anti-seizure medications: carbamazepine, phenytoin, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, primidone, rufinamide, eslicarbazepine, topiramate
- Antibiotics: rifampicin, rifabutin
- Antiretrovirals: nevirapine, nelfinavir, tipranavir/ritonavir, efavirenz, etravirine
- Others: St John's wort, bosentan, modafinil, aprepitant, sugammadex

It takes 28 days for the effect of liver enzyme-inducing drugs to dissipate, even after a single dose. If you are on any of these medications, your doctor will strongly recommend the Cu-IUD as the most effective option.

If you have a BMI greater than 30, your doctor will strongly recommend the Cu-IUD.

**If you vomit within 3 hours of taking LNG-EC, a repeat dose is needed.**

## What to Expect After Emergency Contraception

**After LNG-EC:**
- You may experience spotting or bleeding, and your next period may arrive early or late
- Uncommon side-effects include headaches, nausea, and altered bleeding pattern

**After Cu-IUD insertion:**
- Heavier periods and period pain — usually improves over time
- Small increased risk of pelvic infection in the first 20 days
- 5% risk of expulsion — your doctor will advise you to check the strings regularly
- Rare risk of uterine perforation during insertion (approximately 1.4 per 1000 insertions)
- If you think you may be pregnant after a Cu-IUD, seek medical advice promptly as there is an increased risk of ectopic pregnancy if pregnancy does occur

---

## Follow-Up

After emergency contraception, your doctor will advise you to:

- Start suitable ongoing contraception immediately
- Use condoms or avoid intercourse until ongoing contraception is established:
  - 7 days for the contraceptive implant, combined oral contraceptive pill, contraceptive injection, or levonorgestrel IUS (Mirena, Jaydess)
  - 3 days for the progestogen-only pill
  - 0 days for the copper IUD (immediately protective)
- Have a pregnancy test 28 days after the last episode of UPSI
- Consider a sexual health check if not already done
