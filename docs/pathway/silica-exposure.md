# Silica Exposure

> Auckland Region HealthPathways clinical guidance for GPs. Source: files/631794.htm
> This pathway covers occupational health risks of workers exposed to crystalline silica from engineered stone.

## Background

Mechanical processing of products containing crystalline silica produces respirable particles. Weathered silica (e.g. sand) is much less hazardous.

**Conditions linked to silica exposure:**
- Interstitial lung disease (ILD) — silicosis, pulmonary sarcoidosis
- Respiratory airway disease — COPD
- Lung cancer
- Kidney disease
- Rheumatoid arthritis

**Silicosis types:**

| Type | Exposure duration | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Acute | < 1 year | Diffuse lung abnormality, acutely unwell. Rare. |
| Accelerated | > 6 months in last 10 years | Aggressive, rapid progression. Most relevant for engineered stone workers. Simple or complicated (nodules > 10 mm, progressive fibrosis). |
| Chronic (nodular) | > 10 years | Simple nodules (upper lobes) or rarely complicated fibrosis. Rare in NZ. |

## Assessment

**Practice point:** Always ask about engineered stone work — accelerated silicosis can occur after relatively short exposures.

ACC permits telehealth for the initial appointment.

### Who to assess

Consider silica exposure in patients who:
- Work in high-risk occupations:
  - Fabrication/dry-cutting/installation of engineered stone bench tops
  - Construction — crushing or polishing cement
  - Mining
  - Sandblasting / abrasive blasting
  - Pottery and sculpture
- Self-report exposure or are referred by their workplace
- Present with relevant symptoms

### Symptoms

**Early:** Shortness of breath on exertion, dry cough

**Advanced:** Productive cough, increasing dyspnoea, loss of appetite and weight, insomnia

**Also consider:**
- Respiratory airway disease symptoms (asthma, COPD)
- Rheumatological symptoms (RA, systemic sclerosis — silicosis is associated with autoimmune disease)

### History

- Type, location, timing, duration, and intensity of silica/dust exposure (all roles including cleaning)
- Dust control measures; type of mask worn
- History of coughing up dust after work
- Smoking status
- Co-morbidities: latent TB, heart disease, joint/skin disease

### Risk stratification

**At risk of accelerated silicosis** = worked with engineered stone for > 6 months in the last 10 years.

Examine for: cardiopulmonary, rheumatological, or skin disease signs.

Review any workplace health monitoring records (spirometry, radiology) — copy to patient record.

## ACC Claims

### Pathway 1: ACC Accelerated Silicosis Assessment Pathway

**Eligibility:**
1. Worked with engineered stone > 6 months in last 10 years
2. Some exposure occurred in a New Zealand workplace

**How to claim:** ACC45 — Read code: silica pneumoconiosis NOS (H42z). Type of claim: "Work related". Include details of how criteria are met.

See: Te Whatu Ora – Accelerated Silicosis Assessment Pathway

ACC may fund a 30-minute appointment to complete the Adapted Crystalline Silica Health Form – Medical Section and arrange investigations.

**ACC-funded investigations (include ACC number and "As per the Accelerated Silicosis Pathway" on request forms):**
- PA chest X-ray (note "screening for silicosis")
- High-resolution chest CT scan (silicosis protocol)
- Spirometry at an accredited facility
- Urinalysis (urine casts and protein)
- Creatinine
- Autoimmune screen: rheumatoid factor (RhF), ANA, ANCA, ENA, anti-dsDNA

### Pathway 2: ACC Personal Injury — Work-related Gradual Process

All 3 criteria must be met:
1. A particular property/characteristic in the work task or environment caused the condition
2. That property is not materially present outside the patient's work
3. Those in that work are at significantly greater risk than those who are not

## Management

### By clinical status

| Situation | Action |
|---|---|
| Severely unwell | Acute general medicine or acute respiratory assessment — before ACC approval |
| Symptomatic + at risk of accelerated silicosis | Urgent non-acute respiratory assessment; contact ACC for prompt approval; arrange ACC-directed investigations; ACC will request occupational physician assessment |
| Asymptomatic + at risk of accelerated silicosis | Await ACC approval for investigations; ACC will request occupational physician assessment |
| Diagnostic uncertainty or imaging abnormality | Seek respiratory advice or request non-acute respiratory assessment (mark "urgent" if imaging abnormal) |
| Unsure of management while awaiting claim or ACC declines | Seek occupational medicine advice (screening, monitoring, workplace assessment, suitability for ongoing employment) |
| ACC declines + patient symptomatic | Arrange investigations; request non-acute respiratory assessment or seek respiratory advice |
| Diagnosed with accelerated silicosis | Add disease code to PMS; await ACC/WorkSafe/specialist guidance; seek respiratory advice on repeat investigations |

### If ACC declines and new information available
Submit to ACC for claim review. If follow-up investigations are recommended in 6–12 months, contact ACC at that time for funding approval.

### Preventing deterioration

- Advise avoidance of further silica exposure — support career change if abnormalities on imaging
- Patient information
- Smoking cessation
- Psychosocial support (see Mental Health Community Support)
- Manage respiratory co-morbidity (e.g. COPD)

### Notifiable disease — mandatory

**Notify the Medical Officer of Health for ANY silica-related injury or disease, regardless of duration of exposure.**

Options:
- eReferral: Notification Referral: NPHS > Reason for referral – General
- Email: notify@adhb.govt.nz
- Phone NPHS – Northern Region: (09) 623-4600
