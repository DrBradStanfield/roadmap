# Trauma-informed Care

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This page covers what trauma is, how it affects health and wellbeing, and how healthcare approaches care for people with a trauma history.

---

## What Is Trauma?

Trauma is a series of lasting adverse effects resulting from experiences or circumstances that are physically or emotionally harmful, or which threaten safety or wellbeing.

Trauma can be caused by:

- A single stressful event (e.g. a physical attack, a distressing medical experience)
- Multiple stressful events over time (e.g. physical or sexual abuse)
- Intergenerational stress (e.g. the effects of colonisation or ongoing poverty)

---

## How Trauma Affects Health

The impact of trauma is different for everyone, even when they have experienced the same event. People react differently depending on their history, feelings, culture, and the support around them.

Trauma creates lasting stress, which can affect:

- Emotional regulation and impulse control
- Current and future relationships
- Overall physical and mental health

The long-term effects of stress are influenced by a person's age and brain development stage — generally, the younger a person is when they experience trauma, and the more adverse experiences they have, the greater the potential impact on health and wellbeing.

The brain is able to change over time. Positive experiences and supportive relationships can help build resilience and counter the effects of trauma.

### Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs)

ACEs are stressful or traumatic events in childhood, including abuse, neglect, and household dysfunction (such as witnessing domestic violence or growing up with family members who have substance use disorders).

ACEs are strongly linked to the development of a wide range of health problems throughout a person's lifespan. Support during pregnancy and parenting may help prevent or reduce the impact of ACEs.

---

## Trauma in the New Zealand Population

It has been estimated that trauma potentially impacts:

- 5 out of 10 people in the general population
- 7 out of 10 Māori people
- 8 out of 10 people in prison
- 9 out of 10 people who have accessed mental health and addiction services

---

## What Trauma-informed Care Means

A trauma-informed approach shifts the focus from "what is wrong with a person" to understanding "what has happened to a person" and the impact that has had on them.

Healthcare that is trauma-informed:

- Recognises how common trauma is and works to avoid inadvertently re-traumatising people
- Promotes safety, confidentiality, and dignity
- Empowers people to have control over their care
- Works collaboratively — exploring treatment options as shared decisions
- Communicates a hopeful expectation of recovery

It is not always necessary for healthcare providers to ask directly about traumatic experiences. A trauma-aware approach means acting with sensitivity even when a history has not been disclosed.

---

## Management: Children and Young People

Your doctor may consider the following when supporting children and young people with a trauma history:

- Providing a calm, predictable, and safe environment
- Being aware that a child may have difficulty:
  - Regulating their behaviour
  - Recalling information or concentrating
  - Adapting to changing environments
  - Attaching to others or making friends
- Asking what makes the child feel safe (they may have difficulty expressing this)
- Working with the child, family, and carers to agree on management plans and follow-up

---

## Management: Adults

Your doctor may consider the following when supporting adults with a trauma history:

- A sensitive and individualised approach, recognising that childhood defence mechanisms can carry into adulthood
- Identifying both helpful (adaptive) and unhelpful (maladaptive) coping strategies
- Establishing a therapeutic relationship built on trust and openness
- Empowering the person and encouraging healthy lifestyle decisions
- Seeking mental health advice if uncertain how best to support someone
