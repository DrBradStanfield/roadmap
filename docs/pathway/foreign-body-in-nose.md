# Foreign Body in Nose

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and initial management of a foreign body lodged in the nose, most commonly seen in young children.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

A button battery in the nose is a medical emergency. Seek urgent assessment immediately if:

- The object is a **button battery** — these can cause severe tissue damage within hours

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history including:

- What the object is (type, size, shape)
- How long the object has been there
- Any symptoms or signs of infection (e.g., discharge)

> A unilateral (one-sided), smelly nasal discharge may be the only sign of a nasal foreign body, especially in a small child.

Physical examination typically includes:

- Using a headlight and nasal speculum to visualise the nostril
- Checking both nostrils
- Looking for signs of trauma
- Assessing how cooperative the child is before attempting removal

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following steps:

**1. Decongest first**
Your doctor may apply a nasal decongestant such as **co-phenylcaine** or **Otrivin nasal spray** to shrink the nasal mucosa before attempting removal, and then ask the patient to gently blow their nose.

**2. Parent's kiss technique (for infants and young children)**
A parent or carer covers the child's mouth with their own, closes the unaffected nostril with a finger, and gently puffs air — this can dislodge the object without instruments.

**3. Instrument removal (if confident)**
Young children are prepared by wrapping their limbs in a sheet and sitting on their carer's lap. The instrument used depends on the type of object:

| Instrument | Best for |
| --- | --- |
| Curved hook or curette | Firm, round objects (e.g., beads, stones) |
| Suction catheter | Anterior soft foreign bodies (e.g., paper) |
| Alligator forceps | Compressible materials (e.g., ear plugs, foam) |

**4. Referral to ENT/otolaryngology**
Your doctor will arrange urgent specialist assessment if:

- The object is a button battery
- The child requires restraint (distress makes removal more difficult and risky)
- The object is longstanding with thick or smelly discharge
- There is associated fever
- The first removal attempt is unsuccessful
- Removal is beyond the GP's ability
