# Intellectual Disability in Adults — Genetics

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the approach to adults with intellectual disability where a genetic cause has not been confirmed, or where reinvestigation with modern techniques may clarify diagnosis and inform reproductive risk for the patient or their family.

---

## When Genetics Referral Is Relevant

Consider genetic reassessment at any age. Modern genetic techniques can identify a specific diagnosis that was missed by older tests, and this can have important implications for reproductive risk in family members.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:

1. **Take a family history** — typically drawing a 3-generation family tree, and asking whether the patient or a close relative is in early pregnancy or planning a family.

2. **Review medical history**, including:
   - Previous diagnoses and any genetic tests or brain imaging already done
   - Developmental milestones — walking, language acquisition, regression, schooling, literacy
   - Possible causes (genetic conditions are common but prenatal, postnatal, or environmental factors may also contribute — these are sometimes overstated when the genetic diagnosis is unknown)

3. **Examine for dysmorphic features** — physical characteristics that may point toward a specific genetic syndrome — as well as head circumference, height, and weight.

4. **Consider referral** for reinvestigation using current genetic techniques if no cause has been found, or if prior testing is more than 5 years old.

---

## When Referral to Genetic Health Service Is Recommended

Referral to Genetic Health Service New Zealand (GHSNZ) is considered for:

- Understanding the diagnosis, likely course, and management options
- Clarifying reproductive risk for the patient and their relatives

**Urgent referral** (acute genetic health assessment) is requested if the patient or a close relative is currently in early pregnancy.

**Non-urgent referral** (non-acute genetic health assessment) is appropriate if:
- The patient or a relative is planning a family and wants to discuss prenatal testing
- No cause for the intellectual disability has been identified
- The patient was previously investigated but it has been more than 5 years

---

## Formal Diagnosis of Intellectual Disability

If a psychological assessment is needed to formally confirm an intellectual disability diagnosis, this is usually arranged privately.

- Some community disability services may be able to help arrange access to assessment for patients who have no other way of accessing one
- Government financial assistance may be available if the lack of a formal diagnosis is impairing the person's ability to access support

---

> **This information is for general understanding only.** Assessment of intellectual disability and decisions about genetic testing are complex and individualised. Always discuss with your doctor, who can coordinate referrals and explain what testing or assessment is most appropriate for your situation.
