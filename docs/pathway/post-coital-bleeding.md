# Post-coital Bleeding

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the initial approach to post-coital bleeding — vaginal bleeding that occurs within 24 hours of vaginal intercourse, not including the expected onset of a period.

---

## Red Flag — Seek Urgent Medical Assessment

See your doctor promptly if:

- The cervix looks abnormal on examination — even if a recent cervical screening result was normal

---

## Background

Post-coital bleeding refers to vaginal bleeding noticed within 24 hours of vaginal intercourse, excluding the normal onset of a menstrual period.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will consider these possible causes:

**Cervical causes:**
- Cervical ectropion (where cells from inside the cervix grow on the outside)
- Cervicitis (inflammation of the cervix)
- Cervical polyps
- Cervical carcinoma — higher risk if cervical screening history is poor
- Pelvic organ prolapse

**Infections:**
- Herpes simplex virus
- Condylomata acuminata (genital warts)
- Chlamydia

**Trauma:**
- Foreign bodies
- Sexual abuse

**Other:**
- Atrophic vaginitis (thinning of vaginal tissue, often related to low oestrogen)

Your doctor will also:

- Review your gynaecological and cervical screening history
- Perform a speculum and bimanual examination to look at the cervix and exclude obvious malignancy
- Arrange an HPV and cytology co-test (a cervical liquid-based cytology sample)
- Arrange an STI screen

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following depending on what is found:

- **Abnormal appearance of the vagina or vulva** — referral for gynaecology assessment
- **Abnormal cervix appearance or abnormal screening result** — follow-up via the cervical screening pathway, which may include colposcopy
- **Cervical polyp identified** — management via the cervical polyps pathway
- **Normal cervix and normal screening result** — your doctor will consider other causes:
  - If bleeding may be related to a contraceptive method, this will be managed accordingly
  - If symptoms continue for 3 months or more and are not thought to be related to hormonal contraception, your doctor may refer you for gynaecology assessment
