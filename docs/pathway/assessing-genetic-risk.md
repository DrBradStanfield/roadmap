# Assessing Genetic Risk

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how doctors identify patients at increased genetic risk and decide on appropriate assessment and referral.

---

## Role of Your GP

Your doctor's role in genetic assessment is to:

- Take a thorough family history
- Identify patients at high genetic risk
- Refer appropriately to Genetic Health Service NZ (where needed)

Your doctor will seek advice from a specialist or Genetic Health Service NZ before ordering specific genetic (DNA) tests, as these are expensive and may not be appropriate for every situation. An exception is cystic fibrosis carrier status testing, which can be arranged in primary care.

---

## Assessment

### Who May Need Genetic Assessment?

Patients may present with a personal or family history of:

**Cancers**
- Breast or ovarian cancer
- Colorectal or endometrial cancer

**Single gene disorders** (e.g. cystic fibrosis, Fragile X, haemophilia, Huntington disease)
- Can be dominant, recessive, or X-linked recessive
- Have the highest risk of recurrence within a family
- Early recognition is important — patients can benefit from genetic diagnosis, surveillance, treatment, and reproductive planning

**Chromosomal conditions** (e.g. Down syndrome, Turner syndrome)
- Involve net loss or gain of chromosomal material
- Can affect growth, development, and intellect
- Includes balanced translocation (chromosomes rearranged but no net loss/gain) — this predisposes to recurrent miscarriages or having a child with a disability

**Multifactorial inheritance**
- Involves a combination of genetic susceptibility and environmental triggers
- May cause a slight increase in risk to other family members
- Accounts for most common adult-onset disorders: diabetes, cardiac disease, psychiatric conditions, and many cancers

---

### What Your Doctor Will Ask

Your doctor will ask about:

- The specific condition and how much you already understand about it
- Which family members are affected

Your doctor will also ask about your **reasons for seeking genetic information**, such as:
- Wanting more information about a specific genetic condition
- Genetic counselling or pregnancy planning
- Genetic testing
- Support — finding out about a hereditary condition in a family can be emotionally difficult, with feelings of guilt and anxiety about the future

Your doctor will discuss the potential implications of any genetic testing — what a positive, negative, or uncertain result would mean for you.

---

### Family History

Your doctor will take a detailed family history and may sketch a **3-generation family tree** (covering you, your parents, and your grandparents' generation).

**Features that raise concern for a hereditary condition:**
- A condition affecting family members at a younger age than usual, or affecting multiple relatives
- Familial high cholesterol (familial hypercholesterolaemia)
- Sudden cardiac deaths in previously healthy family members (e.g. long QT syndrome, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy)
- Three or more miscarriages or stillbirths
- Multiple congenital anomalies, unusual physical features, or growth and developmental delay
- Ashkenazi Jewish ancestry (associated with higher rates of certain hereditary conditions)

---

## Management

Management depends on the specific family history and your circumstances:

| Situation | Approach |
|-----------|----------|
| Pregnant with a history of chromosomal anomaly in a previous pregnancy, or family history of a genetic condition | Urgent referral to fetal medicine; may also need simultaneous referral to Genetic Health Services NZ |
| Child with possible genetic condition (unusual features, developmental or growth delay) | Referral to paediatric medicine |
| Family history of cancer | Managed via the Familial Cancer Syndromes pathway |
| Recurrent miscarriages | Managed via the Recurrent Miscarriage pathway |
| Suspected genetic condition in any other context | Referral to Genetic Health Services NZ |

**If a genetic condition is suspected**, your doctor may request a Genetic Health Services NZ assessment, which can include:
- Advice on prenatal testing
- Diagnostic testing (for someone with symptoms)
- Predictive testing (risk assessment for unaffected people at risk of a hereditary condition)
- Carrier testing

---

## Referral

Your doctor may refer you to:

- **Genetic Health Services NZ** — for assessment, diagnosis, counselling, and testing (phone advice also available)
- **Paediatric medicine** — if a child has dysmorphic features, developmental delay, or intellectual disability
- **Fetal medicine** — urgently if pregnant and there are special risk circumstances
