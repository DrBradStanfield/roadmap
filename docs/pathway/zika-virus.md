# Zika Virus

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of Zika virus infection, including travel precautions, pregnancy considerations, and post-travel transmission prevention.

> **Always discuss with your doctor** about any concerns relating to Zika virus, particularly if you are pregnant, planning pregnancy, or have recently returned from a Zika-affected area. The information below is for educational understanding only.

---

## Background

Zika virus is a flavivirus closely related to dengue and chikungunya, and is often clinically indistinguishable from them.

Key features:
- **Transmission:** Primarily by Aedes mosquitoes. Sexual transmission is possible but rare.
- **Course:** Generally a mild febrile illness lasting 4 to 7 days. Incubation period is 3 to 14 days.
- **Asymptomatic:** About 75–80% of infections produce no symptoms.
- **Congenital risk:** Infection during pregnancy can cause congenital Zika syndrome — including microcephaly, decreased brain tissue, eye damage, and congenital contractures.
- **Neurological risk:** Associated with Guillain-Barré Syndrome.

### Zika-affected areas

Major mosquito vectors for Zika are not established in New Zealand. Previous outbreaks have occurred in:
- Sub-Saharan Africa
- Southeast Asia
- The Pacific Islands
- The Americas and the Caribbean

Since 2017, cases have decreased markedly, though low-level transmission persists in some countries. Current outbreak information can be checked at ProMED-mail (global) or the Pacific Community (SPC) (Pacific region) websites. Low-level or undetected transmission in any region with previously documented Zika cannot be excluded.

## Assessment

### History — ask about

- Travel history to or from a Zika-affected area (obtain travel dates)
- Sexual exposure to a traveller from a Zika-affected area, especially an area with active transmission
- Symptoms (see below) — incubation period is typically 3 to 12 days
- Pregnancy, plans for pregnancy, or intended sperm donation

### Symptoms of Zika virus

- Fever
- Maculopapular rash
- Arthralgia (usually small joints of hands and feet, possibly with swelling)
- Myalgia
- Headache
- Conjunctivitis
- Post-infectious lethargy and fatigue

Mild or even asymptomatic infections are common.

### Special considerations in pregnancy

- Zika virus in pregnancy can lead to severe birth defects, including microcephaly.
- The absolute risk of congenital Zika syndrome is unknown.
- The fetus appears most vulnerable in the first trimester, but risk persists throughout pregnancy.

Include Zika virus in the differential diagnosis of a pregnant woman who develops compatible symptoms within 14 days of travel to a Zika-affected area.

### Differential diagnosis

If symptomatic, consider exclusion of:
- Other arboviral diseases: dengue, chikungunya
- Measles
- Rubella (in unvaccinated people)

A mosquito-borne viral PCR panel (testing Zika, chikungunya, and dengue together) can be requested in days 0–5 of illness.

## Laboratory Testing

> **Discuss with your doctor.** Testing decisions should be made in consultation with a clinical microbiologist or virologist.

### When to test

**Always discuss testing** in the following situations:

- **Symptomatic or asymptomatic pregnant women** with travel exposure
- **Symptomatic patients with history of exposure:**
  - If ≤ 14 days after symptom onset or last possible exposure: urine PCR is preferred (due to the short blood viraemic period) plus whole blood PCR. Include travel dates and locations on the request form.
  - If > 14 days after exposure: follow specialist advice.

### When not to test

Testing is **not advised** for asymptomatic, non-pregnant people who have recently travelled to a Zika-affected area. Results can be difficult to interpret, and a negative result does not guarantee absence of infection.

## Management

### Confirmed infection

- Zika virus is a **notifiable disease** — confirmed infection must be notified to the Medical Officer of Health. Your doctor will arrange this.
- In a confirmed case during pregnancy, specialist Maternal Fetal Medicine advice should be sought.

### Supportive care

There is no specific antiviral therapy for Zika virus. Symptomatic treatment:
- Rest, adequate hydration
- Paracetamol for fever and pain
- Symptoms typically resolve within 4 to 7 days

### Mosquito bite avoidance (if travelling)

If travel to a Zika-affected area is unavoidable:
- Wear long-sleeved shirts, long pants, and closed footwear
- Use insect repellents containing DEET or picaridin — applied over sunscreen. Both are safe for pregnant and breastfeeding women.
- Use permethrin-treated clothing and gear
- Use mosquito nets — *Aedes aegypti* mosquitoes bite during the day
- Stay and sleep in screened or air-conditioned rooms

### Travel and pregnancy

Health New Zealand | Te Whatu Ora recommends that **pregnant women, or men and women planning pregnancy in the near term, delay travel** to Zika-affected areas until after pregnancy.

Offer individualised discussion of contemporary risk. Consider referral to a travel clinic.

## Post-Travel Precautions Against Sexual Transmission

> **Discuss with your doctor** if you are returning from a Zika-affected area and planning pregnancy or have a pregnant partner.

### Men (returning from a Zika-affected area)

- Use condoms or abstain from sex for **6 months** after return
- Avoid getting a partner pregnant for **6 months** after return
- Do not donate sperm for **6 months** after infection or travel

### Women (returning from a Zika-affected area)

- Use barrier methods or abstain from sex for **2 months** after return (if male partner has not also travelled to a Zika-affected area)
- Avoid pregnancy for **2 months** after return (if partner has not travelled to a Zika-affected area)
- Avoid pregnancy for **6 months** after return if male partner has also travelled to a Zika-affected area

If pregnancy is unavoidable, discuss possible testing for Zika virus infection with your doctor.

---

## When to See Your Doctor

See your doctor if:
- You are pregnant (or planning pregnancy) and have returned from or had sexual contact with a traveller from a Zika-affected area
- You develop fever, rash, joint pain, or eye redness after travel to a Zika-affected area
- You have any questions about testing, mosquito bite prevention, or travel plans during pregnancy
