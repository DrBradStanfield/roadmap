# HPV Vaccination

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers HPV (human papillomavirus) vaccination — who should be vaccinated, when, and the recommended schedule.

---

## Background

HPV is very common, with over 80% lifetime risk of infection, often acquired soon after sexual activity begins. HPV types are categorised by risk:

- **High-risk HPV (hrHPV)** — approximately 14 oncogenic types linked to almost all cervical cancers, and a high proportion of vulvar, vaginal, penile, anal, and oropharyngeal cancers
- **Low-risk HPV (lrHPV)** — cause approximately 90% of genital warts, not associated with cancer

Around 600 New Zealanders are diagnosed with an HPV-related cancer each year. Oropharyngeal cancers represent over half of these cases.

**Gardasil 9** (the funded vaccine) covers 9 HPV types — high-risk types 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, and 58, plus low-risk types 6 and 11. It can prevent up to 92% of cervical cancers attributable to HPV. It is approved for use in people aged 9 to 45 years.

Vaccination is most effective when given before the onset of sexual activity (ideally ages 9–13), but people beyond this age or with prior sexual activity still benefit from protection against HPV types they have not yet been exposed to.

## Recommended Schedule

Your doctor will advise on the appropriate schedule based on your age and circumstances.

### Ages 9 to 14 years — 2 doses (funded)

- **2 doses** at 0 and 6–12 months
- Often given at school at age 11 or 12
- **3 doses required** (at 0, 2, and 6 months) if any of the following apply:
  - Confirmed HIV infection
  - Solid organ or stem cell transplant
  - Pre-dialysis, on dialysis, or pre/post kidney transplantation

### Ages 15 to 26 years — 3 doses (funded)

- **3 doses** at 0, 2, and 6 months

All patients who began a funded course before age 27 are still funded to complete the course, even if subsequent doses are given after age 27.

### Ages 27 and older — 3 doses (not funded)

Your doctor may consider recommending vaccination (at your own cost) if you:

- Have had little previous exposure to HPV and are now likely to be exposed
- Are a man who has sex with men
- Have a confirmed diagnosis of HIV

If recommended, your doctor will prescribe 3 doses at 0, 2, and 6 months.

### After systemic anticancer therapy (SACT)

Your doctor may consider offering vaccination following chemotherapy. Timing and schedule depends on age and lymphocyte count — discuss with your doctor or specialist.

---

## Special Considerations

### Pregnancy

- HPV vaccines are **not recommended** during pregnancy
- If pregnancy is discovered after vaccination has started, there is no need for alarm — no adverse effects have been identified — but remaining doses should be delayed until after delivery
- Those who are breastfeeding can safely receive HPV vaccines

### Immunocompromised patients

Patients with HIV, organ transplants, or kidney disease on dialysis may require a 3-dose schedule regardless of age. Your doctor will advise on the appropriate schedule.
