# Urethritis

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of urethritis (inflammation of the urethra) in adults.

> This information is for general education. Urethritis requires diagnosis and treatment by a doctor. Do not attempt self-treatment.

---

## What Is Urethritis?

Urethritis is inflammation of the urethra. Most cases are linked to a sexually transmitted infection (STI), but not all.

**Types:**
- **Gonococcal urethritis:** caused by Neisseria gonorrhoeae (gonorrhoea)
- **Non-gonococcal urethritis (NGU):** no gonorrhoea detected; often due to Chlamydia trachomatis, sometimes Mycoplasma genitalium, or less commonly trichomonas, herpes simplex virus, adenovirus, or other organisms transmitted during oral or anal sex
- **Persistent or recurrent urethritis:** symptoms persist more than 2 weeks after treatment, or return within 90 days. Mycoplasma genitalium accounts for more than 40% of cases that fail initial treatment.

**Possible complications:**
- Epididymo-orchitis (inflammation of the epididymis and testes)
- Reactive arthritis

---

## Symptoms

- Pain or burning on urination (dysuria)
- Urethral discharge
- Urethral irritation

> Note: urinary frequency, urgency, blood in urine, or nocturia are more suggestive of a urinary tract infection (UTI) rather than urethritis.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:
- Ask about your symptoms and sexual history
- Examine you (checking for discharge, urethral inflammation, testicular/epididymal tenderness, genital skin changes)
- Arrange investigations including:
  - First-catch urine (first 20–30 mL of the urinary stream) for chlamydia and gonorrhoea
  - A swab culture for gonorrhoea if there is visible discharge
  - A herpes swab if there are ulcers, meatitis, or inguinal lymph node swelling
  - A midstream urine sample if a urinary tract infection is also suspected

### If Symptoms Persist or Recur

If symptoms persist after initial treatment (chlamydia and gonorrhoea having been excluded), your doctor may arrange additional testing for trichomonas or Mycoplasma genitalium. Specialist advice is usually sought before arranging these tests.

Your doctor will also complete a full sexual health check.

---

## Treatment

Treatment depends on how your urethritis presents. Your doctor will choose the appropriate antibiotic regimen.

### Purulent (heavy) Discharge or Known Gonorrhoea Contact

Your doctor will treat for both gonorrhoea and chlamydia at the same time:
- Ceftriaxone 500 mg injection (single dose), AND
- Azithromycin 1 g orally (single dose)

### Minimal or No Visible Discharge

- Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days (first-line)
- Azithromycin 1 g orally as single dose is an alternative only if there is a contraindication to doxycycline (not preferred due to inferior efficacy and resistance concerns)

### Confirmed Gonorrhoea and Chlamydia Co-Infection

- Ceftriaxone 1 g injection (single dose), PLUS
- Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days

### Allergy or Contraindications

Your doctor will seek specialist advice — alternative treatments are limited due to resistance.

---

## Preventing Reinfection

**Sexual contact:** Abstain from sex (including oral sex) or use condoms:
- Until 7 days after you complete treatment
- Until 7 days after your sexual contacts have also been treated

**Your sexual contacts:** All sexual contacts from the last 3 months should be notified. For non-gonococcal urethritis contacts, your doctor may recommend empirical doxycycline treatment. Note that patient-delivered partner therapy (giving your partner your own prescription) is not legal in New Zealand.

---

## Follow-Up

Your doctor will review you in 1 week to:
- Discuss test results
- Check whether symptoms have resolved
- Confirm you completed the medication
- Ensure contacts have been notified
- Assess risk of reinfection

**Re-screening:** Your doctor will arrange re-screening 3 months after infection to check for reinfection and test for other STIs if not already done.
