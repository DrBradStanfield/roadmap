# Zoledronic Acid Infusion Checklist

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This is the safety checklist your doctor completes before giving you a zoledronic acid (Aclasta/zoledronate) infusion for osteoporosis. All items must be confirmed before the infusion proceeds.

---

## Pre-Infusion Safety Checklist

Your doctor will work through the following checks on the day of your infusion:

### 1. Frequency — no infusion within the last 12 months

Zoledronic acid for osteoporosis is given no more than once every 12 months. More frequent infusions are not indicated and the infusion will not proceed if one has been given in the past year.

### 2. Kidney function — creatinine clearance or eGFR

Your doctor will check that your kidney function is adequate:
- Creatinine clearance ≥ 35 mL/min, **or**
- eGFR ≥ 35 mL/min/1.73m²

**If kidney function is below this threshold, the infusion will not proceed.** Your doctor uses the Cockcroft–Gault formula (see below) to calculate creatinine clearance.

### 3. Serum calcium — must be normal

Your blood calcium level must be within the normal range before the infusion. **If calcium is abnormal, the infusion will not proceed.** Low calcium before infusion significantly increases the risk of severe hypocalcaemia.

### 4. Medication review — nephrotoxic drugs and contraindications

Your doctor will review your full medication list to check for:
- Nephrotoxic drugs (medications that can harm kidneys)
- Other contraindications to zoledronic acid (see NZ Formulary)

### 5. Medication adjustments on the day

You will need to have stopped:
- **Oral bisphosphonate tablets** (e.g., alendronate/Fosamax) — stopped **permanently** before switching to zoledronic acid infusion
- **NSAIDs** (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen) — withheld **on the day of infusion**
- **Diuretics** (e.g., frusemide) — withheld **on the day of infusion**

### 6. Vitamin D — adequate levels required

You should either:
- Already be taking a regular vitamin D supplement, **or**
- Have received a loading dose of vitamin D (2 × 1.25 mg tablets) before the infusion if your levels may be low

A younger, physically active person who gets regular sun exposure may not need a loading dose. Adequate vitamin D is important to prevent hypocalcaemia after infusion.

### 7. Hydration — must be well-hydrated

You should have had your normal fluid intake (tea, coffee, water) before arriving. During the infusion, you will be given 2 large glasses of water to drink.

**The infusion will be postponed if you are dehydrated.**

### 8. Patient information — questions answered

Before the infusion, you should have read the patient information sheet and had any questions answered by your doctor or nurse.

### 9. Consent — signed and costs discussed

You need to have signed a consent form. The cost of the infusion will have been discussed with you beforehand.

### 10. Post-infusion flu-like symptoms — paracetamol

You will be advised that flu-like symptoms (fever, muscle aches, fatigue) are common in the first 1–3 days after infusion. You can take:
- **Paracetamol 1 g (2 × 500 mg tablets), up to 3 times a day for 3 days** if needed

This is a well-recognised side effect and is not dangerous — it typically resolves within a few days.

### 11. Post-infusion blood test — if relevant

If you have borderline low kidney function, or if you develop symptoms of low calcium (muscle spasms, cramps, weakness, tingling) after the infusion, your doctor will arrange a blood test within 1 week to check:
- Renal function (creatinine/eGFR)
- Serum calcium

## Cockcroft–Gault Formula (Creatinine Clearance)

Your doctor uses this formula to check that your kidneys can safely handle zoledronic acid:

**Creatinine Clearance = [(140 − age in years) × weight in kg] ÷ (0.814 × serum creatinine in µmol/L)**
× **0.85 if female**

Weight used:
- **Males:** Ideal body weight = 50 kg + 0.9 kg per cm over 150 cm height
- **Females:** Ideal body weight = 45 kg + 0.9 kg per cm over 150 cm height
- If actual body weight is **lower** than ideal, use actual weight

The threshold for proceeding is ≥ 35 mL/min.

## Key Safety Points

| Absolute stop criteria | Action |
|------------------------|--------|
| Infusion given in past 12 months | Do not proceed |
| Creatinine clearance or eGFR < 35 | Do not proceed |
| Serum calcium abnormal | Do not proceed |
| Patient dehydrated | Postpone |

| Side effect to watch for | When | What to do |
|--------------------------|------|------------|
| Flu-like symptoms (fever, aches, fatigue) | Days 1–3 post-infusion | Paracetamol 1 g up to 3×/day for 3 days |
| Muscle spasms, cramps, weakness, tingling | Days 1–7 post-infusion | See your doctor — may indicate low calcium |
