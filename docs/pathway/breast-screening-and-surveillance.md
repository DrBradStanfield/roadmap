# Breast Screening and Surveillance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers routine breast screening for women at average or increased risk, and surveillance mammography after breast cancer treatment. If you have a breast lump, symptoms, or your doctor has a high clinical suspicion of breast cancer, this is managed separately under the Breast Symptoms pathway.

---

## Background

Breast cancer is the most common cancer affecting New Zealand women.

- Screening mammograms cannot prevent breast cancer from developing, but can reduce the chance of dying from it through early detection and treatment.
- While mammography likely reduces death rates from breast cancer, it also results in some overdiagnosis and over-treatment.
- There is no evidence to support ultrasound as an alternative to mammography for screening in women at average risk. Ultrasound is only funded as an additional assessment tool alongside mammography.
- Women who request screening ultrasound should be aware that ultrasound leads to substantially more false-positive findings and biopsies compared with mammography, and that some lesions detected by mammography may not be detected by ultrasound.
- Mammography screening in women younger than 50 years leads to more overdiagnosis and reduced benefit overall.
- Breast cancer screening benefits occur after a lag time of around 10 years, so there is less benefit for those with a life expectancy of less than 10 years.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will typically:

1. **Encourage breast awareness** — knowing the normal look and feel of your breasts, and promptly reporting any persistent or unusual changes to your GP.
2. **Complete a breast cancer risk assessment** before recommending routine screening — your individual risk level may change which screening approach is recommended.
3. **Rule out symptoms** — if you have a lump, symptoms, or there is clinical suspicion of breast cancer, your doctor will manage this through the Breast Symptoms pathway rather than routine screening.

---

## Routine Breast Screening (BreastScreen Aotearoa)

The national screening programme provides screening mammograms every 2 years for eligible women.

### Who is eligible

- Women aged **45 to 74 years** (age range extended from October 2025 in a phased approach — women turning 70 on or after 1 October 2025 are now eligible for free mammograms every 2 years until age 75).
- Must be eligible for publicly funded healthcare in New Zealand.

### Who is excluded from the programme

- Had a mammogram in the past 12 months
- Has symptoms of breast cancer (managed through Breast Symptoms pathway instead)
- Had breast cancer in the past 5 years (see surveillance section below)
- Currently pregnant or breastfeeding

### What to expect

Your doctor will discuss the benefits and risks of participating in population-based breast screening so you can make an informed decision. If the result is normal, both you and your registered GP will be notified. If an abnormality is detected, a BreastScreen nurse will contact you and arrange follow-up, and your GP will also be advised.

---

## Screening for Women at Increased Risk

Some women may be eligible for annual mammography if they meet specific risk criteria. Your doctor will complete a breast cancer risk assessment and manage your screening according to your risk level.

---

## Surveillance After Breast Cancer

If you have been treated for breast cancer, ongoing mammography surveillance is recommended in most cases.

- **After bilateral mastectomy** (both breasts removed, either for treatment or prevention): routine breast surveillance imaging is generally not required. If you develop any new symptoms, your doctor will arrange breast clinic assessment.
- **For all other patients after breast cancer treatment:** your doctor will arrange regular mammography for follow-up. The frequency and duration depends on your age at diagnosis and how long it has been since diagnosis.

Your doctor will specify your date of diagnosis when arranging follow-up imaging and will coordinate the appropriate schedule with the relevant radiology service.

---

## Management

- **Routine screening:** Your doctor will support you to enrol in BreastScreen Aotearoa if you are eligible, after discussing the benefits and limitations.
- **Increased-risk screening:** Your doctor may consider annual mammography based on your individual risk assessment.
- **Post-breast cancer surveillance:** Your doctor will arrange a schedule of follow-up mammograms based on your age at diagnosis and time since treatment.
- **Symptomatic breast changes:** If symptoms develop at any point, your doctor will refer you through the Breast Symptoms pathway rather than routine screening.
