# Adalimumab Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway supports GPs managing patients on adalimumab following specialist initiation. Adalimumab is a biologic medicine — your doctor will manage it in partnership with the specialist who started it.

---

## Stop the Drug Urgently — Contact Your Doctor

Stop adalimumab and seek urgent specialist advice if any of the following occur:

- Serious infection or sepsis
- Signs of reactivation or new tuberculosis (TB)
- Anaphylaxis
- Reactivation of a demyelinating disorder (e.g. multiple sclerosis symptoms)
- Signs of new or worsening congestive cardiac failure
- Thrombocytopenia (platelets less than 50)
- Neutropenia (neutrophils less than 1)
- Markedly elevated liver enzymes (AST or ALT greater than three times the upper limit of normal)

---

## Background

Adalimumab is an anti-TNF-α monoclonal antibody that reduces inflammation by targeting tumour necrosis factor alpha.

**Brands available in New Zealand:**
- **Amgevita** (biosimilar) — subsidised for patients who started adalimumab after October 2022
- **Humira** — funded only for patients previously on Humira who experience intolerable side effects or loss of disease control with Amgevita, or who have Crohn's disease or ocular inflammation at risk of destabilisation

All prescriptions should clearly specify the brand to avoid inadvertent substitution.

**Indications** (specialist-initiated):
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Severe chronic plaque psoriasis
- Ulcerative colitis
- Crohn's disease
- Other severe inflammatory illnesses (as per Pharmac special authority)

**Contraindications** (assessed by specialist before starting):
- Active infection, including tuberculosis
- Moderate to severe congestive cardiac failure
- Demyelinating disorders

## Ongoing Management in Primary Care

### Prescribing

Your doctor may prescribe adalimumab for continued treatment if a specialist recommends it:

- Symptoms may take up to 6 months to improve
- Ensure a specialist review has taken place before signing repeat scripts

**Administration:**
- Given by subcutaneous injection — self-administered by syringe or auto-injector
- Dose and frequency vary by indication; can be escalated to weekly for some conditions
- Store in the fridge; keep out of direct light (important when travelling)
- Once removed from the fridge, injection lasts up to 14 days at less than 25°C

### Monitoring

- No specific blood monitoring is required for adalimumab alone
- If the patient is on other medications alongside adalimumab, arrange monitoring according to the requirements of those other medicines
- Follow up annually once stable

## Adverse Effects

| Adverse effect | What to watch for | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Infection** | Increased risk of serious or life-threatening infection. Note: typical signs such as fever may be masked | Treat infection; withhold drug until infection clears. Contact specialist. Low threshold for hospital admission |
| **Injection site reaction** | Local rash or bruise (common) | Usually self-limiting. Trial topical antihistamine and ice |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Rash, anaphylaxis, angioedema | Oral antihistamine; stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Tuberculosis** | Reactivation of latent TB — may be non-pulmonary | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Neurological** | Reactivation of demyelinating disorder | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Malignancy** | Increased risk of skin cancers; possible increased risk of lymphoma | Skin cancers — usually managed by local excision, adalimumab can generally be continued. Other malignancies — consult specialist |
| **Heart failure** | Severe heart failure | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Blood abnormalities** | Thrombocytopenia, leukopenia | Consult specialist |
| **Immune** | Drug-induced lupus or psoriasis | Stop drug; consult specialist |

For a full list, see the NZ Formulary (adalimumab) or Medsafe.

## Skin Cancer Surveillance

Your doctor will reinforce sun-safe behaviour and arrange regular skin checks due to an increased long-term risk of skin cancer:

- Avoid direct sunlight during peak UV hours
- Use sunscreen correctly — apply at least 20 minutes before going outside, reapply every 2 hours
- Avoid sunbeds and artificial UV exposure
- Report any new or persistent skin lesions promptly

---

## Cervical Screening

Women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 years on adalimumab have an increased risk of HPV infection due to immunosuppression:

- Your doctor will recall you for cervical screening every 3 years
- If aged 20–24, sexually active, and on immunosuppressants for more than 5 years, a single HPV test may be considered
- All those with HPV detected (any type) should be referred to colposcopy

---

## Vaccinations

No special timing is required between vaccinations and adalimumab doses.

**Recommended:**
- Annual influenza vaccine
- COVID-19 vaccination (when recommended)
- Pneumococcal vaccination (not usually funded)
- Shingrix (zoster) vaccination — funded for patients aged 65 years, and for those aged 18 or older receiving disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs for certain rheumatological conditions

**Avoid live vaccines** during treatment. Examples include:
- MMR (measles, mumps, rubella)
- Oral polio
- Oral typhoid
- BCG (tuberculosis)
- Varicella zoster (chickenpox, Zostavax)
- Yellow fever

*Exception:* If adalimumab was used during pregnancy, the infant can receive the rotavirus live vaccine in accordance with the New Zealand immunisation schedule. For all other live vaccines in infants under 1 year of age, seek specialist advice.

## Special Situations

### Surgery

Adalimumab may need to be discontinued 4 weeks before an operation and restarted once there is satisfactory wound healing (10 days to 2 weeks). Your doctor will discuss this with the prescribing specialist or surgeon.

### Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

- Emerging evidence suggests anti-TNF therapies are generally safe in pregnancy
- Controlling the underlying condition (e.g. arthritis, Crohn's disease) also improves the chances of conception
- Use during pregnancy and breastfeeding should be discussed with a specialist before conception
- See the NZ Formulary (adalimumab) for full guidance
