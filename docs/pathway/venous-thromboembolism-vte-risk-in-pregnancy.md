# Venous Thromboembolism (VTE) Risk in Pregnancy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment of VTE risk in pregnancy and the postpartum period, and when thromboprophylaxis may be recommended.

---

## Background

VTE (deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism) is a significant risk during pregnancy and the postpartum period:

- Approximately 50% of pregnancy-related VTE episodes occur during pregnancy itself, and 50% in the 6 weeks after delivery.
- A previous personal history of VTE is the strongest risk factor for VTE in pregnancy.

**Factor V Leiden (FVL):**
- FVL is a relatively common gene mutation; the prevalence of heterozygous FVL in the general population is approximately 5%.
- VTE risk is highest for FVL homozygotes and for women with combined heterozygosity for both FVL and prothrombin G20210A.
- There is no clear increase in the risk of early fetal loss, and only a possible very small increase in risk of placental insufficiency or late fetal loss — most prospective studies find no association.

---

## Assessment

Your healthcare provider will take a thorough history checking for the following risk factors:

### Personal History of VTE

- Whether any previous VTE was unprovoked, estrogen-related (e.g., pregnancy or oral contraceptive pill), or related to a transient risk factor (e.g., major surgery)
- Whether there has been more than one VTE episode (recurrent)

### Family History

- First-degree relative with a history of inherited thrombophilia
- First-degree relative with a history of VTE

### Known Thrombophilia

**Higher-risk thrombophilias in pregnancy:**
- Antithrombin deficiency with personal or family history of VTE
- Factor V Leiden homozygosity, or compound heterozygosity (with prothrombin gene mutation)
- Prothrombin gene mutation homozygous state with personal or family history of VTE
- Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS)

**Lower-risk thrombophilias in pregnancy:**
- Heterozygous Factor V Leiden or heterozygous prothrombin gene mutation
- Protein C or S deficiency
- Antithrombin deficiency (without personal/family VTE history)

### Additional Risk Factors

- Age 35 or older
- Obesity (BMI 30 kg/m² or greater)
- Active medical illness (e.g., malignancy, inflammatory bowel disease, joint disease, cardiac disease, nephrotic syndrome)
- Smoking
- Immobility (e.g., long-distance travel 4 hours or longer, bed rest 24 hours or longer, plaster cast, paraplegia)
- Extensive varicose veins
- Current systemic infection (e.g., pneumonia, sepsis)

### Obstetric Risk Factors

- Multiparity (3 or more pregnancies)
- Multiple pregnancy
- Pre-eclampsia in current pregnancy
- Caesarean section
- Antepartum or postpartum haemorrhage
- Transient factors: ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (first trimester), severe hyperemesis requiring inpatient management

---

## Risk Scoring

Your healthcare provider may use the following scoring table to assess your antenatal VTE risk:

| Risk Factor | Score |
|---|---|
| Homozygous Factor V Leiden (FVL) | 3 |
| Heterozygous FVL | 1 |
| Previous VTE — unprovoked, hormonally provoked (pregnancy/OCP), or recurrent (>1) | 6 |
| Previous VTE — provoked, not hormonally (e.g., associated with immobility, surgery) | 3 |
| Weak hereditary thrombophilia — heterozygous prothrombin G20210A mutation | 1 |
| Family history of VTE in a first-degree relative | 3 |
| BMI 30 kg/m² or higher | 3 |
| Active medical illness (malignancy, nephrotic syndrome, inflammatory conditions, cardiac disease, sickle cell disease, severe infection) | 3 |
| Immobilisation — bed rest, plaster cast, travel >4 hours, paraplegia | 1 |
| Age 35 years or older | 1 |
| Dehydration or severe hyperemesis | 1 |
| Smoker | 1 |
| Severe varicose veins | 1 |
| Multiple pregnancy | 1 |
| Parity 3 or higher | 1 |

---

## Management

### Score 5 or Higher — Thromboprophylaxis Indicated

If your risk score is 5 or higher, your healthcare provider will seek urgent specialist (haematology) advice for starting thromboprophylaxis:

- **Low molecular weight heparin (LMWH)** is the standard treatment. It should be started as soon as a positive pregnancy test is confirmed, and continued throughout pregnancy and for 6 weeks postpartum.
- LMWH is indicated even if termination of pregnancy (TOP) is being considered, as the thrombosis risk increases immediately.
- LMWH dosing is guided by your risk factors and weight, and should be determined in consultation with a haematologist.

### Score 4 or Lower — Vigilance Recommended

- Your healthcare provider will ensure you are aware of the symptoms and signs of VTE (see below).
- You should seek urgent medical attention if any symptoms of DVT or PE develop.

### Miscarriage or Termination of Pregnancy

- If you have been on LMWH and have a miscarriage or TOP, continue LMWH for 2 weeks after the pregnancy loss.

### Homozygous Factor V Leiden

Regardless of VTE risk score, if you are homozygous for Factor V Leiden, your healthcare team will:
- Arrange an obstetric assessment to plan for delivery and the postpartum period
- Seek haematology advice for postpartum thromboprophylaxis

### Postpartum

- If you are identified as high-risk postpartum but have not been started on LMWH after delivery, your doctor will seek haematology advice for postpartum thromboprophylaxis.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Help

Discuss with your doctor or midwife urgently if you develop symptoms of DVT or pulmonary embolism:

**Symptoms of DVT:**
- Swelling, pain, warmth, or redness in one leg (usually the calf)

**Symptoms of pulmonary embolism:**
- Sudden shortness of breath
- Chest pain (may be worse on breathing in)
- Coughing up blood
- Feeling faint or collapsing

For possible DVT or PE in pregnancy, seek emergency assessment — do not wait.

---

## Contraindications and Precautions to Thromboprophylaxis

Your healthcare provider will assess for factors that may affect the decision to start pharmacological thromboprophylaxis:

**Contraindications:**
- Known hypersensitivity to heparin
- History of or current heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT)
- Significant renal impairment with platelet dysfunction

**Precautions (require specialist guidance):**
- Renal impairment (creatinine clearance less than 30 mL/min)
- Moderate to severe hepatic impairment
- Low platelet count or downward trend in platelets

**Bleeding risk factors:**
- Active antenatal or postpartum bleeding
- Placenta praevia or risk of major haemorrhage
- Inherited or acquired bleeding disorders
- Thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction
- Recent major surgery or central nervous system bleeding
- Use of antiplatelet medications or other drugs affecting clotting
