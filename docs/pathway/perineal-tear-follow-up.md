# Perineal Tear Follow-up

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers follow-up care after a third- or fourth-degree perineal tear sustained during vaginal birth.

---

## Background

A **third-degree tear** involves the anal sphincter muscles. A **fourth-degree tear** involves the anal sphincter muscles (external and internal) and the rectal mucosa.

Third- and fourth-degree tears occur in 1–2% of vaginal births and are more common in first deliveries. After anal sphincter repair, 60–80% of women are asymptomatic at 12 months.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will check for symptoms including:

- Passing wind without control (flatus incontinence)
- An urgent need to reach the toilet before a bowel motion (faecal urgency)
- Inability to control bowel motions (faecal incontinence)

---

## Management

Women who sustained a third- or fourth-degree tear during vaginal delivery require follow-up at **6 weeks** and **6 months** after delivery.

### 6-Week Follow-up

Your doctor may:

1. Review the postnatal discharge summary for perineal tear details
2. Arrange a recall for a 6-month follow-up appointment
3. Check that perineal healing has resolved and there is no faecal incontinence
4. Complete a structured perineal checklist — if issues are identified, refer for physiotherapy assessment (by phone or clinic)
5. Check that cervical screening information is up to date
6. If you have a third- or fourth-degree tear, note this for future pregnancies — early discussion about whether to plan an elective caesarean section or vaginal birth is recommended

### 6-Month Follow-up

Your doctor may:

1. Complete a 6-month perineal checklist (by phone or during a booked appointment)
2. If significant issues are found: perform a pelvic examination, arrange cervical screening if needed, and refer for a non-acute gynaecology assessment
3. If you are asymptomatic: no further follow-up is required unless problems develop
4. If you have a third- or fourth-degree tear: your doctor will recommend obstetric assessment early in any future pregnancy
