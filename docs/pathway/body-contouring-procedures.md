# Body Contouring Procedures

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment of adults being considered for body contouring surgery — primarily excess skin removal after significant weight loss — and what is required to be considered for publicly funded surgery.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a detailed history to understand the impact of excess skin on your life, including:

**Physical symptoms:**
- Severe or persistent intertrigo (skin rash or infection in skin folds)
- Lipodystrophy (abnormal distribution of fat tissue)
- Difficulties with personal hygiene

**Functional and social impact:**
- Difficulty with physical activity, exercise, or wearing clothes
- Social embarrassment or harassment related to excess skin

Your doctor will also check whether the procedure would be an adjunct to other planned reconstructive surgery (e.g., hernia repair).

---

## Eligibility for Publicly Funded Surgery

Body contouring surgery through the public system is not performed for cosmetic appearance alone. Your doctor will assess whether you meet the criteria for each specific procedure.

> **Note:** The public system has extremely limited capacity for body contouring surgery. Even if you meet the eligibility criteria, your case will be reviewed and prioritised, and may be declined if capacity is limited.

### Abdominal Lipectomy, Abdominoplasty, or Apronectomy

Your doctor may consider referral if **all** of the following apply:
- Disabling or persistent physical discomfort
- Severe or persistent intertrigo
- Clinical symptoms persist after treatment of morbid obesity and BMI has stabilised at ≤ 30
- Or: the procedure is required prior to hernia repair or other abdominal surgery
- Or: there are poorly fitting stoma bags due to excess abdominal skin

### Other Skin Excisions (Arm, Thigh, or Buttock Lift)

Your doctor may consider referral if:
- BMI has stabilised at ≤ 30
- Clinical symptoms persist after treatment of morbid obesity

### Bariatric Surgery Patients

If you have had weight loss (bariatric) surgery, you must be at least **2 years post-surgery** and your weight must have been **stable for at least 6 months** before being considered.

---

## Surgical Risk Factors

The following factors may exclude you from publicly funded surgery:

- **Smoking** — you must be a non-smoker
- **BMI ≥ 30** — exceptions may be considered in extenuating circumstances for BMI between 30 and 35, but this is unusual
- **History of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), or cardiovascular disease**

---

## Management

### If You Have Modifiable Risk Factors

Your doctor may first recommend addressing modifiable risk factors before referral:
- Smoking cessation support
- Further weight loss to achieve BMI ≤ 30

Your doctor will reassess once these goals have been achieved.

### If You Meet Eligibility Criteria

Your doctor will refer you to a plastic surgery service for assessment. The referral will include your BMI, clinical reasons for surgery (beyond cosmetic), any surgical risk factors or co-morbidities, clinical photographs, and confirmation of non-smoking status.

### Private Surgery

If you do not meet public criteria, or wish to avoid waiting times, your doctor can discuss a private referral to a plastic surgeon.

---

*Always discuss your specific situation with your doctor. This pathway provides general guidance only and individual circumstances vary.*
