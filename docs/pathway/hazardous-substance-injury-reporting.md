# Hazardous Substance Injury Reporting

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers what counts as a hazardous substance injury in New Zealand, what exposures require formal notification, and what patients should know about the reporting process.

---

## What Is a Hazardous Substance?

A hazardous substance is any substance that is explosive, flammable, oxidising, toxic, corrosive, or harmful to the environment.

Examples of events that require notification include:

- Fireworks injury
- Ingestion of cleaning products or cosmetics
- Carbon monoxide poisoning
- Chemical burns, including all chemical eye injuries
- Lead absorption with a blood lead level of 0.24 micromole/L or higher
- Injury or disease caused by silica exposure
- Environmental contamination by chemicals (e.g. solvents, agrichemical spray-drift, cyanotoxin poisoning)
- Huffing of butane or similar substances
- Occupational and non-occupational exposures, whether intentional or not
- Poisoning from contamination of the environment with chemicals in any form, including gases

It is important to note that exposure to hazardous substances in the workplace may not be a one-time occurrence — it can be a prolonged, gradual process leading to serious illness.

---

## What Is Not Covered

The following are **not** reportable under hazardous substance notification:

- Poisoning or overdose from prescription or over-the-counter medications
- Alcohol consumption (except industrial alcohol)
- Exposure to radioactive materials

---

## Why Notification Happens

Your doctor is legally required under the Hazardous Substances and New Organisms Act 1996 to notify the Medical Officer of Health if they find you are suffering from an injury or disease caused by a hazardous substance. This applies whether you are seen in a hospital or in a general practice setting.

This is a separate process from any ACC claim.

---

## What Patients Should Know About Notification

Your doctor may advise you about the following:

**Who can report:**
- Hazardous substance notification is a legal requirement for clinicians.
- Anyone can report a hazardous substance injury.

**What information is shared:**
- Only injury- or disease-specific information related to the hazardous substance exposure is included in the notification.
- Unrelated health information is not included.
- Information is used primarily for surveillance and injury prevention.
- Your data may be added to a national database to monitor and compare disease rates.

**Public Health follow-up:**
- The National Public Health Service (NPHS) may contact you if they need more information, or to arrange a home visit to collect soil and water samples where relevant.

---

## If You Have Been Exposed to a Poison

For information about poisons, or in case of poisoning, call the **National Poisons Centre on 0800 764 766** (available 24 hours).
