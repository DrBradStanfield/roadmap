# ACC Musculoskeletal Integrated Care (ICP-MSK)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway explains what the ACC ICP-MSK programme is, which injuries may be eligible, and what patients can expect. Your doctor manages the referral process.

---

## What Is the ICP-MSK?

The ACC Integrated Care Pathway for Musculoskeletal injuries (ICP-MSK) is an ACC-funded initiative designed to improve outcomes after a significant injury. It uses an interdisciplinary team approach to provide coordinated, specialist-overseen rehabilitation.

The team may include:

- Physiotherapists
- Orthopaedic surgeons
- Occupational, musculoskeletal, rehabilitation medicine, and pain management specialists
- ICP navigators
- Vocational rehabilitation providers
- Psychological services
- Kaupapa Māori health providers

Benefits of the ICP-MSK include:

- Timely access to specialist assessment, diagnostics, and rehabilitation
- Patient-centred goal setting
- Fully funded rehabilitation (no co-payment for ICP-MSK services)
- Operative and non-operative treatment options
- Cultural support

---

## Eligibility Criteria

To be eligible, the patient must:

1. Have an accepted injury diagnosis from the list below (within the relevant time frame)
2. Require interdisciplinary treatment with specialist oversight and/or surgery to achieve return to work or independence
3. Plan to reside in New Zealand for the duration of treatment

### Knee Injuries

Eligible if the patient has an accepted knee injury within the last 12 months, **or** a previous ACL injury managed conservatively for which they are now considering surgical reconstruction (no time limit).

Accepted diagnoses:

- Fracture involving the tibial or femoral condyle
- Anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) rupture, with or without meniscal tear
- Posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) rupture
- Medial or lateral ligament rupture
- Patellar tendon rupture
- Traumatic patellar dislocation
- Fracture of the patella
- Osteochondral fracture
- Medial or lateral meniscal tear or other internal derangement
- Post-traumatic osteoarthritis (requires ACC acceptance before referral; may be accepted as a work-related gradual process injury for some occupations)
- Previous ACC-funded knee surgery requiring removal of metalware

Note: for meniscal tear or internal derangement, weight-bearing X-rays (AP/Lateral and PA45 Rosenberg views) are required before referral. Joint space narrowing suggesting osteoarthritis as the underlying cause will likely make the patient ineligible.

### Shoulder Injuries

Eligible if the patient has an accepted shoulder injury within the last 12 months, **or** a previous shoulder dislocation requiring further rehabilitation or surgical assessment (no time limit).

Accepted diagnoses:

- Fracture of the clavicle
- Fracture of the humerus (or humeral end of shoulder)
- AC joint dislocation
- Fracture of the glenoid
- Glenohumeral joint dislocation
- Rotator cuff full-thickness tear (rupture), with or without biceps tendon high-grade tear or traumatic labral tear
- Post-traumatic osteoarthritis (requires ACC acceptance before referral)
- Previous ACC-funded shoulder surgery requiring removal of metalware

### Lumbar Spine Injuries

Eligible if the patient has an accepted lumbar spine injury within the last 12 months.

Accepted diagnoses:

- Lumbar disc prolapse or extrusion **with** radiculopathy below the knee
- Lumbar fracture
- Post-traumatic osteoarthritis (requires ACC acceptance before referral)
- Previous ACC-funded lumbar surgery requiring removal of metalware

## What to Expect After Referral

Your doctor will send a referral to an ICP-MSK supplier. After referral:

1. The supplier may conduct an initial phone pre-screen to assess suitability.
2. If suitable, a full triage assessment (typically by a physiotherapist) will be arranged.
3. If found unsuitable, the supplier will notify your doctor, you, and ACC, and recommend alternative services.

Being accepted into the ICP-MSK does not automatically mean you will see a specialist (e.g., orthopaedic surgeon). However, specialist oversight is involved throughout — including triaging, developing management plans, reviewing imaging, and surgical intervention if required.

The ICP-MSK is fully funded. However, any treatment received outside the ICP-MSK while eligibility is being determined will attract the usual co-payment.
