# Adverse Reaction Reporting

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers what counts as a reportable adverse reaction to a medicine, medical device, vaccine, or alternative therapy — and how your doctor documents and reports it.

---

## What Is an Adverse Reaction?

An adverse reaction is an unwanted or harmful effect that occurs after taking a medicine, receiving a vaccine, using a medical device, or using an alternative therapy.

Anyone may report a suspected adverse reaction — patients, family members, and health professionals alike.

---

## Types of Reportable Events

### Adverse Events

These are unexpected or serious reactions that doctors are responsible for reporting:

- Anaphylaxis (severe allergic reaction)
- Blood disorders
- Endocrine (hormonal) disturbances
- Effects on fertility
- Haemorrhage (bleeding) from any site
- Renal (kidney) impairment
- Jaundice
- Eye (ophthalmic) disorders
- Severe central nervous system (CNS) effects
- Rhabdomyolysis (muscle breakdown)
- Severe skin reactions
- Reactions in pregnant women
- Congenital (birth) abnormalities

### Serious Events

An event is classified as "serious" if it results in:

- Death
- Danger to life
- Hospitalisation
- Prolongation of existing hospitalisation
- Significant interruption to daily activities in an adult
- Substantially increased investigation or treatment costs
- Birth defects

---

## What Your Doctor Will Do

If you experience a suspected adverse reaction, your doctor may:

- Document the reaction in your patient record
- Complete a **MedicAlert Clinical Update Form** so the reaction is linked to your identity for future safety
- Register a warning or danger alert against your NHI (National Health Index) number for severe or life-threatening reactions — this alert is accessible to healthcare facilities across New Zealand via the Ministry of Health's Medical Warning System

---

## How Reporting Works (CARM)

Your doctor may submit a report to the **Centre for Adverse Reactions Monitoring (CARM)**, which is part of the NZ Pharmacovigilance Centre. CARM relies on voluntary reporting from health professionals and the public.

When a report is submitted, it typically includes:

- Description of the reaction and when it started
- The suspected medicine, device, or vaccine (including dose, route, dates started and stopped, and batch number if applicable)
- Your current status — recovered, improving, not yet recovered, fatal, or unknown
- Outcome if the medicine was tried again (rechallenge)

CARM sends a response letter to whoever submitted the report.

Reports can be submitted online at [nzphvc.otago.ac.nz/report](https://nzphvc.otago.ac.nz/report/).

---

## When to Seek Help

If you think you are having a serious or life-threatening reaction to a medicine or vaccine right now:

- **Call 111** or go to the emergency department immediately
- Signs of anaphylaxis include throat swelling, difficulty breathing, rapid pulse, collapse, or a widespread rash after taking a medicine or vaccine

For non-urgent suspected reactions, discuss with your doctor at your next appointment. They can assess whether the reaction is likely related to the medicine and whether it should be reported.

---

*Always discuss suspected adverse reactions with your doctor before stopping any prescribed medicine.*
