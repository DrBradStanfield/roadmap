# Increased Haemoglobin

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the investigation of increased haemoglobin (erythrocytosis/polycythaemia) — working through the likely cause and determining urgency.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Assessment

Go to the emergency department or seek urgent medical review if there is:

- Evidence of **thrombosis** (blood clot — e.g. deep vein thrombosis, stroke, pulmonary embolism)
- A **vascular event** (e.g. heart attack, TIA)
- Other haematological emergency signs

These require same-day acute medical assessment.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will typically arrange:

- **Full blood count (FBC)**
- **Renal function**
- **Ferritin**
- **Midstream urine (MSU)**
- **Oxygen saturation**

### Haematocrit Thresholds

The key question is whether haematocrit (the proportion of red blood cells in blood) is elevated:

- **Males:** haematocrit > 0.54 is elevated
- **Females:** haematocrit > 0.48 is elevated

If haematocrit is **not** elevated → monitor with repeat bloods.

If haematocrit **is** elevated, your doctor will investigate further.

---

## Investigation of Elevated Haematocrit

### Step 1 — Look for secondary causes

Your doctor will ask:

- Is there **respiratory disease** (e.g. COPD, sleep apnoea, chronic hypoxia)? These conditions drive the body to produce more red blood cells as a compensatory response.
- Is there **renal disease** (e.g. renal cell carcinoma, renal cysts, polycystic kidney disease)? The kidneys produce erythropoietin (EPO), so renal pathology can cause excess red cell production.

If a secondary cause is found → treat and manage the underlying condition, and monitor haematocrit.

### Step 2 — If no secondary cause found

If the haematocrit remains elevated for **more than 2 months** with no clear secondary cause, your doctor will arrange:

- **EPO (erythropoietin) level**
- **JAK2 mutation testing**

These tests help distinguish between polycythaemia vera (a bone marrow disorder) and other causes.

---

## Interpretation of EPO and JAK2

| Result | Interpretation |
|--------|----------------|
| Low EPO + JAK2 mutation positive | Strongly suggests **polycythaemia vera** → refer to haematologist |
| Normal/high EPO + JAK2 negative | Polycythaemia vera unlikely → investigate other causes |

### If polycythaemia vera is probable

Your doctor will refer you to a **haematologist** for further assessment and management. Polycythaemia vera is a myeloproliferative neoplasm that requires specialist oversight — treatment options include venesection (bloodletting), aspirin, and sometimes cytoreductive therapy.

### If polycythaemia vera is unlikely

Your doctor will look for other causes, which may include:

- **Secondary erythrocytosis** from a cause not yet identified
- **Relative/apparent polycythaemia** (reduced plasma volume — e.g. dehydration, smoking, diuretic use)
- Less common causes: EPO-secreting tumours, high-altitude living, congenital high-affinity haemoglobin variants

---

## Monitoring

For haematocrit that is mildly elevated or where a cause is being investigated, your doctor will arrange follow-up blood tests to monitor trends over time.
