# Cervical Shock

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers recognition and immediate management of cervical shock — a vasovagal reaction that can occur during or after procedures involving the cervix.

---

## Background

Cervical shock refers to a prolonged vasovagal reaction caused by dilatation or instrumentation of the cervix. It can occur during or within a few minutes of:

- Taking a pipelle biopsy
- Inserting an intrauterine device (IUD/IUCD)
- Products of conception becoming stuck within the cervical canal during a miscarriage

Recovery typically takes 20 to 30 minutes.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

Call an ambulance if:

- Cervical shock is severe or prolonged
- The patient is unresponsive to treatment
- Persistent bradycardia (pulse < 60 beats per minute) after atropine
- Persistent hypotension (systolic blood pressure < 90 mmHg) after atropine

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about symptoms, which can include:

- Light-headedness or dizziness
- Nausea
- Feeling unwell or agitated

Signs to check for:

- Pale and sweaty appearance
- Thready, slow pulse
- Hypotension (low blood pressure) — this may progress to loss of consciousness or tonic-clonic movements

Other diagnoses to consider include anaphylaxis, panic attack, or hyperventilation.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following steps:

1. Lie the patient flat
2. Remove the stimulus — instruments and IUCD (if not fully inserted), or products of conception from the cervical os during a miscarriage
3. Elevate the patient's legs
4. Give high-flow oxygen
5. If the patient fails to improve or the reaction is prolonged:
   - Call for help and initiate emergency assessment (airway, breathing, circulation)
   - Remove the IUD if not already done — clinical judgement is required based on symptoms and findings
6. If pulse < 40 beats per minute, or systolic blood pressure < 90 mmHg:
   - Insert an intravenous (IV) line
   - Give **atropine 0.6 mg** by slow IV injection, followed by a 10 mL saline flush
   - If response is not satisfactory, repeat every 3 to 5 minutes, up to a total dose of **3 mg**
   - Consider an IV fluid bolus of 500 to 1000 mL normal saline
   - Monitor and record pulse, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation; consider ECG
   - Record the dose and time of all medications
7. Once pulse and blood pressure recover, slowly raise the patient to a seated position
8. Allow 20 to 30 minutes for full recovery
