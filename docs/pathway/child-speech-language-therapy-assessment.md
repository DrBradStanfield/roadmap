# Child Speech Language Therapy Assessment

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the scope of speech language therapy for children, including what difficulties are assessed and where families can find support.

---

## What Speech Language Therapists Treat

Speech language therapists (SLTs) work with children who have the following difficulties or disorders:

- **Speech difficulties or delays** — problems with producing speech sounds clearly
- **Receptive language difficulties** — difficulty understanding spoken language
- **Expressive language difficulties** — difficulty using language to communicate
- **Stuttering (fluency disorders)**
- **Voice difficulties**
- **Specific speech disorders** — including developmental verbal dyspraxia and childhood apraxia of speech, or speech difficulties associated with hearing impairment
- **Complex needs** — such as those associated with Down syndrome or cerebral palsy
- **Social communication difficulties** — such as those associated with autistic spectrum disorder
- **Feeding and swallowing difficulties (dysphagia)**
- **Reading and writing difficulties**

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will consider:

- The nature and duration of the speech or language concern
- Impact on the child's socialisation, ability to learn, and participation in family life
- Outcomes of any hearing tests (hearing assessment is often an important first step)
- Any broader developmental concerns
- For feeding difficulties: growth, current feeding arrangements, concerns about swallowing or aspiration, and history of respiratory infections or gastro-oesophageal reflux

A hearing test is often arranged before or alongside a speech language therapy referral, as hearing loss is a common contributor to speech and language delay.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider:

- **Referral to speech language therapy** — the appropriate service depends on the child's age, diagnosis, and the nature of the difficulty. Your doctor will determine the most appropriate referral pathway.
- **Hearing assessment** — if not already completed, to rule out hearing loss as a contributing factor
- **Monitoring with review** — for mild or borderline concerns, a watchful waiting approach with a planned review may be appropriate
- **Private speech language therapy** — available without a referral if families wish to access assessment and treatment directly

For children with isolated speech or language difficulties (no other disability or developmental condition), the Ministry of Education Early Intervention Service is typically the first point of contact rather than hospital-based services.
