# Non-suicidal Deliberate Self-harm

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of non-suicidal deliberate self-harm. If self-harm appears to have suicidal intent, seek urgent medical assessment immediately.

---

## When to Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency department or call an ambulance if:

- The self-harm has caused a potentially life-threatening injury
- There is significant or serious physical injury requiring urgent treatment

---

## Background

Self-harm is when somebody intentionally damages or injures their body. It is a way of coping with or expressing overwhelming emotional distress.

Key points:

- Self-harm is a behaviour, not a disorder
- It takes many forms including poisoning, overdoses, cutting, burning, and other physical injury
- It may or may not involve suicidal intent
- It can cause disability or death, and may be a sign of underlying mental illness
- It often occurs in the context of a recent stress, such as relationship conflict

Approximately 90% of people who present to hospital with self-harm have a mental disorder — commonly depression, psychosis, anxiety, eating disorder, substance abuse disorder, or personality disorder.

Assessment has several purposes: understanding the nature and frequency of self-harm and its underlying motivation, screening for mental disorder, engaging with appropriate treatments and supports, and preventing future recurrence.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess you over one or more appointments. Assessment often involves both a GP and a practice nurse.

### ABCS Approach

Your doctor will take a history using the ABCS framework:

**Antecedents** — what happened before:
- Recent relationship breakdown or rejection
- Changes to personal circumstances (employment, financial, occupational)
- Other risky or impulsive behaviour
- Current sexual, physical, or emotional abuse
- Peer group influences, including social media

**Behaviours** — details of the self-harm:
- Previous or current history of self-harm or suicide attempts
- Method (e.g. cutting, burning, poisoning, overdose, asphyxiation)
- Frequency, intensity, and duration
- Whether help was sought after the event, or whether it was concealed

**Consequences** — the role self-harm plays:
- Relieving emotional distress, communicating distress, giving a sense of control, reducing anxiety or anger, self-punishment, reducing numbness or dissociation
- Care or attention received
- Injuries or scars

**Supports and strategies** — what helps:
- Does the person have a support network?
- What previous strategies have helped avoid or interrupt self-harm?

### Suicidality Assessment

Your doctor will assess for suicidal thoughts and intent as a routine part of this evaluation.

### Underlying Mental Health Conditions

Your doctor will consider whether there is an underlying condition, including:

- Borderline personality disorder
- Anxiety disorder
- Depression
- Eating disorder
- Early psychosis
- Substance abuse
- Social exclusion (e.g. bullying, minority group membership, gender identity concerns)
- Impact of chronic physical illness (e.g. diabetes, cancer, chronic pain)
- Recent serious diagnosis (e.g. cancer, stroke, early dementia)

### Protective Factors

Your doctor will ask about factors that support resilience and safety:

- Family connections, particularly dependent children
- Strong cultural identity
- Spiritual beliefs
- Sense of belonging to a community
- Ability to make a valued contribution
- Supportive friendships
- Developing a skill, volunteering, or contributing to a cause

### Young People

In adolescents, your doctor may use the HEeADSSSSS assessment tool, which covers home, education, eating, activities, drugs, sexuality, suicide/self-harm, safety, and screens.

---

## Management

Your doctor will tailor management to your individual situation.

### Safety Planning

Your doctor will work with you to develop a self-harm emergency plan and a contact list for crisis situations.

### Confidentiality and Limits

Your doctor will explain the limits of confidentiality. Information is kept private unless there is a serious and imminent risk of harm to you or others — in which case your doctor will take steps to protect your safety, and will discuss this with you wherever possible.

### Building a Therapeutic Relationship

Your doctor will aim to be open and non-judgemental, validate your distress, explore the impact of self-harm, and communicate hope for the future.

### Coping Strategies

Your doctor may discuss and offer information about alternative coping mechanisms, such as:

- Distraction techniques and ways to keep occupied
- Self-soothing strategies
- Problem-solving approaches
- Conflict resolution skills
- Mindful breathing

### Managing Underlying Conditions

Your doctor will manage any identified underlying mental health condition (e.g. depression, anxiety, eating disorder, substance use) according to appropriate clinical guidelines.

### For Young People

If you are under 18 with chronic self-harm and distress, your doctor may refer you to youth mental health counselling and therapy, including e-therapy and helpline support.

### When a Specialist Assessment May Be Needed

Your doctor may consider referral for specialist mental health assessment if there is:

- Significant risk of suicide or imminent serious self-harm
- Difficulty confirming an underlying diagnosis
- Suspected borderline personality disorder
- Suspected psychosis
- Severe or treatment-resistant major mood disorder
- Recurrent episodes of high-risk self-harm (with risk of accidental severe injury or death)
