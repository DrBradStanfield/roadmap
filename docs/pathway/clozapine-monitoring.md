# Clozapine Monitoring

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway is for adults taking clozapine who have been transferred to general practice for ongoing monitoring. Clozapine can only be initiated by a specialist psychiatrist and is subject to strict blood monitoring requirements.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

Call an ambulance immediately if:

- **Life-threatening constipation** — no bowel motions for 5 or more days
- **Neutropenic sepsis** — low white cell count with fever or signs of infection
- **Severe cardiomyopathy** — new or worsening heart failure symptoms
- **Uncontrolled seizures**

---

## Background

Clozapine is a highly effective atypical antipsychotic. It is the only antipsychotic associated with a reduced risk of suicide, and one of only two drugs in psychiatry that reduce mortality.

It is reserved for treatment-resistant psychosis because of its wide range of serious potential side effects — including a rare but serious risk of agranulocytosis (dangerously low white blood cell count). Serious adverse effects are most likely in the first 18 weeks of treatment or during dose changes.

**Stopping clozapine — even for 48 hours — carries a high risk of relapse.** Restarting after a break re-exposes the patient to the higher risks of the initiation period, and after each relapse clozapine may be less effective when reintroduced. This makes continuity of treatment essential.

Patients generally remain on clozapine indefinitely unless there is no improvement in symptoms, severe side effects occur, or the patient withdraws consent.

## Assessment

Your doctor will review you at least every 3 months. Each review includes:

### Physical Health Review

Your doctor will ask about:
- Breathlessness, ankle swelling, worsening fatigue, or sleeping propped up (possible heart symptoms)
- Bowel motion frequency and character — constipation is under-reported and can be life-threatening on clozapine
- Excessive saliva (hypersalivation)
- Thirst, frequent urination, weight loss, or tiredness (possible high blood sugar)
- Loss of consciousness
- Signs or symptoms of infection
- Indigestion
- Weight gain

Physical checks include: temperature, weight, waist circumference, BMI, blood pressure (lying and standing), pulse, and abdominal examination.

### Mental Health Review

Your doctor will ask about:
- Psychotic symptoms — hallucinations, delusions, disorganised thinking
- Negative symptoms — withdrawal, loss of motivation, flat mood
- Early warning signs of deterioration: changes in mood, cognition, unusual thinking, odd behaviour, sleep disturbance, social withdrawal, or decline in self-care

### Investigations — Blood Monitoring Schedule

Regular blood tests are required for as long as you take clozapine, and for 4 weeks after stopping.

| Investigation | Frequency |
|---|---|
| Full blood count (FBC) | Every 4 weeks (more often if abnormal) |
| ECG | Annually; repeat if dose changes or new medications started |
| Weight, waist circumference, BMI | At 3 months, 6 months, then annually |
| Lipids | At 6 months, then annually (every 6 months if high cardiovascular risk) |
| HbA1c | At 6 months, then annually (every 6 months if high cardiovascular risk) |
| Liver function | At 3 months, 6 months, then annually |
| Renal function | Annually |
| Clozapine serum level | Annually |

Clozapine blood levels should be drawn at least 12 hours after the last dose. For most patients, the therapeutic range is below 2000 nmol/L. Levels above 2000 nmol/L increase the risk of sedation; levels above 3000 nmol/L increase the risk of seizures.

### Prescribing Review

At each visit your doctor will also check:

- **Compliance** — missed doses for 48 hours or more require urgent mental health input
- **Smoking status** — starting or stopping smoking (or switching to vaping) significantly changes clozapine metabolism and may require a dose adjustment. Note: nicotine patches and vaping do not affect clozapine levels — it is the acryl carbon in tobacco smoke that matters
- **Caffeine intake** — caffeine can raise clozapine levels
- **New medications** — clozapine has significant drug interactions. Common ones to be aware of:
  - Avoid or use with caution: co-trimoxazole, carbimazole, carbamazepine (risk of agranulocytosis)
  - Elevate clozapine levels: erythromycin, itraconazole, ketoconazole, ciprofloxacin
  - Tramadol (increased seizure risk)
  - Use caution with: antihypertensives, benzodiazepines, alcohol, antihistamines, and medications that cause constipation (codeine, iron, anticholinergics)

## Management

Management of problems that arise is always guided by your doctor in discussion with the mental health team. Your doctor may consider the following approaches:

### Constipation

Constipation is the most serious and most commonly fatal side effect of clozapine — it causes gut hypomotility and can lead to bowel obstruction, perforation, or toxic megacolon.

Your doctor may:
- Check for signs of serious bowel pathology (severe abdominal pain, distension, vomiting, absence of bowel sounds, signs of sepsis)
- Arrange urgent surgical assessment if signs of serious bowel pathology are present
- Treat early and aggressively with laxatives:
  - First-line: docusate/senna (Laxsol) where impaction is not suspected
  - Macrogols (e.g., Molaxole)
  - Lactulose (requires adequate fluid intake)
- Consider enemas if no improvement (phosphate or sodium citrate). Note: enemas are contraindicated in bowel obstruction
- Avoid prescribing anti-diarrhoeal medications — diarrhoea may be overflow from impaction
- Seek mental health advice about reducing or pausing clozapine if no bowel motion for 4 or more days

Advise your practice if you have had no bowel motions for 2 days.

**Seek emergency care** if no bowel motion for 5 or more days despite laxatives and enemas.

### Neutropenia (Low White Cell Count)

Blood monitoring is used to detect this early. If you develop flu-like symptoms, sore throat, or fever, your doctor will arrange an urgent FBC immediately.

FBC monitoring guide:

| Status | White cell count | Neutrophil count | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green | ≥ 3.5 × 10⁹/L AND | ≥ 2 × 10⁹/L | Continue clozapine |
| Amber | 3.0–3.5 × 10⁹/L OR | 1.5–2.0 × 10⁹/L | Continue, check FBC twice weekly until stable |
| Red | < 3.0 × 10⁹/L OR | < 1.5 × 10⁹/L | Stop clozapine immediately; urgent FBC and mental health advice |

If neutropenic sepsis (neutrophils below 1.0 × 10⁹/L with fever or signs of sepsis), an ambulance is required.

### Cardiomyopathy and Myocarditis

Cardiomyopathy is a rare but ongoing risk after the first 12 weeks of treatment. Symptoms resemble heart failure:
- New or worsening fatigue or breathlessness
- Ankle oedema
- Orthopnoea (needing to sleep propped up)
- Pulmonary oedema or elevated jugular venous pressure
- Abnormal heart sounds, ECG changes, or enlarged heart on chest X-ray

Cardiomyopathy is irreversible — your doctor will seek psychiatric advice and may need to discontinue treatment.

**Severe cardiomyopathy requires emergency assessment.**

### Hypersalivation

Excessive saliva usually improves over weeks to months. If it persists or worsens, your doctor may consider:
- Non-pharmacological measures: sugar-free gum, adjusted pillow position
- Medications (all off-label, and only after mental health advice):
  - First-line: ipratropium nasal spray 0.03% sublingually, 1–2 sprays at night as needed
  - Second-line: atropine 1% eye drops (3–6 drops in water) gargled or dropped onto the tongue, up to 3 times daily
  - Third-line (consultant psychiatrist input required): hyoscine patch or benzatropine

### Hyperglycaemia (High Blood Sugar)

On rare occasions, severe hyperglycaemia — including diabetic ketoacidosis — has been reported. Your doctor will manage this as they would for diabetes.

### Tachycardia (Fast Heart Rate)

Around 1 in 3 patients on clozapine have a stable, ongoing tachycardia. Your doctor will record your baseline heart rate. A new or worsening tachycardia warrants psychiatric consultation.

### Metabolic Effects

Clozapine can cause weight gain, raised blood sugar, abnormal cholesterol (especially triglycerides and HDL), and new-onset diabetes. Regular monitoring and lifestyle advice are important.

### Sedation

Sedation usually improves over time. Your doctor may encourage good sleep habits, reduce other sedating medications where possible, and consider dose timing adjustments in consultation with your psychiatrist.

### Liver Enzyme Abnormalities

Elevated liver enzymes (AST, ALT) are most common in the first 4–5 weeks and are usually transient. If clozapine is suspected as the cause, your doctor will seek mental health advice.

### Urinary Incontinence and Retention

Your doctor may advise avoiding fluids in the evening and voiding before sleep. Desmopressin may be considered after mental health advice.

### Indigestion and Nausea

Your doctor may prescribe a proton pump inhibitor. Pantoprazole is preferred as it does not interact with clozapine.

### Postural Hypotension or Hypertension

More likely during dose initiation or rapid increases. Your doctor will seek mental health advice about management.

### Seizures

Occur in about 3% of patients, usually during rapid dose increases or at higher doses (above 600 mg). If seizures occur, hospital assessment is required. Prophylactic antiepileptic medication (e.g., lamotrigine or sodium valproate) is considered when clozapine levels exceed 1800 nmol/L.

### Sexual Dysfunction

Clozapine generally causes lower rates of sexual dysfunction than other antipsychotics, but side effects including erectile and ejaculatory problems can occur. Your doctor will discuss with mental health if this is significant.

### Pregnancy

If you are planning a pregnancy or become pregnant while taking clozapine, your doctor will arrange discussion with your psychiatrist or a maternal mental health service. Breastfeeding is not recommended as clozapine passes into breast milk.

### Missed Doses

- A dose can be taken within 6 hours of the usual time (for twice-daily dosing) or 12 hours (for once-daily dosing)
- Do not double up on doses
- If you have missed fewer than 48 hours of doses, you can restart at the usual dose
- **If you have missed 48 hours or more, seek mental health advice before restarting** — restarting requires careful monitoring

## When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention

Contact your doctor or seek emergency care if you experience:

**Bowel symptoms:**
- Reduced frequency of bowel motions
- Abdominal pain or nausea/vomiting

**Infection symptoms:**
- Flu-like symptoms, fever, or sore throat
- COVID-19 positive test (clozapine levels can rise during COVID-19 infection)

**Cardiac symptoms:**
- Chest pain, shortness of breath, palpitations
- Decreased exercise tolerance or ankle swelling

**Thromboembolism:**
- Leg pain or swelling
- Sudden shortness of breath
