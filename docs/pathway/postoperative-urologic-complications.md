# Postoperative Urologic Complications

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the most common problems that can arise after urologic procedures — including transurethral prostate resection (TURP), bladder tumour resection (TURBT), prostate biopsy (TRUS), urethrotomy, and ureteroscopy.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency department or call an ambulance if, after any urologic procedure, you develop:

- Fever, chills, feeling severely unwell, low blood pressure, or a racing heart rate — these may be signs of **sepsis**, which is a medical emergency
- An inability to pass urine at all
- Dark red urine with large clots causing difficulty urinating (clot retention)
- Signs of severe blood loss (dizziness, pallor, weakness)

---

## Background

Urologic procedures that can lead to postoperative complications include:

- **TURP** — transurethral endoscopic prostate resection for obstruction
- **TURBT** — transurethral endoscopic bladder tumour resection
- **TRUS biopsy** — transrectal ultrasound-guided prostate biopsy
- **Optical urethrotomy** — transurethral incision of urethral stricture
- **Ureteroscopy** — endoscopic procedure for kidney or ureteric stone or tumour

Bleeding is common in the first 6 weeks after any urologic intervention, especially TURP. Most complications settle with time, but some require medical review.

---

## Haematuria (Blood in the Urine)

Blood in the urine is a common complication, particularly in the first 6 weeks after surgery.

Your doctor will assess:

- Whether bleeding is mild (rose-coloured urine) or severe (dark red urine, often with clots)
- Whether there are signs of significant blood loss (anaemia)
- Whether you are taking anticoagulants (blood thinners) — your doctor may adjust the dose if it is safe to do so, and may check your clotting with a blood test (INR, APTT, fibrinogen)

Your doctor may consider:

- Checking your haemoglobin (blood count) to assess blood loss
- Arranging a urology assessment if haematuria continues beyond 6 weeks and your haemoglobin has dropped significantly (more than 10 g/L)
- Seeking urology advice if there is ongoing concern about bleeding at any stage

---

## Infection / Dysuria

Burning or discomfort when urinating is common after urologic procedures. This can be due to urethral irritation from the procedure rather than true infection.

Your doctor will:

- Test your urine with a dipstick and send it for microscopy and culture

Your doctor may consider:

- A urinary alkaliniser such as **citrate sodium + citric acid + sodium bicarbonate + tartaric acid** (e.g. Ural sachets) to relieve irritation symptoms
- If alkalinisers do not help, managing as a urinary tract infection (UTI)
- **Urgent urology assessment** if you have signs of sepsis (fever, low blood pressure, fast heart rate) — especially after TRUS prostate biopsy, where infection risk is higher

---

## Flow Reduction (Difficulty Passing Urine)

Some reduction in urinary flow is common after urologic procedures, but severe difficulty may indicate a significant obstruction.

Your doctor will:

- Test your urine for infection

Your doctor may consider:

- Catheterisation if you have a severe feeling of incomplete emptying or cannot pass urine at all
- Whether frequent, urgent, small-volume urination suggests bladder overactivity rather than obstruction (see below)
- A non-acute urology assessment if flow problems continue beyond 3 months

---

## Bladder Overactivity

Frequency, urgency, and mild urge incontinence (leaking before reaching the toilet) are common after urinary tract procedures. These symptoms usually settle over time with reassurance.

Your doctor will:

- Test your urine for infection
- Examine your abdomen to check for urinary retention (urine not emptying properly)

Your doctor may consider:

- **Solifenacin succinate** — a bladder-calming medication started at the lowest dose and increased slowly to minimise side effects
- A non-acute urology assessment if symptoms persist beyond 3 months

---

## Catheter Problems

The most common catheter issues after urologic procedures are:

- **Leakage** around the catheter
- **Blockage and encrustation** — the catheter becoming blocked, often with mucus or debris

These problems should always be discussed with your doctor or nurse. Do not attempt to flush or manipulate a blocked catheter without medical guidance.

---

## When to See Your Doctor

See your doctor or seek medical advice if:

- You develop fever or signs of infection after any urologic procedure — this is urgent
- Urine remains visibly bloody beyond 6 weeks
- You cannot pass urine
- Urinary flow problems or bladder overactivity symptoms persist beyond 3 months

This information is a general guide only. Your doctor will tailor assessment and management to your specific situation, procedure, and medical history.
