# Pipelle Biopsy Procedure

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the pipelle endometrial biopsy — a procedure in which a small sample of the lining of the uterus (endometrium) is taken using a thin, flexible tube. It is performed by a trained clinician to investigate abnormal uterine bleeding and related conditions.

---

## Indications

A pipelle biopsy takes a limited sample of the endometrium and is only indicated as part of a full clinical assessment, in the following situations:

- Heavy menstrual bleeding
- Post-coital bleeding
- Postmenopausal bleeding
- Endometrial cells found on a cervical smear
- Perimenopausal bleeding while on menopause hormone therapy (MHT)
- Incidental ultrasound finding of an abnormal endometrium

---

## Contraindications

Your doctor will check for reasons the procedure should not go ahead:

**Absolute contraindications:**
- Pregnancy
- Cervicitis or endometritis (inflammation or infection of the cervix or uterine lining)
- Uterine infection, such as pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)
- Blood disorders (dyscrasias)
- Fever

**Relative contraindications:**
- A nearly or completely closed (stenosed) cervical os — insertion may not be possible
- Rheumatic heart disease — antibiotic prophylaxis may be needed before the procedure

Note: Patients with coagulation disorders or on anticoagulant medication are not automatically excluded.

---

## Before the Procedure

Your doctor will:

- Review the indication and confirm you have had a relevant ultrasound
- Discuss the procedure with you and obtain your informed consent — including the risks of pelvic infection, uterine cramps, and vasovagal (fainting) reaction
- Recommend taking an NSAID (such as ibuprofen) 1–2 hours before the appointment to reduce cramping during the procedure
- Review how to manage cervical shock, which can rarely occur during or after the procedure

---

## The Procedure

Your doctor will follow these steps:

1. **Review the ultrasound** — confirm the size and position of your uterus from the scan report
2. **Check vitals** — measure and record your baseline pulse
3. **Bimanual examination** — confirm the size and position of the uterus by internal examination
4. **Insert speculum** — a vaginal speculum is inserted to visualise the cervix
5. **Prepare the cervix** — the cervix is cleansed with normal saline
6. **Gain traction** — the cervix is gently held with a small instrument (tenaculum) to straighten the cervical canal
7. **Insert the pipelle** — the thin sampling tube is gently introduced until it reaches the top of the uterus (fundus). There may be resistance at the internal os (at around 2–4 cm)
8. **Document cavity length** — the uterine cavity length is noted from the markings on the pipelle. Average length is around 7–8 cm
9. **Take the sample** — a vacuum is created by withdrawing the internal piston to the end of the tube. The pipelle is moved back and forth 3–4 times inside the cavity while being rotated, then removed
10. **Collect the specimen** — the sample is expelled into a formalin-filled pot. The specimen is checked — tissue should hold together and remain distinct from blood
11. **Finish** — the tenaculum is removed, any cervical bleeding is managed with brief pressure, and the speculum is removed. You will be allowed to recover before getting up

If the procedure is too difficult or painful at any stage, your doctor may abandon it and arrange a specialist review.

---

## After the Procedure

- Remain lying down for a few minutes after the procedure
- Avoid intercourse and tampons for 48 hours
- Contact your doctor if you develop a fever or persistent cramping

---

## Results and Management

Your doctor will discuss the results with you once they are available:

- **Abnormal results** (including endometrial hyperplasia — simple or complex — or malignancy): Your doctor will refer you to a gynaecologist for further assessment
- **Normal or benign results**: Further management depends on the original reason the biopsy was performed (e.g., abnormal uterine bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding), and your doctor will guide next steps based on the relevant pathway
- **Inadequate sample**: If insufficient tissue was obtained, your doctor may discuss whether the procedure should be repeated or whether specialist referral is appropriate
