# Cyclophosphamide (Oral) Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This guidance supports GPs in the ongoing management of oral cyclophosphamide, after it has been initiated by a specialist. It applies to **rheumatology and autoimmune conditions only** — doses and advice differ for haematology, nephrology, and oncology conditions.

---

## Background

Cyclophosphamide is an anti-neoplastic alkylating agent with immunosuppressive properties. It is available as 50 mg tablets.

**Usual dose:** 50–250 mg daily (up to 2 mg/kg/day). Lower doses are used for autoimmune conditions than for malignancies.

**Response:** Generally occurs within 6–12 weeks. If there is no response by 12 weeks at maximum doses, the drug should be discontinued.

**Duration:** For rheumatology patients, therapy is typically 3–6 months due to cumulative toxicity.

**Indications:**
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Vasculitis and connective tissue disease with major organ involvement (e.g. SLE)
- Leukaemia, lymphoma, and solid tumours (specialist-managed)

**Contraindications:**
- Haemorrhagic cystitis
- Life-threatening infections
- Pregnancy or breastfeeding

**Before starting**, specialists will confirm baseline FBC, sodium, potassium, creatinine, LFTs, urinalysis, and hepatitis B/C serology and HIV.

---

## Red Flags — Stop Drug and Seek Urgent Specialist Advice

Contact your specialist immediately if any of the following occur:

- **Haemorrhagic cystitis** (blood in urine, painful urination)
- **Leukopenia** (WBC less than 4.0 × 10⁹/L)
- **Lymphopenia** (lymphocytes less than 0.3 × 10⁹/L)

---

## Monitoring

Your doctor will arrange regular monitoring as directed by the specialist. This typically involves:

- **FBC and differential, creatinine, AST, ALT, urinalysis** — frequency determined by specialist, often every 2 weeks for the first 6 weeks, then monthly
- **Dose adjustment or cessation** if recurrent infections, leukopenia (WBC < 4.0 × 10⁹/L), or neutropenia (neutrophils < 2.0 × 10⁹/L)
- **Cervical screening** — women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 years on long-term immunosuppression should attend 3-yearly cervical screening (increased HPV risk). If aged 20–24 years, sexually active, and on immunosuppressants for more than 5 years, consider a single HPV test. Refer all those with any HPV detected to colposcopy.

---

## Precautions

Your doctor will exercise caution in the presence of:

- Severe bone marrow depression
- Poor nutritional state
- Renal or hepatic impairment
- Lymphopenia

---

## Adverse Effects and Management

| System | Adverse Effect | Action |
|---|---|---|
| General | Nausea, vomiting | Anti-emetics. If persists, stop drug and consult specialist. |
| General | Mucosal ulceration | If persists, stop drug and consult specialist. |
| Skin | Pigmentation of palms, soles, nails | Monitor. Advise sun protection. |
| Skin | Alopecia (may progress to baldness with higher doses) | Reversible with discontinuation. Regrowth expected but may differ in texture and colour. |
| Lung | Interstitial pulmonary fibrosis | Consult specialist. |
| Urinary tract | Haemorrhagic cystitis | Immediately stop drug and consult specialist. |
| Kidney | Fluid retention | Consider adding diuretic. |
| Kidney | Creatinine > 120 µmol/L | Reduce dose. Consult specialist. |
| Blood | Leukopenia (< 4.0 × 10⁹/L), neutropenia (< 2.0 × 10⁹/L), lymphopenia (< 0.3 × 10⁹/L) | Stop drug. Consult specialist. |
| Heart | Cardiotoxicity | Stop drug. Consult specialist. |
| Liver | Elevated LFTs | Reduce dose. |
| Nervous system | Progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) — rare | Stop drug. Consult specialist. |

---

## Pneumocystis Prophylaxis

*Pneumocystis jirovecii* pneumonia (PCP) prophylaxis is required. Your doctor will prescribe **trimethoprim + sulfamethoxazole (co-trimoxazole) 480 mg daily**.

---

## Vaccinations

Your doctor may consider and recommend the following:

- **Annual influenza vaccine** — recommended
- **COVID-19 vaccination** — recommended at any time offered. Oral cyclophosphamide does not need to be paused.
- **Pneumococcal vaccination** — recommended
- **Hepatitis B vaccination** — consider in at-risk individuals
- **Live vaccines — avoid.** This includes MMR, oral rotavirus, oral polio, oral typhoid, BCG, varicella zoster (chickenpox and Zostavax), and yellow fever.

---

## Drug Interactions

Your doctor will check for potential interactions. Key interactions include:

- **Neuromuscular blocking agents and skeletal muscle relaxants** — prolonged activity, as cyclophosphamide decreases serum pseudocholinesterase levels.

For a full interaction check, your doctor may consult a drug reference guide or Christchurch Medicines Information on (03) 364-0900.

---

## Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Cyclophosphamide is an **absolute contraindication** in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

- Effective contraception is required for **1 month before treatment and for 2 years after**.
- Breastfeeding must be avoided during treatment.

---

## Surgery

If surgery is planned, your doctor will discuss with both the surgical team and the prescribing specialist, as cyclophosphamide may need to be discontinued before the procedure.
