# Hip Imaging in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when and why hip imaging is arranged in children — including which conditions require urgent assessment, which warrant X-ray, and which require ultrasound.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Assessment Immediately

If any of the following are suspected, **do not delay** — your doctor will arrange acute hospital assessment rather than arranging imaging first:

- **Septic arthritis** — bacterial infection inside the hip joint. A medical emergency.
- **Osteomyelitis** — bone infection.
- **Slipped upper femoral epiphysis (SUFE)** — the ball of the hip joint slips off the thigh bone at the growth plate. Can be subtle; your doctor will have a low threshold to seek specialist advice even if the X-ray looks normal.

---

## Conditions That May Require Hip Imaging

### Slipped Upper Femoral Epiphysis (SUFE)

- If SUFE is suspected but not acute, your doctor may arrange a same-day hip X-ray (AP pelvis and frog lateral views).
- Very early slips can be subtle and missed on X-ray — specialist review may still be recommended even with a normal result.

### Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH)

DDH is abnormal development of the hip joint. Imaging approach depends on the child's age.

**X-ray** is used in children aged 4 months or older who have not previously had hip imaging, and who have:

- Risk factors for DDH (see below), or
- Examination findings suggestive of DDH:
  - Limited hip abduction
  - Leg length discrepancy
  - Abnormal, waddling gait

**Ultrasound** is used in younger infants (between 4 weeks and 5 months) who have not previously been scanned, and who have:

- Abnormal clinical examination (e.g., limited hip abduction, especially if asymmetrical, or leg length discrepancy), or
- One or more risk factors for DDH

**Risk factors for DDH include:**
- Breech presentation at any time in the third trimester
- Strong family history of DDH (especially in a parent or sibling)
- Intrauterine "packaging" problems:
  - Oligohydramnios
  - Bicornuate uterus
  - Birth weight of 4000 grams or more
  - Club foot
  - Metatarsus adductus
  - Torticollis
  - Dislocated knee

**Soft signs for DDH** (less specific — more than 95% of babies with these signs will have normal hips):
- Asymmetric thigh or buttock creases
- Clicky hips
- Slight limb length differences that may be hard to detect consistently

If a dislocated or dislocatable hip is detected on clinical examination (Ortolani or Barlow test with a distinct clunk) in a child younger than 3 months, your doctor will seek paediatric orthopaedic advice before arranging imaging.

### Perthes Disease

Perthes disease is a condition where the blood supply to the head of the femur is disrupted, causing bone breakdown and remodelling. It typically affects children aged 4 to 8 years.

Your doctor may consider Perthes disease if a child presents with:
- Pain in the hip, groin, or knee — especially when weight bearing
- A limp that starts occasionally but becomes more progressive over time
- Reluctance to put weight on the leg

Your doctor may arrange an AP hip and frog leg lateral X-ray if Perthes disease is suspected.

### Transient Synovitis (Irritable Hip)

Transient synovitis is a common cause of hip pain in children, usually seen in those younger than 5 years (though it can occur up to age 10 or older).

Key features:
- Often follows a viral illness by one to two weeks
- Child is usually afebrile and otherwise well
- The child is more likely to be limping than completely refusing to weight bear — and may bear weight after simple pain relief
- Mild to moderate reduction in hip movement, particularly internal rotation

Your doctor may arrange a hip X-ray to help exclude more serious conditions, since transient synovitis can be difficult to distinguish from septic arthritis in the early stages. It is usually a diagnosis of exclusion.

Transient synovitis is a benign, self-limiting condition.

### Apophysitis

Inflammation or stress injury to the growth plate area of the hip. Your doctor may consider imaging if:
- Pain has been present for more than one week
- Pain is made worse by activity
- Other causes have been excluded but the diagnosis remains uncertain

### Knee Pain Without Knee Pathology

Hip problems can sometimes cause referred pain felt in the knee. If a child has knee pain but no evidence of a knee problem on examination, your doctor may arrange a hip X-ray (AP pelvis and frog lateral views) to look for hip pathology.

### Unexplained Bony Swelling or Joint Deformity

New, unexplained bony swelling or joint deformity warrants urgent X-ray within 2 weeks.

---

## Imaging Types

### X-ray

Used for:
- Suspected SUFE (same-day)
- DDH in children 4 months and older
- Suspected Perthes disease
- Suspected transient synovitis (to exclude serious causes)
- Uncertain apophysitis
- Knee pain without knee pathology
- Unexplained bony swelling or deformity

### Ultrasound

Used for:
- Suspected DDH in infants aged 4 weeks to 5 months
- Follow-up of borderline dysplasia on initial hip ultrasound (if recommended by a radiologist)

---

## Management

Your doctor will determine the appropriate next steps based on their assessment:

- **Suspected septic arthritis, osteomyelitis, or acute SUFE:** Immediate hospital referral — do not wait for imaging.
- **Suspected SUFE (non-acute):** Same-day hip X-ray; your doctor will seek orthopaedic advice if there is any doubt, even with a normal X-ray.
- **DDH:** Imaging urgency depends on age and findings. Your doctor will arrange X-ray or ultrasound within 2 weeks if indicated. A dislocated hip in an infant younger than 3 months is referred directly to a paediatric orthopaedic specialist.
- **Suspected Perthes disease:** X-ray within 2 weeks; further specialist review as needed.
- **Transient synovitis:** Symptomatic management. X-ray to exclude other causes. Reassurance that this is benign and self-limiting.
- **Apophysitis:** Rest and activity modification; imaging if the diagnosis remains uncertain.
