# Slipped Upper Femoral Epiphysis (SUFE)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of SUFE — a condition where the ball of the hip joint (femoral head) slips off the top of the thigh bone through the growth plate. It typically affects children during their growth spurt in puberty.

---

## Background

- SUFE occurs in a predictable age range due to a weakness in the growth plate that normally occurs during the accelerated growth of puberty. It may affect both hips.
- The process is often gradual and symptoms are initially subtle.
- Early recognition and treatment is important. Once the epiphysis is fully slipped, it can cause irretrievable damage.

---

## Key Alert — Always Consider SUFE

Your doctor will consider SUFE in any child over 8 years of age with hip, thigh, or knee pain.

---

## Assessment

### Who is at risk?

Characteristic features your doctor will look for:

- Age 8 to 15 years, with a limp, or stiffness or pain in the hip, groin, knee, or thigh
- Weight often above the 90th percentile
- More common in boys

### Stable vs unstable SUFE

Your doctor will distinguish between two presentations:

**Unstable SUFE (acute symptoms):**
- Sudden onset of severe pain and inability to bear weight, often following a fall or sports injury
- Presents like a fracture
- Sometimes occurs after weeks of mild intermittent pain or limp (acute on chronic)
- Most patients with unstable SUFE will present directly to an emergency department

**Stable SUFE (subacute or chronic symptoms):**
- Most common presentation
- Several weeks of mild or intermittent pain or limp, often worse after activity, but still able to bear weight
- Can be difficult to diagnose due to gradual onset — 15% have only thigh or knee pain with no hip symptoms

### Physical examination

Your doctor will look for:

- Antalgic gait or limp due to shortening and external rotation of the affected leg
- Inability to weight bear (if acute/unstable SUFE)
- Reduced internal rotation of the hip; the hip will externally rotate during flexion
- The foot on the affected side may be turned out
- Examination of the knee to rule out local processes at the knee

### Investigations

- If unstable SUFE is suspected, X-rays are not performed in the community — these are done during acute hospital assessment
- If stable SUFE is suspected, your doctor will arrange same-day X-ray (AP pelvis and frog lateral views of both hips) and ensure same-day follow-up of results

---

## Management

Your doctor will manage SUFE as follows:

- **Confirmed SUFE (stable or unstable):** Urgent same-day paediatric orthopaedic assessment is arranged. Delay may allow a stable slip to become unstable.
- **Non-weight-bearing:** Children with confirmed SUFE are kept non-weight-bearing on crutches until orthopaedic assessment.
- **Surgery:** Parents are advised that surgical stabilisation is likely.
- **Suspected unstable SUFE:** Acute orthopaedic assessment is arranged without X-rays in the community.
- **Strong suspicion of stable SUFE but X-rays unavailable or normal:** Acute orthopaedic assessment is still arranged given the clinical concern.
- **Normal imaging and SUFE unlikely:** Your doctor will consider other causes for hip or knee pain.
