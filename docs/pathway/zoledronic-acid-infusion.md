# Zoledronic Acid Infusion (Aclasta)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the clinical approach to zoledronic acid (Aclasta) infusion for osteoporosis — who it is appropriate for, how to prepare, how it is given, and follow-up care. All decisions about your treatment should be made with your doctor.

---

## Background

Zoledronic acid is a bisphosphonate — a class of medication used to strengthen bones and reduce fracture risk. It has a long duration of action compared to oral bisphosphonates.

For osteoporosis, the formulation used is **5 mg/100 mL**, given as a single annual (or less frequent) intravenous infusion. A separate 4 mg/5 mL formulation is used for oncology patients only.

---

## Who Is It Appropriate For?

Your doctor will consider zoledronic acid infusion for osteoporosis if you:

- Cannot tolerate oral bisphosphonates (e.g., alendronate)
- Have difficulty with the weekly oral regime — staying upright for 30 minutes after the tablet is a requirement that some people find difficult
- Have had declining bone density despite weekly alendronate
- Have requested the procedure
- Have a creatinine clearance ≥ 35 mL per minute (a measure of kidney function)

---

## Contraindications — When It Should Not Be Used

Your doctor will not proceed with zoledronic acid infusion if you have:

- Low blood calcium (hypocalcaemia)
- Reduced kidney function: creatinine clearance < 35 mL/min or eGFR < 35 mL/min/1.73m²
- Known allergy or hypersensitivity to zoledronic acid or other bisphosphonates
- Already had a zoledronic acid infusion for osteoporosis within the last 12 months

---

## Pre-Procedure Preparation

Your doctor will carry out the following approximately one week before your infusion:

### Blood Tests

- **Creatinine** (kidney function): Required if not tested within the last 6 months, or if there has been a significant illness or medication change since. If kidney function is too low, the infusion will not proceed.
- **Serum calcium**: Assessed to detect hypocalcaemia. Any low calcium must be corrected before the infusion.
- Vitamin D level does **not** need to be measured routinely.

**Risk factors for low calcium that your doctor will consider:**
- Vitamin D deficiency
- Recent thyroid surgery
- Intestinal calcium malabsorption (e.g., coeliac disease)

### Medications

- **Vitamin D:** If there is a reason to suspect vitamin D deficiency (limited sun exposure, intestinal malabsorption, poor nutrition), your doctor may give a loading dose of colecalciferol before the infusion. Ongoing vitamin D supplementation will be discussed.
- **NSAIDs and diuretics** should be withheld on the day of the infusion.
- **Oral bisphosphonates** should be stopped. No washout period is needed before switching to zoledronic acid.
- You will be given a prescription for zoledronic acid to collect from a pharmacy and bring to your appointment.

### Hydration

You should be well hydrated on the day of the infusion. Drink at least 2 large glasses of water before or during the procedure. Dehydration substantially increases the risk of kidney complications.

---

## Side Effects

Your doctor will explain these before your infusion.

### Acute phase reaction (flu-like illness)

- Occurs in up to **30% of people after their first infusion**, but only 2–5% after subsequent infusions
- Symptoms include fever, muscle and joint aches, and fatigue
- Usually mild and always self-limiting, but can occasionally be debilitating and last several days
- **Taking paracetamol before the infusion, and regularly for up to 72 hours afterwards, reduces symptom frequency by about 50%**

### Eye inflammation (uveitis or scleritis)

- Very rare; tends to occur within one month of treatment
- If you experience redness, photophobia, blurred vision, eye pain, or floaters after the infusion, contact your doctor promptly for referral to an eye specialist

### Osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ)

- Very rare at osteoporosis doses; mostly reported in cancer patients receiving much higher doses
- Dental treatment is **not contraindicated** — you do not need to avoid dental work
- If dental treatment is planned around the time of infusion, your doctor may discuss timing
- If you have risk factors for ONJ, a dental check and preventive dentistry before therapy may be recommended

---

## The Infusion

- The infusion is given intravenously over **30 minutes** (minimum 15 minutes — it must not be rushed)
- **Dose:** Zoledronic acid 5 mg in 100 mL aqueous solution
- A vented infusion line is used at a constant rate
- After the infusion is complete, the line is flushed with 20 mL of normal saline
- Paracetamol 1 g orally may be given after the infusion

---

## After the Infusion

### Immediately after

- Your doctor will remind you about the possibility of flu-like symptoms in the first 3 days
- Paracetamol is recommended for 3 days (preferred over NSAIDs)

### Follow-up

- A phone call from your doctor at 2–3 days is often arranged to check for side effects
- If you are older with significant other health conditions, or have borderline kidney function, blood tests (kidney function and calcium) will be checked within **1 week** of the infusion — Aclasta can cause low calcium and kidney problems in these patients, particularly if they were not well hydrated
- Your doctor will record the infusion in your medical records and set a recall for your next infusion
- Vitamin D supplementation will be reviewed and continued if appropriate

### Timing of further infusions

- Local bone physicians suggest infusions at **0, 18, and 36 months** as a standard schedule (not annually for most people)
- Annual infusions may be appropriate if you have severe osteoporosis, very high fracture risk, or ongoing fragility fractures — your doctor will advise

---

## When Specialist Input Is Needed

Your doctor will seek specialist advice in certain situations:

- **Endocrinology advice** if kidney function is too low (creatinine clearance < 35 mL/min or eGFR < 35 mL/min/1.73m²) but zoledronic acid is still being considered
- **Non-acute endocrinology assessment** if you have severe osteoporosis with very high fracture risk, or ongoing fragility fractures
- **Ophthalmology** if eye symptoms develop after the infusion
