# Fetal Renal Tract Dilatation

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the detection and follow-up of fetal renal tract dilatation found on routine antenatal ultrasound scans. Management is guided by your lead maternity carer (LMC) during pregnancy and your GP after the baby is born.

> **Note:** For anatomically anomalous kidneys (e.g. duplex, single, horseshoe, or multi-cystic dysplastic kidneys), specialist paediatric advice is required.

---

## Background

Fetal renal tract dilatation may be detected at routine antenatal ultrasound scans offered to all pregnant patients:

- **Nuchal translucency scan** at 12 weeks gestation
- **Fetal anatomy scan** at 18 weeks gestation

Renal dilatation in the **first trimester is always abnormal** and requires specialist assessment.

In the second and third trimesters, the significance depends on the degree of dilatation, measured as the anteroposterior renal pelvis diameter (APRPD), and whether other findings are present.

Ultrasound results are reported using a standardised classification system:
- **Antenatal (A1–A3):** used during pregnancy
- **Postnatal (P1–P3):** used after birth

---

## Antenatal Classification (Second and Third Trimesters)

Your doctor uses the following classification based on APRPD measurements and associated findings:

| Classification | APRPD at 16–28 weeks | APRPD at 28+ weeks | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N (Normal)** | Less than 4 mm | Less than 7 mm | No follow-up required |
| **A1 (Low risk)** | 4–6 mm | 7–9 mm | Repeat ultrasound may be arranged |
| **A2 (Intermediate risk)** | 7 mm or larger | 10 mm or larger | Specialist assessment recommended |
| **A3 (High risk)** | 4 mm or larger **plus** additional findings | 7 mm or larger **plus** additional findings | Specialist assessment required |

Additional findings that increase risk (pushing toward A3):
- Peripheral calyceal dilatation
- Abnormal parenchymal thickness or appearance
- Dilated ureters
- Abnormal bladder wall or ureterocele
- Unexplained reduced amniotic fluid (oligohydramnios)

---

## Management During Pregnancy

Your doctor may consider the following based on the antenatal classification:

- **Normal:** No further ultrasound follow-up required
- **A1 (Low risk):** If the initial scan was at 28 weeks or less, your doctor will arrange a repeat ultrasound at or near 32 weeks gestation to recheck the classification
- **A2 or A3 (Intermediate or High risk):** Your doctor will refer to the Maternal Fetal Medicine Service for specialist assessment. If there is any delay, a repeat ultrasound at 32 weeks (or 4–6 weeks later, whichever is later) may be arranged

---

## After Birth — Postnatal Follow-Up

### Who arranges follow-up?

- From birth to 4 weeks postnatal: your **lead maternity carer (LMC)** is responsible
- From 4 weeks postnatal (after formal written handover from the LMC): your **GP** takes over follow-up

### When is the first postnatal renal ultrasound needed?

This depends on the antenatal classification:

- **A2 or A3 (Intermediate or High risk):** Your doctor will seek urgent paediatric phone advice within 24–48 hours after birth. A postnatal renal ultrasound is generally arranged from **day 7** after delivery (not before day 7)
- **A1 (Low risk):** A postnatal renal ultrasound is arranged between **4 weeks and 3 months** of age. Your GP will be notified by the LMC and will coordinate this
- **Normal antenatal result:** No postnatal renal ultrasound is required

---

## Postnatal Classification

The first postnatal renal ultrasound result is classified using the following system:

| Classification | APRPD (mm) | Other findings |
|---|---|---|
| **N (Normal)** | Less than 10 mm | Central calyceal dilatation only (no peripheral). No additional findings |
| **P1 (Low risk)** | 10–14 mm | Central calyceal dilatation only (no peripheral). No additional findings |
| **P2 (Intermediate risk)** | 15 mm or larger | Central calyceal dilatation only. No additional findings |
| **P2 (Intermediate risk)** | 14 mm or smaller | Peripheral calyceal dilatation and/or dilated ureters. No additional findings |
| **P3 (High risk)** | 15 mm or larger | Peripheral calyceal dilatation and/or dilated ureters. No additional findings |
| **P3 (High risk)** | Any measurement | Abnormal parenchymal thickness or appearance, dilated ureters, abnormal bladder wall, or ureterocele |

### Management after the first postnatal ultrasound

Your doctor may consider:

- **P3 (High risk):** Urgent paediatric specialist advice will be sought
- **P2 (Intermediate risk):** Non-acute paediatric assessment will be arranged
- **P1 (Low risk):**
  - If the baby was younger than 1 month at the time of the scan: repeat renal ultrasound at 3 months of age
  - If the baby was 1 month or older: repeat renal ultrasound at 12 months of age, with a recall set up at that time
- **Normal result:**
  - If the baby was 1 month or older: repeat renal ultrasound at 3 months of age
  - If the baby was younger than 1 month: no further follow-up required

---

## Follow-Up Ultrasound at 12 Months

If a repeat renal ultrasound is performed at 12 months of age, the result is classified using the same postnatal (P1–P3) system:

- **P2 or P3:** Your doctor will arrange non-acute paediatric assessment
- **Normal or P1:** No further follow-up is required
