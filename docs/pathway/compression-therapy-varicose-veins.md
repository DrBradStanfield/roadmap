# Compression Therapy for Varicose Veins

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the use of compression stockings for varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency — including when they are appropriate, how circulation is assessed beforehand, and how to use them safely.

---

## Background

Compression stockings help manage pain, aching, and swelling caused by varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency. They are particularly useful in:

- Frail patients where symptoms are significant
- While waiting for surgery
- Maintaining healing of venous leg ulcers, or preventing ulcers from forming

Important limitations:

- Around 25% of patients are unable to use compression stockings consistently
- Compression is **contraindicated** if significant arterial disease is present — your doctor must assess circulation before prescribing stockings
- All compression therapy requires proper fitting and patient education to work safely and effectively

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess whether compression is appropriate for you. This involves:

### 1. Ruling Out Arterial Disease

Before compression stockings are used, your doctor will check for signs of arterial disease (poor circulation). If your foot pulses are normal, there are no signs of arterial disease, and you do not have diabetes, a formal arterial pressure test (ABPI) may not be needed before trialling lower-grade stockings.

If arterial disease is suspected, your doctor may arrange an **ankle brachial pressure index (ABPI)** — a painless test comparing blood pressure at the ankle and the arm using a Doppler ultrasound device.

**ABPI results guide compression decisions:**

| ABPI Value | What it means | Compression guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 0.5 | Severe arterial disease likely | Compression stockings should **not** be worn |
| 0.5 to 0.79 | Arterial disease likely | Only light compression (Class I, 18–21 mmHg) may be considered |
| 0.8 or greater | Compression is safe | Standard compression (Class II, 20–30 mmHg) appropriate; higher compression if ulcers present |
| Greater than 1.3 | Calcified arteries may give falsely high reading | Compression should be avoided; your doctor may consider a vascular specialist review |

### 2. Practical Assessment

Your doctor will also consider whether compression stockings are practical for you:

- Frail or elderly patients may need help from a carer to put on and remove stockings
- Fragile or sensitive skin may be at risk of damage during application
- Conditions like arthritis can make stockings difficult to manage
- If your legs are an unusual shape, careful fitting is needed to ensure the stockings work correctly
- If swelling (oedema) is present, compression bandaging is used first to reduce it — smaller hosiery is then measured once the swelling has gone down

Your doctor will discuss your willingness and ability to use stockings long-term before recommending them.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following:

### Choosing the Right Grade

- **Class II (moderate compression, up to 30 mmHg at the ankle)** is the usual starting point for varicose veins and chronic venous insufficiency
- High compression is reserved for severe cases and is not generally used in general practice

### Stocking Length

- **Knee-length stockings** are usually sufficient for chronic venous insufficiency
- **Full-length stockings with a waistband** may be needed if there is swelling in the thigh, or in post-phlebitic limb or lymphoedema

### Fitting and Use

- Stockings must be accurately measured and fitted — comfort and correct fit are checked before regular use begins
- If oedema is present, compression bandaging is applied first to reduce swelling before measuring for hosiery
- An application aid may be helpful if you have difficulty putting the stockings on
- Community nursing support is available if needed

### Ongoing Use

- You will be provided with instructions on how to apply, remove, and care for your stockings, and when to seek help
- Stockings are replaced regularly — typically 2 pairs per year
- If you meet the criteria for a Disability Allowance, you may be able to claim for the cost of compression hosiery
