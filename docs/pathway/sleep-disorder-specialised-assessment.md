# Sleep Disorder Specialised Assessment

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway describes when your doctor may refer you to a specialist sleep service for further assessment — including complex sleep apnoea, narcolepsy, parasomnia, and unexplained daytime sleepiness.

---

## When Specialist Assessment May Be Needed

Your doctor may refer you to a specialist sleep clinic if:

**Suspected obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) with high-risk features:**
- A sleepy-driver episode — a motor vehicle collision or near-miss linked to sleepiness, or falling asleep while driving
- A high-risk occupation (e.g., heavy machinery operator, professional driver)
- You are a surgical patient with significant perioperative risk
- Significant active cardiovascular conditions — severe congestive heart failure or uncontrolled high blood pressure
- A significant respiratory or neurological condition
- Risk of obesity hypoventilation syndrome — extreme obesity (BMI greater than 50), particularly if daytime oxygen levels are 95% or below
- An Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) score of 18 or higher

**After a community sleep test:**
- A sleep test shows at least moderate sleep apnoea together with excessive daytime sleepiness

**Other sleep conditions:**
- Symptoms suggesting narcolepsy, parasomnia (abnormal behaviours during sleep), or periodic limb movement disorder — conditions that need more detailed assessment and monitoring
- The cause of your excessive daytime sleepiness is still unknown after initial assessment
- A non-respiratory sleep disorder is suspected

---

## Driving and Occupational Safety

If you have symptoms of excessive sleepiness, your doctor will advise you about driving safety. If there is concern about fitness to drive:

- You may be advised not to drive until you have been assessed by a specialist
- If cleared to drive, your doctor will provide self-management guidance, which typically includes:
  - Minimising unnecessary driving
  - Taking regular rest breaks
  - Pulling over and stopping as soon as any warning signs of sleepiness appear
  - Avoiding driving when sleep deprived, at times when you would normally be asleep, or over long distances without a break
  - Avoiding alcohol or sedating medications before driving

OSA can significantly increase the risk of workplace accidents — this also applies to operating machinery and other safety-critical tasks.

---

## What to Expect

Depending on your clinical needs and service capacity, the specialist sleep service may offer:

- A full clinic appointment with assessment and monitoring
- Advice-only consultation

Your doctor will discuss the referral process with you, including any wait times.
