# Kidney Disease in Diabetes

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the detection and management of kidney disease in people with diabetes (diabetic kidney disease / diabetic nephropathy).

> **Note (March 2026):** From 1 July 2026, HbA1c diagnostic thresholds are changing — diabetes will be defined as HbA1c ≥ 48 mmol/mol (down from 50), and prediabetes as 42–47 mmol/mol. There is no change to glucose diagnostic criteria.

---

## Screening

Your doctor will screen for diabetic kidney disease at diagnosis and then annually (or more often if results are abnormal).

**Tests ordered:**

- **Urine albumin:creatinine ratio (ACR)** — estimates daily urinary albumin excretion
  - A first morning urine sample is preferred (random urine can vary by up to 50%)
  - An abnormal result needs to be confirmed on two further occasions before diagnosing albuminuria
  - Causes of a falsely raised ACR include: urinary tract infection, intercurrent illness, vigorous exercise, haematuria, significant hyperglycaemia
- **Serum creatinine with eGFR** — if you are under- or overweight, your doctor may use the Cockcroft-Gault formula instead

**ACR definitions:**

| Category | ACR |
|---|---|
| Normal to mildly raised | < 3 mg/mmol |
| Microalbuminuria | 3–30 mg/mmol |
| Macroalbuminuria | > 30 mg/mmol |

**eGFR categories (GFR stages):**

| Stage | eGFR (mL/min/1.73 m²) | Description |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | > 90 | Normal or high |
| G2 | 60–89 | Mildly decreased |
| G3a | 45–59 | Mildly to moderately decreased |
| G3b | 30–44 | Moderately to severely decreased |
| G4 | 15–29 | Severely decreased |
| G5 | < 15 | Renal failure |

**Screening frequency:**
- At diagnosis of diabetes, then annually
- Every 3–6 months if results are abnormal
- Every 6 months if you are high risk for diabetes complications
- Māori, Pacific, and Indo-Asian patients have a higher risk of diabetic kidney disease, which may present up to 10 years earlier — your doctor will monitor accordingly

**Note:** The first sign of diabetic kidney disease is usually albuminuria, but some people initially present with a reduced eGFR.

---

## Diagnosis

Your doctor will diagnose diabetic kidney disease if:

- ACR > 3 mg/mmol (albuminuria), **and/or**
- eGFR < 60

In older patients with microalbuminuria and multiple health conditions, your doctor will consider whether other causes could explain the result.

Your doctor will also check blood pressure and lipid levels, and may do a full cardiovascular risk assessment.

---

## When the Cause May Not Be Diabetes

Your doctor will look for signs that kidney disease might have a cause other than diabetes. These include:

- Diabetes diagnosed less than 5 years ago
- Age under 30 years
- Rapidly declining eGFR (> 1 mL/min per month for 6+ months, or > 10 mL/min per year)
- Diabetes present but no diabetic changes in the eyes (retinopathy)
- Family history of kidney disease
- Features suggesting another cause (e.g. connective tissue disease, recurrent UTIs, hypertension out of proportion)

---

## Management

Management is led by your doctor and typically involves several parallel strategies.

### 1. Blood Pressure Control and RAA Blockade

**Target:** Blood pressure < 130/80 mmHg

Your doctor will usually start an **ACE inhibitor** (e.g. ramipril, perindopril) regardless of your blood pressure level. This class of drug reduces protein in the urine and slows kidney disease progression.

- eGFR and potassium will be rechecked 2–4 weeks after starting or increasing the dose
- If your eGFR falls by less than 30% and stabilises within 2 months, the ACE inhibitor is continued
- If potassium rises above 6 mmol/L, the dose may need to be reduced or stopped
- Women of childbearing age must have effective contraception before starting (ACE inhibitors are harmful in pregnancy)

If you cannot tolerate an ACE inhibitor, an **angiotensin-II receptor blocker (ARB)** is used instead.

If blood pressure remains above target:
- A **calcium channel blocker** is added
- A **thiazide diuretic** (chlortalidone preferred, as it is long-acting) may be added as a third agent
- Further options include spironolactone/eplerenone, alpha blockers, or beta blockers

**Important:**
- ACE inhibitors and ARBs should not be combined without specialist input
- Neither should be used if you are already on Entresto (sacubitril/valsartan)
- If eGFR drops by more than 25–30% after starting, your doctor will consider renal artery stenosis and may reduce or stop the medication

### 2. Glycaemic Control

**Target HbA1c:** Individualised, typically < 53 mmol/mol for most people

- Lower targets (< 48 mmol/mol) may be set for younger people or those with early complications
- Higher targets (54–70 mmol/mol) may be appropriate for frail or elderly patients, those with frequent hypoglycaemia, or limited life expectancy

**SGLT2 inhibitors** (e.g. empagliflozin) are preferred in diabetic kidney disease:
- Recommended regardless of glycaemic control or other diabetes medications
- Can be started if eGFR > 20 mL/min; once started, can be continued until renal replacement therapy begins
- Maximum empagliflozin dose is 10 mg/day if eGFR 20–30 mL/min

**SGLT2 inhibitor contraindications:**
- Previous severe genitourinary infection
- Ketogenic diets (risk of DKA)
- Type 1 diabetes or loss of pancreatic function — only with specialist approval
- History of DKA — only with specialist approval

**SGLT2 inhibitor cautions:** pregnancy and breastfeeding, eGFR 20–30 mL/min (as above), high risk of DKA, insulin deficiency, malnutrition, low-carbohydrate diet, hazardous alcohol use.

**Signs of DKA** (seek urgent medical care if suspected):
- Blood glucose > 11 mmol/L (note: may be lower if on empagliflozin — "euglycaemic DKA")
- Blood ketones > 1.5 mmol/L
- Nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, dehydration, rapid or deep breathing, confusion

**GLP-1 receptor agonists** (e.g. dulaglutide, liraglutide) are an alternative if SGLT2 inhibitors are not tolerated, but have not yet been shown to prevent dialysis or renal death.

GLP-1 receptor agonist contraindications: pregnancy/breastfeeding, eGFR < 15 mL/min, type 1 diabetes (specialist input required). Cautions include: gastroparesis, pancreatitis, liver failure, medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN2 syndrome.

### 3. Medication Review as eGFR Declines

Your doctor will review your medications as kidney function falls:

- **Metformin:** dose reduced if eGFR < 60; contraindicated if eGFR < 15
- **Insulin and sulfonylureas:** doses may need reducing as eGFR falls (insulin is renally cleared and gluconeogenesis is reduced)

### 4. Cardiovascular Risk Reduction

- **Statins:** Strongly recommended to slow both kidney disease and cardiovascular disease progression
  - Typically atorvastatin 20 mg, increased as needed
  - Target LDL < 1.4 mmol/L in those with established macrovascular disease or 5-year CVD risk > 15%
  - Ezetimibe added if LDL > 2 mmol/L on maximal statin despite 5-year CVD risk > 15%
- **Smoking cessation** is strongly advised
- **Weight reduction** and **regular exercise** are recommended

### 5. Ongoing Monitoring

Your doctor will monitor the following at least every 3 months:

- eGFR
- Serum potassium
- HbA1c
- Blood pressure
- Urine ACR

## When Specialist Referral Is Needed

Your doctor will refer you to a kidney specialist (nephrologist) if:

- eGFR < 60 **and** it has dropped by 15 or more in the past 12 months (progressive kidney disease)
- eGFR < 45 (referral may not be needed if eGFR is stable, ACR < 70, and cardiovascular risk is well controlled)
- eGFR < 30
- ACR > 70
- Sustained decline in eGFR ≥ 5 mL/min/1.73m² over 12 months (confirmed on repeat testing)
- Signs suggesting a cause other than diabetes (see above)
- eGFR drops > 25% after starting ACE inhibitor or ARB (possible renal artery stenosis)

A **renal ultrasound** is also arranged in most of the above situations if one has not been done recently.

Your doctor may also consider referral if:
- ACR > 30, especially in younger patients
- Hyperkalaemia is limiting ACE inhibitor or ARB use (referral for dietitian input or specialist advice)
- You have type 1 diabetes and GLP-1 receptor agonists are being considered
