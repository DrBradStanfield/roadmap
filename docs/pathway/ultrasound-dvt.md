# Ultrasound for DVT (Deep Vein Thrombosis)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when an ultrasound scan is used to investigate suspected deep vein thrombosis (DVT), and how your doctor manages the interim period while waiting for results.

---

## When an Ultrasound Is Considered

Your doctor may consider a DVT ultrasound if you have clinically suspected lower limb DVT and one or more of the following:

- **Wells score ≥ 2** — a clinical scoring tool your doctor uses to estimate the probability of DVT based on symptoms, signs, and risk factors
- **Wells score < 2 but a positive D-dimer blood test** — D-dimer is a blood marker that rises when clotting is occurring
- **You are pregnant**

An ultrasound may also be considered for:

- Suspected upper limb DVT
- Superficial venous thrombosis (a clot in a vein close to the skin surface) if it involves the long saphenous vein in the thigh, or symptoms extend more than 5 cm in the calf

> Note: Up to 25% of people with superficial venous thrombosis also have an occult (hidden) DVT, which is why scanning is sometimes needed even when the clot appears to be superficial.

---

## While Awaiting the Ultrasound

If there is a delay of more than 6 hours between presentation and the ultrasound appointment, your doctor may consider short-term anticoagulation (blood thinning medication) to reduce clot risk in the interim.

### Rivaroxaban (preferred)

Your doctor may prescribe rivaroxaban as a single 15 mg oral dose for short-term cover while awaiting the scan. A second dose may be given if the delay exceeds 12 hours (except in kidney impairment or older adults).

Rivaroxaban is **not** used if:
- You are pregnant or breastfeeding
- You have severe kidney impairment
- You are at significant risk of major bleeding (e.g., recent gastrointestinal ulcer, recent intracranial haemorrhage, or thrombocytopenia)
- You are taking certain HIV medications or ketoconazole

### Enoxaparin (injection, alternative)

If rivaroxaban is not suitable — particularly in pregnancy or severe kidney disease — your doctor may use enoxaparin (a low-molecular-weight heparin given by injection). Dosing is based on your weight and kidney function.

---

## Special Situations

- **Bilateral leg swelling or age 15–18 years:** Your doctor will seek specialist advice before proceeding
- **Children under 15 years:** All DVT ultrasound requests require specialist paediatric haematology input
- **Weekend delays:** If the scan cannot happen within 24–36 hours (e.g., over a long weekend), your doctor will seek specialist advice about appropriate anticoagulation coverage
