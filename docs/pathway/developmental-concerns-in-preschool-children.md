# Developmental Concerns in Preschool Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway helps determine whether a preschool-aged child has no obvious delay but significant risk factors, isolated or global developmental delay, or features strongly suggestive of autism spectrum disorder (ASD).

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Medical Assessment

Contact your doctor promptly if any of the following are present:

- Suspected child abuse or neglect
- Signs of a serious underlying medical condition
- Global developmental delay (delay in two or more developmental domains)
- Developmental regression — loss of previously acquired skills or milestones

---

## Background

Development is a dynamic process best assessed by regular longitudinal review. Any consultation is an opportunity for developmental assessment.

- Parental concerns are usually a reliable indicator of developmental delay — take them seriously
- Early detection and referral improves long-term outcomes
- Development spans five domains: **fine motor, gross motor, language, social,** and **cognitive**

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take time to thoroughly assess your child's development. Information may be gathered across more than one visit — for example, using parental questionnaires or arranging hearing and vision tests between appointments.

### Risk Factors Your Doctor Will Consider

**Biological risk factors:**
- Premature birth (under 28 weeks) or low birth weight (less than 1,500 g)
- Birth injury
- Vision or hearing impairment
- Chronic illness
- In-utero exposure to alcohol or drugs

**Environmental risk factors:**
- Low parental education
- Parental mental illness
- Social isolation or poverty
- Poor housing or lack of access to services
- Family violence or care and protection concerns
- Household exposure to alcohol or drugs

### Signs of a Serious Underlying Medical Condition

Your doctor will look for:
- Loose, floppy movements (low tone) or stiff, tense movements (high tone)
- Weakness on one side of the body
- Absent or reduced response to sound or visual stimuli
- Features suggestive of seizures, including absence episodes

### Physical Examination

Your doctor will typically assess:
- Height, weight, and head circumference plotted on percentile charts
- Response to visual and auditory stimuli
- Signs of non-accidental injury (e.g., unexplained bruises or burns)
- Dysmorphic features (e.g., low-set ears)
- Neurological signs (e.g., skin lesions, unilateral weakness, abnormal tone)
- ENT examination (e.g., glue ear)
- Parent's mental and emotional state, and the parent-child interaction

### Autism Spectrum Disorder Screening

Your doctor may perform a brief clinical screen for ASD during the consultation, including:

1. **Eye contact** — Has the child made eye contact during the appointment?
2. **Gaze monitoring** — When the doctor points to an object across the room, does the child look at the object (not just at the hand)?
3. **Pretend play** — Given a toy cup and teapot, does the child pretend to pour and drink?
4. **Protodeclarative pointing** — When asked "Where's the light?", does the child point with their index finger and look up at the adult's face?
5. **Block tower** — Can the child build a tower of blocks? How many?

### Investigations

Your doctor will arrange:
- Audiometry (hearing assessment)
- Visual testing

---

## Management

Your doctor will tailor management to the child's presentation and needs. Possible approaches include:

**If urgent concerns are identified:**
- If physical or sexual abuse, an underlying medical condition, or developmental regression is suspected, your doctor will seek urgent paediatric advice.

**If further developmental assessment is needed:**
Your doctor may consider referral for non-acute paediatric assessment if any of the following criteria are met:
- Severe delay in one developmental domain
- Global developmental delay (two or more domains)
- Strong suspicion of autism spectrum disorder

Early intervention services can be arranged at the same time — a formal diagnosis is not required to begin referral.

**If isolated speech or language delay:**
- Your doctor may refer to early childhood learning support services.

**Supports for the child and family:**
Your doctor may consider:
- Identifying and managing parental mental illness, alcohol, or drug problems
- Referral to intensive home visiting programmes (e.g., Family Start)
- Ensuring the child is enrolled with the Well Child / Tamariki Ora service
- Needs assessment for disability support services
- Recommending evidence-based parenting programmes (e.g., Incredible Years, Triple-P)

**General ongoing monitoring:**
- Continue monitoring growth on percentile charts
- Treat simple underlying medical conditions (e.g., glue ear, constipation, eczema, asthma)
- Ensure immunisations are up to date

Even when no developmental delay is found, your GP has an important ongoing role in developmental surveillance.
