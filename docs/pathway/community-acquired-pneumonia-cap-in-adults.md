# Community Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) in Adults

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of community acquired pneumonia (CAP) in adults — including severity scoring, antibiotic selection, and when hospital admission is needed.

---

## When to Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency department or call an ambulance if you have any of the following:

- Confusion (new or worsening)
- Oxygen saturation (SaO2) 90% or less on air
- Respiratory rate 30 or more breaths per minute
- Blood pressure below 90/60 mmHg
- Oxygen saturation below 92% (if no previous lung disease)

These are signs of severe pneumonia requiring urgent hospital assessment.

---

## What Is CAP?

Community acquired pneumonia is an infection of the lungs acquired outside of hospital. It can range from mild (manageable at home with antibiotics) to severe (requiring hospital admission).

Your doctor will assess severity using the **CRB-65 score** to help decide whether you can be treated at home or need hospital care.

---

## Severity Assessment — CRB-65 Score

Your doctor scores 1 point for each of the following features:

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **C** — Confusion | New or worsening confusion (assessed with a short mental test) |
| **R** — Respiratory rate | 30 or more breaths per minute |
| **B** — Blood pressure | Systolic <90 mmHg OR diastolic ≤60 mmHg |
| **65** — Age | 65 years or older |

**What the score means:**

| Score | Severity | Mortality Risk | Typical Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Low | Less than 1% | Home treatment likely appropriate |
| 1–2 | Moderate | 1–10% | Hospital referral considered |
| 3–4 | High | Greater than 10% | Urgent hospital admission |

*Note: As of March 2026, national guidelines recommend the CORB score (similar criteria, without the age component) and suggest hospital admission for a score of 1 or more. Your doctor will discuss which scoring system applies to your situation.*

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:

- Ask about your symptoms: cough, shortness of breath, sputum production, pleuritic chest pain (pain with breathing)
- Ask about systemic symptoms: fever above 38°C, sweating, chills, aches and pains
- Examine your chest for signs of consolidation (infection)
- Measure your respiratory rate, temperature, heart rate, blood pressure, and oxygen saturation
- Consider other possible diagnoses such as heart failure, pulmonary embolism, COPD, or tuberculosis

### Important: Legionella (Legionnaires' Disease)

Your doctor will always consider Legionella as a possible cause. This is more common than previously recognised.

**Key risk factors:**
- Recent potting mix or compost use (even brief exposure — tipping, trowelling, or touching your face before washing hands)
- Exposure to humidifiers, air conditioners, or hot water systems
- Spring and summer months (more common)

If you have used potting mix or compost in the last 2 weeks, tell your doctor — this changes the antibiotic treatment needed.

---

## Investigations

### Mild CAP (CRB-65 score 0)

- Usually no investigations needed
- Chest X-ray is not routinely necessary — your doctor will consider it only if it would change how they manage your illness
- Blood tests usually not required

### Moderate CAP (CRB-65 score 1)

- Blood tests: full blood count, kidney function, electrolytes
- Chest X-ray considered (especially with other health conditions)
- Sputum sample may be taken

---

## Treatment

### Mild CAP (CRB-65 score 0) — Home Treatment

Your doctor will prescribe antibiotics. Standard options include:

- **Amoxicillin** 1 g three times a day for 5 days, OR
- **Doxycycline** 200 mg twice daily on day 1, then 100 mg twice daily for days 2–5, OR
- **Cefalexin** 1 g three times a day for 5 days

*Ciprofloxacin is not used for pneumonia as it does not reliably treat the most common cause (Streptococcus pneumoniae).*

**If Legionella is suspected** (potting mix exposure, water source risk):
- Roxithromycin 300 mg once a day is added for 14 days
- Treatment starts immediately — waiting for test results is not recommended

**Supportive care at home:**
- Take simple pain relief (e.g. paracetamol) for chest pain with breathing
- Drink plenty of fluids
- Avoid smoking and passive smoke exposure
- Monitor your symptoms closely

**Follow-up:**
- Your doctor will review you within 72 hours
- Antibiotics typically take 3–5 days to show improvement
- Cough and tiredness can last 4–6 weeks even after successful treatment

### Moderate CAP (CRB-65 score 1) — Home Treatment with Closer Monitoring

- Same antibiotic options as mild CAP
- If symptoms are more severe but hospital admission is not needed, your doctor may prescribe a combination of amoxicillin plus a macrolide antibiotic
- Your doctor will check on you daily until you are stable or improving
- If you deteriorate, hospital assessment will be arranged

### Severe CAP (CRB-65 score ≥2) — Hospital Admission

Severe pneumonia requires hospital care. Your doctor may give you a dose of antibiotics before transfer if you are significantly unwell.

---

## Antibiotic Duration

| Type | Duration |
|---|---|
| Uncomplicated pneumonia | 5 days |
| Mycoplasma pneumonia | 7 days |
| Legionella pneumonia | 14 days |

---

## Follow-Up After Recovery

Your doctor may arrange follow-up to:

- Check a chest X-ray at 6 weeks (if you have ongoing symptoms, suspicious findings, or a significant smoking history — not for cough or tiredness alone)
- Consider lung function testing (spirometry) if you are a current or ex-smoker
- Discuss annual influenza and pneumococcal vaccinations
- Support with smoking cessation and housing or heating improvements if these are risk factors

---

## Risk Factors for CAP

The following increase the risk of developing pneumonia:

- Poor nutrition or housing (damp, mould, overcrowding)
- Tobacco smoke exposure
- Diabetes, COPD, chronic liver disease, ischaemic heart disease
- Alcohol excess
- Immunosuppression (cancer, HIV, long-term steroids)
- Frailty or residential care living
