# Legionellosis

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of legionellosis — including Legionnaires' disease and the milder Pontiac fever — in adults.

---

## Background

Legionella species cause two distinct illnesses:

- **Legionnaires' disease** — the more severe form, producing high fever and pneumonia. Incubation period 2–10 days (up to 14 days).
- **Pontiac fever** — a milder, flu-like respiratory illness without pneumonia that resolves on its own. Incubation period 24–48 hours (can be 5 hours to 3 days).

The most common species are *Legionella pneumophila* (thrives in warm water — plumbing, hot water systems, cooling towers) and *Legionella longbeachae* (found in soils, compost, and potting mix).

There is no person-to-person transmission of Legionella, so no specific isolation is required.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

Call an ambulance or go to the emergency department if any of the following are present (CORB score of 1 or more usually warrants hospital admission):

- **Confusion** — new or worsening
- **Oxygenation** — SpO2 90% or less on air
- **Respiratory rate** 30 or more breaths per minute
- **Blood pressure** less than 90/60 mmHg

Also seek urgent assessment if:
- Severe dehydration
- SaO2 less than 92% (or 3% or more below your baseline if you have existing lung disease)
- Urea greater than 7 mmol/L if previously normal
- Significant underlying health conditions (diabetes, chronic lung disease, immunosuppression)

## Assessment

Your doctor will consider legionellosis in a patient presenting with acute respiratory illness, particularly those who:

- Have been exposed to potting mix, compost, or soil products
- Have prominent symptoms beyond the lungs — headaches, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting
- Have not responded to penicillin-based antibiotics

### Symptoms

- Dry cough
- High fever and chills
- Shortness of breath, chest pain
- Headaches, excessive sweating, nausea, vomiting
- Abdominal pain
- Diarrhoea (more common than with other forms of pneumonia)

### Risk Factors for Legionnaires' Disease

- Older age
- Smoking
- Immunosuppression
- Underlying health conditions such as diabetes or chronic lung disease

### CRB-65 Severity Score

Your doctor will calculate a severity score. Score 1 point for each feature present:

- **C**onfusion (abbreviated mental test score 8 or less)
- **R**espiratory rate 30 or more breaths per minute
- **B**lood pressure: systolic < 90 mmHg or diastolic ≤ 60 mmHg
- **65** years or older

| CRB-65 Score | Severity | Mortality Risk | Likely Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Low | < 1% | Community management may be appropriate |
| 1 or 2 | Moderate | 1–10% | Hospital referral considered |
| 3 or 4 | High | > 10% | Urgent hospital admission |

Social circumstances and home support are also taken into account.

### Diagnostic Testing

Routine diagnostic testing for Legionella is not recommended in the community unless directed by Public Health during an outbreak investigation. If Legionella pneumonia is clinically suspected:

- Patients unwell enough for hospital admission may be tested as inpatients.
- If not unwell enough for admission, your doctor may seek advice from a clinical microbiologist.

## Management

Management depends on severity. Your doctor will determine the appropriate approach based on your CRB-65 score, oxygen levels, and clinical judgment.

### Mild (CRB-65 score 0)

Your doctor may consider:

- **Doxycycline** 200 mg immediately, then 100 mg twice daily for 10–14 days — preferred if Legionella or another atypical pathogen is suspected
- **Roxithromycin** 300 mg once daily for 10 days — an alternative macrolide antibiotic
- If Legionnaires' disease is specifically suspected, a macrolide (roxithromycin) is added to cover Legionella; total antibiotic course is 14 days
- Empiric treatment should not be delayed while waiting for microbiology results

Note: Standard penicillin-based antibiotics (such as amoxicillin) are not effective against Legionella. Ciprofloxacin is generally avoided due to resistance concerns.

Supportive measures your doctor may recommend:

- Increased fluid intake
- Avoid smoking (including passive smoke)
- Simple analgesia for chest pain
- Monitoring of oxygen levels and vital signs
- Bronchodilators if airflow limitation is present
- Monitoring of underlying conditions such as diabetes

### Moderate (CRB-65 score 1)

Your doctor may consider:

- Blood tests (full blood count, kidney function, electrolytes)
- Chest X-ray if it will influence management
- Sputum sample for culture and microscopy
- Close monitoring with daily review until stable or improving

### Severe (CRB-65 score 2 or greater)

Urgent hospital assessment is required. Your doctor will arrange this. Some patients with significant co-morbidities may choose home care if fully informed and consent is documented — your doctor will discuss this with you.

### If Exposure but No Pneumonia (Pontiac Fever)

If you have been exposed to Legionella but have no signs of pneumonia, your doctor will likely recommend supportive care — rest, fluids, and symptom management. Pontiac fever resolves on its own.

## Prevention Advice

### Soil and Potting Mix (most common route in New Zealand)

- Wear a disposable face mask and gloves when opening bags of potting mix or compost
- Open bags carefully with scissors rather than ripping them open
- Do potting outdoors in a well-ventilated area
- Dampen potting mix or compost with a sprinkle of water to reduce dust
- Wash hands thoroughly after handling soil products or gardening

### Aerosolised Water

Sources include hot tubs, cooling towers, decorative fountains, swimming pools, and water systems in hotels or hospitals. Public health authorities help coordinate disinfection of contaminated water sources.

There is no risk of catching Legionella from another person.
