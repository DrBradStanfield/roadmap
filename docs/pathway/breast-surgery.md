# Breast Surgery

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers plastic and reconstructive breast surgery — including breast absence, asymmetry, anomalies, reconstruction after cancer treatment, breast reduction, and gynaecomastia. Breast augmentation (implants for cosmetic purposes) is not publicly funded.

---

## Who Is Not Eligible for Publicly Funded Elective Breast Surgery

Your doctor cannot refer you for publicly funded elective breast surgery if:

- You are currently smoking or vaping nicotine products
- Your BMI is greater than 30

Public resources for elective breast surgery are extremely limited. Even if you meet eligibility criteria, your referral may be declined if capacity is full. Your doctor will advise you of this upfront.

---

## Surgical Risk Factors

Your doctor will check for factors that increase surgical risk:

- Smoking or vaping nicotine-containing products
- BMI > 30
- Past history of deep vein thrombosis (DVT), pulmonary embolism (PE), or cardiovascular disease

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess:

- Clinical features: whether the problem is one-sided or both sides, presence of asymmetry, whether it is congenital (e.g. breast absence), secondary to breast cancer treatment (including radiotherapy), genetic risk, bra size, and previous breast surgery
- Impact on your life, including:
  - Physical symptoms: back, neck, or shoulder pain, skin irritation, infections, shoulder strap indentations, poor posture
  - Social factors: difficulty with physical activities, difficulty with clothes or personal hygiene, social embarrassment or harassment, personal relationship issues

## Management by Condition

### Breast Absence

Your doctor may consider:
- A breast prosthesis — a subsidy may be available for some women with congenital needs
- Referral to plastic surgery if surgery may be appropriate

### Breast Asymmetry

- **Mild asymmetry:** Your doctor will advise that mild asymmetry is very common and considered normal. Management with bras and inserts.
- **Moderate to severe asymmetry:** Your doctor may consider a breast prosthesis (subsidy may apply), or referral to plastic surgery if quality of life is significantly affected.

### Breast Anomalies

Your doctor may refer you to plastic surgery if anomalies in breast shape (e.g. restricted or "tuberous" breasts) may be treatable and are causing a significant reduction in quality of life.

### Breast Augmentation Complications

Complications from cosmetic breast augmentation (e.g. painful or distorted breasts, implant rupture) are generally not managed through the public system, since augmentation itself is not publicly funded.

Your doctor will advise you to return to your original surgeon. If your original surgeon is no longer available, they may suggest seeing another private breast or plastic surgeon.

If you have persistent and severe symptoms and cannot afford private treatment, your doctor may consider a referral for implant removal only. If accepted:
- Only explantation (implant removal) would be offered — not implant replacement or mastopexy (breast lift)
- The cosmetic outcome of implant removal alone can be poor

### Breast Reconstruction

Breast reconstruction after mastectomy or significant breast surgery is publicly funded for eligible patients.

Your doctor may discuss this if:
- You have had a mastectomy or breast surgery for cancer
- You have breast deformity from non-cancer causes (e.g. infection, benign tumour removal, trauma)

Eligibility for delayed breast reconstruction requires:
- BMI < 30
- Non-smoker and non-vaper

If immediate reconstruction was not possible at the time of initial surgery (e.g. due to need for radiotherapy or chemotherapy), delayed reconstruction may be considered later.

A breast prosthesis is an alternative option following lumpectomy or mastectomy, or after failed reconstructive surgery. A government subsidy is available to help with the cost.

### Breast Reduction

For breast reduction, your doctor will first suggest:
- Weight loss if you are overweight, as this often improves symptoms
- Professionally fitted or custom-made bras

To be considered for publicly funded breast reduction surgery, you must meet **all** of the following criteria:
- Not smoked for at least 3 months
- BMI < 30
- Significant reduction in quality of life

Even with these criteria met, referrals are compared against other similar patients and only the most clinically severe cases are seen. Most referrals are declined due to very high demand.

### Gynaecomastia or Pseudo-Gynaecomastia

Your doctor will manage this condition according to the Gynaecomastia pathway first. If all other management has failed and you are requesting surgery, referral to plastic surgery may be considered.

---

## General Information About Breast Surgery

If your doctor refers you, they will discuss possible side-effects, including:
- Infection
- Scarring
- Loss of sensation in the breast (especially the nipple)
- Possible effects on breastfeeding

Clinical photographs of the breast are required for all referrals — without these, referrals are not accepted.
