# Contact Tracing (Partner Notification)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how to manage partner notification after a positive sexually transmitted infection (STI) result — which infections require it, how to advise patients, and when secondary care input is needed.

---

## Which Infections Require Partner Notification?

**Partner notification IS required for:**
- Chlamydia
- Gonorrhoea
- Trichomoniasis
- Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)
- Epididymo-orchitis — if due to an STI
- Urethritis
- Syphilis — your doctor will refer to sexual health; contact tracing is initiated in secondary care
- HIV — your doctor will refer to sexual health or infectious diseases; contact tracing is initiated in secondary care

**Partner notification is NOT required for:**
- Genital herpes
- Genital warts (HPV)

---

## Why Partner Notification Matters

Your doctor will explain to patients:
- Contacts need testing and treatment to avoid reinfection or further spread
- Most people with an STI have no symptoms but can still have complications or pass the infection on
- The more times a person is reinfected, the greater the risk of complications

---

## The 3-Month Lookback Window

All sexual contacts from the **past 3 months** need to be contacted. Your doctor will:
- Ask about and document the number of sexual contacts in that period
- Ask whether the patient is able to contact those people (not always possible — e.g. insufficient contact details or risk of violence)
- Advise that using condoms does not remove the requirement for contact tracing and treatment

---

## How to Notify Contacts

**Patient-initiated notification is the recommended approach.** Your doctor will advise:
- Notify in person, by phone, SMS, social media, or email — whatever is appropriate
- Providing written material about the STI helps contacts seek treatment (see justthefacts.co.nz)
- Suggested text message wording: *"I have [X]. You need treatment. Check out www.justthefacts.co.nz and see a doctor"*
- For more phrasing options, see Burnett Foundation Aotearoa – Message Generator

**Important:** Patient-delivered partner therapy (giving your own prescription to a contact) is **illegal** under New Zealand prescribing law.

---

## Provider Referral (Clinician Notifies Contacts)

Your doctor may take on the role of notifying contacts directly (with patient consent) if:
- The patient is unable or unwilling to notify all contacts from the past 3 months
- A contact is incarcerated
- There are repeated infections with uncertainty about whether contacts have been treated

When the clinician notifies a contact, they:
- Advise the contact they have been named as a contact of a specific infection
- Do **not** disclose the identity of the index patient (though the contact may be able to identify them)
- Advise the contact to attend for sexual health check and treatment (GP, Sexual Health clinic, or Sexual Wellbeing Aotearoa)

For complex situations, your doctor can contact the sexual health service to discuss contact tracing on an anonymous basis.

---

## Syphilis and HIV — Secondary Care Involvement

For **syphilis**: Your doctor will seek sexual health advice or refer for sexual health assessment. Contact tracing is coordinated through secondary care.

For **HIV**: Contact tracing is organised through secondary care. The laboratory automatically notifies a positive HIV result; the ordering clinician will receive a questionnaire to complete. Your doctor will seek sexual health or infectious diseases advice or refer for assessment.

---

## When Your Doctor Will Seek Specialist Advice

- Complex contact tracing situations
- Syphilis diagnosis — seek sexual health advice or request sexual health assessment
- HIV diagnosis — seek sexual health or infectious diseases advice or request sexual health assessment
