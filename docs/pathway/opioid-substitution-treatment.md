# Opioid Substitution Treatment

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers opioid substitution treatment (OST) — a medically supervised approach to treating opioid dependence using longer-acting opioid medications to reduce withdrawal, cravings, and illicit drug use.

---

## Important — Prescribing Requires Specialist Authorisation

Prescribing controlled drugs for the treatment of addiction is only legal when authorised by a gazetted (government-approved) service. Your doctor cannot prescribe OST medications independently — treatment must be arranged through an authorised specialist service. Discuss this with your doctor, who can make a referral.

---

## What Is Opioid Substitution Treatment?

OST uses a prescribed, longer-acting opioid medication to:

- Reduce or eliminate withdrawal symptoms
- Reduce cravings for illicit opioids
- Lower the risk of overdose, infection, and other harms associated with illicit drug use
- Support stability in daily life, allowing engagement with other health and social support

Treatment is ongoing — your doctor and specialist team will monitor your progress and adjust the plan as needed.

---

## Medications Used in OST

Your doctor and the specialist service may consider the following medications:

- **Methadone** — a long-acting opioid liquid, taken once daily under supervision (at least initially). Dose is carefully titrated to your needs.
- **Buprenorphine + naloxone (Suboxone)** — a combination tablet or film placed under the tongue. Buprenorphine is a partial opioid agonist; naloxone is added to deter injection misuse. Often preferred for its safety profile (ceiling effect reduces overdose risk).

The choice of medication depends on your clinical situation, preferences, and the specialist team's assessment. Both are effective — your doctor can discuss the differences with you.

---

## Overdose Risk — Naloxone

People on OST can still be at risk of accidental overdose, particularly when:

- Starting or restarting treatment after a break
- Using other substances alongside OST medications (e.g., alcohol, benzodiazepines)
- Dose changes occur

**Naloxone** (also called Narcan) is a medication that rapidly reverses opioid overdose. It is available free of charge through the specialist OST service and can be kept at home for emergency use by the person on treatment or those around them.

Ask your doctor or specialist team about getting naloxone and how to use it.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess you and, if appropriate, refer you to the specialist opioid treatment service. The assessment typically covers:

- Current opioid use — type, frequency, route, duration
- History of previous OST or other treatment attempts
- Physical health, including any complications of opioid use (e.g., infections, liver disease)
- Mental health and other substance use
- Social circumstances and support network

The specialist service conducts its own detailed assessment before starting treatment.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following as part of your care:

- Referral to the specialist opioid treatment service for assessment and OST initiation
- Ongoing shared care once you are stable in treatment (your GP prescribes with specialist oversight)
- Naloxone prescription for overdose prevention
- Support for other health needs related to opioid use (e.g., hepatitis C treatment, mental health, wound care)
- Coordination with pharmacy for supervised dosing arrangements
- Gradual increase in "takeaway" doses as treatment stability is established

All prescribing decisions are made by your doctor in collaboration with the specialist team.
