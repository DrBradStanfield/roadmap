# Progestogen-only Contraceptive Pills (POP)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers what to know about progestogen-only contraceptive pills — who they suit, how to start, and what to do if you miss a pill.

---

## Background

Progestogen-only pills (POPs) work by thickening cervical mucus and altering the uterine lining to prevent implantation. In some people, they may also prevent or disrupt ovulation.

**Key facts:**
- Over 99% effective with perfect use; 92% effective with typical use
- Safe for people with contraindications to estrogen (e.g. those who cannot take the combined pill)
- Safe to use while breastfeeding and up to menopause
- Contain less than 20% of the progestogen found in the combined oral contraceptive pill

Note: Long-acting reversible contraceptives (LARCs — implant and IUDs) are generally recommended as first-choice contraception in New Zealand due to better efficacy and lower user-error risk. Your doctor can discuss all options with you.

---

## Contraindications

Your doctor will check for reasons a POP may not be suitable.

**Absolute contraindications (POP should not be used):**
- Known or suspected pregnancy
- Unexplained vaginal bleeding (cause must be established first)
- Currently being treated for breast cancer

**Relative contraindications (specialist judgement required):**
- Decompensated liver cirrhosis
- Liver tumour (benign or malignant)
- Completed treatment for breast cancer
- If ischaemic heart disease, stroke, or TIA develops while already taking a POP

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will typically:

- Review your medical history and check for contraindications
- Ask about current medications that could interact with the POP
- Check for abnormal uterine bleeding
- Assess whether pregnancy can be reasonably excluded
- Measure blood pressure and BMI
- Check cervical screening is up to date
- Offer STI screening if appropriate

Pelvic examination is not routinely required before starting a POP.

---

## Drug Interactions

Certain medications reduce POP effectiveness. Your doctor will check these, but key ones include:

**Liver enzyme-inducing drugs** (reduce contraceptive efficacy):
- Anti-seizure medications: carbamazepine, phenytoin, oxcarbazepine, phenobarbital, primidone, rufinamide, eslicarbazepine, topiramate
- Antibiotics: rifampicin, rifabutin
- Antiretrovirals: nevirapine, nelfinavir, efavirenz, etravirine, tipranavir/ritonavir
- Others: St John's wort, bosentan, modafinil, aprepitant, sugammadex
- The enzyme-inducing effect persists for 28 days after stopping these drugs, even after a single dose

**Other interactions:**
- Griseofulvin
- Lamotrigine: POPs may increase lamotrigine levels — no extra contraceptive precautions needed, but watch for side effects
- Drugs causing severe vomiting or diarrhoea (e.g. orlistat) — may reduce absorption

## POPs Available in New Zealand

All three options below are fully funded:

| Drug | Brand | Pill window |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Desogestrel 75 micrograms | Cerazette | 12-hour window before considered missed |
| Levonorgestrel 30 micrograms | Microlut | 3-hour window before considered missed |
| Norethisterone 350 micrograms | Noriday | 3-hour window before considered missed |

Your doctor may consider cerazette (desogestrel) if a more forgiving schedule is needed, as it has a 12-hour window rather than 3 hours.

---

## Starting the POP

**Traditional start:** Begin on day 1–5 of your menstrual cycle. Effective immediately.

**Quick start (at any time in the cycle):**
- Can be used when prompt cover is needed, periods are irregular or infrequent, or after pregnancy
- Your doctor will exclude current pregnancy first
- If you have had unprotected sex in the last 5 days, emergency contraception will be offered
- Use condoms for the first 48 hours (until 3 consecutive pills have been taken correctly)
- A follow-up pregnancy test is recommended at least 21 days after last unprotected sex

**After pregnancy:**
- Ideally start within 21 days of giving birth, or within 5 days of miscarriage, abortion, ectopic pregnancy, or GTD treatment — effective immediately
- If starting later, use traditional or quick start method

**Switching from another method:** Your doctor will advise timing based on your previous contraceptive.

---

## How to Take the POP

- Take one pill every day continuously — no pill-free break
- Take at the same time each day (within the 3-hour or 12-hour window depending on the pill)
- Tips to remember: set a phone alarm, take it after brushing teeth
- Does not protect against sexually transmitted infections — use condoms for STI protection

**Emergency pill supply:** Accredited pharmacists can supply POP (desogestrel, levonorgestrel, or norethisterone) without a prescription for patients aged 16–52 years, provided it was prescribed by a doctor in the last 3 years and you have had at least one review since.

---

## Missed or Ineffective Pills

**If a pill is late or missed:**

- Levonorgestrel (Microlut) or Norethisterone (Noriday): missed if more than 3 hours late
- Desogestrel (Cerazette): missed if more than 12 hours late

If a pill is missed:
1. Take it as soon as you remember
2. Use condoms or avoid sex for the next 48 hours (until 3 consecutive pills taken on time)
3. Consider emergency contraception if you have had unprotected sex

**If vomiting within 2 hours of taking the pill:**
- Take another pill immediately
- If a replacement pill is not taken within the window (3 or 12 hours), treat as a missed pill

**If severe diarrhoea lasting more than 24 hours:**
- Treat as missed pills — use condoms for 48 hours after symptoms resolve

**If starting a new medication that may interact:**
- See your doctor promptly — you may need emergency contraception or to switch method

---

## Side Effects

Common effects reported by POP users:
- Altered bleeding patterns (very common): 20% become amenorrhoeic, 40% have regular bleeding, 45% have irregular unpredictable bleeding
- Headaches
- Mood changes
- Weight gain — there is no evidence of a causal link between POPs and weight gain

**Ectopic pregnancy risk:** POPs reduce overall pregnancy risk. However, if pregnancy does occur, 10% may be ectopic. Report suspected pregnancy early.

**Common concerns:** There is no evidence linking POPs to cardiovascular disease or loss of libido.

---

## Benefits

- No delay in return to fertility when stopped
- No procedure required
- Suitable option when estrogen is contraindicated
- Safe during breastfeeding
- Can be used from puberty through to age 55

---

## Irregular Bleeding

Altered bleeding is common and expected, especially in the first few months.

Your doctor may consider:
- Ruling out underlying causes (pregnancy, STI, endometrial or cervical pathology)
- If bleeding does not settle within 3 months, further investigation
- Changing to a different POP preparation, the combined pill, or a long-acting method if bleeding is troublesome

---

## When to Stop

- **Wanting to become pregnant:** Stop at any time — fertility returns immediately
- **If pregnancy occurs:** Stop the POP; progestogen exposure in early pregnancy is not known to harm the fetus
- **Menopause:** Your doctor will review regularly from age 50. Natural loss of fertility can be assumed from age 55 — stop the POP at that point

---

## Follow-up

Your doctor will typically review:
- After 3 months (some patients benefit from a 1-month early check)
- Annually thereafter

Review includes: blood pressure, BMI, any new medications or conditions, side effects, missed pill frequency, and satisfaction with the method.
