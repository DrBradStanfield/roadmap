# Interpreting SDQ Results

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how to interpret Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) results from B4 School Checks (B4SC), and how to act on them based on the level of concern identified.

---

## Background

The SDQ is a validated tool that considers aspects of a child's behaviour and emotional well-being. As part of the B4 School Check, both parents (SDQ/P) and early childhood education centre teachers (SDQ/T) complete their respective versions of the form.

There are two versions:
- **SDQ/P** — completed by parents
- **SDQ/T** — completed by teachers

---

## Assessment

### Step 1: Review the SDQ Score

Determine the level of concern based on which form was completed:

**SDQ/P (parents' questionnaire):**
- Score 14–16 — some concern
- Score 17 or more — of concern

**SDQ/T (teacher's questionnaire):**
- Score 12–15 — some concern
- Score 16 or more — of concern

### Step 2: Consider the Broader Context

Your doctor will also consider:

- Current and previous social and family circumstances
- Any involvement of Oranga Tamariki (Ministry for Children)
- Family violence
- Difficulties with early childhood education centre performance
- Any previous formal assessments
- Evidence of major home or school disruption
- Whether any services are currently involved with the family
- Whether there are significant mental health issues
- Whether there are significant health or developmental concerns

---

## Management

Management is guided by the level of concern identified.

**For all children with elevated scores:**

Your doctor may recommend discussing the SDQ result with the child's early childhood education centre, so that consistent behavioural management strategies can be used both at home and at the centre.

If the child is not enrolled at an early childhood education centre, your doctor may advise on local options and assist with accessing them.

**Based on score level, your doctor may consider:**

- **SDQ/P score 16 or more, or SDQ/T score 15 or more:** Referral to the Ministry of Education Early Intervention Service. High scores generally indicate referral is expected, unless the child is already receiving appropriate care.

- **Score below the high threshold but with ongoing concerns:** Referral to appropriate community supports, such as family social services, child and parenting community support, or other local agencies.

- **Significant mental health concerns:** Referral for non-acute child and adolescent mental health assessment.

- **Significant health or developmental concerns:** Referral for non-acute paediatric medicine assessment.

- **Severe behaviour and complex family issues:** Early referral to Ministry of Education Early Intervention Service, to ensure eligible children receive a comprehensive service before starting school.

Any referrals are made in consultation with the child's usual general practitioner.

---

*This information is intended to help you understand what your doctor may assess and consider. It is not a substitute for professional medical advice. Always discuss your child's results and next steps with your doctor.*
