# Metabolic Syndrome (Insulin Resistance Syndrome)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways. Last reviewed: September 2018.*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of metabolic syndrome. Metabolic syndrome is an important risk factor for type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

---

## Background

Metabolic syndrome is common in New Zealand because of high levels of obesity.

Metabolic syndrome consists of a combination of:

- Raised blood pressure
- Abnormal lipids
- Increased waist circumference
- Impaired glucose metabolism

People with metabolic syndrome have 2 to 3 times the risk of developing diabetes and cardiovascular disease, and a 1.6 times higher overall mortality rate.

---

## Assessment

### Risk Factors

Your doctor may consider general risk factors for developing metabolic syndrome:

- Increased body weight or waist circumference
- Older age
- Genetic risk of diabetes or heart disease
- Physical inactivity
- Early lifestyle influences
- Unhealthy dietary patterns (e.g. high saturated fat, sugar-sweetened drinks)
- Smoking

**Medications that increase risk:** Many medications used in mental health raise the risk of metabolic syndrome, including:

- Atypical antipsychotics (e.g. olanzapine, clozapine)
- SSRI antidepressants used long-term

Some anticonvulsants also increase the risk.

### Associated Medical Conditions

Metabolic syndrome is associated with a number of other conditions your doctor may consider:

- Type 2 diabetes
- Ischaemic heart disease
- Fatty liver disease (MASLD)
- Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)
- Chronic kidney disease
- Obstructive sleep apnoea
- Gout and hyperuricaemia
- Rare endocrine conditions (e.g. Cushing syndrome)

### Physical Examination

Your doctor will typically measure:

- Height, weight, and body mass index (BMI)
- Waist circumference
- Blood pressure

BMI is not part of the diagnosis of metabolic syndrome, but it is still useful to know. BMI is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared:

- Less than 18.5: underweight
- Between 18.5 and 24.9: healthy weight
- Between 25 and 29.9: overweight
- Greater than 30: obese (mild greater than 30, moderate 35 or more, extreme 40 or more)

Waist circumference is measured halfway between the lowest rib and the top of the hip bone. If that is hard to find, it is measured at the narrowest point seen from the front. The tape should be level and lie loosely against the waist.

**Waist circumference thresholds for increased risk of chronic disease:**

| Population | Gender | Increased risk | Greatly increased risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| European | Men | ≥ 94 cm | ≥ 102 cm |
| European | Women | ≥ 80 cm | ≥ 88 cm |
| South Asian (incl. Chinese, Malay, Asian-Indian) | Men | ≥ 90 cm | Not given |
| South Asian | Women | ≥ 80 cm | Not given |
| Māori and Pacific Islander | Men | ≥ 102 cm | Not given |
| Māori and Pacific Islander | Women | ≥ 88 cm | Not given |
| Eastern Mediterranean or Middle Eastern | Men | Use European recommendations | Not given |
| Eastern Mediterranean or Middle Eastern | Women | Use European recommendations | Not given |
| South and Central American | Men | Use South Asian recommendations | Not given |
| South and Central American | Women | Use South Asian recommendations | Not given |
| Sub-Saharan African | Men | Use European recommendations | Not given |
| Sub-Saharan African | Women | Use European recommendations | Not given |

### Investigations

Your doctor may arrange:

- Lipids (including triglycerides and HDL cholesterol)
- Fasting glucose
- Full blood count, liver function tests, renal function
- Tests for other possible diagnoses where needed

---

## Diagnosis

Metabolic syndrome is diagnosed when there is **elevated ethnic-specific waist circumference** (see table above) **plus at least 2 of the following 4 criteria:**

1. **Elevated triglycerides:** ≥ 1.7 mmol/L
2. **Reduced HDL cholesterol:** Men: < 1.0 mmol/L; Women: < 1.3 mmol/L (or on drug treatment for reduced HDL)
3. **Elevated blood pressure:** ≥ 130 mmHg systolic or ≥ 85 mmHg diastolic
4. **Elevated fasting glucose:** > 5.5 mmol/L

---

## Management

There is no specific medication for metabolic syndrome itself. Treatment focuses on three areas.

### 1. Treat Individual Risk Factors

Your doctor may consider treating each risk factor you have:

- Raised lipids
- High blood pressure
- Fatty liver disease
- Prediabetes or type 2 diabetes

### 2. Lifestyle Modification

Lifestyle change matters most, especially weight reduction and more physical activity. Your doctor may refer you to support services, such as:

- **Green Prescription:** support to become more active
- **Weight management** support
- **Smoking cessation:** if applicable
- **Alcohol reduction:** if applicable

### 3. Medication Review

Your doctor may review your current medicines, including any that can raise the risk of metabolic syndrome (see Medications that increase risk above).

---

> This information is a general guide only. The right approach for you depends on your individual circumstances. Always discuss assessment and treatment decisions with your doctor.
