# Metabolic Syndrome (Insulin Resistance Syndrome)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of metabolic syndrome — a cluster of risk factors that significantly increase the risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.

---

## Background

Metabolic syndrome consists of a combination of:

- Raised blood pressure
- Abnormal lipids
- Increased waist circumference
- Impaired glucose metabolism

People with metabolic syndrome have 2 to 3 times the risk of developing diabetes and cardiovascular disease, and a 1.6 times higher overall mortality rate. The primary goal of identifying metabolic syndrome is early intervention to prevent these outcomes.

---

## Assessment

### Risk Factors

Your doctor will consider general risk factors for developing metabolic syndrome:

- Increased body weight or waist circumference
- Older age
- Genetic risk of diabetes or heart disease
- Physical inactivity
- Unhealthy dietary patterns (e.g. high saturated fat, sugar-sweetened drinks)
- Smoking

**Medications that increase risk:** Some medications raise the risk of metabolic syndrome, including:

- Atypical antipsychotics (e.g. olanzapine, clozapine)
- SSRI antidepressants used long-term
- Some anticonvulsants

### Associated Medical Conditions

Metabolic syndrome is associated with a number of other conditions your doctor may consider:

- Type 2 diabetes
- Ischaemic heart disease
- Fatty liver disease (MASLD)
- Polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)
- Chronic kidney disease
- Obstructive sleep apnoea
- Gout and hyperuricaemia
- Rare endocrine conditions (e.g. Cushing syndrome)

### Physical Examination

Your doctor will typically measure:

- Height, weight, and body mass index (BMI)
- Waist circumference
- Blood pressure

**Waist circumference thresholds for increased risk of chronic disease:**

| Population | Gender | Increased risk | Greatly increased risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| European | Men | ≥ 94 cm | ≥ 102 cm |
| European | Women | ≥ 80 cm | ≥ 88 cm |
| South Asian (incl. Chinese, Malay, Asian-Indian) | Men | ≥ 90 cm | — |
| South Asian | Women | ≥ 80 cm | — |
| Māori and Pacific Islander | Men | ≥ 102 cm | — |
| Māori and Pacific Islander | Women | ≥ 88 cm | — |
| Eastern Mediterranean or Middle Eastern | Men | Use European recommendations | — |
| Eastern Mediterranean or Middle Eastern | Women | Use European recommendations | — |
| South and Central American | Men | Use South Asian recommendations | — |
| South and Central American | Women | Use South Asian recommendations | — |
| Sub-Saharan African | Men | Use European recommendations | — |
| Sub-Saharan African | Women | Use European recommendations | — |

### Investigations

Your doctor may arrange:

- Lipids (including triglycerides and HDL cholesterol)
- Fasting glucose
- Full blood count, liver function tests, renal function
- Additional tests based on clinical picture

---

## Diagnosis

Metabolic syndrome is diagnosed when there is **elevated ethnic-specific waist circumference** (see table above) **plus at least 2 of the following 4 criteria:**

1. **Elevated triglycerides** — ≥ 1.7 mmol/L (or on drug treatment for raised triglycerides)
2. **Reduced HDL cholesterol** — Men: < 1.0 mmol/L; Women: < 1.3 mmol/L (or on drug treatment for low HDL)
3. **Elevated blood pressure** — ≥ 130 mmHg systolic or ≥ 85 mmHg diastolic (or on drug treatment for hypertension)
4. **Elevated fasting glucose** — > 5.5 mmol/L (or previously diagnosed type 2 diabetes)

---

## Management

There is no single medication for metabolic syndrome itself. Your doctor will focus on two main approaches:

### 1. Treat Individual Risk Factors

Your doctor may consider treating each component separately:

- **Raised lipids** — lifestyle changes and possibly lipid-lowering medication (e.g. statins, fibrates)
- **High blood pressure** — lifestyle changes and possibly antihypertensive medication
- **Fatty liver disease** — weight loss, alcohol reduction, metabolic management
- **Prediabetes or type 2 diabetes** — dietary changes, physical activity, and possibly medication (e.g. metformin)

### 2. Lifestyle Modification

Lifestyle change is the cornerstone of treatment. Your doctor may refer you to support services for:

- **Weight reduction** — even modest weight loss (5–10%) significantly reduces metabolic risk
- **Increased physical activity** — regular aerobic and resistance exercise improves insulin sensitivity, lipids, and blood pressure
- **Smoking cessation** — if applicable
- **Alcohol reduction** — if applicable

### 3. Medication Review

Your doctor will review any existing medications that may be contributing to metabolic syndrome (e.g. antipsychotics, antidepressants, anticonvulsants) and consider whether alternatives are appropriate.

---

> This information is a general guide only. The right approach for you depends on your individual circumstances. Always discuss assessment and treatment decisions with your doctor.
