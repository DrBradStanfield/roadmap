# Insulin for Type 2 Diabetes

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways. Last reviewed: March 2026.*

This pathway covers the stepped approach to starting and intensifying insulin therapy in adults with type 2 diabetes.

> Any changes to your insulin regimen should be made in discussion with your doctor.

---

## Stepped Treatment Overview

Insulin is stepped up when HbA1c remains above target despite lifestyle management and other medications:

1. **Step 1: Basal insulin** (while continuing oral medications)
2. **Step 2: Add bolus or switch to premixed** (chosen based on lifestyle, diet, monitoring preferences, and ability to inject)
3. **Step 3: Additional bolus insulin** with other meals
4. **Beyond step 3:** If HbA1c is still above target, your doctor may change the regimen or seek specialist diabetes advice

Sulfonylureas are stopped when a patient moves to step 2 or beyond. Your doctor may consider switching to the other second-step regimen before stepping up.

---

## Step 1: Basal Insulin

### When to start

Your doctor will consider starting basal insulin when:

- Blood sugar targets are not met despite lifestyle changes and maximum oral/GLP-1 therapy
- Symptoms of insulin deficiency (excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss)
- Significantly elevated HbA1c (e.g. above 90 mmol/mol) at any stage, including at diagnosis
- Previous diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic syndrome (HHS)
- Pregnancy or planning a pregnancy requiring insulin, as other glucose-lowering medicines need to be stopped

Other diabetes medicines are usually continued.

### Types of basal insulin

- **Isophane insulin:** Humulin NPH. Protaphane supply will end at the end of 2026. People on Protaphane can be switched to Humulin NPH at the same dose, but will need a different pen.
- **Glargine:** Lantus

### Starting dose

Your doctor will calculate your starting dose:

- **Standard dose:** 0.2 units/kg daily
- **Lower starting dose (0.1 units/kg)** if: HbA1c below 64 mmol/mol, low BMI (under 18 kg/m²), elderly, or kidney/liver problems

Basal insulin is taken at night. Its main role is to reduce overnight glucose production by the liver.

### Titration (adjusting your dose)

- Check your fasting blood glucose each morning
- If fasting blood glucose stays above 7 mmol/L on 3 consecutive days, increase dose by 10% (or 2 units)
- Stop increasing when fasting glucose is below 7 mmol/L, or if hypoglycaemia occurs, or if daily dose reaches 0.5 units/kg
- Your doctor may consider reducing the dose with major changes in diet (e.g. Ramadan), significant weight loss, or when new glucose-lowering medicines are added, especially if HbA1c is less than 64 mmol/mol
- A dose reduction of 20% or more may be considered for frequent or severe hypoglycaemia

### Monitoring

- HbA1c at 3 months after starting, then every 6 months once stable

### Administration tips

- Establish a consistent routine (e.g., take it with dinner or before bed)
- Use 4–5 mm pen needles for best absorption and less discomfort
- Rotate injection sites
- Doses may need to be reduced the night before and on the day of strenuous exercise

## Step 2: Adding or Switching Insulin

If HbA1c remains above target despite optimised basal insulin, your doctor will move to a second-step regimen. There are two main options:

### Choosing between Basal-Bolus and Premixed

| | Basal-bolus | Premixed |
|-|-------------|---------|
| **Best if** | Flexible schedule, varied diet, irregular mealtimes | Regular schedule, consistent diet and mealtimes |
| **Monitoring** | More frequent blood glucose checks | Less frequent checks |
| **Injections** | More injections | Fewer injections |
| **Intensification** | Easier to adjust rapidly | Slower intensification |
| **Ability to inject** (thinking, dexterity, supervision) | Good ability | Reduced ability |

---

### Option A: Basal Plus Bolus Insulin

Your doctor will add a rapid-acting insulin before your largest meal.

**When considered:** Fasting glucose below 7 mmol/L but HbA1c still above target, or maximum basal dose reached (0.5 units/kg/day).

#### Rapid-acting insulins used

- NovoRapid, Humalog, or Apidra

#### Starting dose

- 4 units, or 10% of basal insulin dose (maximum 10 units)
- Your basal dose may be reduced to prevent hypoglycaemia, especially if HbA1c is less than 64 mmol/mol

#### Titration

- Check blood glucose before the meal and 2 hours after
- Increase rapid-acting dose by 2 units if the rise with the meal is consistently above 3 mmol/L (on 3 occasions)
- Stop increasing if the meal rise is typically below 3 mmol/L, or if hypoglycaemia occurs

#### Administration tips

- Inject before the meal. Don't inject a meal dose if skipping that meal
- Use different coloured pens for different insulin types
- Doses may need to be reduced by 20–50% for meals immediately before and after strenuous exercise
- Your doctor may suggest a memory aid if you sometimes forget a dose
- If you are very sensitive to insulin (e.g. a thin older adult), your doctor may consider a pen with half-unit increments

A dietitian can help match your insulin dose to your carbohydrate intake. Bolus doses will likely differ between meals.

---

### Option B: Premixed or Co-formulated Insulin

Combines a basal and rapid/short-acting insulin in one injection.

**When considered (with largest meal):** HbA1c above target on basal insulin despite fasting glucose below 7 mmol/L and/or a basal dose of 0.5 units/kg/day, and the patient eats one regular meal per day.

**When considered (breakfast and dinner):** As above, but the patient regularly eats both breakfast and dinner.

**Starting dose:** Your doctor converts your daily basal dose to the equivalent dose of premixed insulin, taken before meals. With breakfast and dinner, it is usually split in half, or one-third and two-thirds if one meal is larger.

#### Types of premixed insulin

| Product | Mix | Notes |
|---------|-----|-------|
| NovoMix 30 | 30% rapid / 70% basal | Reduced risk of delayed hypoglycaemia. Being discontinued 2026 |
| Humalog Mix 25 | 25% rapid / 75% basal | Reduced risk of delayed hypoglycaemia |
| Humalog Mix 50 | 50% rapid / 50% basal | May be useful with large carbohydrate meals, especially if post-meal glucose stays high on 25% or 30% mixes |
| Humulin 30/70 | 30% short-acting / 70% basal | Higher risk of delayed hypoglycaemia |
| Ryzodeg 70/30 | 30% rapid / 70% basal (degludec) | Twice daily maximum; long half-life (25 hours). A dose change takes at least 5 days to take full effect, so dose changes are usually made only every 5–7 days; use with caution in elderly and kidney disease |

NovoMix 30 supply will likely end at the end of 2026. Your doctor may switch you dose for dose to Humalog Mix 25 or Ryzodeg. With Ryzodeg, a lower dose may be needed if you are at risk of hypoglycaemia (e.g. older age, kidney problems).

#### Pen requirements

- Ryzodeg 70/30, Humalog Mix 25, and Humalog Mix 50 come only in 3 mL cartridges, so each needs a reusable pen that fits it
- Premixed insulins must be gently inverted (mixed) before each use; co-formulated (Ryzodeg) does not

#### Titration (premixed)

- **With the largest meal:** monitor fasting glucose, pre-meal glucose, and 2–3 hours after the meal. Increase dose by 10% every 3 days if glucose rises more than 3 mmol/L with the meal and fasting glucose is above 10 mmol/L
- **With breakfast and dinner:** monitor before each meal and 2 hours after. Increase each meal dose separately by 10% if, on 3 consecutive occasions, the glucose rise with the meal is greater than 3 mmol/L and pre-meal glucose is greater than 10 mmol/L
- Stop increasing if these levels are not reached or any hypoglycaemia occurs
- Reduce dose before meals that are significantly smaller than usual, and before/after strenuous exercise

If HbA1c stays above target, your doctor may switch to Humalog Mix 50 for high after-meal glucose, or to basal plus bolus if hypoglycaemia or an irregular diet is a problem.

---

## Step 3: Additional Bolus Insulin with Other Meals

If HbA1c remains above target on step 2, with fasting glucose less than 7 mmol/L or the maximum dose (0.5 units/kg per day) reached, your doctor may add rapid-acting insulin before other meals.

**Starting dose:** 4 units, or 10% of daily equivalent dose (maximum 10 units).

**Titration:** Increase by 2 units if the post-meal blood glucose rise is consistently above 3 mmol/L on 3 occasions.

---

## Correction Doses of Rapid-acting Insulin

Your doctor may prescribe correction doses to lower blood glucose before meals, at bedtime, or on sick days. Use rapid-acting insulin only (NovoRapid, Humalog, or Apidra), never basal or premixed for corrections. Leave at least 3 hours between correction doses.

| Total daily insulin dose | Correction dose |
|--------------------------|----------------|
| Up to 25 units/day | 1 unit per 4 mmol above 8 mmol/L |
| 26–40 units/day | 1 unit per 3 mmol above 8 mmol/L |
| 41–75 units/day | 1 unit per 2 mmol above 8 mmol/L |
| 76+ units/day | 1 unit per 1 mmol above 8 mmol/L |

Start conservatively (limit to 6–10 units) until you know how your body responds.

## HbA1c Targets and Thresholds

HbA1c is usually measured 3 months after starting a new regimen or reaching a stable dose, then every 6 months while on target.

If HbA1c stays above target, your doctor may first try non-insulin options before changing regimens:

- Optimising lifestyle and other treatment
- Checking how and where you inject, and whether doses are being missed
- Referral to a dietitian or diabetes service
- Screening for depression and diabetes distress
