# Insulin for Type 2 Diabetes

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the stepped approach to starting and intensifying insulin therapy in type 2 diabetes. Insulin for type 1 diabetes is managed differently — always in specialist care. Any changes to your insulin regimen should be made in discussion with your doctor.

---

## Stepped Treatment Overview

Insulin is stepped up when HbA1c remains above target despite lifestyle management and other medications:

1. **Step 1 — Basal insulin** (while continuing oral medications)
2. **Step 2 — Add bolus or switch to premixed** (chosen based on lifestyle, diet, and monitoring preferences)
3. **Step 3 — Additional bolus insulin** with other meals
4. **Beyond step 3** — Specialist review if HbA1c still above target

Sulfonylureas are stopped when a patient moves to step 2 or beyond.

---

## Step 1: Basal Insulin

### When to start

Your doctor will consider starting basal insulin when:

- Blood sugar targets are not met despite lifestyle changes and maximum oral/GLP-1 therapy
- Symptoms of insulin deficiency (excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss)
- Significantly elevated HbA1c (e.g. above 90 mmol/mol) at any stage, including at diagnosis
- Previous diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) or hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic syndrome (HHS)
- Pregnancy or planning a pregnancy requiring insulin

### Types of basal insulin

- **Isophane insulin** — Protaphane (being discontinued 2026; switch to Humulin NPH) or Humulin NPH
- **Glargine** — Lantus

### Starting dose

Your doctor will calculate your starting dose:

- **Standard dose:** 0.2 units/kg daily
- **Lower starting dose (0.1 units/kg)** if: HbA1c below 64 mmol/mol, low BMI (under 18 kg/m²), elderly, or kidney/liver problems

Basal insulin is taken at night — its main role is to reduce overnight glucose production by the liver.

### Titration (adjusting your dose)

- Check your fasting blood glucose each morning
- If fasting blood glucose stays above 7 mmol/L on 3 consecutive days — increase dose by 10% (or 2 units)
- Stop increasing when fasting glucose is below 7 mmol/L, or if hypoglycaemia occurs, or if daily dose reaches 0.5 units/kg

### Monitoring

- HbA1c at 3 months after starting, then every 6 months once stable

### Administration tips

- Establish a consistent routine (e.g., take it with dinner or before bed)
- Use 4–5 mm pen needles for best absorption and less discomfort
- Rotate injection sites
- Reduce dose the night before and day of strenuous exercise
- If thin and insulin-sensitive, your doctor may prescribe a pen with half-unit increments

## Step 2: Adding or Switching Insulin

If HbA1c remains above target despite optimised basal insulin, your doctor will move to a second-step regimen. There are two main options:

### Choosing between Basal-Bolus and Premixed

| | Basal-bolus | Premixed |
|-|-------------|---------|
| **Best if** | Flexible schedule, varied diet, irregular mealtimes | Regular schedule, consistent diet and mealtimes |
| **Monitoring** | More frequent blood glucose checks | Less frequent checks |
| **Injections** | More injections | Fewer injections |
| **Intensification** | Easier to adjust rapidly | Slower intensification |

---

### Option A: Basal Plus Bolus Insulin

Your doctor will add a rapid-acting insulin before your largest meal.

**When considered:** Fasting glucose below 7 mmol/L but HbA1c still above target, or maximum basal dose reached (0.5 units/kg/day).

#### Rapid-acting insulins used

- NovoRapid, Humalog, or Apidra

#### Starting dose

- 4 units, OR 10% of basal insulin dose — whichever is lower (maximum 10 units)

#### Titration

- Check blood glucose before the meal and 2 hours after
- Increase rapid-acting dose by 2 units if the rise with the meal is consistently above 3 mmol/L (on 3 occasions)
- Stop increasing if the meal rise is typically below 3 mmol/L, or if hypoglycaemia occurs

#### Administration tips

- Inject before the meal — don't inject if skipping that meal
- Use different coloured pens for different insulin types
- Reduce doses by 20–50% for meals before or after strenuous exercise
- Memory aids (e.g. NovoPen Echo or InsulCheck) can help if you sometimes forget

A dietitian can help match your insulin dose to your carbohydrate intake.

---

### Option B: Premixed or Co-formulated Insulin

Combines a basal and rapid/short-acting insulin in one injection.

**When considered (with largest meal):** HbA1c above target despite fasting glucose below 7 mmol/L, and the patient eats one regular meal per day.

**When considered (breakfast and dinner):** As above, but the patient regularly eats both breakfast and dinner.

#### Types of premixed insulin

| Product | Mix | Notes |
|---------|-----|-------|
| NovoMix 30 | 30% rapid / 70% basal | Being discontinued 2026 |
| Humalog Mix 25 | 25% rapid / 75% basal | Reduced risk of delayed hypoglycaemia |
| Humalog Mix 50 | 50% rapid / 50% basal | Useful if large carbohydrate meals cause post-meal spikes |
| Humulin 30/70 | 30% short-acting / 70% basal | Higher risk of delayed hypoglycaemia |
| Ryzodeg 70/30 | 30% rapid / 70% basal (degludec) | Once or twice daily maximum; long half-life (25 hours) — only adjust dose every 5–7 days; use with caution in elderly and kidney disease |

#### Pen requirements

- Ryzodeg 70/30 — requires a NovoPen 4
- Humalog Mix 25 and Mix 50 — require a Savvio pen
- Premixed insulins must be gently inverted (mixed) before each use; co-formulated (Ryzodeg) does not

#### Titration (premixed)

- Monitor fasting glucose, pre-meal glucose, and 2–3 hours after the meal
- Increase dose by 10% every 3 days if post-meal glucose rises more than 3 mmol/L and pre-meal glucose is above 10 mmol/L
- Reduce dose before meals that are significantly smaller than usual, and before/after strenuous exercise

---

## Step 3: Additional Bolus Insulin with Other Meals

If HbA1c remains above target on step 2, your doctor may add rapid-acting insulin before other meals.

**Starting dose:** 4 units, or 10% of daily equivalent dose (maximum 10 units).

**Titration:** Increase by 2 units if the post-meal blood glucose rise is consistently above 3 mmol/L on 3 occasions.

---

## Correction Doses of Rapid-acting Insulin

Your doctor may prescribe correction doses to lower blood glucose before meals, at bedtime, or on sick days. Use rapid-acting insulin only (NovoRapid, Humalog, or Apidra) — never basal or premixed for corrections. Leave at least 3 hours between correction doses.

| Total daily insulin dose | Correction dose |
|--------------------------|----------------|
| Up to 25 units/day | 1 unit per 4 mmol above 8 mmol/L |
| 26–40 units/day | 1 unit per 3 mmol above 8 mmol/L |
| 41–75 units/day | 1 unit per 2 mmol above 8 mmol/L |
| 76+ units/day | 1 unit per 1 mmol above 8 mmol/L |

Start conservatively (limit to 6–10 units) until you know how your body responds.

---

## Hypoglycaemia (Low Blood Sugar)

Hypoglycaemia is the most important safety issue with insulin. Your doctor will provide specific hypoglycaemia management instructions as part of your education. Key points:

- Know your symptoms: shakiness, sweating, confusion, rapid heartbeat, hunger
- Always have fast-acting carbohydrates available (e.g. glucose tablets, juice)
- Follow the sick-day rules provided by your doctor
- Discuss driving safety — current NZ rules require monitoring before and during driving on insulin

---

## HbA1c Targets and Thresholds

From 1 July 2026, the national diagnostic thresholds for HbA1c are changing:
- **Normal:** below 42 mmol/mol
- **Prediabetes:** 42–47 mmol/mol
- **Diabetes:** 48 mmol/mol or above

Your personal HbA1c target will be set by your doctor based on your individual circumstances.
