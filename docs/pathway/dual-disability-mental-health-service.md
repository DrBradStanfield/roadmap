# Dual Disability Mental Health Service

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers support for people with intellectual disability who also have significant mental health concerns. Your doctor can refer to the Regional Dual Disability Mental Health Service when community mental health services alone are not sufficient.

---

## Background

People with intellectual disability can experience mental health conditions just like anyone else — including depression, anxiety, psychosis, and other concerns. However, these conditions can be harder to recognise and assess because of communication difficulties, behavioural changes that may mask symptoms, or co-existing conditions such as autism spectrum disorder.

This service provides specialist assessment and support for people whose level of disability means they need input beyond what standard community mental health services can reasonably provide.

---

## Physical Causes to Rule Out First

Before mental health symptoms are attributed to a psychiatric cause, your doctor will consider whether a physical condition could be contributing. Common physical contributors include:

- **Delirium** (acute confusion from any cause)
- **Constipation** (a frequent and often overlooked cause of distress and behavioural change in this population)
- **Dental pain or other unrecognised pain**
- **Urinary tract infection**
- **Thyroid dysfunction**

If a physical cause is identified, it should be addressed before or alongside mental health assessment.

---

## Who This Service Is For

Your doctor may consider a referral to the Dual Disability Mental Health Service if:

- You have a confirmed diagnosis of intellectual disability
- There are current concerns about your mental wellbeing, in addition to your disability
- Your level of disability means you need specialist input — for example, because of limited communication skills, co-existing autism, or sensory or neurological impairment

The service is for people aged 18 and older. It does not manage situations where the primary concern is behavioural rather than mental health in nature.

---

## Management

Your doctor will assess whether the presentation is best managed by:

- Identifying and treating any physical contributors (see above)
- Engaging standard community mental health services for less complex presentations
- Referring to the Regional Dual Disability Mental Health Service for specialist input when the above are insufficient

Your doctor may consider involving other professionals already supporting you (e.g., disability support workers, community teams, other specialists) as part of a coordinated approach.

---

*Always discuss your concerns with your doctor. This information is a general guide and does not replace a clinical assessment.*
