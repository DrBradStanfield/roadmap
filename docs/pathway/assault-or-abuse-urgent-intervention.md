# Assault or Abuse — Urgent Intervention

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways (last reviewed July 2024)*

This pathway covers urgent intervention steps when there are immediate safety concerns related to assault or abuse — for both children and adults.

---

## Emergency — Immediate Danger

If anyone is in immediate danger, call **111** (Police or Ambulance).

---

## Children

### Oranga Tamariki (Child Protection)

Contact **Oranga Tamariki National Call Centre** for:

- Immediate concerns about the safety of children or pregnant women
- When a child's behaviour is putting their own or family members' safety at risk
- Uncertainty about what to do next or when advice is needed

**How to contact:**
- Phone: **0508 326 459**
- Email: [contact@ot.govt.nz](mailto:contact@ot.govt.nz)

### Police

Contact police on **111** if:

- There are immediate concerns about the safety of a child
- A child's behaviour is putting themselves or family members at immediate risk
- There is significant concern that a crime has been committed

---

## Adults

### Police

- **Emergency (happening now or acute danger):** Phone **111**
- **Non-emergency report:** Phone **105**, visit any police station, or use the [police online reporting form](https://webforms.police.govt.nz/en/health-practitioner-reporting#no-back) — a police officer will follow up

### Crisis Services — Sexual Abuse or Assault

Crisis support counsellors can:

- Provide support regardless of whether the patient chooses to go to the police
- Offer 24-hour crisis support and advice by phone
- Be available by phone during a GP consultation
- Attend the forensic medical assessment and police interview
- Help the patient make contact with police if they wish

**Contact options vary by region — ask your GP or emergency department for the appropriate local crisis service.**

### Women's Refuge

- National crisis line: **0800 REFUGE** (0800 733 843) — free, 24 hours
- Local refuges: [womensrefuge.org.nz](https://womensrefuge.org.nz/contact-us/find-your-local-refuge/)

---

## Key Principles

- Safety is the first priority — act on concern, do not wait for certainty
- Patients can access support whether or not they choose to involve police
- Forensic medical assessment may be available if the patient wishes to pursue this — ask your doctor or the crisis service
- For sexual assault, time-sensitive evidence collection may be relevant — seek advice promptly
