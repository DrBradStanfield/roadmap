# Punch or Shave Biopsy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers shave and punch biopsies performed for histological diagnosis of skin lesions. It does not cover excision biopsies.

---

## Key Warning — Melanoma

**Never perform a punch, shave, or incisional biopsy if melanoma is suspected.** Suspected melanoma always requires a full excision biopsy with a 2 mm margin. Your doctor will advise on the appropriate approach.

---

## About Skin Biopsy

A biopsy is taken when a skin lesion needs a histological (laboratory) diagnosis to guide further treatment.

A partial shave or punch biopsy may only sample part of a lesion, which can lead to false-negative results. Your doctor will consider whether a partial biopsy is appropriate or whether full excision is needed.

---

## Choosing the Right Method

Your doctor will consider the lesion's site, size, ability to heal, and the desired cosmetic result.

### Shave Biopsy

A superficial technique using a scalpel blade or curette to remove lesion tissue from the surface.

**Used for:**
- Lesions that are benign or have very low suspicion for malignancy, including those removed for cosmetic reasons (e.g., skin tags, filiform viral warts, seborrhoeic keratosis, papillomatous melanocytic naevi, hyperkeratotic actinic keratosis)
- Large macules that are not easily excised
- Cosmetically sensitive sites or areas with difficulty healing

**Not suitable for:**
- Suspected melanoma, BCC, or SCC
- Excluding melanoma in a suspicious naevus

A shave biopsy is always preferable to cryotherapy when histopathological confirmation is needed.

### Punch Biopsy

A cylindrical blade that removes a full-thickness core of skin including epidermis, dermis, and into subcutaneous fat.

**Used for:**
- Confirming histology of non-pigmented lesions when excision biopsy cannot be performed
- Lesions on the lower leg
- Sampling ulcerated, eroded, or excoriated lesions (always include a normal edge in the sample)

**Not suitable for:**
- Suspected melanoma
- Situations where excision biopsy would be more appropriate

**Note:** Punch biopsy obtains full-thickness tissue but may miss pathology due to sampling error — your doctor will always consider whether excision is preferable.

---

## The Procedure

Your doctor will:

1. **Obtain your written consent**, including a discussion of potential risks and complications.

2. **Mark the area** and photograph the biopsy site (best practice — photographs help locate the site if further excision is needed later).

3. **Administer local anaesthetic** (lidocaine/lignocaine, usually with adrenaline to reduce bleeding and extend anaesthetic time to about 90 minutes).

4. **Perform the biopsy:**
   - *Shave:* The blade is held parallel to the skin and the lesion is shaved off with tension applied to the surrounding skin. Only small spots of bleeding are expected.
   - *Punch:* The skin is stretched perpendicular to skin tension lines, then the punch is rotated through the skin into the subcutaneous fat. The specimen is elevated and freed, then the wound is closed with a suture.

5. **Send the specimen** to the laboratory for histology, along with a clearly labelled request form including: body site, specimen orientation, lesion history, diagnoses being considered, and any relevant medications.

6. **Set a reminder** to check histology results at 14 days.

---

## Risks and Complications

Your doctor will discuss these with you before the procedure:

- Adverse reactions to medications or dressings
- Bleeding (higher risk if you take blood thinners or anti-platelet medication)
- Infection
- Scarring — shave biopsy may leave a depressed, hypopigmented, hypertrophic, or keloid scar
- Undesirable cosmetic result
- Incomplete removal requiring further treatment
- Nerve injury or damage to superficial neurovascular structures
- Wound breakdown or delayed healing

---

## Aftercare

Healing times vary:
- **Shave biopsy:** Forms a scab and typically heals in 1–3 weeks
- **Punch biopsy:** Linear scar; healing usually takes 5–10 days depending on the body site

General aftercare:
- Keep the wound clean and protected with a dressing for at least 24 hours
- Follow your doctor's specific wound care instructions

---

## Follow-up

Your doctor will review the histology result (typically available within 14 days) and discuss next steps. Management depends on the result:

- **Melanoma** — always requires further wide excision; your doctor will refer to a specialist
- **Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC)** — your doctor will discuss further treatment options
- **Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)** — your doctor will discuss further treatment options
- **Benign or premalignant lesion** — management depends on the specific diagnosis
