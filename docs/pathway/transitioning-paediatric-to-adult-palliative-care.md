# Transitioning from Paediatric to Adult Palliative Care Services

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Life-prolonging therapies mean more children with life-limiting illnesses now survive into adolescence and adulthood. Transition to adult palliative care services is patient-centred and occurs across three phases, with general practice playing a key role throughout.

---

## Overview

- Transition planning begins from **age 12 years**
- Aim for full transition to adult services by **age 18 years**
- Three phases: Preparation → Step-up → Settling in
- Key documents used throughout: **summary letter**, **Parent/Caregiver Checklist**, **Young Person Checklist**, and **advance care plan**

---

## Phase 1 — Preparing for Adulthood and Planning Transition

### Paediatric palliative care team

- Identify patients for transition from age 12
- Discuss transition with the young person and their family/Whānau; provide Parent/Caregiver Checklist and Young Person Checklist
- Arrange a multidisciplinary meeting (MDM) involving: paediatric palliative care, adult palliative care, children's community nurse, adult district nurse, and general practice
- Share the summary letter, checklists, and advance care plan with adult palliative care and general practice
  - Summary letter covers: medical condition, expected prognosis, social background, current palliative team involvement, and current issues
- Refer to hospice or adult palliative care as appropriate

### Adult palliative care team

- Review summary letter, checklists, and advance care plan
- Nominate a key contact person (social care or nurse) for the transition
- Key contact attends the MDM

### General practice team

- Review summary letter, checklists, and advance care plan
- Nominate a key contact (GP or nurse) for the transition
- Key contact attends the MDM
- If concerns arise, seek children's palliative care input

---

## Phase 2 — Preparing to Step Up to Adult Services

### Paediatric palliative care team

- Arrange joint visits with the young person and family/Whānau, paediatric palliative care, adult palliative care, and general practice — can be at the practice, off-site, or via video-conference
- Establish the key family/whānau decision-maker for communication
- Continue using Parent/Caregiver Checklist and Young Person Checklist to guide transition during joint visits
- Develop or update a crisis plan and/or advance care plan with the young person and family/Whānau

### Adult palliative care team

- Attend joint visits

### General practice team

- Attend joint visits (at practice, off-site, or via video-conference)
- Gradually take over primary prescribing responsibilities, guided by the paediatric palliative care team
- If concerns arise, seek children's palliative care input

---

## Phase 3 — Settling into Adult Services

### Paediatric palliative care team

- Discharge the young person to primary care and adult palliative care

### Adult palliative care team

- Nominate a key contact for the young person and family/Whānau
- Become the primary service for specialist support and advice

### General practice team

- Nominate a key contact for the young person and family/Whānau
- Become the lead carer and primary prescriber
- If concerns arise, seek adult palliative care support and advice
