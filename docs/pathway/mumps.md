# Mumps

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of mumps — a contagious viral infection — including isolation advice, contact management, and vaccination.

---

## What Is Mumps?

Mumps is a contagious viral infection spread via saliva, respiratory droplets, or contaminated surfaces.

- **Incubation period:** 12 to 25 days
- **Infectious period:** from 2 days before to 5 days after the onset of parotid (cheek/jaw) swelling
- **At least 30% of cases are asymptomatic**

Infection typically starts with a prodrome of low-grade fever, malaise, headache, myalgia, and poor appetite, followed by unilateral or bilateral parotid gland swelling.

---

## When to Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency department or call an ambulance if you develop:

- Signs of **meningitis**: severe headache, stiff neck, light sensitivity, confusion
- Signs of **encephalitis**: altered consciousness, seizures, severe neurological symptoms
- **Orchitis** with severe pain, high fever (39–41°C), and significant testicular swelling

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

- Symptoms (parotid swelling, jaw pain, dry mouth, fever, myalgia, headache)
- Contact with confirmed or suspected mumps cases (household, school, work, tertiary institution)
- Immunisation history (number of MMR doses and ages when given)
- Travel history (some countries do not include mumps vaccine in their schedule)

Your doctor will examine for:

- Bilateral or unilateral parotid swelling (may obscure the angle of the jaw)
- Submandibular gland swelling (rare)
- Signs of complications (see below)

**Differential diagnosis** includes bacterial parotitis, EBV, other viral infections, salivary stones, Sjögren's syndrome, and (rarely) tumour.

---

## Testing

- **Mumps PCR** is the recommended test — swab taken from around the upper rear molars between the cheek and gum on the affected side
- Optimal collection is **within 5 days of symptom onset** (and up to 9 days after parotitis onset)
- A positive PCR confirms mumps
- Mumps serology is **not** routinely recommended (risk of false positives)
- If 10 or more days have passed since parotitis onset, your doctor will seek specialist advice about testing

**Mumps is a notifiable disease** — your doctor is required by law to notify the Medical Officer of Health on suspicion, before laboratory confirmation.

---

## Isolation

If mumps is suspected or confirmed:

- **Isolate at home for 5 days** after the onset of parotid swelling (the day swelling starts = day 0)
- Avoid healthcare facilities unless you need urgent attention; if you must attend, wear a mask and call ahead
- Public Health will contact you with further advice once test results are available

---

## Treatment

There is no specific antiviral treatment for mumps. Antibiotics and immunoglobulin are not indicated.

**For parotitis (cheek/jaw swelling):**
- Rest
- Drink plenty of cool water
- Avoid acidic foods
- Take analgesia (e.g. paracetamol or ibuprofen)
- Apply cool or warm compresses to swollen glands

**For orchitis (testicular swelling):**
- Physical support for the testis (e.g. supportive underwear)
- Ice packs
- Bed rest
- Analgesia

**Personal hygiene to reduce spread:**
- Wash and dry hands thoroughly
- Cover coughs and sneezes; dispose of tissues
- Avoid sharing saliva (food, drinks, kissing)
- Clean and disinfect contaminated surfaces

---

## Complications

Complications can occur **without parotitis**, which may delay diagnosis. Your doctor will monitor for:

- **Orchitis** — 15–20% of post-pubertal males; usually unilateral; sterility is rare
- **Aseptic meningitis** — 15% of cases; usually without permanent effects
- **Oophoritis** — 5% of post-pubertal females; sterility is rare
- **Encephalitis** — approximately 0.1% of cases
- Nerve deafness (unilateral)
- Pancreatitis, arthritis, thyroiditis, pericarditis

If meningitis or encephalitis is suspected, your doctor will arrange urgent hospital assessment.

---

## If You Are a Contact of a Mumps Case

If you've been in contact with someone who has mumps, your doctor will determine your immune status:

**You are presumed immune if you:**
- Were born before 1982
- Have a documented previous mumps infection
- Have had two doses of mumps-containing vaccine, given at least 4 weeks apart and after your first birthday
- Have pre-exposure blood test (IgG) evidence of immunity

**Note:** Immunity may not be lifelong, even with vaccination.

**By contact type:**

- **Immune contact:** No exclusion required, but self-monitor for symptoms from 12 to 25 days after exposure. MMR vaccination may be offered.

- **Susceptible casual contact:** No exclusion required. Self-monitor for symptoms from 12 to 25 days after exposure. MMR vaccination will be offered (not effective against incubating infection, but protects against future exposure).

- **Susceptible close contact:** Exclusion is required from 12 days after first exposure to 25 days after last exposure. Avoid healthcare settings and contact with immunocompromised people. Wear a mask outside the home during the exclusion period. MMR vaccination will be offered (will not change exclusion requirements).

Do not request post-exposure blood testing to check immunity of contacts.

---

## MMR Vaccination

MMR vaccine protects against measles, mumps, and rubella. Two doses are required for full protection.

- Two doses are needed, given at age 12 months or older, at least 4 weeks apart
- MMR is a live vaccine — **not suitable for pregnant people or those who are immunocompromised**
- Avoid pregnancy for 4 weeks after receiving MMR

**After recovery from mumps**, your doctor may recommend completing your MMR course if you have not had two documented doses.
