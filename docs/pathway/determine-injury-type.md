# Determine Injury Type

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Ageing of injuries is a difficult and potentially contentious issue. Many factors influence healing, including the site of injury, force applied, age and health of the patient, and presence of infection.

---

## Blunt Force Injuries

Caused by impact, traction, or shearing forces — such as fists, boots, hard surfaces, or batons. Injuries can range from minimal redness through to abrasions or broken bones.

Blunt force injuries include: bruise, abrasion, laceration, and transient lesions.

### Bruise

An area of bleeding beneath the skin due to leakage or rupture of small vessels. Caused by blunt trauma — either at the site of the bruise, or blood may track from elsewhere.

Bruises form and resolve at various rates. **Colour is an unreliable indicator of age** — patients who sustain major trauma at a single point in time may have bruises of various colours on different parts of the body.

**Bruise sub-types:**

- **Petechial (pinpoint)** — often caused by traction, suction, or shearing force
- **Imprint** — pattern of the implement used (e.g., buckle)
- **Tram line** — parallel linear bruising with central pallor, caused by a rod-type force
- **Finger tip** — 2 to 4 small circular or oval bruises, often in a line

### Abrasion

Disruption to the outer layers of the skin surface (commonly called a scratch or graze). Caused by a combination of force plus movement across the skin surface. May exude serum which hardens into a scab; may bleed a little.

Abrasions are moist with serous ooze during the first 24 hours, then dry out and form a scab after this interval, depending on size and site.

**Abrasion sub-types:**

- **Linear or scratch** — from pointed objects such as fingernails
- **Scrape or graze** (wider injury) — e.g., caused by gravel
- **Imprint** — pattern of the object (e.g., tyre tread)

### Laceration

An irregular tear or split of the full thickness of skin. Has irregular, often bruised and gaping edges. Caused by direct blunt force splitting tissue, or blunt force distending an opening beyond its elastic potential.

Lacerations heal with scarring over several days or weeks without medical intervention.

### Transient Lesions

Include redness, swelling, and tenderness.

- **Redness:** vasodilatation — may result from infection, inflammation, or injury (and may not indicate assault)
- **Swelling:** could be an early bruise or inflammation
- **Tenderness:** could result from pressure, blunt trauma, infection, or processes affecting underlying structures such as muscles or bones

Patterned transient lesions (e.g., hand print) should be recorded with a photograph if possible.

## Sharp Force Injuries

Caused by objects with a cutting edge, such as knives, scissors, or glass. Injuries vary depending on the force used, the sharpness of the object, and the presence of vital organ damage.

Sharp force injuries include: incision, stab, and bite mark.

### Incision

Clean division of tissue planes with minimal tissue damage; may bleed profusely. Caused by sharp objects.

**Incision sub-types:**

- **Defence injuries** — often seen on the ulnar border of the forearms, as the person tries to protect the head and neck
- **Self-inflicted** — often multiple parallel injuries in an accessible area such as the wrists or thighs

### Stab

Appearance varies depending on the implement. Caused by a sharp object inflicting an injury with greater depth than length.

**Stab sub-types:**

- **Elliptical** — double-edged knife
- **Fish-tail** — single-edged knife
- **Bruised ovoid** — blunter object such as scissors

### Bite Mark

Injuries caused by teeth can range from bruises to abrasions or even lacerations. Be aware that fragments of teeth may be left in the wound.

Teeth can be used as a weapon during fights. Bite marks can also occur as a form of abuse — biting may occur through frustration, rage, or to demonstrate power.

Provide prompt medical attention: human bites carry a higher potential for infection than animal bites. Consider the risk of body fluid exposure and disease transmission from human bites.

---

## Strangulation

Sometimes incorrectly called "choking." Strangulation is a common form of intimidation, particularly in intimate partner violence.

Police recognise the potential lethal outcome and charge offenders with more serious crimes. Supporting medical evidence — such as notes or diagrams about impaired breathing, loss of consciousness, or impaired circulation — may be important in these circumstances.

---

## Burn Injuries

Burn injuries may be thermal, electrical, or chemical.

- **Severe burns** may be among the most severe injuries encountered and require appropriate, effective care for survival. If more than 15% of body surface area is burnt, progressive fluid loss from the circulation will lead to hypovolaemic shock.
- **Burn depth** may vary from superficial through to full thickness; the true depth may not become evident for several days.
- **Electrical burns** can cause significant internal damage even when minimal damage is visible on the skin.
