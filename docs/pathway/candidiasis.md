# Candidiasis (Thrush)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of candidiasis (thrush) in adults, including vulvovaginal and penile presentations, recurrent cases, and pregnancy.

*Note: This pathway uses the terms male, female, men, and women to refer to genital anatomy. It recognises that gender identity may not match the sex assigned at birth.*

---

## About Candidiasis

Candidiasis, also known as thrush, is caused by an overgrowth of fungal organisms normally present in small amounts in the genital tract.

- *Candida albicans* causes 80 to 95% of cases.
- Less common causes include *Candida glabrata*, *Candida tropicalis*, *Candida krusei*, and *Candida parapsilosis*.
- It is **not** considered a sexually transmitted infection (STI).
- Asymptomatic people do not require treatment.
- Approximately 75% of women will experience at least one episode in their lifetime. Less than 5% will have recurrent candidiasis (defined as 4 or more episodes in 12 months).
- It can cause balanitis (inflammation of the glans penis), especially in uncircumcised individuals.

---

## What Your Doctor Will Assess

Your doctor will ask about your symptoms and any risk factors.

### Risk Factors

- Antibiotic use within the past 3 months
- Poorly controlled diabetes
- Raised oestrogen levels (pregnancy, menopausal hormone therapy, combined oral contraceptive pill)
- Skin conditions such as eczema or dermatitis affecting the genital area
- Immunosuppression (e.g., from medications or illness)

### Symptoms

- Vaginal discharge
- Vaginal odour or itch
- Genital discomfort
- Superficial pain during intercourse (dyspareunia)
- Burning with urination (external dysuria)
- Genital rash

### Examination

If you are symptomatic, your doctor will examine the genital area. They look for:

**In women/those with a vagina:**
- White, "curd-like" vaginal discharge (though discharge can also appear normal)
- Vulval or perianal fissures, swelling, redness, or scratch marks
- Vestibular tenderness and satellite lesions

**In men/those with a penis:**
- Red rash on the genitals, especially under the foreskin
- Swelling of the foreskin (if severe)
- Redness of the glans, fissures, superficial erosions, or balanitis

---

## Investigations

Your doctor may arrange swabs to confirm the diagnosis:

- **High vaginal swab (Amies culture)** — for women/those with a vagina, to check for Candida and bacterial vaginosis.
- **Swab from affected area (Amies culture)** — for men/those with a penis.
- If there is painful fissuring, a herpes simplex virus (HSV) PCR swab and syphilis serology may also be considered.
- If thrush keeps recurring, your doctor may screen for diabetes.
- Self-collected swabs can be offered if examination is declined.

> **Important:** Treatment is only indicated when you have symptoms. Positive swabs in an asymptomatic person do not require treatment.

## Treatment

### Acute Candidiasis

**Vulvovaginal candidiasis — first-line options:**
- Clotrimazole 2% vaginal cream, one applicator nightly for 3 days, **or**
- Fluconazole 150 mg orally as a single dose.

**Vulvovaginal candidiasis — second-line options:**
- Nystatin vaginal cream, one applicator 1–2 times daily for two weeks, **or**
- Miconazole 2% vaginal cream, one applicator nightly for 7 days.

If you also have inflammatory vulval symptoms, your doctor may recommend applying hydrocortisone 1% cream directly to the vulva.

**Penile candidiasis:**
- First-line: Clotrimazole 1% topical cream, twice daily for 5–7 days.
- Second-line: Miconazole 2% topical cream, twice daily for 5–7 days.

---

### Recurrent Vulvovaginal Candidiasis

Recurrent thrush means 4 or more confirmed symptomatic episodes in 12 months. Your doctor will confirm this with culture or microscopy on at least 2 occasions (ideally at least one month after antifungal treatment) before starting a suppression regimen.

**Treatment of acute flares (longer course):**
- Clotrimazole 1% vaginal cream, one applicator nightly for 10–14 days, **or**
- Nystatin vaginal cream, one applicator at bedtime until symptoms resolve, **or**
- Fluconazole 150 mg orally every 72 hours for three doses *(contraindicated in pregnancy — see below)*.

> **Fluconazole safety note:** Fluconazole can prolong the QT-interval. Your doctor will check whether you take other QT-prolonging medications (such as SSRIs) and may consider an ECG before and during treatment.

**Maintenance regimen (to prevent recurrence):**
- First-line: Fluconazole 150 mg orally once weekly for 6 months.
- Second-line: Clotrimazole 2% vaginal cream, one applicator twice weekly at night for 6 months.

If breakthrough symptoms occur during maintenance therapy, your doctor will confirm ongoing Candida with a culture swab for speciation and sensitivities before changing the regimen.

If recurrent thrush is caused by non-*albicans* Candida species or resistant *Candida albicans*, specialist sexual health advice is needed.

---

### Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Candidiasis is common during pregnancy and requires a **longer treatment course**.

- Clotrimazole 1% vaginal cream, one applicator nightly for 6 days, **or**
- Miconazole 2% vaginal cream, one applicator nightly for 7 days.

> **Fluconazole is contraindicated in pregnancy.** Do not use oral fluconazole if you are pregnant.

During breastfeeding, treatment is as for pregnancy.

## General Genital Skin Care

Your doctor will advise on the following measures to reduce irritation and recurrence:

**Things to avoid:**
- Soaps and detergents on the affected area — use plain water or soap substitutes.
- Perfumed products and any topical preparations other than those prescribed.
- Exposure to urine, faeces, and menstrual blood on the affected skin.
- Scratching or rubbing the area — even a single scratch can prolong itching for a week.
- Staying in wet swimwear or clothing.

**Helpful habits:**
- Use tampons or menstrual cups rather than scented or deodorised pads or panty liners.
- Keep cool, particularly at night.
- Wear loose-fitting, natural fibre underwear and clothing.
- Apply cold compresses to reduce the urge to scratch.

**Protective agents:**
- Emollients (e.g., sorbolene) to prevent fissuring — often more soothing when kept refrigerated.
- Barrier creams (e.g., zinc and castor oil, or Vaseline).

**Other important points:**
- Latex condoms, diaphragms, and cervical caps can be damaged by antifungal vaginal creams — use alternative contraception during treatment.
- Sexual partners only need treatment if they have symptoms.
- There is no strong evidence that specific diets or probiotics reduce candidiasis recurrence.

---

## Partner Treatment

- Male partners of women with candidiasis do **not** need treatment unless they have signs or symptoms of balanitis.
- Female partners may require treatment if symptomatic.

---

## When to Seek Further Help

Your doctor will refer you or seek specialist sexual health advice if:

- Recurrent thrush fails to respond to standard treatment.
- Recurrent vulvovaginal candidiasis is caused by non-*albicans* species or resistant *Candida albicans*.
- There is uncertainty about whether to use fluconazole for recurrent cases.
- The clinical situation is complicated and needs specialist input.

> **Always discuss your symptoms and treatment options with your doctor.** This information is a guide only and does not replace personalised medical advice.
