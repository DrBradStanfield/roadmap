# Warfarin Over-anticoagulation

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the management of warfarin over-anticoagulation — both warfarin-associated bleeding and a raised INR without bleeding.

> **Important:** Warfarin management requires careful medical supervision. All decisions about stopping warfarin, giving vitamin K, or seeking emergency assessment should be made by your doctor. This information is a guide only.

---

## When to Seek Emergency Care

Call an ambulance or go to the emergency department immediately if you have:

- Significant or life-threatening bleeding while on warfarin (e.g. coughing or vomiting blood, blood in urine or stool, severe bruising, head injury, or stroke symptoms)
- A very high INR (especially above 10) combined with high bleeding risk

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will first determine:

1. **Is there active bleeding, and how severe?**
   - **Significant bleeding** — requires urgent hospital assessment
   - **Minor bleeding** — small cuts, bruising, minor nosebleed, small haematomas

2. **If no bleeding but INR is elevated** — your doctor will assess whether you have strong bleeding risk factors and look for the cause of the elevated INR.

### Strong Bleeding Risk Factors

Your doctor will check for:
- A major bleed within the previous 4 weeks
- Surgery within the previous 2 weeks
- Platelet count less than 50 x 10⁹/L
- Known liver disease
- Active gastrointestinal disorders (e.g. ulcers)
- Concurrent antiplatelet therapy (e.g. aspirin, clopidogrel)
- Medications that increase bleeding risk (e.g. NSAIDs like ibuprofen)

### Common Causes of Elevated INR

The most common reasons warfarin levels go too high include:
- Changes in medications (new drugs added or dose changed)
- Acute illness such as infection or gastroenteritis
- Worsening underlying conditions such as heart failure
- Alcohol consumption or dietary changes (especially vitamin K-rich foods)
- Missing or taking extra warfarin doses

---

## Management by Situation

### Significant Active Bleeding (Any INR)

This requires urgent hospital assessment. Your doctor will:

- Request acute medical assessment without delay
- Stop warfarin
- Control external bleeding using compression or suture
- If the bleed is life-threatening and INR is over 1.5, give **vitamin K (phytomenadione) 10 mg orally** using the intravenous preparation

### Minor Bleeding (Any INR)

Your doctor will:

- Omit your next warfarin dose
- Recheck your INR the following day
- Adjust your warfarin dose to bring INR back into the target range
- Control external bleeding using compression or suture
- If you have high bleeding risk factors or INR is above 4.5, consider giving **vitamin K (phytomenadione) 2 mg orally**
- Consider requesting hospital assessment depending on clinical judgement

### High INR Without Bleeding

Management depends on how high the INR is:

**INR above 10:**
- Stop warfarin
- If high bleeding risk factors are present, your doctor may request urgent hospital assessment
- If lower risk, give **vitamin K 2.5–5 mg orally**
- Recheck INR in 6–12 hours, then daily until stable
- Restart warfarin at a lower dose once INR returns to the therapeutic range

**INR 4.5 to 10:**
- Stop warfarin
- Recheck INR within 24 hours — if it is falling, restart warfarin at a reduced dose once INR approaches the therapeutic range
- If high bleeding risk factors are present, or INR is not falling, give **vitamin K 2 mg orally**
- INR of 6 or above requires close monitoring — bleeding risk rises steeply in this range

**INR less than 4.5 but above your target range:**
- Lower or omit your next warfarin dose
- Resume at a lower dose when INR approaches therapeutic range
- If INR is only slightly above target (up to 10% over), dose reduction may not be necessary

---

## About Vitamin K (Phytomenadione)

When vitamin K is needed, your doctor will use the oral route (the intravenous liquid preparation can be swallowed). Key points:

- The oral form is well absorbed from the mucous membrane
- Small doses use the paediatric ampoule (2 mg/0.2 mL)
- Larger doses use the 10 mg/mL ampoule
- Effect on INR can be expected within **6 to 12 hours**

---

## Ongoing Warfarin Management

If you are frequently having elevated INRs or bleeding episodes on warfarin, your doctor will:

- Review the risks and benefits of continuing warfarin with you
- Discuss whether switching to a **direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC)** — such as rivaroxaban or dabigatran — may be more suitable
