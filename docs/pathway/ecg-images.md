# ECG Images — Arrhythmias and Conduction Abnormalities

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This page is a reference for common arrhythmias and conduction abnormalities seen on ECG, with brief clinical notes on management and urgency. It is a companion to the Palpitations pathway.

---

## 2:1 AV Block

P waves occur at twice the rate of QRS complexes — every second P wave is blocked and not conducted to the ventricles.

---

## Atrial Fibrillation (AF)

Irregularly irregular rhythm with absent P waves. See the Atrial Fibrillation pathway for full assessment and management, including anticoagulation.

---

## Atrial Flutter

Typically shows a regular "sawtooth" flutter wave pattern, often at 300 bpm atrial rate with 2:1 or variable block.

**Clinical notes:**
- Rate control is often difficult — consider cardioversion.
- Electrophysiology (EP) ablation can be curative.
- Anticoagulate as per the Atrial Fibrillation pathway.

---

## Complete Heart Block (Third Degree AV Block)

No relationship between P waves and QRS complexes — the atria and ventricles beat independently.

**Clinical note:** Request acute general medicine assessment.

---

## First Degree AV Block

Prolonged PR interval (>200 ms) with every P wave followed by a QRS complex. Usually benign in isolation.

---

## Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB)

Broad, notched QRS with characteristic M-shaped pattern in lateral leads (I, aVL, V5–V6) and W-pattern in V1.

**Clinical note:** New LBBB with chest pain should be treated as a potential ACS until proven otherwise.

---

## Mobitz Type 1 Heart Block (Wenckebach)

Progressive lengthening of the PR interval until a P wave is blocked (QRS dropped), then the cycle repeats.

**Clinical note:** Usually benign.

---

## Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB)

Broad QRS with RSR' ("bunny ears") pattern in V1–V2 and wide, slurred S wave in lateral leads.

**Clinical note:** Can be a normal variant.

---

## Right Bundle Branch Block (RBBB) with Left Axis Deviation (Bifascicular Block)

Combination of RBBB and left anterior fascicular block, producing right bundle branch pattern with a leftward axis.

**Clinical note:** The patient may be at increased risk of developing a higher degree heart block in the future. In the absence of symptoms, no immediate management is required.

---

## Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT)

Regular narrow-complex tachycardia, typically 150–250 bpm. P waves may be absent, retrograde, or buried in the QRS. See the Palpitations pathway for management.

---

## Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)

Broad-complex tachycardia (QRS >120 ms) at rate >100 bpm, originating in the ventricles.

**Clinical note:** VT is a potentially life-threatening arrhythmia. Unstable VT requires emergency assessment. Any broad-complex tachycardia should be presumed VT until proven otherwise.

---

## Pre-excitation Syndrome (Wolff-Parkinson-White, WPW)

Short PR interval, delta wave (slurred QRS upstroke), and broad QRS due to an accessory conduction pathway bypassing the AV node.

**Clinical note:** Request non-acute cardiology assessment for consideration of ablation.
