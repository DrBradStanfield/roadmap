# Contraceptive Implant Insertion (Jadelle)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the insertion of Jadelle — a two-rod subdermal contraceptive implant — including pre-procedure counselling, the insertion procedure, and aftercare.

---

## Before the Procedure

Your doctor will confirm that you are a suitable candidate for a contraceptive implant and will discuss timing of insertion.

### Side-Effects

Changes in bleeding pattern are common — a normal bleeding pattern is unlikely to continue:

- Less than one-quarter of people will have regular bleeds
- About one-third will have infrequent bleeding
- About one-fifth will have no bleeding
- About one-quarter will have prolonged or frequent bleeding

Altered bleeding patterns are likely to remain irregular. Medical management may help, but approximately 1 in 7 people have the implant removed due to bleeding problems.

Other possible side-effects include:

- Acne (may develop, improve, or worsen during use)
- Local reaction or scarring at the insertion site
- Functional ovarian cysts
- The following have been reported by some patients, though no causal link has been established:
  - Headache
  - Mood changes
  - Weight gain
  - Breast tenderness
  - Loss of libido
  - Abdominal pain

### Risks of Insertion

- Bruising and discomfort for up to 1 week
- Small scar at the insertion site
- Possible allergic reactions
- Possible infection
- Small risk of the implant migrating or being expelled

### Informed Consent

Informed consent is required before the procedure. Your doctor will document your consent in your medical records.

---

## Quick Start Method

Your doctor will determine the best timing for insertion:

- **If your last period started 1 to 5 days ago:** The implant can be inserted today and is effective immediately — no additional contraception needed.
- **If your last period started more than 5 days ago, or you have no periods:**
  - A pregnancy test will be done first. If negative, there is still a small possibility of early pregnancy if you have had unprotected sex since your last period.
  - If you have had unprotected sex in the last 5 days, your doctor may offer levonorgestrel as emergency contraception.
  - The implant can still be inserted today, but you should avoid sex or use condoms for 7 days.
  - A repeat pregnancy test is recommended 4 weeks later, even if you have had bleeding.

---

## The Insertion Procedure

Your doctor will use strict aseptic (sterile) technique throughout.

1. The inner upper aspect of your non-dominant arm is exposed, approximately 4 cm above the elbow fold.
2. The correct position is identified, staying away from the neurovascular bundle that runs behind the biceps.
3. A small area is numbed using 1% plain lidocaine (local anaesthetic) injected with a fine needle — approximately 5 mL.
4. A 2 mm incision is made in the skin, and the two rods are inserted under the skin in a V-shape pointing toward the armpit using a trocar.
5. After insertion, both rods are checked to ensure they can be felt (by both the doctor and yourself).
6. The incision is closed with Steri-Strips, covered with a waterproof dressing, and a pressure bandage is applied.

---

## After the Procedure

### Wound Care

- Keep the insertion area dry for 4 to 5 days
- The pressure bandage can be removed after 1 day
- The waterproof dressing and Steri-Strips should be removed after 4 to 5 days
- Avoid heavy lifting or bumping the insertion site
- Expect some bruising and discomfort for a few days — this will settle

### Checking the Rods

You should be able to feel both rods through the skin. If at any time you cannot feel one or both rods, or if a rod appears to have changed shape:

- See a health professional promptly
- Avoid sex or use condoms until the presence of both rods has been confirmed

### Ongoing Contraception

You may need to use additional contraception (condoms or your current method) for 7 days after insertion if any of the following apply:

- The implant was inserted after day 5 of your menstrual cycle (quick start method)
- You switched from a combined oral contraceptive (COC), progestogen-only pill (POP), or levonorgestrel intrauterine system (LNG-IUS)
- You were late for re-insertion
- After emergency contraception
- 5 days or more after an abortion, miscarriage, or uterine evacuation
- 21 days or more after giving birth

Your doctor will advise whether this applies to you.

### STI Prevention

Condoms are recommended to help prevent sexually transmitted infections (STIs) — the implant does not protect against STIs.

### Follow-Up

No routine follow-up is needed, but you can return at any time to discuss problems or to change your contraceptive method. A recall will be set for when the implant is due for removal.
