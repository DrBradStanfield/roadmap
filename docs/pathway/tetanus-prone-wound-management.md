# Tetanus-prone Wound Management

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how your doctor will assess a wound for tetanus risk and decide whether vaccination and/or immunoglobulin are needed.

---

## Background

Tetanus is caused by a toxin released by *Clostridium tetani*, a spore-forming bacterium. Tetanus spores can be introduced through injuries, even apparently trivial ones. The two key factors in management are the wound type (clean vs dirty) and the patient's tetanus vaccination history.

---

## Wound Classification

Your doctor will classify the wound as **clean** or **dirty**:

**Clean wound** — all of the following apply:
- Minor, non-penetrating injury
- Negligible tissue damage
- Less than 6 hours old

**Dirty wound** — any of the following apply:
- May be contaminated or infected (e.g. re-implantation of an avulsed tooth)
- Penetrating injury (e.g. bite wounds, compound fractures, wounds containing foreign bodies)
- More than 6 hours old
- Significant tissue damage (e.g. crush injuries, avulsions, burns)

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:

- Clean the wound thoroughly and remove any devitalised tissue
- Check your tetanus immunisation history — specifically whether you have completed a primary course (3 doses of tetanus-containing vaccine) and when your last booster was
- Note: boosters are routinely offered at ages 11, 45, and 65 years, and at the time of wound management

**Primary course status:**
- **Complete** — three doses of age-appropriate tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine received (interrupted courses can be resumed without repeating previous doses)
- **Incomplete** — no previous tetanus immunisation, incomplete primary course, or unknown immunisation history

---

## Management

Your doctor will determine the appropriate treatment based on wound type and vaccination history.

### If primary course is incomplete

Your doctor will give a tetanus vaccine. If the wound is also **dirty**, tetanus immunoglobulin (TIG) is given as well.

- **Tetanus vaccine:** An age-appropriate tetanus toxoid-containing vaccine is given, with catch-up immunisation arranged to complete the course if needed.
- **Tetanus immunoglobulin (TIG):**
  - 250 units for recent injuries
  - 500 units if more than 24 hours have passed since the injury, or if there is heavy contamination, or following burns
  - TIG and the tetanus vaccine are given at the same time but into different limbs, each by intramuscular injection
  - TIG use in pregnancy does not appear harmful and is unlikely to increase the risk of malformations

### If primary course is complete

Your doctor may consider a tetanus vaccine (Tdap, e.g. Boostrix) if:
- The wound is **clean** and more than 10 years have passed since the last dose or booster, OR
- The wound is **dirty** and more than 5 years have passed since the last dose or booster

When a tetanus vaccine is indicated for a tetanus-prone wound, your doctor will give it regardless of any recent COVID-19 vaccination.
