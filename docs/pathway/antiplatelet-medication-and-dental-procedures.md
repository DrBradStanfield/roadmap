# Antiplatelet Medication and Dental Procedures

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the management of patients taking antiplatelet medication who need dental procedures, including extractions.

---

## Key Principle

Stopping antiplatelet therapy before dental surgery increases the risk of stroke or myocardial infarction (MI). Bleeding complications from dental surgery, while inconvenient, carry significantly lower risk than thromboembolic complications such as stroke or heart attack.

In most cases, antiplatelet therapy should be **continued** through dental procedures.

---

## Background

Antiplatelet drugs decrease platelet aggregation and inhibit blood clot formation. They are most commonly used for:

- Prevention of cardiovascular disease
- After MI, TIA (mini-stroke), or stroke
- After coronary artery stenting

Common antiplatelet medications include:
- **Aspirin**
- **Clopidogrel**
- **Dipyridamole**
- **Ticagrelor**

---

## Management

### Do Not Stop Antiplatelet Therapy

Patients taking antiplatelet monotherapy (e.g. aspirin alone) or dual therapy (e.g. aspirin and dipyridamole) should **not stop** these medications before a dental procedure, including simple tooth extractions.

### Haemostatic Measures After Extractions

Your doctor or dentist may use standard local measures to control bleeding following extractions and dental procedures:

- Suturing of sockets
- Oxidised cellulose or gelatine foam placed in the socket
- Prolonged pressure on the socket
- Tranexamic acid 5% mouth soak (500 mg tranexamic acid tablet crushed and dissolved in 10 mL water), used as a mouth rinse 4 times a day for 4 days

---

## When to Discuss With Your Doctor

Talk to your doctor before any dental procedure if you are on antiplatelet medication and have complex medical problems that make tooth extraction more risky. Your doctor may consider referral to a specialist dental service in this situation.

---

*Always discuss with your doctor before making any changes to your antiplatelet medication.*
