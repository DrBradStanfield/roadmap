# Teriflunomide Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway supports primary care management of teriflunomide (Aubagio) after it has been initiated by a neurologist for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis.

---

## Background

Teriflunomide is a disease-modifying drug (DMD) used for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. It has immunomodulatory and anti-inflammatory properties. It has a long half-life of 2 to 4 weeks. If a serious side effect or unplanned pregnancy occurs, an accelerated elimination procedure can be used.

**Dose:** 14 mg tablet once daily.

---

## When to Stop the Drug and Seek Urgent Advice

Stop teriflunomide immediately and seek urgent neurology advice if:

- GGT, AST, or ALT is greater than 5 times the upper limit of normal
- Symptomatic liver injury develops

---

## Monitoring Schedule

Your doctor will monitor you regularly while you are on teriflunomide:

**Every month for the first 6 months, then every 2 months (or as clinically indicated):**

- **Liver function tests (LFTs):** If GGT, AST, or ALT is greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal, your doctor will seek neurology advice.
- **Full blood count (FBC):**
  - A low lymphocyte count is expected and is common.
  - A lymphocyte count of more than 0.2 × 10⁹/L is acceptable.
  - If lymphocyte count falls below 0.2 × 10⁹/L, your doctor will seek neurology advice.
- **Blood pressure:** Teriflunomide may raise blood pressure. Your doctor will monitor this and manage it if persistently elevated.

**Routine preventative health:**

- Dental and skin assessments
- Breast, prostate, and other cancer screenings as appropriate
- Women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 should have cervical screening every 3 years (immunosuppression increases HPV risk)

---

## Adverse Effects

The most commonly reported side effects are nausea, diarrhoea, hair thinning (alopecia), and a rise in liver enzymes. These are usually temporary.

| Area | Adverse effect | What your doctor may do |
|------|---------------|-------------------------|
| Gastrointestinal | Diarrhoea, nausea | Monitor — usually transient |
| Hair | Hair thinning (alopecia) | Monitor — generally transient |
| Blood | Decreased lymphocyte count | Monthly FBC for first 6 months, then every 2 months. If count < 0.2 × 10⁹/L, your doctor will seek neurology advice |
| Blood pressure | Raised diastolic and systolic blood pressure | Regular monitoring; your doctor will manage if persistently elevated |
| Liver | Raised ALT and other liver enzymes | Monthly LFTs for first 6 months, then every 2 months. Stop drug and seek urgent neurology advice if ≥ 5 × upper limit of normal, or if symptomatic liver injury |

---

## Drug Interactions

Tell your doctor about all medications you take, including over-the-counter products and herbal supplements. Key interactions include:

- **Rifampicin, carbamazepine, St John's Wort** — these reduce the amount of teriflunomide available in your body
- **Warfarin** — teriflunomide may reduce INR; close INR monitoring is needed
- **Pravastatin and rosuvastatin, methotrexate, penicillin G** — teriflunomide increases the availability of these medications; your doctor may consider dose reduction and closer monitoring for side effects

---

## Vaccinations

- **Avoid live vaccines** during treatment and for up to 6 months after stopping teriflunomide. Live vaccines include: MMR (measles, mumps, rubella), oral rotavirus, oral polio, oral typhoid, BCG (tuberculosis), varicella zoster (chickenpox and Zostavax), and yellow fever.
- Other vaccines may be less effective during treatment.
- Annual influenza immunisation is funded for people with multiple sclerosis.

---

## Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Teriflunomide is **contraindicated in pregnancy**.

- **Women of childbearing age:** Effective contraception is required during treatment and for a period after stopping — your neurologist will advise on the exact timeframe.
- **If planning a pregnancy:** Your doctor will refer you for pre-conception neurology advice.
- **If pregnancy is confirmed:** Seek urgent neurology advice. An accelerated elimination procedure may be needed due to the drug's slow clearance and known toxicity to the developing baby.
- **Male patients planning to father children:** Teriflunomide can be detected at low levels in semen. Your doctor will seek neurology advice.
- **Breastfeeding:** Your doctor will discuss the risks and benefits of continuing or stopping breastfeeding while on teriflunomide.

---

## Accelerated Elimination Procedure

If a serious side effect or unplanned pregnancy occurs, an accelerated elimination procedure can speed up removal of teriflunomide from the body. Your doctor will seek urgent neurology advice if this is needed.
