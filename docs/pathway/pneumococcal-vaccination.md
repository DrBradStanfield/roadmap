# Pneumococcal Vaccination

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers who should receive pneumococcal vaccination, which vaccines are used, and the groups at highest risk of invasive pneumococcal disease.

---

## Background

Pneumococcal vaccination protects against disease caused by the bacterium *Streptococcus pneumoniae*. It can prevent serious invasive disease including meningitis, pneumonia, and septicaemia, as well as non-invasive disease such as otitis media. The highest rates of invasive pneumococcal disease (IPD) are seen in infants, the elderly, and — disproportionately — Māori and Pacific peoples.

### The two vaccines

- **PCV13 (Prevenar)** — conjugate vaccine. Produces a stronger immune response and is expected to provide longer-lasting protection against the 12 serotypes it shares with 23PPV.
- **23PPV (Pneumovax)** — polysaccharide vaccine. Broadens coverage against an additional 11 pneumococcal serotypes.

Your doctor will determine which vaccine(s) you need and in what order.

---

## Who Should Be Vaccinated

### Funded for children under 5 years

PCV13 is part of the standard childhood immunisation schedule for children younger than 5 years. Your doctor or Well Child provider will manage this.

**High-risk infants under 12 months** (e.g., preterm infants, chronic pulmonary or cardiac disease) may be recommended an additional PCV13 dose at 3 months, a booster at or after 12 months, and 23PPV at or after 2 years. There must be at least 8 weeks between the last PCV13 dose and 23PPV.

### Funded for eligible patients — extended schedule

Your doctor may consider extended pneumococcal immunisation (PCV13 and/or 23PPV) if you have certain high-risk conditions. Eligibility is often identified and coordinated by your specialist. Risk conditions that may qualify include:

- HIV infection
- Cochlear implants or intracranial shunts (if younger than 18 years)
- Primary immunodeficiency
- Pre- or post-splenectomy, or functional asplenia
- On renal dialysis
- Pre- or post-solid organ transplant

Your doctor will check which schedule applies based on your age and condition.

### Recommended but not funded

Your doctor may recommend pneumococcal vaccination even where government funding is not available, particularly if you:

- Are aged 65 or older with no other risk factors (one dose PCV13 followed at least 8 weeks later by 23PPV)
- Are at higher risk of IPD due to cumulative risk factors ("risk-stacking"), including:
  - Smoking
  - Alcohol dependency
  - Chronic disease (e.g., chronic heart, renal, liver, or pulmonary disease; diabetes; nephrotic syndrome; multiple myeloma; lymphoma; Hodgkin's disease)
  - A previous episode of IPD
- Have intracranial shunts or cerebrospinal fluid leakage (aged 18 or older — PCV13 is funded but 23PPV is also recommended and not funded)

In these cases, your doctor will discuss the benefits and out-of-pocket cost with you.

---

## Special Considerations

- If there is concern about fever or febrile convulsions in infants and young children, PCV13 and influenza vaccine should be separated by at least 2 days.
- Side-effects and contraindications: discuss with your doctor or pharmacist before vaccination.
