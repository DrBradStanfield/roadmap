# Influenza Immunisation

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Annual influenza vaccination is recommended to protect against the predominant circulating strains each season. The programme runs from April through December each year. Your doctor can help determine whether you are eligible for a funded vaccine and which vaccine is most appropriate for you.

---

## Who Should Get the Flu Vaccine

The flu vaccine is recommended for everyone aged 6 months and older, especially those at higher risk of complications.

### Funded Vaccine (Free)

Your doctor may consider you eligible for the **funded influenza vaccine** (Influvac Tetra) if you are:

- **Pregnant** — the vaccine is safe at any stage of pregnancy
- **Aged 65 years or older**
- **Aged 6 months to under 65 years** with certain eligible conditions, including:
  - Serious mental health and addiction conditions
  - Heart, lung, kidney, or liver disease
  - Diabetes
  - Immunocompromising conditions
- **Aged 6 months to 4 years** with a history of hospitalisation for respiratory illness or significant respiratory illness
- **Health and disability workers** in patient- or client-facing roles

Eligibility for funded vaccines in those aged 18 and older depends on residency status. Children under 18 who meet the criteria are eligible regardless of citizenship or immigration status.

### Recommended but Not Funded

The flu vaccine is also **recommended** (though you pay for it) if you are:

- Māori or Pacific aged 55 years or older (at increased risk of complications and hospitalisation)
- A person with asthma not requiring regular preventive therapy
- A caregiver, family member, or close contact of someone at high risk of complications
- A resident or staff member of a residential care facility
- A person with hypertension or high cholesterol without end-organ disease
- A healthcare or childcare worker not covered by the funded programme

## Contraindications — When the Vaccine Should Be Delayed or Avoided

Your doctor will check for the following before vaccinating:

- **Current fever** — if you are unwell with a temperature of 38°C or higher, vaccination should be deferred until you have recovered
- **Anaphylaxis to a previous influenza vaccine** or to any ingredient in the vaccine

**Egg allergy is not a contraindication** — even anaphylaxis to eggs. The vaccine can still be given safely.

---

## Vaccine Options

### Funded Vaccine

- **Influvac Tetra** — the standard funded quadrivalent (4-strain) egg-based vaccine, available from age 6 months

### Unfunded Vaccine Options

Your doctor may discuss the following unfunded options depending on your circumstances:

- **Flucelvax** — cell-cultured vaccine (no eggs used in production), available from age 6 months. May better match circulating strains than egg-based vaccines. The only cell-based option available.
- **Fluad** — for adults aged 50 years and older. Contains an adjuvant that may improve effectiveness in those aged 65 and older compared to the standard non-adjuvanted vaccine.
- **Fluzone** — for ages 6 months and older. Manufactured without antibiotics; latex-free.

All unfunded vaccines are trivalent (cover 3 strains).

### Egg-Based vs Cell-Based

- Egg-based vaccines grow the virus in fertilised eggs; cell-based vaccines grow the virus in mammalian cells
- Cell-based vaccines may be more effective when there is a mismatch between the egg-adapted vaccine strains and the circulating strains
- Some patients prefer cell-based for ethical reasons

## Special Considerations

### Pregnancy

The flu vaccine is safe and recommended at any stage of pregnancy. Vaccination protects both mother and newborn.

### Immunocompromise

Your doctor may consider vaccinating before immunosuppressive therapy begins where possible, but vaccination can still be given during most treatments. Timing will be guided by your specialist or GP.

### Anticoagulants or Bleeding Disorders

The vaccine can be given by intramuscular injection even if you are on anticoagulants (e.g., warfarin, dabigatran), have a clotting disorder, or low platelets. Firm pressure is applied over the injection site afterwards to reduce the risk of bruising.

### Cancer Treatment (Oncology)

Timing of vaccination around chemotherapy or stem cell transplant requires individual assessment. Your doctor will consider the most appropriate timing and may liaise with your oncologist.

### International Travel

If you are travelling internationally and were not vaccinated in the preceding autumn/winter, or it has been close to 6 months since your last flu vaccine, your doctor may recommend a pre-travel vaccination — even outside the standard flu season.

### Co-Administration with Other Vaccines

The flu vaccine can be given at the same time as most other vaccines. There is a small increased risk of fever and febrile convulsions when the flu vaccine is given alongside the pneumococcal vaccine in children under 5 years — your doctor may offer to separate these by 2 days, though it is not essential.

---

## Timing

- Vaccination takes **10 to 14 days** to become effective
- Best protection occurs in the **first 3 to 4 months** after vaccination
- Vaccination can be given after recovery from any illness, including influenza itself (unvaccinated patients who have had flu are only protected against that one strain)
- Routine re-vaccination within the same season is not recommended unless you are planning international travel
