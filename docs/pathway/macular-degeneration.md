# Macular Degeneration

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of age-related macular degeneration (AMD) — the leading cause of central vision loss in adults over 55.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Assessment

See your doctor urgently if you have:

- Recent decrease in central vision
- Recent distortion of vision (e.g. straight lines appear bent, letters missing)
- Past history of vision loss from macular degeneration in the other eye

Any sudden change or decline in vision in one eye should be assessed promptly — the neovascular form of AMD is now treatable, and better outcomes are achieved with earlier treatment.

---

## Background

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is classified into three stages:

**Early AMD**
- Only small drusen (≤63µm), no pigmentary abnormalities
- Changes very gradually over time

**Intermediate AMD**
- Large drusen (>125µm) or medium drusen (>63µm) with pigmentary abnormalities
- Can cause mild distortion and gaps in vision
- Both distance and near vision are affected; peripheral vision is normal
- May progress to advanced AMD

**Advanced AMD — neovascular (wet)**
- Abnormal blood vessels grow beneath the retina and leak, causing fluid or blood to collect
- Results in distortion and loss of central vision
- Occurs in up to 25% of people with AMD
- Now a treatable condition with good visual outcomes when treated early

**Advanced AMD — geographic atrophy (dry)**
- Slow, progressive loss of central retinal cells causing a blind spot
- Currently no treatments available

**Risk factors include:** older age, smoking (3–4 times higher risk, and onset 5–10 years earlier), and family history (approximately 50% chance of developing AMD if a first-degree relative is affected).

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history including:

- Recent distortion of vision (e.g. straight lines bent, letters missing)
- Notable decrease in central vision — difficulty seeing the TV or a face
- Whether onset was sudden or over weeks to months
- Diabetes (especially if poorly controlled or known retinopathy)
- Past history of vision loss from macular degeneration in the other eye
- Family history of macular degeneration
- Smoking history

Physical examination typically includes:

- **Amsler grid test** — each eye separately. Wavy or distorted lines indicate further assessment is needed. You can also [take the test yourself](https://www.mdnz.org.nz/take-the-test).
- **Visual acuity** — tested in each eye separately, with and without glasses
- **Visual field** — should be normal in AMD
- **Pupil reflexes** — checking for a relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD)
- **Red reflex** — assessed with an ophthalmoscope
- **Fundus examination** — looking for haemorrhages or exudate in the central visual area (macula)

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following:

**Urgent ophthalmology referral (within 1 week)** for:
- Recent central vision loss or new onset distortion of vision
- Diabetes with eye concerns

If a neovascular membrane is confirmed by a specialist, treatment with an anti-VEGF (vascular endothelial growth factor) antibody injected into the eye is usually offered. This often preserves good vision when started promptly, with initial injections given monthly.

**For early or intermediate AMD, your doctor may advise:**
- Annual retinal examination with an optometrist or ophthalmologist
- Weekly self-monitoring with an [Amsler grid](https://www.mdnz.org.nz/take-the-test) — testing each eye separately. Seek advice promptly if any changes are noticed.

**Diet and supplements:**
- Including fruits, vegetables, and fish in your diet may help slow AMD progression
- For intermediate or advanced AMD, your doctor may discuss high-dose antioxidant supplements (such as Ocuvite PreserVision, based on the AREDS 2 trial) — these have evidence for reducing the risk of progression to neovascular AMD
- Clinicians VisionCare with Lutein is available over the counter as an AREDS 2-formulation option
- Supplements that do not follow the AREDS 2 formulation have not been shown to be effective

**Lifestyle:**
- Smoking cessation is recommended — smoking significantly increases risk of AMD and accelerates its progression

**Driving:**
- Your doctor will discuss whether it is safe for you to continue driving. Vision requirements for driving apply, and guidance should be followed.
