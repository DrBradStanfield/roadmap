# Videofluoroscopy and Barium Swallow

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the two main swallowing investigations — videofluoroscopy (VFSS) and barium swallow — including when each is used and what symptoms may prompt your doctor to request one.

---

## Choosing the Right Investigation

Videofluoroscopy and barium swallow are related but distinct investigations. Your healthcare provider will choose based on where the swallowing problem is thought to be:

| Videofluoroscopy (VFSS) | Barium Swallow |
| --- | --- |
| Focuses on the mouth and pharynx (throat); screens the oesophagus. | Focuses on the oesophagus (food pipe); screens the mouth and pharynx. |
| Small amounts of food and fluid coated with barium are swallowed. | Larger amounts of barium are swallowed. |
| Led by a speech language therapist (SLT) who diagnoses oropharyngeal dysphagia and directs a treatment plan. | Led and reported by a radiologist. |

**Key principle:** Oropharyngeal dysphagia (difficulty swallowing in the throat/mouth area) always has either a structural or neurological underlying cause — that cause must be identified to guide appropriate management.

---

## Videofluoroscopy (VFSS)

### When It May Be Recommended

Your healthcare provider may recommend a VFSS if there is suspected oropharyngeal dysphagia, including:

- Coughing, choking, or recurrent throat clearing when swallowing
- Sensation of food sticking in the throat
- Recurrent unexplained chest infections, especially right middle or lower lobe (aspiration) pneumonias

Most patients with these symptoms are best assessed first by a speech language therapist, who can order the fluoroscopy along with other investigations.

### When It Is Not Appropriate

VFSS is **not** the right investigation for lower oesophageal dysphagia (food sticking lower in the chest/food pipe). Discuss with your doctor — gastroscopy is generally the preferred investigation in that situation.

### What Your Doctor Will Consider

Before requesting a VFSS referral, your healthcare provider will consider:

- The clinical question for the speech language therapist
- Your relevant medical history — including any prior malignancy, radiotherapy, neurological conditions, previous gastroenterology or ear/nose/throat (ENT) procedures, and chest history including smoking and infections
- Any disability, cognitive, mobility, or infection risk factors

A speech language therapist must perform a clinical assessment before VFSS to determine whether the investigation is appropriate.

---

## Barium Swallow

### When It May Be Recommended

Your healthcare provider may discuss a barium swallow if you have dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), particularly:

- **Retrosternal dysphagia** (food sticking behind the breastbone) — endoscopy or a double contrast/multiphasic swallow are both options your doctor may consider
- **Oropharyngeal dysphagia with a neurological disorder** (e.g. following a stroke) — your doctor may recommend speech language therapy assessment first

For immunocompromised patients, your healthcare provider will seek specialist ENT/otolaryngology advice before arranging a barium swallow.

### When It Is Not Appropriate

A barium swallow is generally **not** the first-choice investigation for:

- **Suspected gastro-oesophageal pathology** — your doctor will consider the dyspepsia/GORD pathway
- **Unexplained chest pain suggestive of reflux** — gastroscopy and manometry are the preferred investigations
- **Suspected obstructing lesion, peptic ulcer disease, or red flags** (e.g. anorexia, unexplained weight loss) — gastroscopy is preferred

Barium meal studies have limited applications and are typically discussed with or recommended by a specialist before being arranged.

---

## Red Flags — Discuss Promptly With Your Doctor

Contact your healthcare provider promptly if you experience:

- Progressive difficulty swallowing solids and then liquids
- Unexplained weight loss alongside swallowing difficulty
- Coughing up blood
- Recurrent chest infections without a clear cause
- Sudden onset of swallowing difficulty

---

## Related Investigations

Your doctor may recommend alternative or additional investigations depending on the suspected cause:

- **Gastroscopy** — preferred for lower oesophageal dysphagia, suspected obstructing lesions, peptic ulcer disease, or red flag symptoms
- **Speech language therapy assessment** — often the first step for oropharyngeal dysphagia, particularly in neurological conditions
- **Gastroenterology review** — your healthcare provider may seek specialist advice before arranging swallowing investigations
- **ENT/otolaryngology review** — relevant for structural causes or in immunocompromised patients
