# Goitre in Adults

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of goitre (abnormal thyroid gland enlargement) in adults — helping determine the cause and urgency.

---

## Red Flags — See Your Doctor Urgently

Contact your doctor promptly if you have a thyroid or neck swelling and any of the following:

- Unexplained voice change or stridor (noisy breathing)
- Swollen lymph nodes in the neck (cervical lymphadenopathy)
- A painless thyroid mass that has grown rapidly over 2 to 3 months
- Family history of multiple endocrine neoplasm (MEN)

These features can indicate thyroid cancer and require urgent specialist assessment.

---

## What Is a Goitre?

Goitre refers to abnormal growth of the thyroid gland. It may be diffuse (the whole gland is enlarged) or nodular (one or more lumps within the gland).

Goitres can be associated with:

- **Iodine deficiency** — can contribute to goitre size
- **Pregnancy** — physiological thyroid enlargement is common
- **Normal thyroid function** — diffuse euthyroid goitre
- **Underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism)** — e.g., Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune destruction of the thyroid gland)
- **Overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism)** — e.g.:
  - **Graves disease:** The most common cause of hyperthyroidism (~70% of cases). An autoimmune condition, 10 times more common in women. Causes a diffuse symmetrical goitre, sometimes with a thyroid bruit. May be associated with thyroid eye disease (Graves' ophthalmopathy) in 20–40% of cases.
  - **Toxic multinodular goitre**
  - **De Quervain (subacute painful) thyroiditis:** A tender diffuse goitre, often with fever. Presumed viral cause. May cause transient hyperthyroidism followed by hypothyroidism, before thyroid function returns to normal.
  - **Post-partum thyroiditis**

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess you by:

**Taking a history, including:**
- Symptoms of an overactive thyroid: weight loss, increased bowel frequency, sweating, heat intolerance, tremor, racing pulse
- Symptoms of an underactive thyroid: weight gain, cold intolerance, cognitive slowing, constipation, fatigue
- Any sudden increase in goitre size or tenderness (may suggest bleeding into a nodule)
- Compressive symptoms (see below) or cosmetic concerns
- Personal or family history of thyroid conditions or multiple endocrine neoplasm (MEN)

**Compressive symptoms to watch for:**
- Hoarseness (may indicate nerve compression)
- Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
- Facial swelling or distended neck/chest veins (superior vena cava obstruction from a large or retrosternal goitre)
- Breathlessness, stridor, or a choking sensation (airway obstruction — stridor at rest suggests tracheal narrowing to less than 5 mm)

**Physical examination:**
- Assessment of the neck lump: size, shape, location (central vs lateral), whether it moves on swallowing, texture (smooth, lobulated), firmness, and tenderness
- Examination of the head, neck, and chest

**Investigation:**
- **TSH (thyroid-stimulating hormone) blood test** — this is the main test. Reflex testing is done if TSH is abnormal.
- **Imaging is not routinely needed** for initial assessment of goitre

## Management

Your doctor will manage goitre based on the findings:

### Emergency: Severe Compressive Symptoms

If you have severe compressive symptoms (significant hoarseness, dysphagia, stridor, facial swelling from vein obstruction, or breathing difficulty), you need urgent assessment by a specialist surgical team (ORL/head and neck surgery).

### Urgent Referral: Red Flags for Thyroid Cancer

If any red flags are present (see above), your doctor will refer you urgently to a thyroid specialist and arrange an urgent thyroid ultrasound. The referral will note high suspicion of thyroid cancer. Referral is not delayed while waiting for the ultrasound.

### Specialist Assessment: Mild to Moderate Compressive Symptoms or Cosmetic Concerns

If there are mild to moderate compressive symptoms, or significant cosmetic impact, your doctor will refer you to a thyroid specialist for non-urgent assessment. A CT scan may be arranged to assess the extent of the goitre.

### Thyroid Function Abnormality

- If TSH is abnormal, your doctor will follow the Thyroid Function Tests pathway to determine whether you have an overactive or underactive thyroid and manage accordingly.
- If pregnant with a thyroid problem, a separate Thyroid Disease in Pregnancy pathway applies.

### Euthyroid Diffuse Goitre (Normal Thyroid Function, No Red Flags)

If your TSH is normal and none of the above apply, the diagnosis is likely a euthyroid diffuse goitre. Your doctor will:

- **Check thyroid antibodies:**
  - If positive: monitor TFTs every 1–2 years (suggests autoimmune thyroid disease)
  - If negative: recheck TFTs only if symptoms of over- or underactive thyroid develop
- **Check your iodine intake:** If you use no salt or only non-iodised salt, your doctor may recommend iodised salt for cooking, or a daily multivitamin containing 100 micrograms of iodine.
- **Ultrasound is not routinely ordered** in this situation in primary care
- If there are any concerns, referral to endocrinology may be arranged
