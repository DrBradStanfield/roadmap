# Influenza

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of influenza (flu) — a common acute respiratory illness — including who is at higher risk of complications and when urgent care is needed.

---

## Background

- Influenza is an acute respiratory viral illness caused by influenza A, B, or C viruses.
- Transmission is by aerosol (coughs, sneezes), personal contact (including hands), and potentially fomites.
- Incubation period is 18 to 72 hours.
- There are 3 types of influenza scenarios:
  - **Seasonal** — every winter. Most people experience 1 to 2 weeks of unpleasant but not usually life-threatening symptoms, except in the very young, very old, pregnant women, or those with chronic illness.
  - **Epidemic** — a new highly pathogenic strain emerges, increasing morbidity and mortality in local populations.
  - **Pandemic** — a new highly pathogenic strain spreads globally, with a higher proportion of deaths in younger people.
- Many other respiratory viruses cause influenza-like illness and are also common in winter.

---

## Clinical Diagnosis

Influenza-like illness is a **clinical diagnosis** based on acute, sudden onset of respiratory illness with at least 2 of:

- Fever
- Cough
- Headache
- Muscle aches (myalgia)

Disease severity ranges from mild or afebrile to severely unwell.

Children may present with febrile illness alone or an upper respiratory illness.

Your doctor will consider alternative diagnoses — other respiratory viruses, pneumonia — and ask about recent travel (relevant to unusual or notifiable strains).

**Examination findings may include:**
- Flushed skin, high fever (38–41°C)
- Post-nasal discharge, mild cervical lymphadenopathy
- Throat usually unremarkable; chest usually unremarkable but wheeze possible

In children: may also see bronchiolitis, croup, or febrile seizures.

---

## High-Risk Groups

The following people are at higher risk of complications from influenza:

- Pregnant women
- Children aged 4 years or younger
- Adults aged over 65 years
- People with pre-existing chronic disease or respiratory conditions (e.g. asthma, COPD)
- Immunosuppressed individuals

---

## Possible Complications

**Common complications:**
- Worsening of pre-existing respiratory conditions (e.g. COPD, asthma)
- Croup, otitis media, sinusitis
- Bronchiolitis and febrile seizures (children)
- Secondary bacterial pneumonia — suspect if, after initial improvement over 2–3 days, fever recurs, sputum becomes purulent, or there are signs of lung consolidation
- Primary influenza pneumonia or mixed viral/bacterial pneumonia

**Rare complications:**
- Cardiac: myocarditis, pericarditis
- Myositis, rhabdomyolysis (typically in the legs)
- Neurological: encephalitis, Guillain-Barré syndrome

## Management

### Supportive care (mainstay of treatment)

- Rest, adequate hydration, and analgesia for fever and pain
- **Do not give aspirin to children or adolescents** with suspected or confirmed influenza — risk of Reye Syndrome. Use paracetamol instead.
- Influenza is self-limiting; most people start to feel better in 3–7 days.
- Remain infectious for 5 days after symptom onset — practise respiratory and hand hygiene.

### Antiviral medications

Antiviral treatment is **controversial** — evidence suggests only modest benefit (reducing symptoms by 12–24 hours if started within 48 hours of onset), with a risk of nausea and vomiting.

Your doctor may consider antivirals for high-risk patients (immunosuppressed, pregnant, significant respiratory or other co-morbidities). Two options are available in New Zealand:

- **Oseltamivir (Tamiflu):** Available without prescription as a Pharmacist Only Medicine; not funded on prescription; approximately $65.
- **Zanamivir (Relenza):** Prescription only; not funded; likely more than $90.

Antivirals are funded only for hospitalised patients.

### Monitoring

Your doctor may arrange follow-up monitoring for immunosuppressed or chronically ill patients to watch for complications.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Care

Seek urgent medical attention if you or your child:

- Are significantly unwell (high fever, difficulty breathing, confusion, inability to stay awake)
- Show signs of a complication (e.g. recurrent fever after initial improvement, worsening chest symptoms, signs of pneumonia)
- Are in a high-risk group and deteriorating

Your doctor will arrange acute hospital assessment if needed.

---

## Prevention

- **Annual influenza vaccination** is the most effective prevention — recommended for high-risk groups and anyone who wants to reduce their risk.
- Practise respiratory hygiene: cover coughs and sneezes, use tissues, wash hands after contact with respiratory secretions.
