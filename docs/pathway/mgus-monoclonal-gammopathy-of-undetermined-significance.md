# MGUS (Monoclonal Gammopathy of Undetermined Significance)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

MGUS is a common, asymptomatic condition where a small clone of plasma cells produces a detectable abnormal protein in the blood. It requires lifelong monitoring because it can occasionally progress to more serious conditions like multiple myeloma. Always discuss your results and monitoring plan with your doctor.

---

## What Is MGUS?

Serum protein electrophoresis (SPE) separates blood proteins into fractions. An increase in gamma globulins is called a gammopathy. These can be:

- **Polyclonal** — a broad, diffuse band; usually caused by reactive or inflammatory processes (liver disease, rheumatoid arthritis, lupus, sarcoidosis); not typically malignant.
- **Monoclonal** — a sharp, well-defined band (paraprotein); can be associated with a single clone of cells that may be malignant or pre-malignant.

**MGUS** is defined by:
- A low level of monoclonal protein (e.g., IgG < 20 g/L, or total < 30 g/L)
- Clonal plasma cells in bone marrow < 10%
- No myeloma-defining events or CRAB features (see below)
- No evidence of B-cell lymphoma or other disease producing the protein

MGUS is extremely common:
- ~1% of people over age 25
- ~3% of people over age 70
- Up to 10% of people over age 80

It is a potential precursor to multiple myeloma, Waldenström's macroglobulinaemia, or B-cell lymphoma, but most people with MGUS never progress.

---

## How MGUS Is Found

MGUS is asymptomatic — most people are identified by an incidental finding of a monoclonal protein on blood tests ordered for other reasons. Your doctor will need to do further tests to rule out more serious conditions.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history asking about:
- Bone and back pain
- Symptoms of anaemia (fatigue, breathlessness)
- Recurrent infections
- Fevers, weight loss, night sweats
- Hyperviscosity symptoms: headache, visual disturbance, increased bleeding tendency

And will examine for:
- Lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes)
- Hepatosplenomegaly (enlarged liver or spleen)
- Neurological signs in the lower limbs if there is any concern about spinal cord involvement

### Urgent Symptoms — Seek Same-Day Medical Attention

If you experience any of the following, contact your doctor urgently or go to an emergency department:
- New onset leg weakness or sensory loss
- Urinary or faecal incontinence
- Saddle anaesthesia (numbness of buttocks and inner thighs)

These may indicate cauda equina syndrome or spinal cord compression — rare but serious complications of multiple myeloma.

### Blood Tests Your Doctor Will Arrange

- Full blood count (FBC)
- Creatinine (kidney function)
- Calcium
- Albumin
- Serum protein electrophoresis (SPE) with immunoglobulin levels
- Serum-free light chains (SFLC)

---

## Conditions Your Doctor Will Consider

Your doctor will use the test results to distinguish between:

### MGUS
- Monoclonal protein < 30 g/L
- Bone marrow clonal plasma cells < 10%
- No myeloma-defining events or CRAB features

### Multiple Myeloma
More serious. Patients may present with bone pain, lytic bone lesions, impaired kidney function, high calcium, anaemia, or low blood counts. Diagnosed when one or more **myeloma-defining events** are present:
- Clonal plasma cells in bone marrow ≥ 60%
- Serum-free light-chain ratio ≥ 100
- More than 1 MRI focal lesion
- One or more **CRAB features:**
  - **C** — Calcium elevation (> 2.75 mmol/L)
  - **R** — Renal insufficiency (CrCl < 40 mL/min)
  - **A** — Anaemia (Hb < 100 g/L)
  - **B** — Bone disease (lytic lesions)

**Smouldering myeloma** is an intermediate stage if: monoclonal protein ≥ 30 g/L, bone marrow clonal cells 10–59%, but no myeloma-defining events.

*Note: Bence Jones protein testing is no longer routinely done in primary care.*

### Lymphoma
Suggested by enlarged lymph nodes and/or enlarged liver or spleen.

### B-Cell Chronic Lymphocytic Leukaemia (B-CLL)
Suggested by lymphocytosis.

### AL Amyloidosis
Light chains from some paraproteins can deposit in organs, causing damage. Clues include:
- Restrictive cardiomyopathy
- Nephrotic syndrome
- Macroglossia (enlarged tongue)
- Unexplained progressive weight loss or gastrointestinal symptoms
- Peripheral neuropathy
- Carpal tunnel syndrome

## Risk of Progression to Multiple Myeloma

Overall, about 1% of people with MGUS progress to myeloma per year. Your doctor will calculate your individual risk using three factors:

1. Paraprotein level > 15 g/L
2. Abnormal serum-free light-chain (SFLC) ratio
3. Non-IgG protein (i.e., IgA or IgM subtype)

| Number of risk factors | Risk of progression at 20 years |
|------------------------|----------------------------------|
| None                   | 5%                               |
| 1 factor               | 21%                              |
| 2 factors              | 37%                              |
| 3 factors              | 58%                              |

---

## Management

### High Risk (3 risk factors)
Your doctor will refer you to a haematologist (blood specialist) for specialist review.

### Low Risk (0–2 risk factors)
Your doctor will manage your monitoring in general practice.

**Monitoring every 6–12 months includes:**
- Repeat history — your doctor will always ask about weight loss, bone pain, and night sweats
- Examination — checking for lymph node or organ enlargement
- Blood tests: FBC, calcium, kidney function, immunoglobulin levels, SPE (and SFLC if abnormal)

**Lifelong monitoring is required.** Even if initial tests exclude myeloma, MGUS can progress at any time.

---

## When Your Doctor Will Refer to a Specialist

### Urgent (Acute) Haematology Referral
- High suspicion of multiple myeloma
- Associated high calcium or kidney failure

### Urgent (Acute) Orthopaedic Referral
- Suspected spinal cord compression or cauda equina syndrome

### Non-Urgent Haematology Referral
- MGUS confirmed with 3 risk factors (high risk)
- New lymphadenopathy
- Significant rise in monoclonal protein (> 25% rise, and absolute rise > 5 g/L, over a few months)
- Significant cytopenias (low blood counts)
- Progressive kidney impairment
- New or worsening bone pain
- Suspected progression to multiple myeloma
