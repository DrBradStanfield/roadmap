# X-ray Musculoskeletal (MSK) — Child

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway applies to children aged 14 years or younger. Before arranging an X-ray, your healthcare provider will consider the potential radiation risks for children.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Assessment Immediately

The following are critical presentations where your doctor will seek urgent specialist assessment rather than arranging community imaging:

- **Suspected septic arthritis** — urgent paediatric orthopaedic assessment is required
- **Suspected osteomyelitis** — specialist advice is required without delay

### Concerning features for septic arthritis or osteomyelitis in children

Consider these diagnoses if a child presents with any of:

- A limp
- A single painful or swollen limb
- Waking at night with pain
- Refusal to weight-bear or unable to weight-bear
- Unable to move a limb normally (in infants not yet mobile)
- Being unwell with a fever and no obvious cause
- A skin infection that is slow to resolve, especially if over a joint

## When X-ray May Be Recommended

Your healthcare provider may recommend a musculoskeletal X-ray based on the following criteria by body region.

### Spine

Accepted indications include:

- New, unexplained bony swelling or joint deformity (urgent — within 2 weeks)
- Spinal deformity (e.g. scoliosis) where a specialist has recommended a community spine X-ray before referral, particularly with any of:
  - Severe pain
  - Clinically rapidly progressing curves
  - Left thoracic curves (curve bulging to the left)
  - Significant curves or significant rib humps
- Routine spine X-ray recommended by a specialist, radiologist, or primary care radiology liaison

### Shoulder and Upper Limb (humerus, elbow, forearm, wrist, hand)

Accepted indications include:

- Persistent, unexplained bone or joint pain for more than 1 week, with pain at rest and/or waking at night with pain (urgent — within 2 weeks)
- New, unexplained bony swelling or joint deformity (urgent — within 2 weeks)
- Persistent, unexplained bone or joint pain for more than 4 weeks
- Elbow pain and joint swelling where inflammatory arthritis is suspected, and an elbow X-ray has been recommended by a specialist or radiologist
- Wrist and hand pain with joint swelling where inflammatory arthritis is suspected, and bilateral hand and wrist X-rays (with or without bilateral feet X-rays) have been recommended by a specialist or radiologist
- Concerns about the child's stature (tall or short) and/or early or delayed puberty, and bilateral hand and wrist X-rays have been recommended by a specialist to assess bone age
- Upper limb X-ray recommended by a specialist or radiologist (urgent within 2 weeks, or routine within 6 weeks)

### Lower Limb (femur, knee, tibia/fibula, ankle, foot)

**All lower limb areas** — accepted indications include:

- Persistent, unexplained bone or joint pain for more than 1 week, with pain at rest and/or waking at night with pain (urgent — within 2 weeks)
- New, unexplained bony swelling or joint deformity (urgent — within 2 weeks)
- Any lower limb X-ray recommended by a specialist or radiologist (urgent within 2 weeks, or routine within 6 weeks)

**Knee X-rays specifically** — additional accepted indications include:

- Suspected osteochondritis dissecans: joint pain typically worsened by activity, with or without locking/giving way, joint swelling, or reduced range of motion — AP, lateral, and tunnel view X-rays may be requested
- Persistent knee pain for more than 2 weeks worsened by activity, including suspected apophysitis (growth plate inflammation or stress injury)
- Metabolic bone disease affecting the knees (e.g. rickets, scurvy) suspected both clinically and by blood tests
- Unilateral bow leg in a child older than 2.5 years with concern for a pathological cause, with or without any of:
  - Pain and/or limp
  - History of infection or trauma to the affected limb
  - Severe bowing with suspected Blount's disease
  - Poor growth (under 3rd height percentile for age)

**Foot X-rays specifically** — additional accepted indications include:

- Persistent, unexplained foot pain for more than 2 weeks without concerning features for septic arthritis or osteomyelitis
- Pain and joint swelling where inflammatory arthritis is suspected, and bilateral feet X-rays (with or without bilateral hand and wrist X-rays) have been recommended by a specialist or radiologist
- Flat feet (pes planus) where the flat foot shows any of:
  - Rigidity at any age
  - Becoming persistently or progressively more painful
  - Significant bone deformity

## When X-ray Is Not Recommended

Your healthcare provider may suggest alternative management in the following situations:

- **Knee pain without knee pathology on examination** — your doctor may recommend a hip X-ray instead to exclude hip pathology
- **Knock knees (genu valgum)** — managed according to clinical assessment, not X-ray
- **Baker's cyst** — managed clinically
- **Osgood-Schlatter disease** — diagnosis is typically made by history and examination alone
- **Bursitis** — diagnosis is typically made by history and examination alone
- **Sinding-Larsen-Johansson syndrome (patellar tendinitis / jumper's knee)** — history and examination are usually sufficient
- **Patellofemoral pain syndrome (including chondromalacia patella)** — history and examination are usually sufficient

---

## What Your Doctor Will Consider

When your healthcare provider refers a child for an X-ray, they will include relevant clinical information for the radiologist, such as:

- The clinical question to be answered and a brief relevant history
- Which side (left or right) is to be examined
- Relevant examination findings and test results
- The child's weight and any relevant disability or mobility considerations
- For follow-up imaging: details of the previous imaging or specialist letter that prompted the referral
