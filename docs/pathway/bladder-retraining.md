# Bladder Retraining

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers self-management strategies for overactive bladder symptoms — going frequently and urgently to the toilet, sometimes with leakage on the way.

---

## When to See a Doctor

Discuss your symptoms with your doctor if:

- You notice blood in your urine
- You have pain when passing urine
- Symptoms are new and unexplained, or getting worse
- Self-management strategies have not helped after several weeks
- You have significant leakage affecting your daily life

Your doctor will assess whether there is an underlying cause and whether medication or specialist referral is appropriate.

---

## Background

Urgency and frequency symptoms may be caused by:

- Drinking too much caffeine (coffee, tea, cola) or drinking too quickly
- Learned responses — such as hearing running water, putting the key in the door, or going to the toilet "just in case"
- Changing position or movement triggering the urge
- Constipation

In some cases, medication that calms the bladder may be helpful. However, in many cases symptoms improve with the self-management steps below.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor may:

- Ask you to complete a **bladder diary** — recording how often you go to the toilet and how much you drink — to identify patterns
- Review your fluid intake and caffeine consumption
- Assess for underlying causes (urinary tract infection, constipation, neurological conditions, prolapse)
- Perform a pelvic examination if appropriate

A normal bladder habit is going to the toilet **6 to 9 times in a 24-hour period** when drinking adequate fluids.

---

## Management

### Fluid Management

- Drink **1.5 to 2.0 L (6 to 8 cups)** of fluid per day — including all drinks
- Aim for at least **4 glasses of water** per day
- **Limit caffeinated drinks** to 2 to 3 cups per day (coffee, tea, cola)
- **Space drinks throughout the day** rather than large volumes at once
- **Avoid drinking large amounts before bedtime**

### Urgency Suppression Techniques

When you feel a sudden strong urge to pass urine:

1. **Sit down** if possible — sitting on the edge of a seat can help
2. **Relax your shoulders** and abdomen
3. **Breathe calmly** — avoid becoming tense or panicky, as this increases the urge
4. **Wait** until the feeling of urgency decreases — this may take a few minutes
5. **Curl your toes gently** or lift your heels off the ground
6. **Gently tighten your pelvic floor muscles** while remaining relaxed and calm
7. **Use a mental distraction** — count backwards, read, or talk to someone

When the urge passes, either continue what you were doing or walk calmly to the toilet.

Practise these strategies every time a sudden urge occurs.

### Pelvic Floor Muscle Training

Strengthening the pelvic floor has been shown to help control urgency symptoms. Your doctor can provide a handout or direct you to resources on pelvic floor exercises.

### Medication

Your doctor may consider bladder-calming medication (anticholinergic or beta-3 agonist agents) if:

- Symptoms are significantly affecting quality of life
- Self-management strategies alone are insufficient after a reasonable trial
