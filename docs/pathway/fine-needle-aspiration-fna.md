# Fine Needle Aspiration (FNA)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when FNA is appropriate, who should perform it, and when a different approach is needed.

---

## Who Should Perform FNA

FNA technique is important for a reliable result. In most cases, your doctor will refer you to a pathologist or pathology registrar to perform the procedure.

General practitioners may perform FNA themselves only if they are experienced with the technique.

---

## Special Rules by Situation

### Suspected Lymphoma

If lymphoma is suspected, FNA **must** be performed by a pathologist — not in a general practice clinic.

This is because:
- FNA is the first-line diagnostic test for lymphoma
- The sample needs to be tested for specific cell markers within a short time frame

Your doctor will arrange a pathologist-performed FNA if lymphoma is a possibility.

### Suspected Sarcoma

FNA is **not** the right test if a soft tissue sarcoma is suspected. Your doctor will manage this according to the soft tissue lumps pathway instead.

### Palpable Thyroid Nodule

If the lump is in your thyroid gland, FNA should always be done under **ultrasound guidance**. Your doctor will follow the thyroid nodule pathway rather than arranging a standard FNA.

### Children Under 15

FNA in children is commonly performed under general anaesthetic. Your doctor will seek advice from the relevant paediatric specialist before arranging this.
