# National Bowel Screening Programme

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers New Zealand's free bowel cancer screening programme — who qualifies, how the test works, and what happens after a result.

---

## Who Is Eligible

Free bowel screening is offered every 2 years to people who are:

- Aged 58 to 74 years, AND
- Eligible for publicly funded health care in New Zealand.

**Note on age range:** As of March 2026, the starting age is 58 in all regions of New Zealand (previously 60 in the Central and Midland regions of the North Island).

The programme does not replace investigation of people with colorectal symptoms or targeted surveillance for those at higher risk.

If you believe you are eligible but have not received a test kit, speak to your doctor — they can request one on your behalf in certain circumstances.

---

## How the Screening Works

The National Coordination Centre (NCC) mails eligible people an information letter, followed two weeks later by a self-test kit.

### The FIT Test

The FIT (faecal immunochemical test) detects traces of human blood in a stool sample using specific antibodies. Key facts:

- No dietary or medication restrictions are needed before the test.
- The kit is designed to be self-administered at home without special training.
- A positive result does not mean you have bowel cancer — it means further investigation is recommended.
- The threshold is set to balance sensitivity and specificity using a single stool sample.

### Do Not Use the Kit If You Have Any of These (Clinical Exclusions)

- Current or previous bowel cancer diagnosis
- Colonoscopy within the last 5 years (unless you have a moderately increased familial bowel cancer risk)
- Already on a surveillance programme for bowel polyps, bowel cancer, or at-risk family history
- Currently being treated for inflammatory bowel disease
- Currently experiencing bowel symptoms — see your doctor instead

If any of these apply, do not complete the test and speak to your doctor.

---

## Why Screening Matters

- More than a third of bowel cancers found by screening are early-stage cancers.
- Colorectal cancer is 95% curable if caught early.
- Of people with a positive FIT result: approximately 5–8% have bowel cancer, and 18–26% have an advanced adenoma (pre-cancerous polyp).

---

## Negative FIT Result

You will be notified directly by the NCC by mail. Your doctor will also receive a copy of the result.

A negative result means no blood was detected above the threshold. It does not guarantee the absence of bowel cancer. You should:

- Continue to participate in bowel screening every 2 years.
- See your doctor promptly if you develop any symptoms suggestive of bowel cancer (e.g. rectal bleeding, change in bowel habit, unexplained weight loss, persistent abdominal pain).

---

## Positive FIT Result

A positive result means blood was detected above the threshold. **Most people with a positive result do not have bowel cancer.** Your doctor will contact you to discuss the result within 10 working days.

What happens:

- **7 out of 100** people with a positive FIT result have bowel cancer (most at an early, curable stage).
- **70 out of 100** have a non-cancerous bowel polyp that can be managed once identified.

### What Your Doctor Will Do

Your doctor will:

1. Discuss the result with you (in person or by phone) before any referral.
2. Reassure you that most positive results are not cancer.
3. Ask about any symptoms. If you have symptoms, they may examine you and arrange blood tests (e.g. full blood count, ferritin, kidney function).
4. Refer you to the National Bowel Screening Programme for further assessment. This referral is required even if you choose private colonoscopy or decline further investigation.

### Colonoscopy Appointment

- A colonoscopy is offered within **45 days** of the positive FIT result being notified.
- There are no delays or waiting lists for public NBSP colonoscopy — your decision about public vs private care should not be influenced by concerns about wait times.
- The colonoscopy unit will determine whether colonoscopy or CT colonography is most appropriate.
- Results are given verbally on the day of the procedure. A detailed letter with histology results and follow-up recommendations is sent within 2–3 weeks.

---

## After Colonoscopy

| Finding | What Happens |
|---------|-------------|
| Low risk adenoma | Discharged; re-register with NBSP for FIT in 5 years if still eligible |
| Polyp requiring surveillance | Regular colonoscopies arranged; no FIT kits sent during surveillance period |
| Bowel cancer | Managed by the specialist team |
| Normal colonoscopy | Return to standard 2-year NBSP screening cycle if still eligible |

---

## FIT or FOBT Testing Outside the NBSP

If you have done a FIT or FOBT test purchased privately (over the counter) and got a positive result:

- A publicly funded NBSP colonoscopy is **not available** based on an over-the-counter FIT/FOBT result alone.
- If you have symptoms, your doctor will manage these via the colorectal symptoms pathway.
- If you are asymptomatic, a private referral for colonoscopy is the preferred option.
