# Bariatric Surgery

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways. Last reviewed: February 2021.*

Bariatric surgery is weight loss surgery for people with severe obesity. This pathway follows the journey from referral to care after surgery.

> **Always discuss your symptoms and any medication changes with your doctor.** This information is a clinical reference, not a substitute for individual medical advice.

---

## Background

- Body mass index (BMI) is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared (kg/m²).
- BMI categories:
  - Less than 18.5: underweight
  - 18.5 to 24.9: healthy weight
  - 25 to 29.9: overweight
  - Greater than 30: obese (mild greater than 30, moderate 35 or more, extreme 40 or more)
- Being referred does not guarantee surgery. A specialist team decides who is suitable, and you may be declined at any step.

---

## Before Surgery

### Assessment

**Your doctor may:**
- Measure your BMI.
- Check whether you meet the current criteria for a bariatric surgery assessment.
- Look for conditions that could be reversed or improved.
- Talk through what the surgery involves and its risks, and how ready you are to follow a restricted diet and follow-up for the long term.
- Arrange tests: HbA1c, urine protein, creatinine, lipids, and liver function tests (LFTs).

Patient information: [Bariatric (weight-loss) surgery](https://www.healthinfo.org.nz/Bariatric-weight-loss-surgery.htm).

### Getting fit for surgery

- Many people need intensive management of conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure before surgery.
- Some people in good condition may still be declined because their surgical risk remains high.
- Who can be referred for publicly funded surgery is covered in the Bariatric Surgery Specialist Assessment pathway.
- If you are not offered surgery, your doctor can refer you again if your condition changes or more information becomes available. Stopping smoking, weight loss, and physical activity could help when you are reassessed.

### Preparing for surgery

If you are selected for surgery, the work-up before the operation may vary between providers. It usually includes:
- **Surgeon:** explains the procedure and checks that surgery is safe for you.
- **Dietitian:** plans a very low calorie diet (700 kcal a day) for at least 2 weeks before surgery, and starts teaching you the diet you will need after surgery.
- **Psychologist:** a full psychological assessment that checks for eating disorders and addresses any barriers to successful surgery.
- **Exercise:** an individual exercise plan to limit the loss of lean body mass after surgery.

You may also attend seminars or individual education sessions, in person or remotely.

### Pregnancy and fertility

- Avoid pregnancy for 12 to 18 months after bariatric surgery.
- Fertility may improve significantly during this time, so you will need contraception.
- If you plan a pregnancy or become pregnant, your doctor may refer you to a bariatric dietitian for diet changes and supplements.

## After Surgery

The surgical team usually provides follow-up for the first year. After that, your GP continues your follow-up.

### Blood tests

**Your doctor may arrange:**
- **HbA1c every 6 months.** Regular testing is recommended because type 2 diabetes can relapse.
- **Blood tests for everyone who has had bariatric surgery:** full blood count (FBC), electrolytes, creatinine, ferritin, vitamin B12 and folate, liver function tests, vitamin D, calcium, parathyroid hormone (PTH), zinc, copper, and lipids if you have a history of abnormal blood fats (dyslipidaemia).
- **Extra tests if your surgery has a higher risk of malabsorption:** vitamin A, vitamin E, and INR (a marker of vitamin K). These operations are Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB), one anastomosis gastric bypass (OAGB, also called the "mini bypass"), and single anastomosis duodeno-ileal bypass with sleeve gastrectomy (SADI-S). The extra tests are done:
  - every 12 months if you are nutritionally stable.
  - every 1 to 3 months as needed: if symptoms suggest a vitamin deficiency, after starting supplements, or during pregnancy.

### Nutrition and supplements

**Nutrition follow-up may cover:**
- Height, weight, BMI, and percentage of excess body weight.
- Protein supplements.
- Daily protein and fluid intake, which foods you tolerate, and keeping to the recommended food textures.
- A medicine review, because conditions such as high blood pressure and diabetes can improve quickly after surgery.
- Lifestyle and education for keeping weight off in the long term.

**Vitamins and minerals:**
- Everyone who has had bariatric surgery needs lifelong vitamin and calcium supplements.
- Extra elemental iron is also needed, particularly for women who menstruate.
- Most commercial multivitamin brands are insufficient after bariatric surgery. The right vitamin and mineral intake depends on the type of surgery you had, so ask your doctor or dietitian.
- Patient information: [Nutrition Supplements After Bariatric (Weight-loss) Surgery](https://www.healthinfo.org.nz/patientinfo/nutrition-supplements-after-bariatric-surgery.pdf).

### Bone health

- At 1 year, your doctor may arrange a bone density (DEXA) scan if you meet the criteria, then follow-up scans as the results indicate.
- After gastric banding, this scan is only needed if you have lost a significant amount of weight.
- Osteopenia or osteoporosis is managed in the usual way.

---

## When to See Your Doctor or Dietitian

Your doctor will look for signs of complications from the surgery. They may refer you to a bariatric dietitian if you have:
- Dumping syndrome, or problems with low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia).
- Malnutrition, or more weight loss than expected.

---

## Key Concepts for Patients

- Bariatric surgery needs lifelong follow-up, including regular blood tests and vitamin and mineral supplements.
- Avoid pregnancy for 12 to 18 months after surgery, and use contraception, because fertility may improve.
- Type 2 diabetes can relapse after surgery, so your HbA1c is checked regularly.
- Always discuss any new symptoms or medication questions with your doctor before making changes.
