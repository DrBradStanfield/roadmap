# Elective Procedures and Diabetes

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This guide covers how to manage your diabetes medications — including oral tablets and insulin — when you are having an elective procedure such as surgery or endoscopy. Your surgical or specialist team will give you personalised instructions; always follow those. This information reflects the general approach used by most hospital teams.

> **Important:** This is general clinical guidance. Always follow the specific instructions given by the team performing your procedure. If you are unsure, contact the hospital or your doctor before the procedure.

---

## Before the Procedure — What to Bring

When you come in for your procedure, bring:
- Your blood glucose meter
- Your insulin and injecting equipment (if you use insulin)
- Dextrose tablets or whatever you normally use to treat low blood glucose

**Do not drive to the hospital while fasting.** Arrange alternative transport.

Monitor your blood glucose more frequently than usual in the days leading up to your procedure.

---

## Oral Diabetes Medication (Tablets)

### Standard procedure (no bowel prep required)

If you are fasting overnight before the procedure:
- **Withhold your oral diabetes medication on the morning of the procedure**
- Restart it with your evening meal after the procedure

### Procedures requiring bowel preparation (e.g., colonoscopy)

- Take your normal oral diabetes medication on the morning of the day **before** the procedure
- Do not take any more oral diabetes medication until lunchtime **after** the procedure

---

## Insulin — Standard Procedures (No Bowel Prep)

### Morning list procedures

If your procedure is early and you will be ready to eat by 10:00 am:
- **Delay your usual morning insulin dose** until after the procedure
- Have a late breakfast and take your normal insulin then

If your procedure is later in the morning:
- Take a **reduced dose** of intermediate or long-acting insulin in the morning if possible
- **Insulin pump users:** Continue your basal rate but give no boluses until you are ready to eat
- Once you are well enough to eat lunch: take **half your usual rapid or short-acting insulin** dose before lunch
- Resume your usual insulin with the evening meal

### Afternoon list procedures

- Take a **reduced dose** of insulin before an **early breakfast**
- You may need an overnight stay if your blood glucose is unstable or you cannot resume eating before discharge

---

## Insulin — Procedures Requiring Bowel Preparation

During the bowel preparation phase (fluids only), your insulin requirements will be lower than usual.

**Check your blood glucose 4–6 times daily** during bowel preparation. Do **not** omit insulin completely if you have type 1 diabetes — this risks diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA).

**Blood glucose targets during fluids-only phase:**
- If glucose drops **below 5.0 mmol/L** — drink clear fluids containing glucose (e.g., fruit juice, lemonade)
- If glucose rises **above 10 mmol/L** — drink water or clear diet drinks

If you are on a large insulin dose or have unstable diabetes, your team may admit you during bowel preparation for an intravenous (IV) insulin and dextrose infusion.

---

### Rapid or Short-Acting Insulin (with bowel prep)

*Examples — Rapid-acting: NovoRapid, Humalog, Apidra. Short-acting: Actrapid, Humulin R*

**Day before the procedure:**
- Take your normal insulin dose at breakfast
- From lunchtime onwards, take a **smaller dose** (typically half the usual dose with lunch and with clear evening fluids)

**Morning of the procedure:**
- **Omit insulin**

**After the procedure:**
- If blood glucose is high: take half your normal insulin with a snack
- If blood glucose is normal: wait until lunch and take your normal dose

---

### Intermediate or Long-Acting Insulin (with bowel prep)

*Examples — Intermediate: Protaphane, Humulin NPH. Long-acting: Glargine (Lantus), Levemir (Detemir)*

- **Morning before the procedure:** Take your normal dose
- **Evening before the procedure:** Take **half** your normal dose
- **Morning of the procedure:** Take **half** your normal dose
- **Evening after the procedure:** Resume your normal dose

---

### Pre-Mixed Insulin (with bowel prep)

*Examples: Humulin 30/70, Humalog Mix 25, NovoMix 30, PenMix 30/40/50*

- **Morning before the procedure:** Take your normal dose
- **Evening before the procedure:** Take **half** your normal dose
- **Morning of the procedure:** Take **one third** of your usual dose
- **Evening after the procedure:** Resume your normal dose

---

### Insulin Pumps (with bowel prep)

Continue your pump at the **basal infusion rate**. Do not give bolus doses while fasting.

---

## When to Contact Your Doctor or Specialist

Contact the team performing your procedure or your doctor if:
- You are unsure how to adjust your medications
- Your blood glucose is very high or very low in the lead-up to the procedure
- You have unstable diabetes or recently changed your insulin regimen

*Your specialist team will give you personalised instructions — these take priority over general guidance.*
