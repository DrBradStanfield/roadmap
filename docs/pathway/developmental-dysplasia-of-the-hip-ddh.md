# Developmental Dysplasia of the Hip (DDH)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the identification and initial management of developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) in newborns, infants, and young children.

---

## Background

DDH is a condition where the hip joint does not develop normally, with the ball of the femur sitting incorrectly in the hip socket.

- Affects about 1 to 2 infants in every 1,000
- Cannot always be detected at birth — it may progress over the first few months of life
- More common in girls (female to male ratio of approximately 5:1)
- Clinical features depend on the child's age and the severity of the abnormality
- The earlier DDH is detected, the simpler and more effective the treatment, and the better the long-term outcome

---

## Assessment

### Risk Factors

Your doctor will check for risk factors for DDH. These include:

- Breech presentation at any time in the third trimester of pregnancy
- Strong family history of DDH, especially in a parent or sibling
- Intrauterine "packaging" problems:
  - Oligohydramnios (low amniotic fluid)
  - Bicornuate uterus
  - Birth weight of 4,000 grams or more
  - Club foot
  - Metatarsus adductus (inward-turning foot)
  - Torticollis (twisted neck)
  - Dislocated knee

### Clinical Examination

Your doctor will examine the hips carefully. Examination timing follows these guidelines:

- Lead maternity carers (LMCs) examine hips at less than 24 hours old, at 7 days old, and before discharge from LMC care
- General practitioners examine hips at the 6-week check, or as indicated by history and clinical presentation
- The examination is best done while the baby is calm and relaxed — if the baby is crying or tense, this can give a false result

**For infants under 6 months:**

Your doctor will perform Barlow and Ortolani tests:

- *Barlow test:* Detects a hip that can be dislocated out of the socket with gentle pressure. A palpable clunk or sensation of movement indicates a positive test.
- *Ortolani test:* Detects a hip that is already out of joint and can be reduced (put back in) with gentle manoeuvre. A clunk felt as the hip relocates indicates a positive test.

A positive Barlow or Ortolani test at this age is strongly predictive of DDH.

Note: Findings of limited hip abduction or limb length discrepancy are less reliable in infants under 6 months.

**For infants 6 months and older:**

- Your doctor will test for reduced hip abduction. Limited abduction (less than 80°, especially if asymmetrical) is the most sensitive sign of DDH in older infants.
- Leg length discrepancy is assessed with the infant lying face down, hips and knees extended.
- Barlow and Ortolani tests become increasingly difficult to perform reliably from 3 to 6 months onward.

**For children who are walking:**

- Limping or toe-walking on one side can indicate DDH in one hip
- A waddling gait, increased lumbar lordosis, or prominent buttocks can indicate DDH in both hips

### Soft Signs

Your doctor may also note soft signs, which were previously thought to indicate DDH but are now known to be less specific:

- Asymmetric thigh or buttock creases
- Clicky hips
- Slight differences in limb lengths that may be inconsistent on repeat examination

More than 95% of infants with these soft signs have normal hips.

### Imaging

Your doctor will arrange imaging based on examination findings and age:

- **X-ray (AP pelvis)** is the preferred investigation for children aged 4 months or older with an abnormal exam, or for children aged 4 to 6 months with risk factors or soft signs.
- **Ultrasound** is no longer routinely recommended and should only be arranged if specifically advised by a paediatric orthopaedic surgeon.
- For children under 4 months with strongly positive examination findings, your doctor will refer directly for paediatric orthopaedic assessment rather than arranging imaging first.

---

## Management

Management depends on the child's age and examination and imaging findings. Your doctor will guide all decisions.

**If there is a dislocated hip on examination (either reducible or irreducible):**
Your doctor will seek urgent paediatric orthopaedic advice. The orthopaedic team will advise whether the child needs to be admitted acutely or seen in an outpatient clinic.

**If examination findings are abnormal:**

- *Under 4 months (positive Barlow or Ortolani test):* Your doctor will refer for non-acute paediatric orthopaedic assessment.
- *4 months or older with an abnormal X-ray:* Your doctor will refer for non-acute paediatric orthopaedic assessment.
- *4 months or older with a normal X-ray but persistent abnormal findings (limited hip abduction, short leg, or limp):* Your doctor will re-examine and, if still abnormal, refer for non-acute paediatric orthopaedic assessment.

**If risk factors or soft signs are present but examination is not strongly predictive of DDH:**
Your doctor will arrange an X-ray at 4 to 6 months of age:

- If the X-ray is abnormal, your doctor will refer for paediatric orthopaedic assessment.
- If the X-ray is normal, DDH is effectively ruled out. If examination findings remain abnormal or other concerns persist, your doctor may still refer for orthopaedic review.
