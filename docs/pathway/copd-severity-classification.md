# COPD Severity Classification

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This page explains how doctors classify the severity of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), using breathlessness scores and lung function tests.

---

## How Severity is Assessed

Your doctor uses two main tools to assess how much COPD is affecting you:

### 1. Modified Medical Research Council (MMRC) Dyspnoea Scale

This scale rates breathlessness from 0 to 4 based on what activities trigger it:

| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0 | Breathless only with strenuous exercise |
| 1 | Short of breath when hurrying on level ground or walking up a slight hill |
| 2 | Walk slower than people of the same age on level ground due to breathlessness, or must stop for breath when walking at own pace |
| 3 | Stop for breath after walking about 100 metres or after a few minutes on level ground |
| 4 | Too breathless to leave the house, or breathless when dressing |

### 2. COPD Assessment Test (CAT)

The CAT is a questionnaire your doctor may use to assess how COPD affects your daily life and symptoms. It helps track changes over time.

### 3. Spirometry (Lung Function Test)

Spirometry measures how much air you can breathe out in one second (FEV1) compared to what is expected for someone your age, sex, and height. Your doctor will use the result after a bronchodilator (reliever) inhaler.

---

## Severity Classification

Your doctor classifies COPD into three categories based on your symptoms and FEV1:

| | Mild | Moderate | Severe |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical Symptoms** | Few symptoms. Breathlessness on moderate exertion only. Little or no effect on daily activities. Cough and sputum production. | Breathlessness walking on level ground. Recurrent chest infections. Exacerbations needing oral steroids and/or antibiotics. | Breathless on minimal exertion. Daily activities severely limited. Frequent and increasingly severe exacerbations. |
| **Lung Function (FEV1)** | 60–80% of predicted | 40–59% of predicted | Less than 40% of predicted |

Note: Exacerbations (flare-ups) can occur at any stage of COPD severity, including mild.

---

## What This Means for Management

Your doctor uses your severity classification to guide treatment decisions — including which inhalers to prescribe, whether pulmonary rehabilitation is appropriate, and how closely you should be monitored. Your doctor is best placed to interpret these results in the context of your full history and circumstances.

Always discuss your COPD classification and treatment plan with your doctor.
