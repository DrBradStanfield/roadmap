# Jaundice in Babies

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of jaundice in newborns and young infants.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Medical Assessment

Take your baby to be seen urgently if any of these apply:

- Baby is aged **less than 24 hours old**
- Baby is aged **more than 2 weeks old** and still jaundiced
- Jaundice with **pale (lemon, beige, or white) stools**
- Signs of **sepsis or systemic illness** (see unwell baby signs below)

---

## Background

Many babies will develop jaundice. The significance depends on the age of the infant, their clinical status, whether they are breast or bottle fed, and the type of jaundice. In most cases, jaundice is benign and physiological, peaking at 5 to 7 days and resolving by day 14.

**Prolonged jaundice** is defined as jaundice persisting:
- beyond 14 days in full-term babies (born at or after 37 weeks gestation), or
- beyond 21 days in preterm babies (born before 37 weeks).

### Types of jaundice

**Unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia:**
- Can be an exaggerated physiological response.
- Commonly caused by breast-milk jaundice — this is a diagnosis of exclusion. Stopping breastfeeding is not indicated.
- Less commonly caused by UTI, sepsis, hypothyroidism, pyloric stenosis, dehydration, and rare enzyme defects.
- Unconjugated bilirubin can cross the blood-brain barrier, where it is neurotoxic and can cause brain injury (kernicterus) and/or hearing impairment.

**Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia:**
- Occurs when the conjugated fraction is greater than 20 micromole/L or more than 20% of the total serum bilirubin (SBR).
- Always requires investigation and prompt specialist assessment, even if the baby appears well.
- Most seriously caused by biliary atresia — important to diagnose early, ideally at 2 to 3 weeks of age.

**Mixed raised conjugated and unconjugated bilirubin:**
- Can be caused by bacterial sepsis, galactosaemia, amino-acidaemias, and congenital hypopituitarism.
- Requires acute paediatric assessment.

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess the following:

### 1. Onset and duration
- Jaundice within 24 hours of birth needs specialist assessment.
- Jaundice appearing after 3 days is more likely to be physiological.
- Prolonged jaundice always needs investigation.

### 2. History
- Perinatal history, birth trauma, and antenatal complications.
- Family history of haemolytic disease, G6PD deficiency, or spherocytosis.
- Maternal and baby blood groups, maternal viral serology.
- Feeding history: breast or formula, frequency, volumes, and weight gain.

### 3. Physical examination
Your doctor will:
- Assess whether the baby appears unwell or has signs of sepsis (see below).
- Assess the clinical extent of jaundice. In good daylight, blanching the skin shows how far down the body jaundice has spread — jaundice extending to the legs is clinically significant. Note: clinical assessment is more challenging in babies with darker skin.
- Weigh the baby.
- Check stool and urine colour — pale stools and/or dark urine are signs of liver disease.

**Signs of an unwell baby:**
- Lethargy
- Poor feeding
- Vomiting
- High-pitched cry
- Hypotonia or hypertonia (floppy or stiff)
- Seizure
- Opisthotonos (back arching)
- Apnoea or fast breathing (tachypnoea)

### 4. Investigations
Your doctor may consider:

**If aged 24 hours to 14 days:**
- Split bilirubin (total serum bilirubin and conjugated fraction).
- Liver function tests if pale stools or dark urine are present.
- Results are compared against phototherapy treatment threshold graphs to decide if acute paediatric assessment is needed.

**If aged 14 days or more (prolonged jaundice):**
- Check the newborn screening test result.
- Arrange split bilirubin to identify the type of hyperbilirubinaemia.

---

## Management

Your doctor will decide on management based on age, bilirubin results, and clinical status.

**Your doctor will arrange urgent paediatric assessment if:**
- Baby is aged less than 24 hours.
- Baby is aged 14 days or more with hyperbilirubinaemia.
- Baby is unwell or has signs of sepsis at any age.
- Unconjugated bilirubin is at or above the phototherapy treatment threshold.
- Mixed raised conjugated and unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia is present.
- Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia, pale stools, and/or dark urine are present at any age.

**If aged less than 14 days and bilirubin is below the phototherapy threshold:**
- Your doctor will ensure adequate feeding and arrange review within 48 hours.
- If jaundice worsens, your doctor may repeat the split bilirubin, check blood groups, and perform a Coombs test.

**If breastfed, aged 14 days or more, appearing well, with unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia:**
- Your doctor will ensure adequate feeding and weight gain and review within 48 hours.
- If jaundice is resolving, weekly review may be arranged.
- If unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia remains unchanged or is increasing, your doctor will seek paediatric medical advice.
