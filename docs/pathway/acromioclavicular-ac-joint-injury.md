# Acromioclavicular (AC) Joint Injury

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of AC joint injuries — typically caused by a fall on the point of the shoulder.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history and examine you. AC joint injury is most common in males and typically follows a fall on the point of the shoulder.

Physical examination looks for:

- Localised pain and tenderness over the AC joint
- A visible prominence or palpable step over the AC joint
- Pain on the **cross chest test** — the arm is held at the elbow and passively moved across the chest; pain suggests AC joint damage (note: this test is not specific and may be positive in other shoulder conditions including supraspinatus injury)
- **Muscle weakness** — your doctor will assess strength using the MRC muscle power scale, as weakness of elevation is the most important factor in deciding treatment

**MRC Muscle Power Scale:**

| Score | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| 0 | No contraction |
| 1 | Flicker or trace of contraction |
| 2 | Active movement with gravity eliminated |
| 3 | Active movement against gravity |
| 4 | Active movement against gravity and resistance |
| 5 | Normal power |

If there is visible joint displacement or significant pain, your doctor will arrange an **X-ray** (with AC joint views) to determine the grade of injury.

---

## Grades of Injury

| Grade | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| Grade 1 | Intact joint with minor tears of the AC ligaments |
| Grade 2 | Up to 50% vertical displacement of the joint |
| Grade 3 | 50–100% displacement with complete rupture of all ligaments |
| Grade 4 | Posterior displacement of the joint |
| Grade 5 | Over 100% displacement; rupture of delto-trapezial fascia; very prominent lateral clavicle; shoulder collapsed inferiorly and medialised |
| Grade 6 | Inferior displacement of clavicle under the coracoid (rare) |

Grades 1 to 3 are the most common.

---

## Management

Management depends on the grade of injury. Your doctor will assess which approach is appropriate for you.

### Grade 1 or 2 — Conservative Management

Grade 1 and 2 injuries are expected to recover full painless function with conservative care:

- Ice and pain relief (analgesia)
- A sling if needed, for 2 to 3 days
- Begin gentle movements after 24 to 48 hours
- Return to normal activities in approximately 2 to 3 weeks
- Avoid heavy lifting and contact sport for 8 to 12 weeks

Your doctor may refer you to **physiotherapy** if:
- Significant movement has not improved after 1 week
- Supervision of return to sport or physical activity is needed

### Grade 3 — Case-by-Case Decision

Grade 3 injuries are managed conservatively or surgically depending on individual factors. Your doctor may consider **urgent orthopaedic referral for surgical repair** (ideally within 1 month) if:

- Muscle weakness at MRC grade 4 or below
- The injury is on your dominant side
- You do overhead work or heavy manual labour
- You are an overhead athlete

If none of these apply, conservative management is used (same approach as Grade 1 and 2). Your doctor will also refer you to **physiotherapy** to strengthen the scapula stabilisers and optimise function, particularly in overhead positions.

If your doctor is unsure about the best approach, they will seek orthopaedic advice.

### Grade 4 to 6 — Urgent Orthopaedic Referral

Grade 4 to 6 injuries require **urgent orthopaedic assessment** for consideration of acute surgical repair within 1 month.

---

## Note on Grade 3 Injuries

There is ongoing controversy about the best management of Grade 3 AC joint injuries, and grading can be inconsistent. Your doctor may refer you to orthopaedics even if the injury appears to be Grade 3 — ideally, if surgery is indicated, it should happen within a month of the injury.

---

## Ongoing Support

If you are experiencing difficulty with everyday activities (self-care, household tasks, driving, work, or leisure), your doctor may consider referral to **occupational therapy**.

If the injury is affecting your ability to return to or sustain work, a **work assessment and rehabilitation** referral may also be considered.
