# Metatarsus Adductus

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers metatarsus adductus — a common congenital foot deformity causing intoeing in infants. Almost all cases resolve without intervention as the child grows.

---

## Background

Metatarsus adductus is the most common congenital foot deformity. The front part of the foot curves inward (medial deviation of the forefoot) while the heel remains in a normal position, creating a C-shape. It is:

- Often present in both feet (bilateral)
- Thought to be caused by the position of the feet in the uterus

The vast majority of cases resolve on their own without treatment as the baby grows.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will examine your child's foot, looking for:

- Incurving of the front of the foot forming a C-shape
- A deep crease on the inner (medial) side of the foot
- Whether the deformity is flexible or rigid — by gently applying outward pressure on the head of the first metatarsal (the base of the big toe) to see how much correction is possible
- Normal ankle and subtalar joint movement
- Other "packaging disorders" such as developmental dysplasia of the hip (DDH) or torticollis, which can occur alongside metatarsus adductus

Your doctor will also assess severity using **Bleck's heel line** — an imaginary line down the middle of the heel that normally passes through the second toe. In metatarsus adductus this line is shifted outward:

- **Mild** — the forefoot can be passively overcorrected into outward position with little effort
- **Moderate** — the forefoot can only be corrected to the neutral (straight) position
- **Severe or rigid** — the forefoot cannot be corrected to the midline

If there is concern about additional foot deformities, your doctor will consider congenital talipes equinovarus (club-foot) as a possible alternative diagnosis.

Your doctor may also arrange a **hip X-ray at 5 to 6 months of age**, as metatarsus adductus is associated with hip dysplasia.

---

## Management

Surgery is rarely recommended. Management depends on severity.

**Mild cases:** No specific treatment is needed. Your doctor will provide reassurance and monitor progress.

**Moderate cases:** Your doctor may consider stretching exercises — demonstrated to you by a clinician:

- Apply gentle outward pressure on the head of the first metatarsal to correct the alignment, hold for 10 seconds
- Perform this 5 times per foot at each nappy change
- Your doctor may consider a private physiotherapy referral if you need support with the exercises
- If there is no improvement after 6 months of stretching, your doctor may consider referral to a paediatric orthopaedic specialist

**Severe or rigid cases:** Your doctor may consider early referral to a paediatric orthopaedic specialist. A short course of casting may be recommended — results are best with early treatment.

Your doctor will also advise that in rare cases, as metatarsus adductus resolves, it may initially cause intoeing when the child first starts walking. This should improve with age.
