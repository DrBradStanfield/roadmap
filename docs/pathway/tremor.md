# Tremor

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of tremor in adults — helping to identify the type of tremor and guide treatment.

---

## What Is Tremor?

A tremor is an involuntary repetitive movement.

The most common types are:
- **Essential tremor** — the most common; runs in families
- **Parkinson disease tremor** — a resting tremor relieved by movement
- **Enhanced physiological tremor** — caused by medications, stimulants, or underlying conditions
- **Cerebellar tremor** — an action tremor associated with other cerebellar signs

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess your tremor by considering:

- **Pattern**: e.g. does it occur at rest (suggesting Parkinson disease) or with movement/posture (suggesting essential tremor)?
- **Relieving factors**: e.g. does movement of the limb reduce the tremor (suggesting Parkinson disease)?
- **Medications and alcohol use**: many medications and alcohol withdrawal can cause or worsen tremor.
- **Family history**: essential tremor has a family history in around 50% of cases.

### Types of Tremor at a Glance

| Type | Character |
|------|-----------|
| Parkinson | Resting tremor; initially one-sided |
| Cerebellar | Action tremor; past-pointing; can be one or both sides |
| Essential | Postural/action (arms outstretched); both sides |
| Physiological | Postural; faster than essential tremor |

### Physical Examination

Your doctor will examine:
- Tremor characteristics (at rest vs. with movement vs. with posture)
- Facial appearance — e.g. "mask-like" face of Parkinson disease
- Speech — slower and quieter with Parkinson disease
- Neurological examination: muscle tone, reflexes, power, coordination, cerebellar signs (nystagmus, dysarthria, ataxia), cognitive function
- Gait and posture
- Lying and standing blood pressure

### Investigations

Your doctor will arrange:
- **Full blood count (FBC)**
- **Thyroid function tests** (thyroid disease can cause tremor)

---

## Management

Treatment depends on the type of tremor.

### Essential Tremor

Essential tremor is managed in primary care. Typical features:
- Affects hands symmetrically; may include head or voice tremor
- Worsens with activity and sustained posture (e.g. arms outstretched)
- Absent at rest
- Can be temporarily suppressed by alcohol
- Normal neurological examination otherwise
- Often a family history

**Self-management advice:**
- Avoid aggravating factors: caffeine, stress, tiredness
- Use heavier cups and utensils, or a straw for drinking
- Use a computer instead of handwriting
- Use wrist weights for tasks like eating or writing (available from most fitness shops)
- Yoga or relaxation exercises

Your doctor will explain the tremor and provide reassurance. They will also monitor for anxiety and depression, as tremor can be embarrassing and disabling.

**Medications your doctor may consider:**
- **Propranolol** — typically started at a low dose (10–20 mg once or twice daily), adjusted up to 240 mg daily if tolerated and no contraindication.
- **Primidone** — if propranolol is not effective. Started at a very low dose (62.5 mg daily); side-effects often limit its usefulness.

### Enhanced Physiological Tremor

Your doctor will look for and treat underlying causes:
- Anxiety or fatigue
- Endocrine conditions: overactive thyroid (thyrotoxicosis), Cushing's disease, phaeochromocytoma, low blood sugar (hypoglycaemia)
- Medications: salbutamol, caffeine, lithium, dopamine agonists, sodium valproate, tricyclic antidepressants, phenothiazines, amphetamines
- Alcohol withdrawal

### Parkinson Disease

If Parkinson disease is suspected, your doctor will follow the Parkinson Disease pathway, which involves specialist input.

### Cerebellar Tremor

If you have a cerebellar tremor with other features of cerebellar disease, your doctor will refer to a neurologist for further assessment.

---

## When Specialist Referral Is Needed

- **Essential and physiological tremor** are managed in primary care — no referral needed.
- **Cerebellar tremor** with other cerebellar features → referral to neurology.
- **Parkinson disease** → managed via the Parkinson Disease pathway, typically involving a neurologist.

---

## Driving

If tremor may affect your ability to drive safely, your doctor will provide guidance about fitness to drive.
