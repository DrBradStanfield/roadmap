# Intrauterine Device (IUD) Removal

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the removal of intrauterine devices — including the copper IUD (Cu-IUD) and levonorgestrel intrauterine systems (LNG-IUS): Mirena (52 mg) and Jaydess (13.5 mg).

---

## When Is Removal Recommended?

Your doctor may consider removing an IUD in the following situations:

- You wish to become pregnant
- The current IUD has expired
- The IUD needs to be replaced with a new one
- You wish to use a different form of contraception (due to side-effects, or a change in fertility status or lifestyle)
- You have severe pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) that is not responding to treatment at around 72 hours
- You are menopausal and the device is no longer needed
- You have *Actinomyces*-like organisms (ALOs) with significant pelvic symptoms and other causes of pelvic pain have been excluded

Note: IUD removal is **not** routinely needed if you have ALOs but no symptoms.

---

## Timing of Removal

Return of fertility is expected to be immediate after IUD removal. Your doctor will discuss ongoing contraception with you if pregnancy is not desired.

### If pregnancy is desired
- The IUD can be removed at any time in the menstrual cycle
- Your doctor will discuss pre-conception advice and folic acid supplementation

### If switching to another contraceptive method (IUD still within licensed duration)

**If having a new IUD inserted:**
- Your doctor will advise avoiding intercourse or using condoms for at least 7 days before the procedure, in case inserting the new device is not possible
- If this is not done and the new device cannot be inserted, emergency contraception may need to be considered

**If switching to a non-IUD method:**
- A copper IUD (Cu-IUD) can be removed up to 4 days after the start of menstruation without needing extra precautions
- For Cu-IUD removal more than 4 days after the start of menstruation, or for LNG-IUS removal at any time, your doctor will advise avoiding intercourse or using an alternative method for at least 7 days beforehand
- Start the alternative contraceptive method as soon as the IUD is removed (if not already started)

### If the IUD is outside its licensed duration of use

**Copper IUD (Cu-IUD):**
- If inserted after age 40 and contains 300 mm² or more of copper, it may be retained until:
  - 1 year after the last menstrual period (LMP), if this occurs when you are aged 50 or older
  - 2 years after the LMP, if this occurs when you are younger than 50

**52 mg LNG-IUS (Mirena):**
- If fitted at age 45 or older, it can be used for up to 8 years (or until menopause if you are no longer having periods), and removed at age 55
- If you were younger than 45 when it was inserted and it is now 8 or more years old, your doctor will advise you to avoid intercourse or use condoms and take a pregnancy test at least 3 weeks after your last unprotected sex before making arrangements for removal or replacement

**13.5 mg LNG-IUS (Jaydess):**
- Your doctor will advise taking a pregnancy test at least 3 weeks after last unprotected sex before arranging removal or replacement

## If Threads Are Not Visible

If the IUD threads cannot be seen at the time of planned removal, your doctor will:

1. Take a menstrual and sexual history
2. Attempt to locate the threads using a thread retriever or narrow forceps in the cervical canal
3. If threads cannot be found, arrange an ultrasound of the pelvis:
   - **IUD correctly placed on ultrasound:** May leave in place until due for removal, with another retrieval attempt. If removal is still wanted, specialist gynaecology assessment may be arranged
   - **IUD not found in the uterus:** An abdominal X-ray will be arranged to check whether the IUD has moved into the abdominal cavity
   - **IUD found in the abdominal cavity:** Specialist assessment for laparoscopic removal will be arranged; your doctor will advise alternative contraception in the meantime
   - **IUD not found on ultrasound or X-ray:** This likely means the IUD was expelled without you realising. Alternative contraception will be discussed

---

## If Part of the IUD Is Missing After Removal

If the device does not appear intact after removal, your doctor will:

- Offer alternative contraception
- Arrange an urgent pelvic ultrasound (within 24 hours if there are symptoms such as cramping pain or unexpected bleeding)
- If the missing piece is not seen on ultrasound, arrange an abdominal X-ray
- If located on imaging, arrange specialist gynaecology assessment
- If not located on any imaging, seek specialist gynaecology advice

## If You Are Pregnant With an IUD in Place

Pregnancy with an IUD in situ carries increased risks, including:

- Infection (e.g., chorioamnionitis)
- Miscarriage — risk approximately 50%
- Antepartum haemorrhage
- Preterm birth
- Placental abruption

If pregnancy occurs with an LNG-IUS (Mirena/Jaydess) in place, the risk of ectopic pregnancy is approximately 50%. With a Cu-IUD, the risk is approximately 6%.

### If you feel unwell or collapse — this is a medical emergency

Call 111 immediately. Signs of a serious problem include:

- Sudden severe abdominal or pelvic pain
- Vaginal bleeding with or without clots
- Dizziness, fainting, or collapse
- Shoulder tip pain
- Tachycardia (heart rate > 100 bpm) or low blood pressure (< 100/60 mmHg)
- Shock

### If clinically stable with possible ectopic pregnancy symptoms

Your doctor will arrange an urgent gynaecology assessment and same or next day ultrasound to exclude ectopic pregnancy.

### If ultrasound confirms intrauterine pregnancy (less than 12 weeks, threads visible)

- Your doctor will explain that IUD removal may improve pregnancy outcomes (risk of miscarriage may reduce from ~50% to ~20%)
- Removal is advised, but the decision is shared with you
- Your doctor may seek specialist advice before proceeding

### If any of the following apply, your doctor will seek specialist advice and not attempt removal:

- Ultrasound cannot be obtained promptly
- Pregnancy is 12 weeks or more
- IUD threads are not visible

## Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID) and IUD

IUD removal is **not** routinely required if you have PID. Your doctor will:

- Treat the PID with antibiotics
- Consider removal only if there is no improvement after approximately 72 hours
- Discuss emergency contraception if intercourse has occurred in the previous 5 days and removal takes place
- Discuss alternative contraception options

---

## Menopause and IUD Removal

### 52 mg LNG-IUS (Mirena)
- If you are aged 50 or older and have had no periods for at least 12 months, your doctor may check a follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) blood test
  - FSH 30 units/L or less: continue contraception; repeat FSH in 12 months
  - FSH more than 30 units/L: contraception can be stopped after a further 12 months with no menstruation
- If fitted at age 45 or older, can be retained for up to 8 years or until menopause, and removed at age 55
- If used as the progestogen component of menopause hormone therapy (MHT), it should be replaced after 5 years
- Any new or changed bleeding pattern should be investigated promptly

### 13.5 mg LNG-IUS (Jaydess)
- Same FSH testing approach as above if aged 50 or older with 12 months of no periods
- Must be replaced after 3 years for continued contraceptive efficacy, regardless of age at insertion
- Any new or changed bleeding pattern should be investigated promptly

### Copper IUD (Cu-IUD)
- If fitted at age 40 or older and contains 300 mm² or more of copper:
  - Can be retained until 1 year after the LMP (if aged 50 or older at LMP)
  - Can be retained until 2 years after the LMP (if younger than 50 at LMP)
- Any new or changed bleeding pattern should be investigated promptly
