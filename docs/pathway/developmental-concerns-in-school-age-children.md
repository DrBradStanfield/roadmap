# Developmental Concerns in School-Age Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of developmental concerns in children aged 5 to 14 years.

---

## Background

- Development occurs in multiple domains — gross motor, fine motor, language, social, emotional, and cognitive.
- Developmental delays and disorders often become clearer as children grow, as the gap between typically developing children and those with delayed development widens.
- More severely affected children tend to present at younger ages; less severely affected children may not present until older, when school and social demands increase.
- At primary and secondary school, developmental disorders can affect academic function, peer relationships, self-management, independence, and behaviour.
- Where there is one developmental diagnosis, co-morbid developmental and mental health diagnoses are common.
- The most common diagnoses considered are autism spectrum disorder (ASD, prevalence ~1%) and intellectual disability (prevalence ~2.5%).

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take caregivers' concerns seriously — they are usually a reliable indicator of problems. An extended appointment is often needed to assess development thoroughly.

### Caregiver history

Your doctor will ask:
- What is the caregiver most concerned about?
- How does the child perform compared to same-aged peers across different areas?
- Which areas of functioning are most affected — self-care, communication, friendships, play, motor skills?
- In which settings do difficulties occur — just at home, or across all settings?

### Developmental and medical history

- Perinatal history: prematurity, low birth weight, birth injury, alcohol or substance exposure in utero
- Early developmental milestones and any previous concerns or interventions
- Chronic or past severe illness
- Vision and hearing status

### Family history and social context

- Family history of developmental disorders (these often have a strong genetic component)
- Social factors that can influence presentation: poverty, family violence, unstable home environment, low caregiver education, social isolation, caregiver mental illness or addiction

### Physical examination

Your doctor will typically check:
- Weight and BMI-for-age
- Head circumference
- Dysmorphic features
- Neurological examination, including ear/nose/throat examination and oro-motor control (for speech or hearing concerns)
- Social interaction, communication, and play (observed during the consultation)

### School information

Your doctor may ask caregivers to:
- Meet with the school principal and special education needs coordinator (SENCO) or resource teacher for learning and behaviour (RTLB)
- Obtain a school report on the child's academic and social functioning

---

## Possible Diagnoses

Your doctor will consider:

- **Isolated delay** — e.g., speech or language disorder
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD)** — a developmental disorder affecting language, social interaction, and behaviour. Features include: lack of interest in others or joint play, unusual use of language, inflexibility, restricted interests, repetitive behaviours, and over- or under-sensitivity to sensory input
- **Intellectual disability**
- **Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder**
- **Behavioural problems**
- **Mental health disorder** — e.g., ADHD, anxiety
- **Medical or genetic diagnosis** — if abnormal examination findings are present
- **Child within normal range**
- **Child abuse or neglect** — this must always be considered

---

## Management

### If abuse or neglect is suspected

Your doctor will manage this according to the child abuse and neglect pathway as a priority.

### If the child has significant difficulties across multiple settings

Your doctor may:
- Arrange hearing (audiometry) and vision testing if not already done
- Refer for non-acute paediatric assessment if ASD, intellectual disability, or a medical or genetic diagnosis is suspected — this assessment helps families access relevant disability support services and diagnosis
- Refer for speech and language therapy if isolated speech problems are present

### If difficulties are mainly school-based

Your doctor may:
- Encourage families to discuss concerns with school staff
- Advise families about school-based support services, including:
  - Resource Teachers: Learning and Behaviour (RTLB), who work across schools to develop learning and behaviour plans
  - Ministry of Education speech therapy, physical disability services, and behaviour support — accessed through the school, not by medical referral
- Consider a privately funded educational psychologist assessment if school-based options have been exhausted

### If the child has primarily behavioural difficulties

Your doctor will consider a wide differential diagnosis, including ADHD, anxiety, and other developmental disorders.

### Associated issues your doctor may also address

- **Constipation** — common in children with developmental disorders
- **Sleep problems** — your doctor may provide information on managing sleep difficulties; there is no standardised approach for developmental conditions specifically
- **Behavioural difficulties with physical, intellectual, sensory disability, or ASD** — families may be eligible for disability support; your doctor can refer for a needs assessment or behaviour support services
