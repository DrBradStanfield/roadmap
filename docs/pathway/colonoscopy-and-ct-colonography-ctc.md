# Colonoscopy and CT Colonography (CTC)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the investigation of lower bowel symptoms in adults — when colonoscopy or CT colonography (CTC) is indicated, which investigation is more appropriate, and what to expect.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Assessment

The following symptoms require urgent evaluation — do not delay:

- **Known or suspected colorectal cancer** (e.g., mass visible on rectal exam, palpable abdominal mass, colorectal cancer reported on imaging)
- **Unexplained rectal bleeding with iron deficiency anaemia**
- **Altered bowel habit** (looser, more frequent, or both) for more than 6 weeks, plus aged 50 or older, plus unexplained rectal bleeding

If you have a palpable abdominal mass or rectal mass, your doctor will arrange urgent specialist assessment rather than a colonoscopy or CTC as a first step.

For acute abdominal pain, an acute surgical assessment is needed — colonoscopy or CTC is not the right first investigation.

---

## When Lower Bowel Investigation Is Considered

Your doctor may consider colonoscopy or CTC if you have:

**Symptoms (typically present for more than 6 weeks):**
- Altered bowel habit — looser stools, more frequent stools, or both
- Unexplained rectal bleeding that persists after benign causes (haemorrhoids, anal fissure, anal fistula, inflammatory bowel disease, radiation proctitis, rectal prolapse) have been excluded or treated
- Unexplained iron deficiency anaemia

**Iron deficiency and anaemia — diagnostic thresholds your doctor may use:**
- Anaemia: haemoglobin below 130 g/L in males, below 115 g/L in females
- Iron deficiency: ferritin below 20 micrograms/L (or below 100 micrograms/L in patients with chronic inflammatory conditions such as heart failure)

**Family history of colorectal cancer:**
- Moderately increased risk — e.g., one first-degree relative with colorectal cancer (CRC) diagnosed under age 55, or two first-degree relatives on the same side of the family with CRC at any age
- Potentially high risk — e.g., two first-degree relatives (or one first-degree plus one or more second-degree relatives) on the same side of the family with CRC, where one was diagnosed under 55, developed multiple CRC, or had a tumour suggestive of Lynch syndrome (endometrial, ovarian, stomach, small bowel, renal pelvis, pancreas, or brain); or a family history of FAP, Lynch syndrome, or other familial CRC syndromes

**Investigations are generally not indicated for:**
- Diarrhoea lasting less than 6 weeks — consider infectious causes first
- Rectal bleeding in patients under 50 with normal haemoglobin — manage along colorectal symptoms pathway first
- Abdominal pain as a single symptom
- Irritable bowel syndrome
- Constipation as a single symptom
- Uncomplicated diverticulitis without suspicious features on imaging
- Low ferritin in patients under 50 with normal haemoglobin — investigate iron deficiency first

---

## Colonoscopy vs CT Colonography — Which Investigation?

Your doctor will recommend one or the other based on your clinical situation.

### Colonoscopy is generally preferred when:

- Diarrhoea or rectal bleeding (not outlet-type) is the main symptom
- You have a moderately or potentially high-risk family history of bowel cancer
- Known polyp syndromes where biopsy or removal may be needed
- Iron deficiency anaemia with rectal bleeding
- Suspected inflammatory bowel disease (visualisation and biopsy are needed for diagnosis)
- You are under 40 years old (lower radiation risk with colonoscopy)

### CT Colonography (CTC) is generally preferred when:

- You are aged 80 or older
- You have significant co-morbidities that make colonoscopy higher risk — for example, COPD (respiratory risk from sedation) or anticoagulant therapy such as warfarin
- A previous colonoscopy was incomplete or unsuccessful

### Important considerations for CTC:

- CTC is not suitable during an acute presentation — there is an increased risk of bowel perforation
- CTC requires you to change position on the scanner table — patients who cannot do this may not be suitable
- Both CTC and colonoscopy require bowel preparation (fasting and oral bowel prep)

---

## What to Expect

Your doctor will discuss the referral with you before proceeding. Key things they will check:

- That you understand the procedure and are willing to proceed
- That you are likely to tolerate the bowel preparation and the procedure itself
- That you are well enough to tolerate further treatment if anything is found — particularly if you are frail, have multiple health conditions, or have advanced illness
- Whether you have had diverticulitis, inflammatory bowel disease flares, bowel surgery, or other bowel investigations in the past 3 months

Your doctor remains responsible for following up your results once the investigation is completed.
