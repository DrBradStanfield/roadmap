# Trichomoniasis

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of trichomoniasis (Trichomonas vaginalis infection) in adults.

> This information is for general education. Trichomoniasis requires diagnosis and treatment by a doctor or trained clinician. Do not attempt self-treatment.

---

## What Is Trichomoniasis?

Trichomoniasis is a sexually transmitted infection caused by the parasite Trichomonas vaginalis. It affects the urogenital tract and is transmitted through genital secretions. It can also be passed from mother to baby during vaginal delivery (usually harmless).

Co-infection with other STIs is common, and 60–80% of cases have co-existing bacterial vaginosis.

It is most commonly diagnosed in people with a vagina who also have other STIs, and in Māori and Pacific populations. It is less commonly diagnosed in people with a penis, as infections are usually asymptomatic and tests are less sensitive.

---

## Symptoms

**In people with a vagina (may be asymptomatic):**
- Profuse, frothy, discoloured, malodorous vaginal discharge
- Vulval and vaginal irritation

**In people with a penis (often asymptomatic):**
- Dysuria (pain on urination)
- Urethral discharge

---

## Who Should Be Tested

Your doctor will arrange testing if you:

- Are a known sexual contact of someone with trichomoniasis
- Are Māori or Pacific with a vagina, have social deprivation, or have a history of incarceration
- Have vaginal discharge, odour, vulval irritation, dysuria, or painful sex
- Have tested positive for another STI
- Have persistent urethritis (inflammation of the urethra) not responding to initial treatment (in people with a penis)
- Have had trichomoniasis found incidentally on cervical screening (PCR confirmation required before treatment)

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:
- Ask about your symptoms
- Examine you if symptomatic (internal examination for those with a vagina; genital examination for those with a penis)
- Arrange tests and consider completing a full sexual health check at the same consultation

### Testing

A low vaginal swab (PCR/NAAT) or first-catch urine sample is used. This can be shared with a chlamydia and gonorrhoea test. Your doctor will request Trichomonas PCR specifically.

**Testing interval:** If you are asymptomatic and concerned about a specific exposure, test at presentation and repeat 2 weeks after your last unprotected sexual activity.

---

## Possible Complications

- Premature rupture of membranes, preterm delivery, and low birth weight (in pregnancy)
- Prostatitis (rare)

---

## Treatment

Treatment must be arranged by your doctor.

### Uncomplicated Infection

**First-line options:**
- Metronidazole 400 mg orally with food, twice daily for 7 days, OR
- Ornidazole 500 mg orally twice daily for 5 days (avoid in pregnancy)

**Alternative (if adherence is difficult):**
- Metronidazole 2 g orally as a single dose (less effective), OR
- Ornidazole 1.5 g orally as a single dose (avoid in pregnancy)

Treatment cures approximately 90% of cases. Single-dose therapy improves adherence but is less effective and has more side effects.

### Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Your doctor will use metronidazole (safe in pregnancy, category B2) for 7 days. If breastfeeding, they may advise avoiding breastfeeding for 24 hours after a single-dose regimen. Breast milk may taste different during treatment.

### Allergy or Contraindications

Seek medical advice — desensitisation through an immunologist may be required.

### Treatment Failure

If symptoms persist or recur, your doctor will check adherence and risk of reinfection before trying a repeat course. If two full 7-day courses fail, specialist advice is needed.

---

## Preventing Reinfection

**Sexual contact:** Abstain from sex (including oral sex) or use condoms:
- Until 7 days after you complete treatment
- Until 7 days after your sexual contacts have also been treated

**Your sexual contacts:** All sexual contacts from the last 3 months should be notified and treated. You can inform them yourself — written information can help. Note that patient-delivered partner therapy (giving your partner your own prescription) is not legal in New Zealand.

---

## Follow-Up

Your doctor will review you in 1 week to:
- Discuss test results
- Check whether symptoms have resolved
- Confirm you completed the medication
- Ensure contacts have been notified
- Assess risk of reinfection

If symptoms persist, a **test of cure** (repeat PCR at least 4 weeks after completing treatment) may be arranged.

**Re-screening:** Your doctor will arrange re-screening 3 months after infection to check for reinfection, and to test for other STIs if not already done.
