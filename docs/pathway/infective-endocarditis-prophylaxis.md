# Infective Endocarditis Prophylaxis

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers which patients need antibiotic prophylaxis to prevent infective endocarditis (IE) before dental or surgical procedures, and the antibiotic regimens used.

---

## Who Needs Prophylaxis

Prophylaxis is recommended for **at-risk cardiac patients** undergoing selected dental procedures, tonsillectomy, adenoidectomy, or any surgical procedure involving infected tissue.

### At-Risk Cardiac Conditions

- Prosthetic heart valves, or any prosthetic material used for cardiac valve repair (mechanical or tissue, surgical or percutaneous)
- Rheumatic valvular heart disease
- Previous endocarditis
- Unrepaired cyanotic congenital heart disease (including palliative shunts and conduits)
- Surgical or catheter repair of congenital heart disease within 6 months of repair
- Repaired congenital heart disease with residual defects at, or adjacent to, the site of a prosthetic device
- Cardiac valvulopathy in a transplanted heart (substantial leaflet pathology and regurgitation)

---

## Procedures Requiring Prophylaxis

### Dental Procedures

Prophylaxis is recommended for all dental procedures that involve:
- Manipulation of gingival tissue
- The periapical region of teeth
- Perforation of the oral mucosa

**Dental procedures and events that do NOT need prophylaxis:**

- Routine anaesthetic injections through non-infected tissue
- Taking dental radiographs
- Placement of removable prosthodontic or orthodontic appliances
- Adjustment of orthodontic appliances
- Placement of orthodontic brackets
- Shedding of deciduous teeth
- Bleeding from trauma to the lips or oral mucosa

### Other Procedures That Do NOT Require Prophylaxis

Unless there is active infection at the site, prophylaxis is **not** recommended for:

- Respiratory tract mucosa procedures (e.g., bronchoscopy) — except tonsillectomy and adenoidectomy, which do require prophylaxis
- Gastrointestinal or genitourinary tract procedures
- Gynaecology or obstetrics procedures, including vaginal or caesarean delivery
- Cardiac procedures, including percutaneous catheterisation
- Other cardiology procedures using prosthetic material (e.g., stent, pacemaker)

---

## Management

Your doctor will ensure at-risk patients maintain good oral health and hygiene. If a qualifying procedure is planned, antibiotic prophylaxis will be prescribed.

### Antibiotic Regimens

**If NOT allergic to penicillin** (and has not taken a penicillin or cephalosporin antibiotic more than once in the previous month):

- **Amoxicillin** 2 g for adults, or 50 mg/kg up to 2 g for children

> Note: Patients on long-term penicillin prophylaxis for rheumatic fever are considered to have recent penicillin exposure and should use an alternative below.

**If allergic to penicillin, OR has taken a penicillin or cephalosporin antibiotic more than once in the previous month:**

- **Clarithromycin** 500 mg for adults, or 15 mg/kg up to 500 mg for children, **or**
- **Clindamycin** 600 mg for adults, or 15 mg/kg up to 600 mg for children

### Timing

- If able to take medications orally: give **one hour before** the procedure
- If unable to take oral medications: give **intravenously just before** the procedure, or **intramuscularly 30 minutes before** the procedure (note: clarithromycin cannot be given intramuscularly)

### If Prophylaxis Was Missed

- Antibiotics can still be given up to **2 hours after** the procedure
- After 2 hours, the additional risk of developing endocarditis, while marginally increased, remains very low — patients should be advised to report any suspicious symptoms of sub-acute bacterial endocarditis (SBE) such as fever or night sweats
