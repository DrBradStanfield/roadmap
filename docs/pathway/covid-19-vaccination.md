# COVID-19 Vaccination

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the clinical aspects of COVID-19 vaccination, including eligibility, vaccine schedules, special situations, and what to expect after vaccination.

---

## Background

- In New Zealand, COVID-19 circulates throughout the year, with peaks in winter and summer.
- Protection against COVID-19 illness wanes within 1 to 2 months after both infection and vaccination, but protection against severe disease persists for longer.
- The key benefit of vaccination is to reduce hospitalisation and death.
- Elderly and immunocompromised people have reduced immune response and increased severity of illness as immunity wanes — additional doses are especially protective for them.
- COVID-19 vaccination improves outcomes for pregnant people, their pregnancies, and their infants.

---

## The Vaccine Available in New Zealand

The **Pfizer-mRNA Comirnaty** vaccine is the funded and preferred COVID-19 vaccine in New Zealand. Three formulations are available:

- **Comirnaty 30 micrograms** — for people aged 12 years and older (primary course and additional doses)
- **Paediatric Comirnaty 10 micrograms** — for children aged 5 to 11 years
- **Paediatric Comirnaty 3 micrograms** — for eligible infants and children aged 6 months to 4 years

Current vaccines target the JN.1 variant, with LP.8.1 variant vaccines being phased in from late December 2025.

---

## Who Should Be Vaccinated

### Primary Course

For people **not** severely immunocompromised:

- **Aged 12 years and older** — a single dose of Comirnaty 30 micrograms, at least 3 months after any previous COVID-19 vaccine dose, to complete a primary course
- **Aged 5 to 11 years** — a single dose of Comirnaty 10 micrograms if they have not completed a primary course

For people who **are** severely immunocompromised, a three-dose primary course is recommended:

- **Aged 12 years and older** — Comirnaty 30 micrograms at 8-week intervals
- **Aged 5 to 11 years** — Comirnaty 10 micrograms at 8-week intervals
- **Aged 6 months to 4 years** (severely immunocompromised or at risk of severe disease) — Comirnaty 3 micrograms, three-dose course

### Additional (Booster) Doses

Give any additional dose at least 6 months after the previous dose or recovery from a COVID-19 infection for most people. A shorter interval of at least 3 months may be appropriate for those at increased risk of severe disease (your doctor will use clinical discretion).

Ongoing additional doses are **available** for all people aged 30 years and older.

Ongoing additional doses are **especially recommended** for those at higher risk of severe illness:

- All people aged 65 years or older
- Māori and Pacific people aged 50 years or older
- People aged 12 years or older who are severely immunocompromised
- Pregnant people (see Pregnancy section below)
- People with a medical condition that increases the risk of severe COVID-19 disease
- People who reside in an aged care or disability care facility
- People with a disability with significant or complex healthcare needs
- People aged 12 to 17 years with a BMI at or above the 95th centile for age
- People aged 18 years or older who are severely obese (BMI 40 or more) or severely underweight (BMI under 16.5)

From 1 February 2026, widened access applies for those who are severely immunocompromised or at high risk of severe disease:

- People aged 6 months to 29 years who are severely immunocompromised may receive additional doses every 6 months
- Children aged 6 months to 11 years at high risk of severe disease may receive additional doses every 12 months
- People aged 12 to 29 years at high risk of severe disease may receive additional doses every 6 months

### After a COVID-19 Infection

Wait 6 months after a positive COVID-19 test before having a vaccine, as antibody levels are likely to have declined. Your doctor may use clinical discretion and recommend an earlier vaccine for those at high risk of severe disease.

## Special Situations

### Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Your doctor may recommend a Comirnaty 30 micrograms dose during pregnancy if you:

- Have not previously been vaccinated against COVID-19
- Have previously been vaccinated and are at increased risk of severe COVID-19 due to:
  - Age 35 years or older
  - A high-risk pregnancy (gestational diabetes, hypertension, thrombosis, obesity with BMI over 35, or history of pregnancy complications)
  - Severe immunocompromise
  - A medical condition that increases risk of severe COVID-19 disease
  - Residing in an aged care or disability care facility
  - A disability with significant or complex healthcare needs
  - Age 12 to 17 years with a BMI at or above the 95th centile for age

The vaccine can be given at any stage of pregnancy and while breastfeeding (for those aged 12 years and older), at least 6 months after a previous COVID-19 vaccination or confirmed acute COVID-19 infection.

Comirnaty, Boostrix (whooping cough), and influenza vaccines can be given at the same time — studies show no increase in adverse effects with combined administration.

### Immunocompromised

- Vaccination is strongly recommended even though vaccine effectiveness may be reduced.
- Severely immunocompromised people are eligible for a three-dose primary course and additional doses at 6-month intervals (aged 12 years and older).
- For patients on ocrelizumab, timing should be discussed with a neurologist.
- If unsure, your doctor will seek advice from the relevant specialist.

### Cancer

Where possible, your doctor will time COVID-19 vaccination to when immune suppression from chemotherapy is least — usually before starting treatment, or shortly before a dose — or as recommended by the treating specialist.

### Rheumatology Disease

Your doctor may consider adjusting the timing of COVID-19 vaccination and immunosuppressive treatment:

- **Methotrexate, leflunomide, mycophenolate, sulfasalazine, or upadacitinib** — if disease activity allows, these may be withheld for 1 to 2 weeks after each COVID-19 vaccine dose
- **Rituximab** — rheumatology advice will be sought for timing; the vaccine may be given when the next rituximab dose is due, with rituximab delayed by 2 weeks
- **Prednisone 20 mg daily or more** — if possible, vaccination may be deferred until the dose is below 20 mg daily

Otherwise, COVID-19 vaccination is recommended without interrupting usual rheumatology medicines.

### Allergic Reactions

Your doctor will take extra precautions if you have:

- A history of anaphylaxis to a PEG-reagent (other than that in Comirnaty vaccines) — when vaccinating with a Comirnaty vaccine
- Anaphylaxis or other generalised allergic reaction to any food or medicine (gastrointestinal symptoms are not a concern)
- Cutaneous or systemic mastocytosis with raised mast cell tryptase requiring treatment

In these situations, the recommendation is to observe for at least 30 minutes after vaccination, and to vaccinate at a medical centre if available.

## Adverse Events After Vaccination

### Very Common (more than 1 in 10)

- Injection site pain and/or swelling
- Headache
- Joint or muscle pain (arthralgia/myalgia)
- Fatigue, chills, fever

These are more frequent after the second dose and may require a day off work.

### Common (1 in 10 to 1 in 100)

- Injection site redness
- Nausea

### Very Rare

- **Anaphylaxis** — your doctor will observe you for at least 15 to 30 minutes post-vaccination
- **Temporary facial paralysis (Bell's palsy)**
- **Myocarditis and pericarditis** — usually reported 1 to 5 days after vaccination

**Seek prompt medical attention** if after vaccination you develop:
- Chest pain or discomfort
- Palpitations
- Dizziness or fainting
- Shortness of breath

---

## COVID-19 Symptoms After Vaccination

Symptoms that may suggest COVID-19 — even shortly after vaccination — include:

- Loss of sense of smell or altered sense of taste
- New onset of sore throat, cough, sneezing, shortness of breath, or runny nose
- Generalised muscle aches worsening over time
- Fever of 38°C or higher

Although these symptoms are common in the first 1 to 2 days after vaccination, they should not be assumed to be vaccine-related. A COVID-19 test is recommended if these symptoms occur.

---

## Important Note

This information is a general guide only. Vaccination schedules, eligibility, and management of side effects depend on your individual circumstances. **Always discuss with your doctor** before making decisions about vaccination, particularly if you have an underlying health condition, are immunocompromised, are pregnant, or have had a previous allergic reaction to a vaccine.
