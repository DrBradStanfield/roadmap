# Snoring and Sleep Apnoea in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how snoring and obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) in children are assessed and managed. Your doctor will guide diagnosis and treatment.

---

## Background

Snoring occurs in more than 10% of healthy children, but 3 to 5% of children who snore have an underlying pathological cause.

Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) is the most common cause of snoring and affects 3 to 5% of otherwise healthy children:

- It is more common in preschool children, with no gender difference
- It is most often caused by enlarged tonsils or adenoids
- Obesity (BMI above the 95th percentile for age) increases the risk by 4 to 5 times
- In children, OSA usually involves partial airway obstruction with continuous snoring and episodic reduced breathing — unlike adults who typically have complete obstruction
- Untreated OSA can cause neurocognitive problems, including daytime behaviour disturbances and cognitive impairment that may resemble ADHD

Severe persistent OSA can cause cardiovascular problems (such as heart failure and high blood pressure) and failure to thrive, due to reduced growth hormone secretion and increased energy expenditure during sleep.

---

## Signs and Symptoms to Watch For

Consider discussing OSA with your doctor if your child has:

- Habitual noisy breathing or snoring (especially more than 3 nights per week when not unwell)
- Breathing difficulties during sleep — snorting, gasping, coughing, or choking
- Witnessed pauses in breathing during sleep where the chest moves but no air seems to pass
- Nasal obstruction or daytime mouth breathing
- A parent feeling frightened by the child's breathing at night
- Difficulty waking in the morning
- Daytime hyperactivity or excessive sleepiness

**Note:** Small tonsils do not rule out OSA. Tonsil size when a child is awake does not reliably predict whether OSA is present.

---

## Risk Factors

Factors that increase the likelihood of OSA include:

- Enlarged tonsils or adenoids, especially in children aged 2 to 6 years
- Family history of OSA
- Allergic rhinitis
- Obesity

Factors that increase complexity and may complicate management:

- Obesity
- Craniofacial abnormalities (e.g., Pierre Robin sequence, repaired cleft palate, micrognathia, retrognathia, laryngomalacia)
- Neuromuscular conditions (e.g., Down syndrome, achondroplasia, spina bifida, cerebral palsy)
- Chronic lung disease (e.g., bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis)
- Age under 2 years
- History of low birth weight, prematurity, or multiple gestation

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess your child for OSA by:

- Reviewing signs, symptoms, and risk factors
- Completing a validated OSA screening questionnaire (the Paediatric OSAS Screening Questionnaire — more than 7 yes responses, or an overall score above 0.33 out of 29, indicates high risk)
- Examining the mouth and jaw — checking for craniofacial abnormalities, tonsillar size, tongue size, and palate or uvula abnormalities
- Checking nasal airway patency and any anatomical abnormalities
- Measuring height, weight, and BMI
- Checking for signs such as pectus excavatum (a chest wall abnormality) or Harrison sulci (grooves on the lower chest)
- Measuring blood pressure

A video taken by parents of the child sleeping can be very helpful to bring to the appointment.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider:

- **A trial of nasal steroids** if allergic rhinitis appears to be contributing
- **Referral for ORL (ear, nose, and throat) assessment** if the OSAS questionnaire suggests moderate to severe OSA — adenotonsillectomy (removal of the adenoids and tonsils) is usually helpful regardless of apparent tonsillar size
- **Referral for paediatric specialist assessment** if there are risk factors that increase complexity (see above), as these children may have higher surgical risk; an overnight sleep study may also be recommended
- **Weight management** if obesity is a contributing factor
- **Re-referral to ORL** if problems continue after surgery

Decisions about referral and surgery are made by your doctor based on the full clinical picture.
