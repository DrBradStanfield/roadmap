# Fingolimod Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This guidance supports GPs following specialist initiation of fingolimod for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (MS). It covers ongoing monitoring and management in primary care.

---

## Background

Fingolimod (brand name Gilenya) is an **immunomodulator** used for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. It is initiated by a neurologist. The GP's role is ongoing monitoring and management of side effects.

**Dose:** 0.5 mg capsule once daily.

---

## When to Stop the Drug — Act Promptly

Stop fingolimod and seek urgent neurology advice if:

- GGT, AST, or ALT is greater than **5 times the upper limit of normal**
- Symptomatic liver injury (jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, fatigue with liver dysfunction)

---

## Monitoring Schedule

Your doctor will arrange the following checks in primary care:

**Every 3 months for the first year, then every 6 months:**

- **Liver function tests (LFTs):** If GGT, AST, or ALT is greater than 3 times the upper limit of normal, seek neurology advice
- **Full blood count (FBC):**
  - A low lymphocyte count is expected and not always dangerous
  - Lymphocyte count of 0.2 × 10⁹/L or greater is acceptable
- **Blood pressure**

**Other monitoring:**

- Patients who develop second-degree or higher heart conduction blocks, sick sinus syndrome, ischaemic heart disease, or congestive heart failure while on fingolimod may require cardiology assessment
- Routine preventive health checks (dental, skin, breast, prostate, and other age-appropriate cancer screening)
- Women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 years: recall for cervical screening every 3 years (fingolimod causes immunosuppression, which increases HPV risk)

---

## Adverse Effects and Management

| System | Adverse Effect | Your Doctor May Consider |
|--------|---------------|--------------------------|
| Heart | Bradyarrhythmia (after first or second dose, usually resolves within a few days) | If persistent heart rate below 45 beats per minute, seek neurology advice |
| Blood | Lymphopenia (0.2–0.5 × 10⁹/L is common and not associated with significant immunosuppression, but may increase risk of herpes zoster, tinea, and bronchitis) | Treat infection. If lymphocyte count falls below 0.2 × 10⁹/L, seek neurology advice |
| Blood | Progressive cytopenia or neutropenia | Mild neutropenia is uncommon. If prolonged or severe (neutrophil count below 0.5 × 10⁹/L), seek neurology advice |
| Blood pressure | Mild or moderate hypertension | Your doctor may consider antihypertensive treatment |
| Infection | Increased risk of infections due to immunosuppression | Treat infection and check FBC. If lymphocyte count below 0.2 × 10⁹/L or neutrophil below 0.5 × 10⁹/L, seek neurology advice |
| Liver | Raised hepatic enzymes (mostly ALT) | If LFTs greater than 5 times the upper limit of normal or symptomatic liver injury: stop drug and seek urgent neurology advice. If greater than 3 times: seek neurology advice |
| Eyes | Macular oedema (with or without visual symptoms, mainly in first 3–4 months); uveitis | Patients are given an Amsler grid chart to self-monitor. If any central vision changes, seek ophthalmology advice |

## Missed Doses

If a patient misses doses, **do not simply restart** — bradycardia monitoring is required before resuming. Contact the neurology team, who will arrange return to the Medical Day Unit for first-dose monitoring.

Missed dose thresholds that require re-monitoring:

- 1 or more doses missed in the **first 2 weeks**
- An interruption of **7 days or more** during weeks 3–4
- An interruption of **2 weeks or more** from week 5 onward

Once back in the Medical Day Unit, the medication is treated as if starting for the first time, and the above thresholds reset.

---

## Drug Interactions

Your doctor will check for interactions. Key categories to be aware of:

- Medications associated with QT prolongation and risk of torsades de pointes (TdP)
- Medications with additive immune system suppression — use with caution and discuss with the neurologist
- Beta blockers (additive heart rate–lowering effect)

For a full interaction check, discuss with a pharmacist or refer to a drug interaction resource.

---

## Vaccinations

- **Avoid live vaccines** while on fingolimod — they carry a risk of infection in an immunosuppressed patient. Examples of live vaccines to avoid: MMR, oral rotavirus, oral polio, oral typhoid, BCG (tuberculosis), varicella zoster (chickenpox and Zostavax), yellow fever
- Fingolimod may reduce the effectiveness of vaccines for up to 2 months after stopping therapy
- **Annual influenza vaccination** is funded for people with MS — encourage this

---

## Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

- **Avoid fingolimod in pregnancy** — advise effective contraception during treatment and for 2 months after stopping
- If pregnancy is planned, neurology input is needed for pre-conception advice before stopping the medication
- If pregnancy is confirmed: fingolimod must be stopped or changed to a safer option, and neurology advice sought
- No known effects on sperm — male patients can father children without known risks from the drug
- **Breastfeeding is not recommended** while on fingolimod
