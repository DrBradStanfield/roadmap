# Warfarin Monitoring

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers ongoing monitoring of warfarin (an anticoagulant blood-thinning medication), including options for INR testing and dose management in the community.

---

## What Is Warfarin Monitoring?

Warfarin requires regular blood tests called **INR (International Normalised Ratio)** checks to ensure the dose is in the safe and effective range. Your healthcare provider will adjust your warfarin dose based on these results.

---

## Community Pharmacy Anticoagulant Management (CPAM)

In some areas, warfarin monitoring can be managed through a community pharmacy rather than at a GP clinic. This service uses INR point-of-care testing (a fingerprick test done at the pharmacy) and a decision support system to adjust warfarin doses.

Your doctor may refer you to this service if you are eligible.

### Eligibility

To be considered for the CPAM service, you must:

- Be on warfarin
- Be registered with a general practitioner
- Consent to being referred to a pharmacy for INR testing and warfarin dose management

### Exclusions

This service is generally **not suitable** for people who:

- Are not registered with a GP
- Are in aged residential care
- Have anti-phospholipid syndrome, anti-cardiolipid syndrome, or lupus anticoagulant syndrome
- Are receiving active anti-cancer (anti-neoplastic) treatment

If you fall into one of these groups, discuss with your doctor what monitoring arrangement is most appropriate for you.

---

## Information Your Healthcare Provider Will Need

When managing your warfarin, your healthcare provider will typically want to know:

- The **reason** (indication) you are taking warfarin
- Your **target INR** range
- The **expected duration** of your treatment
- Your **three most recent warfarin doses** (with dates)
- Whether you have:
  - Excess alcohol intake
  - Persistent unstable INR results
  - Any other relevant risks or changes in your health

Keeping a record of your warfarin doses and INR results can help your doctor or pharmacist manage your treatment safely.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Help

Contact your doctor or seek urgent care if you experience:

- Unusual or excessive bleeding (e.g., prolonged bleeding from cuts, blood in urine or stools, coughing up blood)
- Unexplained bruising
- A significant change in your diet, other medications, or health — these can affect your INR
