# Myotonic Dystrophy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the approach to suspected or confirmed myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1) — the most common adult-onset muscular dystrophy.

---

## Red Flag — Seek Urgent Care

**Cardiac-related blackouts or syncope** in a person with myotonic dystrophy require prompt medical assessment. Cardiac conduction disturbances (including heart block) are a recognised complication and can be life-threatening.

---

## Background

Myotonic dystrophy type 1 (DM1) is an autosomal dominant multisystem disorder:
- Each child of an affected parent has a 50:50 chance of inheriting the mutation
- It affects males and females equally
- The condition may become more severe with each generation (anticipation)

**Severity ranges widely:**

| Form | Features | Prognosis |
|------|----------|-----------|
| Mild DM1 | Cataracts, mild myotonia | Normal lifespan |
| Classic DM1 | Muscle weakness and wasting, myotonia, cataracts, cardiac conduction abnormalities | Progressive disability, possibly shortened lifespan |
| Congenital DM1 | Severe hypotonia and weakness at birth, respiratory problems, intellectual disability | High early mortality risk |

**Genetic testing** looks for a CTG triplet repeat expansion in the DMPK gene. A normal result rules out the diagnosis; an expansion confirms it in a symptomatic person.

---

## Features Doctors Look For

### Signs
- **Unexplained cataracts** — may be the only sign in people over 60
- **Myotonia** (delayed muscle relaxation) — tested by asking the patient to grip and release quickly; a delayed release indicates myotonia
- **Muscle weakness**, particularly in facial and distal muscles:
  - Hands — reduced finger dexterity
  - Ankles — foot drop (bilateral)
  - Neck — dropped head posture
  - Proximal muscles are usually spared early on
- Frontal balding
- Mild learning difficulties

### Symptoms to Ask About
- **Cardiac symptoms:** blackouts, syncope, palpitations, breathlessness — these may indicate heart block, bundle branch block, atrial flutter (less commonly atrial fibrillation or ventricular arrhythmias)
- **Sleep symptoms:** obstructive sleep apnoea, excessive daytime sleepiness
- Gastrointestinal and urinary symptoms

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a detailed family history focused on signs and symptoms of myotonic dystrophy, and draw a 3-generation family tree.

---

## Referral

- **Personal or family history of myotonic dystrophy:** referral to genetic health services for genetic counselling and testing
- **No family history but neuromuscular weakness present:** referral to neurology for assessment

---

## Ongoing Monitoring (Once Diagnosed)

Regular general practice review includes:
- HbA1c and thyroid function tests
- Monitoring for cataracts
- ECG to check for cardiac conduction disturbances
- Screening for sleep apnoea; arrange sleep study if appropriate
- Weight monitoring (increased risk of insulin resistance)

**Cardiac symptoms:** prompt cardiology referral.

---

## Treatment of Symptomatic Myotonia

Muscle stiffness (myotonia) can be treated with medication if needed. Options include:
- Mexiletine 150–200 mg three times daily
- Phenytoin at therapeutic doses (similar to epilepsy dosing)
- Acetazolamide 250–500 mg twice or three times daily

Treatment decisions are made by your doctor based on symptom severity and individual circumstances.
