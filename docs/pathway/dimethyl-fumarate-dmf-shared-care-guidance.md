# Dimethyl Fumarate (DMF) Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This guidance supports GPs managing patients who have been started on dimethyl fumarate (Tecfidera) by a specialist for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (MS). Treatment is initiated by a neurologist — this guidance covers ongoing primary care monitoring and management.

---

## Background

Dimethyl fumarate (Tecfidera) is an immunomodulator used for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. Its exact mechanism of action is unknown. It has a short half-life of approximately 1 hour — no circulating drug is present at 24 hours in the majority of people.

**Full dosage:** 240 mg modified-release capsules twice daily. Dose is titrated over the first month to improve tolerability:

| Week | Dose |
|------|------|
| Week 1 | 120 mg once daily |
| Week 2 | 120 mg twice daily |
| Week 3 | 120 mg morning, 240 mg at night |
| Week 4 onwards | 240 mg twice daily (full dose) |

---

## When to Stop the Drug Immediately

Stop dimethyl fumarate and seek urgent neurology advice if:

- GGT, AST, or ALT is **≥ 5 times** the upper limit of normal
- Symptomatic liver injury (jaundice, right upper quadrant pain, fatigue with elevated LFTs)
- Serious infection — discuss with neurology whether to stop DMF

---

## Monitoring Schedule

Your doctor will arrange the following checks at **3 months, 6 months, then every 6 months**:

**Liver function tests (LFTs):**
- If GGT, AST, or ALT > 3 times the upper limit of normal → seek neurology advice
- If ≥ 5 times the upper limit of normal → stop drug and seek urgent neurology advice

**Full blood count (FBC):**
- A low lymphocyte count is expected and is not associated with increased infection risk on its own
- Lymphocytes persistently < 0.5 × 10⁹/L for more than 6 months → seek neurology advice
- Lymphocytes < 0.2 × 10⁹/L (severe lymphopenia) → seek neurology advice

**Routine health monitoring:**
- Regular blood pressure checks
- Dental and skin assessment
- Breast, prostate, and other age-appropriate cancer screening
- Urinalysis as clinically indicated (to check for proteinuria)

**Cervical screening:**
- Recall women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 years for three-yearly cervical screening — patients on long-term immunosuppression have an increased risk of HPV infection
- If aged 20–24, sexually active, and on immunosuppressants for more than 5 years, consider a single HPV test
- Refer all those with HPV detected (any type) to colposcopy

## Adverse Effects and Management

Your doctor may consider the following approaches to manage side effects:

### General Side Effects (Common, Usually Subside Within 2–3 Months)

| Adverse Effect | Management |
|----------------|------------|
| Flushing | Aspirin 300 mg taken 30 minutes before DMF dose. Encourage taking DMF with a fatty or protein-rich meal (e.g., avocado, nuts). |
| Nausea, diarrhoea, abdominal pain | Take DMF with food (fatty or protein-rich). Your doctor may consider a proton pump inhibitor, metoclopramide, or Gaviscon. |
| Headaches | Paracetamol |

### Blood

- **Lymphopenia (lymphocytes < 0.5 × 10⁹/L):** Common; not associated with increased infection risk at this level alone. Seek neurology advice if persistent beyond 6 months or if < 0.2 × 10⁹/L.

### Infection

- Patients have an increased risk of serious infections
- Treat infections promptly
- For serious infections, seek urgent neurology advice — stopping DMF may need to be considered

### Liver

- LFT increases are most commonly seen in the first 6 months of treatment and are usually less than 3 times the upper limit of normal
- See monitoring thresholds above

### Kidneys

- Proteinuria may occur — perform urinalysis as clinically indicated

---

## Drug Interactions

Dimethyl fumarate has potential interactions including over-the-counter medications. Your doctor will check for interactions using the NZ Formulary or a drug reference guide, or by consulting a pharmacist.

---

## Vaccinations

- **Live vaccines are contraindicated** due to the risk of serious infection. Examples include MMR, varicella zoster (chickenpox, Zostavax), oral rotavirus, oral polio, oral typhoid, BCG (tuberculosis), and yellow fever.
- DMF may reduce the effectiveness of vaccines for up to 2 months after stopping therapy.
- Annual influenza immunisation is funded for people with MS.

---

## Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Your doctor will discuss the following with the neurologist before making any changes:

- **Pre-conception planning:** If a patient on DMF is planning a pregnancy, your doctor will arrange a neurology assessment for pre-conception advice.
- **Male patients:** If a male patient is planning to father children, neurology advice should be sought.
- **Pregnancy diagnosed:** Neurology should be alerted by same-day phone call if pregnancy is confirmed.
- **Breastfeeding:** The decision to continue or stop breastfeeding or treatment will be made in discussion with the patient, weighing the benefits of both.
- Effective contraception is recommended while on DMF.
