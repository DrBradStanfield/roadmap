# Scabies in Adults

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and treatment of scabies — a highly contagious skin infestation caused by a tiny mite.

---

## What Is Scabies?

Scabies is caused by a microscopic mite (*Sarcoptes scabiei*) that burrows into the skin and triggers an allergic reaction, causing an intensely itchy rash. The mite cannot live on animals or survive in the environment for more than 1 to 4 days.

**Transmission:** Skin-to-skin contact, or via contaminated bedding and furniture.

**Symptoms:**
- Intensely itchy rash — an allergic reaction to mite faeces and saliva
- Itching may start from a few days to 6 weeks after infestation; typically worse at night, in bed, or after a hot shower
- The rash may take 2 weeks to develop
- Rash does not usually affect the scalp or face (except in babies, the bed-bound elderly, or debilitated patients)
- Nodules may develop in armpits, groin, or shaft of the penis
- Blisters, pustules, and secondary infection can occur

**Complications:** Secondary skin infections (impetigo, cellulitis), and rarely systemic sepsis, post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis, or acute rheumatic fever.

**Crusted (Norwegian) scabies** is a rare, very contagious variant involving thousands of mites, causing a scaly generalised rash with very little itch. It mainly affects immunocompromised, elderly, or neurologically impaired people, and is frequently misdiagnosed as psoriasis.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

- Previous episodes of scabies or skin conditions
- Contact with another itchy person
- Whether other family members or housemates are affected
- Risk factors: crowded housing, institutional care (rest homes, prisons), immunosuppression

Your doctor will examine the skin for:

- Intensely itchy rash with scratch marks, papules, nodules, or excoriations
- Burrows or tracks — especially on the wrists, hands, finger web spaces, outer elbows, abdomen, sides or soles of feet, and genitals in men
- Dermoscopy may reveal mites at the end of burrows (diagnostic when positive)

**Scabies is usually diagnosed clinically.** Skin scraping has low diagnostic sensitivity.

**Other conditions that can look like scabies:**
- Flea or bed bug bites
- Eczema, contact dermatitis, viral rash
- Allergic reactions (papular urticaria)
- Bullous pemphigoid, pityriasis rosea

If there is a generalised scaly rash with very little itch, your doctor will consider crusted scabies.

---

## Treatment

### First Line: Topical Permethrin

**5% permethrin lotion or cream — applied twice (day 0 and day 7):**

- Apply to the **whole body**, including scalp, neck, face, and ears
- Pay special attention to: webs between fingers and toes, under fingernails and toenails (use a nail brush), belly button, genitals, and all skin folds
- Leave on for at least **8 hours** (up to 14 hours) before washing off
- If you wash your hands or any area during this time, reapply immediately
- Apply before bed to reduce the chance of washing it off too soon
- **Repeat exactly 7 days later**

All close household contacts should be treated at the same time, even if they have no symptoms.

### Second Line: Oral Ivermectin

Used when topical permethrin has failed, or for patients who cannot physically complete topical treatment (e.g. frail elderly, cognitively impaired).

- Requires Special Authority (can be arranged by your GP)
- Two doses, one week apart
- Dose: 200 micrograms per kilogram of body weight (rounded to the nearest 3 mg tablet)
- Ivermectin kills mites but **not eggs** — the second dose is essential to kill newly hatched mites
- Two-dose regimen achieves 98% cure rate

**Crusted scabies** requires combined oral ivermectin (multiple doses on a set schedule) and daily topical permethrin — your doctor will direct this treatment.

### Managing the Itch

The itch is an allergic reaction and may persist for **weeks after the mites are gone**. This does not mean treatment has failed. Your doctor may recommend:

- Antihistamines (oral)
- Emollients (moisturisers)
- Mild to moderate topical corticosteroids
- Crotamiton cream 10% or calamine lotion

---

## Environmental Decontamination

**On the morning after everyone has been treated**, wash or decontaminate items that have been in contact with skin in the last 4 days:

**Bedlinen, towels, clothes, and soft toys — choose one method:**
- Hot wash at 50°C for at least 10 minutes, OR
- Tumble dry at the hottest setting for at least 10 minutes, OR
- Seal in a plastic bag for 4 days at room temperature, OR
- Seal in a plastic bag and freeze overnight (below −20°C for >5 hours)

**Other actions:**
- Vacuum carpets and upholstered furniture every day for 4 days
- Mattresses do not usually need washing (mites cannot pass through sheet weave) — if directly slept on, wrap in plastic for 4 days
- Do **not** use pesticide sprays or fogs — they do not kill mites and can irritate skin
- Do **not** treat pets — they do not spread human scabies
- Repeat environmental decontamination after the second treatment

---

## Follow-Up

- Your doctor will check for new lesions **at least 2 to 3 weeks after** initial treatment
- The rash commonly lasts more than 6 weeks — this does not automatically mean treatment failure
- If symptoms are not improving, your doctor will ask about:
  - How thoroughly the treatment was applied
  - Whether all household members were treated at the same time
  - Whether environmental decontamination was completed
- If the correct process was followed but symptoms persist beyond 6 weeks, your doctor will seek dermatology advice

**If secondary skin infection develops** (impetigo or cellulitis), your doctor may prescribe oral antibiotics.

---

## If You Are a Contact

All people in the same household as someone with scabies should be treated simultaneously, even without symptoms. Treating separately leads to re-infestation.
