# Iodine Supplementation in Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Iodine is essential for both mother and baby during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Supplementation is recommended for all pregnant or breastfeeding patients, and for those planning a pregnancy.

---

## Why Iodine Matters

- **Mild to moderate deficiency** is associated with goitre, thyroid disorders in the mother, reduced placental weight, smaller neonatal head circumference, ADHD, and impaired cognitive outcomes in children.
- **Severe deficiency** is associated with maternal and fetal goitre, thyroid nodules in up to 30% of pregnant patients, increased pregnancy loss, stillbirth, and higher perinatal and infant mortality.
- Supplementation has clear benefits in severe deficiency; benefits are less pronounced in mild to moderate deficiency.

---

## Recommended Supplementation — No Known Thyroid Condition

Your doctor may prescribe **potassium iodate 150 micrograms daily**. This is the standard recommended dose for patients with normal thyroid function (euthyroid).

**When to start:**

- **Vegetarian or vegan patients** who do not use iodised salt and do not eat seaweed: start iodine when beginning to plan a pregnancy, and continue until breastfeeding is complete.
- **All other patients:** start at conception and continue until breastfeeding is complete.

**What to know:**

- Seaweed and kelp tablets are **not recommended** — the iodine content is highly variable and can reach toxic levels.
- Not all over-the-counter antenatal vitamins contain the full recommended dose of iodine — check the label or ask your doctor.

---

## If You Have a Thyroid Condition

This is an area where your doctor's individual assessment is essential. General guidance:

- **Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid):** Your doctor will manage this with thyroxine. Iodine may be prescribed alongside thyroxine during pregnancy, and should be prescribed during breastfeeding.
- **Hyperthyroidism or untreated Graves' disease:** Iodine supplements are generally **not prescribed** during pregnancy, as they may worsen thyroid disease.
- **Thyrotoxicosis without definitive treatment** (radioiodine or surgery): Iodine is generally **not prescribed**.
- **Taking thyroxine with a normal T4:** Iodine is generally **not prescribed**.
- **Hashimoto's (autoimmune thyroid disease):** This is commonly listed as a contraindication, but research to date (in small studies) has not shown adverse outcomes with the standard 150 microgram dose. Your doctor will assess this individually.

If there is any uncertainty about whether iodine is appropriate for you, your doctor will seek specialist (endocrinology or obstetric) advice.

---

## Medication Interactions

Your doctor will consider any medications that may interact with iodine supplementation:

- **Amiodarone** — This heart medication already contains high levels of iodine, so additional iodine supplements are not prescribed. Amiodarone use in pregnancy also carries a risk of thyroid problems in the baby; your doctor will seek cardiology advice about alternatives and will monitor both mother and baby carefully.
- **Lithium** — Does not directly affect iodine levels, but can affect thyroid function (causing either under- or overactivity). Your doctor will check and monitor thyroid function before and during supplementation.
