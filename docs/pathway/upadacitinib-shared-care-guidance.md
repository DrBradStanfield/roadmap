# Upadacitinib Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This guidance supports GPs following specialist initiation of upadacitinib. Use alongside the New Zealand Formulary entry for upadacitinib.

---

## Background

Upadacitinib is a second-generation selective Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor, also referred to as a targeted synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (DMARD). It targets the JAK pathway to reduce inflammation.

**Common indications:**
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Atopic dermatitis
- Crohn's disease
- Ulcerative colitis

**Therapy is usually initiated under specialist care**, although any relevant practitioner can apply for special authority to prescribe upadacitinib.

---

## Red Flags — Stop Drug and Seek Urgent Care

Stop upadacitinib immediately and arrange urgent specialist consult if any of the following occur:

- Serious or opportunistic infection
- Reactivation tuberculosis
- Anaphylaxis

---

## Monitoring

Your doctor will arrange regular monitoring. Check patients have received and understood the patient information sheet.

**Blood tests every 3 months:**
- Full blood count (FBC)
- Renal function
- Liver function tests (LFTs)
- Lipid profile
- CRP (not required for dermatological conditions)

**Check creatine kinase (CK)** only if the patient reports myopathy symptoms — upadacitinib can be associated with raised CK levels.

**Sun safety and skin checks:**
- Reinforce sun-smart behaviour due to increased risk of skin cancer
- Encourage the patient to report new persistent skin lesions promptly

**Cervical screening:**
- Recall women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 years for three-yearly cervical screening
- Patients on long-term immunosuppression have an increased risk of HPV infection
- If aged 20–24, sexually active, and on immunosuppressants for more than 5 years, consider a single HPV test
- Refer all those with HPV detected (any type) to colposcopy

---

## Dosage

Doses vary by condition. Your doctor may consider the following:

- **Rheumatoid arthritis:** 15 mg orally once a day
- **Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD):** Induction dose of 45 mg for 8–12 weeks, then maintenance of 15–30 mg once a day
- **Atopic dermatitis:** 15–30 mg once a day

Repeat prescriptions can be written by a specialist or GP. Symptoms may improve anywhere from 2 weeks to 6 months after starting treatment.

## Adverse Effects and Management

Your doctor will be aware of these adverse effects and will act accordingly. Key effects and recommended actions:

| Adverse Effect | Details | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Infection | Increased risk of serious or life-threatening infection | Treat infection; withhold drug until infection clears; consult specialist; low threshold for hospital admission |
| Tuberculosis | Reactivation of latent TB; may be non-pulmonary | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| Varicella zoster (shingles) | Increased risk | Withhold drug; treat at immunosuppressed dose; restart once resolved |
| Hyperlipidaemia | — | Treat as appropriate |
| Acne | Dose-dependent; more common in IBD patients | Treat as appropriate |
| Diverticular perforation | Increased risk | Treat as appropriate; inform specialist |
| Lymphopenia (< 0.5 × 10⁹/L) | — | Stop drug; seek specialist advice |
| Neutropenia (< 1.0 × 10⁹/L) | — | Stop drug; seek specialist advice |
| Anaemia (Hb < 80 g/L) | — | Stop drug; seek specialist advice |
| Transaminitis (ALT/AST > 2× normal, unexplained) | — | Stop drug; seek specialist advice |
| Hypersensitivity reaction | Rash, anaphylaxis, angioedema (uncommon) | Oral antihistamine; stop drug; consult specialist |
| Malignancy | Confirmed malignancy | Stop drug; consult specialist; caution with pre-malignant conditions or fully excised BCC |
| Major cardiovascular event (MACE) | New onset | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| DVT or PE | Increased risk | Consult specialist |

For a full list of adverse effects, see the New Zealand Formulary or the Medsafe datasheet for upadacitinib (Rinvoq).

---

## Drug Interactions

Upadacitinib has additive effects with other immunosuppressants. Your doctor will check for interactions before prescribing or adjusting medications. Discuss any new medications or supplements with your doctor or pharmacist.

---

## Special Situations

### COVID-19

If a patient on upadacitinib develops COVID-19:
- Ideally withhold oral DMARDs and biologics (excluding prednisone) until recovery, if disease activity allows
- Consider eligibility for antivirals — your doctor will assess this
- Patients on high doses of immunosuppression may need specialist infectious diseases input

### Surgery

- Stop upadacitinib 1 week before elective surgery
- Decision on restarting is made by the prescribing specialist or surgeon

### Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

- Upadacitinib is **contraindicated** in pregnancy and breastfeeding
- Ensure effective contraception in patients of childbearing age
- Stop the drug immediately if pregnancy occurs
- Stop 4 weeks before planned conception
- Seek specialist advice about alternative treatments if pregnancy is being planned

---

## Vaccinations

Your doctor will offer appropriate vaccinations. Live vaccines must be avoided during treatment.

**Recommended:**
- Annual influenza vaccination
- COVID-19 booster vaccinations (withhold upadacitinib for 1–2 weeks after each dose if disease activity allows)
- Pneumococcal vaccination (not usually funded)
- Zoster vaccination (Shingrix): two doses are funded for people aged 65 years and older, and for people aged 18 years and older who are receiving DMARDs for rheumatological conditions

**Avoid during treatment (live vaccines):**
- MMR (measles, mumps, rubella)
- Oral rotavirus
- Oral polio
- Oral typhoid
- BCG (tuberculosis)
- Varicella zoster (chickenpox and Zostavax)
- Yellow fever

Discuss timing of any required live vaccine with your prescribing specialist before stopping treatment.
