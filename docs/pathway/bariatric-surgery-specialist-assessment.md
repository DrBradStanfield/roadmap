# Bariatric Surgery — Who May Be Eligible

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Bariatric surgery (weight loss surgery) is considered for people with significant obesity who have related health conditions. This page explains the criteria used to assess eligibility.

---

## Who May Be Considered

Your doctor may consider a referral for bariatric surgery assessment if you meet all of the following:

- **Age:** 18 years or older
- **BMI:** 35 or above (with related health conditions)
- **Previous attempts:** Previous failed attempts at weight loss
- **Readiness:** You understand what surgery involves, are motivated for long-term lifestyle change and follow-up, and have a support system

### BMI and Associated Conditions

- **BMI 35 to less than 60:** Eligible if you have associated health conditions likely to improve with weight loss
- **BMI 60 to 70:** An individualised approach is taken — your doctor would support you to lose some weight before surgery through intensive medical support (e.g. meal replacements, weight loss medications)
- **BMI above 70:** Not eligible for publicly funded surgery

### Health Conditions That May Improve With Weight Loss

Bariatric surgery is most likely to be considered if you have one or more of:

- Type 2 diabetes
- High blood pressure (hypertension)
- High cholesterol (dyslipidaemia)
- Urological problems, e.g. stress incontinence
- Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GORD)
- Arthritis
- Fatty liver disease (MASLD — metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease)
- Kidney problems, including hyperfiltration
- Obstetric, gynaecological, or fertility problems, e.g. gestational diabetes, preeclampsia, polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)
- Obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA)
- Significant lifestyle limitations (e.g. difficulty participating in family activities, self-esteem)

---

## Who Is Not Eligible

### Absolute Exclusions (surgery cannot proceed)

- Under 18 years of age
- BMI above 70
- Obesity without any associated health conditions
- Alcohol or drug abuse within the last 12 months
- Active psychiatric or neurological conditions — e.g. psychosis, severe neurosis, personality disorder, brain injury
- Active non-trivial cancer diagnosis or treatment
- Severe heart disease — e.g. dilated cardiomyopathy, cardiac failure, unstable ischaemic heart disease
- Severe lung disease causing pulmonary hypertension
- Severe liver disease — established cirrhosis or portal hypertension
- Severe kidney disease — creatinine clearance less than 30 mL/min, or currently on dialysis
- Less than 12 months postpartum

### Relative Exclusions (may still be considered case by case)

- Smoking or vaping (increases surgical risk — discuss with your doctor)
- Limited life expectancy unrelated to obesity
- Previous bariatric surgery (revision surgery)
- HbA1c 70 mmol/mol or higher

---

## Smoking and Vaping

If you smoke or vape, you can still be considered for bariatric surgery — but smoking increases the risks of the procedure and may affect which operation is offered. Your doctor will discuss this with you. If you vape, switching to a nicotine-free product is recommended before surgery.

---

## What Your Doctor Will Check

Before a referral, your doctor will typically assess:

- Height, weight, and blood pressure
- HbA1c (blood sugar control) — within the last 6 months
- Kidney function (creatinine) and urinary protein
- Liver function tests (LFTs)
- Lipid levels (cholesterol)
- Any history of blood clots, clotting disorders, or cancer

---

## Publicly Funded Surgery

The number of publicly funded bariatric surgeries is limited. Meeting the criteria does not guarantee acceptance. Priority is generally given to:

1. People with type 2 diabetes diagnosed less than 5 years ago who are not on insulin
2. People who need weight loss for life-extending treatments

Most people with obesity without associated health conditions will not qualify for publicly funded surgery.
