# Congenital Heart Disease - Genetics

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the genetic considerations for congenital heart disease (CHD) — including recurrence risk for families and when genetic counselling is appropriate.

---

## About Congenital Heart Disease

Congenital heart disease refers to structural heart abnormalities present from birth. The most common defects are:

- Atrial septal defect (ASD) — a hole between the upper chambers of the heart
- Ventricular septal defect (VSD) — a hole between the lower chambers
- Transposition of the great vessels — major arteries connected to the wrong chambers
- Tetralogy of Fallot — a combination of four heart abnormalities

CHD can sometimes be associated with chromosomal or genetic conditions, such as Down syndrome or Turner syndrome. These conditions may also affect growth, development, and intellect, as many genes can be involved.

---

## Recurrence Risk

For parents of a child with isolated CHD (not associated with a syndrome), the recurrence risk in a future pregnancy is low — generally less than 5%. Your doctor will discuss this with you in the context of your specific situation.

If CHD is linked to a chromosomal condition, the recurrence risk depends on the specific condition.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will typically:

- Sketch a 3-generation family tree, noting any history of congenital heart disease, miscarriages, stillbirths, neonatal deaths, or other congenital anomalies and intellectual disability

This family history helps identify whether CHD in your family may have a genetic pattern.

---

## Management

- If CHD is diagnosed before birth (antenatally), your care will be coordinated through a Fetal Medicine Unit. After birth, the Neonatal, Paediatric, and/or Cardiology teams take over.
- Your doctor will discuss the recurrence risk for future pregnancies.
- If you have a personal or family history of CHD and want to better understand the recurrence risk, your doctor can refer you to a genetic health service for counselling.

---

## When Genetic Counselling Is Recommended

Genetic counselling is appropriate if:

- You or a close family member has been diagnosed with CHD, and
- You want to understand the risk for future pregnancies

Your doctor will include the 3-generation family tree in any referral to the genetic health service.
