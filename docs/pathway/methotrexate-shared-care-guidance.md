# Methotrexate Shared Care

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Methotrexate is an anti-metabolite drug with anti-inflammatory effects. It is used for conditions including rheumatoid arthritis, psoriasis, and inflammatory bowel disease. It is always started by a specialist, and ongoing monitoring is shared with your GP.

---

## How It Is Taken

Methotrexate is taken once a week, on the same day every week:

- **Oral tablets:** 2.5 to 25 mg once a week
- **Subcutaneous injection:** 10 to 25 mg once a week

Improvements in your condition may take 2 to 6 months.

---

## Folic Acid — Always Take With Methotrexate

Folic acid must always be prescribed alongside methotrexate:

- 5 mg once a week, taken 2 to 4 days after your methotrexate dose
- Can be increased to 5 mg taken 2 to 6 times per week if you develop mouth ulcers

Folic acid helps reduce side effects. Do not stop it without talking to your doctor.

---

## Blood Test Monitoring

Your doctor will check your blood tests regularly. The schedule depends on what condition is being treated:

**For rheumatology (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis) and gastroenterology (e.g., Crohn's disease):**
- Once a month for the first 3 months
- Then every 3 months if results are normal

**For dermatology (e.g., psoriasis):**
- Once a week for the first 4 weeks
- Then once a month for 3 months
- Then every 3 months

Tests checked include: FBC (full blood count), albumin, creatinine, bilirubin, GGT, ALP, AST, ALT.

For psoriasis patients: a P3NP (Procollagen-3 N-Terminal Peptide) blood test is done every 6 months to check for liver fibrosis risk. If elevated, a Fibroscan may be arranged.

Your doctor will also review your overall condition every 3 months.

---

## When to Stop the Drug and Seek Urgent Help

**Stop methotrexate and contact your doctor or seek urgent care if you develop:**

- Low neutrophils (neutropenia: neutrophils < 1)
- Low platelets (thrombocytopenia: platelets < 50)
- Significantly elevated liver enzymes (AST or ALT more than twice the upper limit of normal)
- Pneumonitis — inflammation of the lungs
- Unexplained shortness of breath or persistent cough

These are serious warning signs. Do not wait for your next scheduled appointment.

## Side Effects and What to Do

| Area | Side Effects | What Your Doctor May Do |
|------|-------------|------------------------|
| **General** | Mouth ulcers, fatigue, malaise, fever, dizziness | Increase folic acid dose for mouth ulcers; consider reducing methotrexate dose if disease is controlled |
| **Gut** | Nausea, abdominal discomfort | Split the dose (12–24 hours apart), take after food, or change to injection route |
| **Liver** | Mildly raised ALT/AST, reduced albumin | Monitor closely; stop drug if levels continue to rise |
| **Blood** | Low platelets or neutrophils (uncommon) | Stop drug immediately; specialist review |
| **Lungs** | Pneumonitis, unexplained breathlessness or cough | Stop drug immediately; specialist review |
| **Skin** | Rashes, itching | Stop drug; specialist review |

For a full side effect list, see [NZF methotrexate](https://nzf.org.nz/nzf_4548) or [Medsafe](https://medsafe.govt.nz/Medicines/infoSearch.asp?txtMedicine=methotrexate).

## Drug Interactions

Tell your doctor or pharmacist about all medicines you take. Important interactions include:

- **NSAIDs** (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen) — avoid if you have significant kidney problems or inflammatory bowel disease
- **Penicillin antibiotics** — can increase methotrexate toxicity; more frequent blood monitoring may be needed
- **Trimethoprim or co-trimoxazole** (common antibiotics) — significant interaction; discuss with your doctor before taking
- **High-dose aspirin**
- **Probenecid** (used for gout)
- **Hepatotoxic drugs** — drugs that affect the liver (e.g., azathioprine, leflunomide, retinoids, sulfasalazine) — if these are necessary, more frequent monitoring is needed

If you are unsure about a drug interaction, ask your pharmacist or GP before starting any new medicine.

---

## Pregnancy

**Methotrexate is absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy (Category X).**

- It carries a high risk of causing permanent damage to the fetus.
- If you are female and could become pregnant, you must use reliable contraception while on methotrexate.
- Avoid pregnancy for **at least 3 months after stopping** the drug. Your specialist may recommend a longer delay depending on your situation.
- Recent evidence suggests it is safe for **men** to father children while taking methotrexate.
- If you are considering pregnancy or discover you are pregnant while on methotrexate, seek urgent specialist advice immediately.

---

## Vaccinations

- **Annual flu vaccine:** Recommended and safe.
- **Pneumococcal vaccine:** Recommended (funding depends on your diagnosis).
- **Shingles vaccine (Shingrix):** May be recommended — discuss with your doctor. The older Zostavax vaccine is a live vaccine and is **absolutely contraindicated** in patients on methotrexate, especially if also using steroids or biologics.
- **COVID vaccines:** Seek specialist advice first. You may need to pause methotrexate for 1–2 weeks after vaccination depending on your circumstances.
- **Live vaccines:** Must be avoided during treatment and for at least 6 months after stopping. This includes: MMR, chickenpox (varicella), Zostavax, BCG, oral polio, oral typhoid, oral rotavirus, and yellow fever.

Note: Methotrexate may reduce the effectiveness of some vaccines.

---

## Lifestyle

- **Alcohol:** Keep consumption to less than 2 units per day. Less than 14 units per week is unlikely to affect liver function test results, but your doctor will assess this individually.
- **Sun exposure:** Methotrexate can increase sun sensitivity — use sunscreen.
