# Weight-related Concerns in Adults

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers assessment and management of weight-related concerns in adults, including overweight, obesity, and unintentional weight change.

---

## Red Flags — Discuss with Your Doctor Promptly

Seek prompt medical review if you experience:

- Unintentional weight change (gaining or losing more than 10% of body weight in 6 months without changing diet or activity)

---

## Background

Excess weight is common and is associated with increased risk of:

- High blood pressure
- High LDL cholesterol, low HDL cholesterol, or high triglycerides
- Type 2 diabetes
- Coronary heart disease and stroke
- Gallbladder disease
- Osteoarthritis
- Sleep apnoea and breathing problems
- Many cancers
- Depression and anxiety
- Reduced quality of life

Achieving a healthy weight underpins the prevention and management of many long-term conditions. Psychological readiness and addressing barriers to change are important parts of any weight management plan.

---

## Assessment

Your healthcare provider will typically:

1. **Ask permission** before discussing your body weight, especially if this was not the primary reason for your visit.

2. **Take a history**, including:
   - Diet and eating habits (types of food, meal patterns, speed of eating, emotional eating, budget constraints)
   - Physical activity (type, frequency, duration — including work, recreational, and incidental activity)
   - Sleep quality and any sleep disorders
   - Smoking status and alcohol use
   - Mental well-being
   - Physical injuries or disabilities affecting weight
   - Underlying medical conditions that may contribute to weight change (e.g., thyroid disease, depression, PCOS, sleep apnoea, COPD, eating disorders)
   - Medications — some can promote weight gain (e.g., insulin, sulfonylureas, antipsychotics, antidepressants such as mirtazapine or amitriptyline, beta blockers, steroids, contraceptives) and others may reduce appetite or weight (e.g., empagliflozin)
   - Psychological factors (e.g., binge eating, poor body image, emotional eating)

3. **Examine you**, including:
   - Height and weight to calculate **BMI** (weight in kg ÷ height in m²):
     - Less than 18.5 = underweight
     - 18.5–24.9 = healthy weight
     - 25–29.9 = overweight
     - 30 or more = obese (moderate ≥35, severe ≥40)
   - Note: BMI has limitations — it may overestimate body fat in muscular individuals and underestimate it in older adults. It is used as a screening tool alongside other measures, not as a sole indicator of health.
   - **Waist circumference** (measured at the midpoint between rib and iliac crest):

     | Population | Men — increased risk | Men — greatly increased risk | Women — increased risk | Women — greatly increased risk |
     |---|---|---|---|---|
     | European | ≥94 cm | ≥102 cm | ≥80 cm | ≥88 cm |
     | South Asian (incl. Chinese, Malay, Asian-Indian) | ≥90 cm | — | ≥80 cm | — |
     | Māori and Pacific Islander | ≥102 cm | — | ≥88 cm | — |

4. **Arrange investigations**, which may include:
   - Blood tests: lipids, HbA1c, creatinine/eGFR, FBC, thyroid function, iron/B12/folate, calcium, liver function
   - Cardiovascular disease risk assessment and diabetes screening (timing depends on age, ethnicity, and risk factors)
   - Chest X-ray if unexplained weight loss and loss of appetite have persisted for more than 3 weeks, or other criteria are met
   - CT chest/abdomen/pelvis if unintentional weight loss and clinical criteria are met

5. **Assess readiness to change** — your provider may use tools such as the Readiness Ruler to understand your motivation and confidence around lifestyle changes.

## Management

### For Everyone

Your healthcare provider may recommend:

- Treating any underlying medical conditions and addressing nutritional deficiencies
- Discussing lifestyle factors such as alcohol, smoking, sleep, and mental well-being
- Advice on healthy eating and regular physical activity
- Setting goals across three areas: food and drink, physical activity, and behaviour support
- Involving family or support people in your plan

**Healthy eating principles** include:
- Plenty of vegetables (especially non-starchy) and fruit
- Mostly wholegrain grain foods
- Some low-fat dairy, legumes, nuts, seeds, fish, eggs, and lean meat
- Minimise processed foods, added sugar, and excess salt
- Choose water as your main drink

For most people, losing 5–10% of body weight (e.g., 5–10 kg if you weigh 100 kg) produces meaningful health benefits. A realistic target is 0.5–1 kg per week.

### If You Are Underweight (BMI < 18.5) or Have Unintentional Weight Loss

Your provider will investigate for underlying causes (e.g., thyroid disease, COPD, eating disorder, malignancy, PCOS, sleep apnoea, Cushing syndrome).

A **Food First approach** may be recommended:
- Three small regular meals with nutritious snacks in between
- High-energy foods: milkshakes, butter, oil, avocado, cream, cheese, honey
- Milky drinks (coffee, hot chocolate, smoothies) instead of water, tea, or plain coffee
- Over-the-counter nutrition supplement drinks (e.g., Complan)

If there is no improvement after 4 weeks, your provider may recommend oral nutrition supplements or referral to a dietitian.

### If You Are Overweight or Obese

Your healthcare provider may recommend:

- Lifestyle changes as first-line management (diet, exercise, behaviour support)
- Referral to a dietitian, health psychologist, or a structured exercise programme (e.g., Green Prescription)
- Review of any medications contributing to weight gain

**Anti-obesity medication** may be considered if:
- Lifestyle changes over at least 6 months have not produced significant benefit, and
- Your BMI is ≥30

Only one weight loss medication should be used at a time. Options your doctor may discuss include:

| Medication | Type | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide (Wegovy) | GLP-1 receptor agonist | Reduces appetite, increases satiety (subcutaneous injection) |
| Tirzepatide (Mounjaro) | GLP-1/GIP receptor agonist | Reduces appetite, increases satiety (subcutaneous injection) |
| Liraglutide (Saxenda) | GLP-1 receptor agonist | Reduces appetite (subcutaneous injection) |
| Naltrexone + bupropion (Contrave) | Combination oral tablet | Suppresses appetite |
| Orlistat (Xenical) | Oral tablet | Reduces absorption of dietary fat |
| Phentermine (Duromine) | Oral tablet | Suppresses appetite |

**Semaglutide (Wegovy) and tirzepatide (Mounjaro)** are available as unfunded treatments for adults aged ≥18 years with:
- BMI above 30 kg/m², or
- BMI above 27 kg/m² with at least one weight-related comorbidity

Your provider will review response to medication monthly for the first 3 months, and continue only if it is well tolerated and has produced at least 5% weight loss.

**Bariatric surgery** may be considered in severe or refractory cases. Your doctor can discuss whether you meet criteria and whether a referral is appropriate.

---

## Follow-up

If your BMI is less than 18.5, greater than 25, or you have had unintentional weight change, your provider will typically follow up within 1 month.

At follow-up, your provider will:
- Review progress against goals
- Identify and address any barriers
- Adjust medications if needed (particularly antihypertensives and antidiabetic medications if weight loss has occurred)
- Consider additional support if weight goals have not been met
