# Abortion for Fetal Anomalies or Genetic Disorders

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the clinical approach to abortion (previously called termination) following fetal anomalies or genetic disorders — including when specialist input is needed, what happens after the procedure, and how your GP supports you through this process.

---

## When Specialist Assessment Is Needed

For any fetal anomaly or abnormal antenatal screening result, specialist Maternal Fetal Medicine assessment is arranged. This applies to:

- Possible fetal anomalies identified on ultrasound
- Abnormal antenatal screening results
- Abnormal chorionic villus sampling (CVS) or amniocentesis results

If not already under specialist care, your lead maternity carer (LMC) or doctor will discuss options including:
- Urgent obstetric assessment
- Genetic counselling
- Abortion

---

## After the Abortion

Abortion for fetal anomalies or genetic disorders is managed by specialist teams. After the procedure:

- Counselling sessions are offered for grief and emotional support
- A specialist review may occur 8 to 12 weeks after the abortion if needed
- A letter is sent to your GP with investigation findings, conclusions, and any guidance for future pregnancies

---

## Your GP's Role After the Abortion

Your doctor will:

- Review the specialist letter and ensure a specific diagnosis was made if possible
- Record details in your medical notes, including any recommendations for future pregnancies
- Assess and discuss the grieving process with you
- Offer access to bereavement support (e.g., [Sands New Zealand](https://www.sands.org.nz/) for families after pregnancy loss)
- Screen for postnatal depression and anxiety — these are recognised risks after pregnancy loss
- Discuss ongoing counselling if needed
- Undertake post-abortion follow-up, monitoring for:
  - Infection
  - Retained products of conception (RPOC)
  - Ongoing contraception needs
  - Planning and timing of a future pregnancy
  - Pre-pregnancy counselling referral if appropriate

If there is a previous abortion for a fetal anomaly without follow-up, your doctor may take a detailed family history. This includes asking about miscarriages, neonatal deaths, congenital malformations, and intellectual disability in the family — and may involve sketching a three-generation family tree to guide future planning.

---

## Genetic Counselling

Genetic counselling may be arranged if:

- It has been more than 4 months since the abortion and specialist follow-up has not occurred
- There is a history of abortion for a fetal anomaly, genetic disorder, or a relevant family history

Genetic counselling helps understand the cause of the anomaly, the likelihood of recurrence, and options for future pregnancies.
