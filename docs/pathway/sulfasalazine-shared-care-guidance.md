# Sulfasalazine Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This guidance supports ongoing management of sulfasalazine after specialist initiation. It covers monitoring, red flags, adverse effects, and patient information.

---

## Background

Sulfasalazine is a mild immunomodulator used for several inflammatory conditions. It is usually started by a specialist, who will have checked baseline blood tests and excluded sulphur allergy before you begin.

**Conditions it is used for:**
- Ulcerative colitis
- Crohn's disease
- Inflammatory arthritis
- Inflammatory eye disease

**It should not be used in:**
- Allergy to sulphur-based drugs
- Intestinal or urinary obstruction
- Porphyria
- Children younger than 2 years

---

## Dosing

A common starting regimen is:

- Week 1: 500 mg twice daily
- Week 2: 500 mg in the morning, 1 g at night
- Week 3: 1 g twice daily

Occasionally higher doses (e.g. 1.5 g twice daily) are used.

Take with food. Response is usually seen within 6 to 16 weeks. If there is no response after 16 weeks, your doctor may consider a change in therapy.

---

## Red Flags — Stop Drug and Seek Urgent Specialist Advice

Contact your specialist urgently if any of the following occur:

- Neutropenia (very low white blood cells)
- Thrombocytopenia (very low platelets)
- Haemolysis (red blood cell breakdown)
- Liver function tests (LFT) more than twice the upper limit of normal
- Rash

---

## Monitoring

Your doctor will arrange regular blood tests to watch for side effects, which most commonly occur in the first 3 months:

- FBC, AST, ALT, and creatinine **monthly for 3 months**, then **every 3 months**
- Report any new symptoms promptly — your doctor will test if concerned

Sulfasalazine is not generally classified as an immunosuppressant, so standard cervical screening intervals remain appropriate.

---

## Adverse Effects

| System | Adverse Effect | What Your Doctor May Consider |
|--------|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Blood | Neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, haemolysis | If mild — monitor. If moderate to severe — stop drug and contact specialist. |
| Liver | Elevated LFTs | If mild (less than 2× upper limit) — observe. If severe (more than 2×) — stop drug and seek urgent specialist advice. |
| Mental health | Depression | Observe. If severe — consult specialist. |
| Gut | Nausea, abdominal discomfort, dyspepsia | Your doctor may consider reducing the dose. |
| Skin | Maculopapular rash, itching, severe skin reactions | If rash — stop drug and consult specialist. If itching — check liver function. If severe skin reaction — stop drug urgently. |
| Lung | Pneumonitis | Stop drug, consult specialist. |
| Fertility | Reversible reduced sperm count and motility | Continue drug. Reverses within 2–3 months of stopping. |
| Nutrition | Folic acid deficiency | Your doctor may consider folic acid supplementation, especially in pregnancy. |
| Immune | Serum sickness | Stop drug, seek urgent specialist advice. |

---

## Drug Interactions

Your doctor will review for interactions. Key ones include:

- **Penicillins** — reduced sulfasalazine absorption
- **Digoxin** — reduced digoxin absorption; your doctor may check digoxin levels
- **Azathioprine** — increased risk of bone marrow suppression; your doctor may consider more frequent FBC monitoring

For a full list, discuss with your pharmacist or prescribing doctor.

---

## Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

- **Pregnancy:** Sulfasalazine is used in pregnancy only when the benefit outweighs the risks. Folic acid supplementation is recommended. Discuss with your doctor.
- **Breastfeeding:** Use with caution. May cause diarrhoea in the infant. Discuss with your doctor.

---

*Always discuss changes to your medication with your doctor before making any adjustments.*
