# Catheter Change or Trial of Void

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers catheter removal and subsequent management after insertion for acute urinary retention, and catheter removal before reinsertion of a long-term catheter.

---

## Before Removal — Timing Considerations

Your doctor will consider the following before proceeding:

- **Do not attempt trial of void** if the catheter was placed for urinary retention with renal failure (obstructive uropathy).
- **After acute retention with symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH):** Your doctor may consider starting an alpha-1-blocker and arranging catheter removal after 2 to 3 days.
- **After retention triggered by an acute event** (e.g., constipation, illness, surgery): catheter is typically removed after 3 to 5 days, or once recovered from the event.
- **After trauma or difficult catheterisation:** catheter may remain in place for up to 6 weeks.
- **Long-term catheters:** first change is planned at 6 weeks; ongoing changes depend on individual needs — catheters may remain in place up to 3 months.
- **Chronic urinary retention or planned TURP (prostate surgery):** catheter stays in until seen by urology.

Your doctor will schedule removal early in the morning so that reinsertion, if needed, can be done within normal working hours.

---

## Removal Procedure

Your doctor will:

1. Attach a syringe to the inflation lumen to deflate the balloon (the syringe self-fills or may need gentle aspiration — continue until the balloon is fully empty).
2. Ask you to relax and, on an outward breath, gently slide the catheter out.

**If the balloon is difficult to deflate**, your doctor may try gentle traction, slightly re-inflating the balloon, or rotating the catheter. If the catheter cannot be removed, emergency department assessment is needed.

---

## After Removal — Trial of Void

After the catheter is removed, you will be sent home with the following advice:

- Continue normal activities and drink slightly more fluids than usual.
- You should be passing urine normally within **3 to 5 hours**.

Your doctor will arrange a review by phone or in person after 4 to 5 hours.

### If urination is successful

- Your doctor will ask whether the first void was symptom-free with good flow.
- A post-void residual bladder volume of less than 200 mL is generally satisfactory.
- You will be advised to attend after-hours care or the emergency department if flow problems develop overnight.
- A follow-up appointment in 3 to 5 days will be arranged to check progress and consider ongoing management of BPH or other causes.

### If urination is unsuccessful

- Your doctor will assess the bladder and check for discomfort.
- The catheter will need to be reinserted. Your doctor will record the residual urine volume and whether symptoms resolved.
- Contributing factors such as constipation will be considered.
- If reinsertion is not possible, emergency department assessment is needed.

---

## After Failed Trial of Void

If urination was unsuccessful, your doctor will arrange a non-acute urology assessment. While waiting, your doctor may consider starting treatment for BPH if appropriate.

---

## After Procedure — Planned Catheter Reinsertion

If the catheter is being changed rather than removed for a trial of void, your doctor will arrange catheter supplies and ongoing care.

---

## Alpha-1-Blockers (for BPH-related retention)

Your doctor may consider an alpha-1-blocker to relax smooth muscle and improve urinary flow:

- **Doxazosin** — typically started at a low dose (2 mg at night), then gradually increased over several weeks up to 8 mg at night. Dose titration is slower in older patients or those with low blood pressure.
- **Tamsulosin** — a selective alpha-1A-blocker with less risk of postural (positional) low blood pressure. May be used if doxazosin is not tolerated or contraindicated.

**Important:** Alpha-1-blockers can cause postural hypotension (dizziness on standing), especially if you are already taking blood pressure medication. Your doctor may need to adjust your other medications.

---

## When to Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency department or call for emergency assessment if:

- The catheter cannot be removed.
- The catheter cannot be reinserted after a failed trial of void.

---

## When Your Doctor Will Seek Specialist Input

Your doctor may discuss your case with urology if:

- It is unclear when to trial catheter removal.
- It is unclear whether to start BPH treatment while waiting for an outpatient appointment.
- The trial of void has failed (non-acute urology referral).

---

*Always discuss your specific situation with your doctor. This information is a general guide and may not apply to every individual.*
