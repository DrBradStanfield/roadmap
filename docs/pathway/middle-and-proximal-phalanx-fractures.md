# Middle and Proximal Phalanx Fractures

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of fractures involving the middle and proximal phalanges (finger bones).

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess for the history and signs of a fracture, including:

- Which hand is dominant
- Checking the fingers for a **rotational deformity** — your doctor will ask you to flex your fingers as fully as possible (even though it may be painful) to observe the nail plates for alignment and compare with the other hand, looking for any malalignment of the flexed fingers

An **X-ray of the hand** is arranged to confirm the fracture and its position.

---

## Management

Your doctor will manage the fracture based on its type and position.

### Phalangeal Shaft Fractures

**If the fracture is undisplaced or in an acceptable position** (no significant loss of length, no rotational deformity, and less than 10 degrees of angulation):

- Your doctor may consider a buddy strap (taping the injured finger to the adjacent finger) or a Zimmer splint for up to 4 weeks
- If there is sagittal displacement (displaced on the side-on X-ray), a Zimmer splint for up to 4 weeks may be used
- If there is coronal displacement or rotation (displaced on the front-on X-ray), your doctor may consider a buddy strap with or without a Zimmer splint
- Hand therapy is usually recommended

**If the fracture is displaced** (angulation greater than 10 degrees), shortened, rotated, or unstable:

- Your doctor will be aware that circulation to the finger can be compromised in these cases
- Referral for acute hand surgery assessment is usually required

**Displaced greenstick fracture at the base of the proximal phalanx** (often the little finger):

- Your doctor may consider a digital nerve block and/or pain relief such as **entonox** (nitrous oxide)
- The fracture may be reduced by placing a pen between the fractured finger and the neighbouring finger as a fulcrum
- A buddy strap is applied and a second X-ray arranged

**Multiple fractures:** Referral for acute hand surgery assessment is usually required.

**If you have been in a splint for more than 1 week,** hand therapy or physiotherapy is often beneficial.

---

### Intra-Articular Fractures (Interphalangeal Joint)

**Condylar fracture or collateral ligament avulsion:**

- If displaced: your doctor will refer for acute hand surgery assessment for possible surgery
- If undisplaced: your doctor may consider a buddy strap and arrange hand therapy

**Avulsion fracture of the dorsal base of the middle phalanx** (central slip avulsion — risk of boutonniere deformity):

- If displaced: your doctor will refer for acute hand surgery assessment for possible surgery
- If undisplaced: your doctor may splint the finger in extension for 4 weeks with a follow-up X-ray at 1 week, plus hand therapy or physiotherapy

**Avulsion fracture of the volar (palm-side) base of the middle phalanx involving more than 20% of the joint area** (unstable): your doctor will refer for acute hand surgery assessment to consider surgery.

---

## Follow-Up

Your doctor will usually review after 1 week and arrange hand therapy or physiotherapy if needed — this is often particularly beneficial if you have been in a splint.
