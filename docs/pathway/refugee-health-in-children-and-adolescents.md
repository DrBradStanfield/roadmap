# Refugee Health in Children and Adolescents

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of children and adolescents who have had refugee-like experiences, following a refugee health screening.

---

## Background

Children and adolescents with refugee-like experiences:

- Have experienced displacement and changes in country, language, culture, and education.
- May not have had access to health services or education.
- May have experienced trauma and human rights violations.
- May have grief around loss of family members left behind.
- May be reluctant to discuss the complex family dynamics they are experiencing.

History, especially trauma history, may only emerge with time as trust and rapport develop.

---

## Assessment

### Preparing for the Appointment

Your doctor will usually arrange an extended appointment or a series of appointments to address all needs. If an interpreter is required:

- Family members and friends should not be used as interpreters.
- The patient's language, dialect, and preference for the interpreter's gender and ethnicity are checked.
- Consent obtained without an appropriate interpreter may be legally invalid.

Cultural expectations around family involvement will be considered, with space made for private conversation where needed.

### Syndrome-Based Approach

Refugee-background children have typical paediatric problems, but may also have issues specific to their background. Your doctor will consider the following:

| Presentation | Common causes | Additional considerations in refugee-background children |
|---|---|---|
| Fever | Viral infections, bacterial infections | Malaria, measles, typhoid fever, dysentery, dengue and arboviral infections, hepatitis A, hepatitis B |
| Respiratory symptoms | Viral RTI (including COVID-19 and influenza), pneumonia, asthma, bronchiolitis, croup | Pertussis (vaccination may not have been available), TB (consider in children with chronic cough or contact history), parasite infections may rarely cause wheeze, sickle cell disease may present with acute chest syndrome |
| Abdominal pain | Acute infection, constipation, surgical or gynaecological causes | Parasite infection, *Helicobacter pylori* gastritis, hepatitis, typhoid fever, female genital mutilation |
| Diarrhoea | Viral or bacterial gastroenteritis, malabsorption | Parasite infection, lactose intolerance (more common in some racial groups), bacillary and amoebic dysentery |
| Hypocalcaemia (tetany, muscle cramps, stridor, seizures) | Vitamin D deficiency | Rickets (bossing, swelling of wrists and ankles, or bony deformity); low calcium more likely in children aged 12 months or younger |
| Skin conditions and rashes | Infective, eczema, dermatophyte (tinea), ulcers | *Strongyloides* infection may cause an intermittent urticarial rash (larva currens), typically on buttocks or perianal area. Untreated *Strongyloides* can cause hyperinfection syndrome (high mortality) if immunosuppressants including steroids are given. Also: measles, leprosy, cutaneous leishmaniasis |
| Continence and UTI | UTI, irritable bladder, enuresis | Chronic UTI may not have been detected or treated. Consider mental health issues as a cause of secondary enuresis. Consider female genital mutilation as a possibility in girls. |
| Eye conditions | Dry eye, conjunctivitis, red eye | Trachoma, onchocerciasis (river blindness) |
| Musculoskeletal pain | Injury, growing pains, joint pathology or inflammation | Vitamin D deficiency is an extremely common cause in refugee children and adolescents |
| Nutrition concerns and fussy eating | Poor intake, increased losses, increased requirements, behavioural issues | Malnutrition, food insecurity, iron deficiency, vitamin B12 deficiency, *Helicobacter pylori* gastritis, dental disease (pain with chewing may restrict intake), rickets restricting linear growth |
| Disability and developmental or learning concerns | Genetic, environmental, trauma–injury | May reflect a combination of antenatal, peri- and post-natal contributors. Be wary of attributing developmental or learning concerns solely to English as an additional language — specialist review should be sought. Consider mental health factors. |
| Mental health | Behaviour concerns, sleep issues, anxiety or separation issues | PTSD, experience of violence or conflict including sexual violence, family separation, parent mental health |

*Adapted with permission from Royal Children's Hospital Melbourne – Immigrant Health: Acute Presentations*

### Other Conditions to Consider

- **Syphilis:** anaemia, rashes, bone and joint problems, seizures, developmental delay.
- **HIV:** lymphadenopathy, recurrent infections, poor growth.

### Oral Health

Your doctor may check for:

- Symptoms: pain, bleeding, halitosis, tooth mobility, missing teeth, reduced oral intake.
- Risk factors for decay: high sugar intake, poor oral hygiene, dry mouth.
- Signs of dental decay, gum disease, oral cancer, or incomplete overseas treatments.

### Tuberculosis Screening

If the child has not been screened within 6 months of arriving, age-appropriate TB screening is arranged:

- Under 2 years — Tuberculin skin test (TST)
- 2 to 10 years — Interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA) / Quantiferon Gold
- 11 to 15 years — IGRA / Quantiferon Gold and chest X-ray
- 16 years or older (not pregnant) — Chest X-ray
- If pregnant — defer chest X-ray until after delivery

### Physical Examination

A full physical examination typically includes:

- Growth (head circumference, height and weight, nutrition status)
- Checking for pallor, rickets, lymph nodes, skin changes (including rash, BCG scar, or other scars)
- ENT and dental exam
- Respiratory, cardiovascular, abdominal, and neurological examinations

## Management

### Infectious Diseases

#### Worms and Intestinal Parasites

Your doctor may consider the following treatments:

**Threadworm / pinworm (*Enterobius vermicularis*):**
- Children aged 6 months and older: mebendazole 100 mg as a single dose, repeated in 2 weeks.

**Roundworm (*Ascaris lumbricoides*), whipworm (*Trichuris trichiura*):**
- Children aged 12 months and older: mebendazole 100 mg twice daily for 3 days.
- Children aged 6 to 11 months: albendazole (requires specialist authority).

**Hookworm (*Necator americanus*):**
- Children aged 6 months and older: albendazole (requires specialist authority). Best taken on an empty stomach for hookworm.

**Tapeworm:**
- *Taenia saginata* (aged 4 years or older, not pregnant): praziquantel 10 mg/kg as a single dose, taken with food.
- *Taenia solium*: your doctor will assess for risk of cysticercosis and seek specialist input, as treatment can rarely precipitate life-threatening cerebral oedema.

**Dwarf tapeworm (*Hymenolepis nana*):**
- Children aged 4 years and older: praziquantel 25 mg/kg as a single dose, taken with food.

***Entamoeba histolytica*:**
- Children aged 1 month and older: metronidazole 40 mg/kg in 3 divided doses for 7 to 10 days, followed by paromomycin for intraluminal elimination (requires specialist authority).

**Non-pathogenic parasites that do not require treatment include:**
- *Entamoeba coli*, *Entamoeba hartmanii*, *Entamoeba gingivalis*, *Endolimax nana*, *Iodamoeba butschlii*, *Dientamoeba fragilis*, *Blastocystis hominis*, *Chilomastix mesnili*, *Trichomonas hominis*

***Giardia*:**
- Ornidazole or metronidazole. If under 35 kg: ornidazole 125 mg per 3 kg as single dose; if 35 kg or more: ornidazole 1.5 g single dose. Alternative: metronidazole for children at 30 mg/kg/dose (max 2 g) once a day for 3 days.
- After treatment, the child may remain infectious for several months.
- Avoid swimming in pools until at least 2 weeks after symptoms resolve.
- If symptoms persist, especially diarrhoea, temporarily avoiding lactose may help as secondary lactose intolerance often follows giardiasis.

#### Schistosomiasis (positive or equivocal serology)

Your doctor will arrange stool microscopy for ova and may treat with:
- Children aged 4 years and older: praziquantel 40 mg/kg in two divided doses for 1 day, taken with food.

#### *Strongyloides* (positive or equivocal serology)

Your doctor will arrange stool microscopy and check whether the patient is from a *Loa loa*-endemic region. Treatment (for children weighing 15 kg or more, not pregnant):
- Ivermectin 0.2 mg/kg once a day for 2 days, taken on an empty stomach.

> **Important:** Untreated *Strongyloides* can cause life-threatening hyperinfection syndrome if immunosuppressants (including steroids) are given. This must be excluded before immunosuppression.

#### Malaria

Suspected malaria in a child is managed with specialist paediatric input.

#### Tuberculosis (TB)

- If TB screening is abnormal, specialist paediatric advice is sought.
- If acute TB is suspected, this is a notifiable disease and management follows the TB pathway without waiting for investigations.

#### Blood-Borne Viruses and Sexually Transmitted Infections

Children found to have the following will be managed with specialist paediatric advice:

- Chronic hepatitis B
- Chronic hepatitis C
- HIV
- Chlamydia
- Gonorrhoea
- Syphilis

#### Skin Infections

**Tinea (excluding *Tinea capitis*):**
- Topical treatment with imidazoles (e.g., clotrimazole, ketoconazole, miconazole) or terbinafine.

***Tinea capitis*:**
- At least 4 weeks of systemic medication such as terbinafine, itraconazole, or fluconazole.

**Scabies and leprosy (Hansen's disease):** managed according to relevant clinical pathways.

#### Eye Infections

**Trachoma** (infection caused by *Chlamydia trachomatis*):
- Repeated infection in childhood results in scarring and lid distortion, which can cause the eyelashes to rub on the cornea (trichiasis), leading to vision loss and blindness.
- Your doctor will refer for ophthalmology assessment and may seek advice about treatment with azithromycin.

**Onchocerciasis (river blindness):**
- A progressive eye, skin, and neurological illness spread by black fly (*Simulium*), prevalent in parts of Africa, Central and South America, and the Arabian peninsula.
- Your doctor will seek specialist advice (dermatology for skin disease, ophthalmology for eye disease).

### Non-Communicable Diseases

#### Nutritional Deficiencies

**Iron deficiency:**
- Causes are assessed and iron supplementation may be prescribed. Patient information is available in multiple languages via Health Translations.

**B12 deficiency:**
- *H. pylori* stool testing may be arranged. Management follows the B12 deficiency pathway.

**Vitamin D deficiency:**
- Managed according to the vitamin D deficiency in children pathway.

**Thiamine deficiency:**
- Deficiency occurs in areas with rice-based diets lacking variety (e.g., parts of Myanmar, Thailand, Nepal, Bhutan).
- Breastfed infants of thiamine-deficient mothers are at risk of beriberi and potentially fatal cardiac compromise.
- Your doctor will have a high index of suspicion if a very unwell infant presents with non-specific illness or cardio-respiratory compromise and their mother has a limited rice-based diet.

#### Blood Disorders

**Anaemia:**
- Causes are investigated and treated. Haemoglobin electrophoresis may be arranged to exclude thalassaemia or other haemoglobinopathy after correcting any iron deficiency.

**Thalassaemia:**
- Thalassaemia major: specialist paediatric assessment arranged.
- Thalassaemia trait: parents are advised that the child's future partner should be screened for haemoglobinopathy when they plan a family.

**Sickle cell disease:**
- Sickle cell crisis: urgent specialist paediatric assessment.
- Sickle cell disease: non-urgent specialist paediatric assessment.
- Parents are advised that the child's future partner should be screened for haemoglobinopathy when they plan a family.

**G6PD deficiency:**
- An X-linked recessive condition occurring in people of Middle Eastern, African, and Mediterranean descent.
- Triggers of haemolysis to avoid include fava beans, mothballs (naphthalene), henna dye, and certain medications (nitrofurantoin, sulfonylureas, fluoroquinolones, some antimalarials).
- Testing is done when the patient is not in acute crisis.

**Eosinophilia:**
- Causes investigated, including allergies, intestinal parasites, *Strongyloides*, and schistosomiasis.

**Benign essential (ethnic) neutropenia:**
- Reduced absolute neutrophil count below 1.5 × 10⁹/L occurs normally in 25–50% of persons of African descent and some from the Middle East. This is not pathological but reflects a different normal range.

#### Dental Issues

- Dental pain is managed with appropriate care.
- All patients under 18 years of age are eligible for free dental care.

#### Hearing and Vision Issues

- If hearing concerns are present, cause is assessed and hearing testing may be arranged.
- If vision concerns are present, eye problems are assessed and visual testing may be arranged.

---

### Pre-Existing Injuries

**Traumatic brain injury:**
- Patients injured before arrival in New Zealand are not eligible for ACC.
- Specialist paediatric assessment may be arranged if required.

**Limb amputations:**
- Patients injured before arrival in New Zealand are not eligible for ACC.
- Prosthetic assessment can be requested via the Peke Waihanga referral pathway (artificial limb service).

**Burn scars:**
- Your doctor may advise gentle stretching and massage of the scar area and prescribe moisturisers.
- Sunscreen use is recommended.
- Occupational therapy referral may be considered if scars are hypertrophic or develop a contracture.

**Other scars:**
- Gentle stretching, massage, moisturisers, and sunscreen are advised.
- If a scar is causing significant pain, deformity, or psychosocial distress, specialist plastic surgery advice may be sought.

---

### Disabilities

Specialist paediatric assessment may be arranged. Needs Assessment and Service Coordination (NASC) may also be considered, along with appropriate community support services such as Blind Low Vision NZ or IHC New Zealand.

---

### Immunisation

If the child is not up to date with vaccinations, a catch-up immunisation schedule will be arranged.

---

### Medication Understanding

Your doctor will check literacy and numeracy to ensure the patient or caregiver understands how to take any prescribed medications. Diagrams or translated instructions may be used where needed.

---

### Mental Health and Well-Being

If mental health or well-being concerns are identified, your doctor may arrange appropriate support through mental health and refugee-specialist services in your region.
