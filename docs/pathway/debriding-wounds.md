# Debriding Wounds

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Debridement is the process of creating a clean wound bed by removing infected, dead, or non-viable tissue to support healing.

---

## Background

Debridement removes:

- Infected or contaminated tissue
- Cell debris
- Dead or devitalised fibrous material, classified as **eschar** or **slough**

**Slough** typically presents as a soft, fibrous yellow layer — usually with high exudate and a locally inflamed border.

**Eschar** typically presents as black, dry, and desiccated — there may be a locally inflamed border.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor or nurse will:

1. Exclude ischaemia before proceeding
2. Consider:
   - The treatment aim (conservative vs curative)
   - The timeline required
   - Risk of haemorrhage
   - Whether pain can be appropriately managed
   - Whether the environment is suitable
3. Select the most appropriate type of debridement based on wound characteristics

### Types of Debridement

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Autolytic** | Uses the body's natural ability to remove non-viable tissue. Wound products provide a moist environment to assist removal of slough and dead tissue. |
| **Enzymatic** | A topical enzyme assists in liquefying slough. |
| **Mechanical** | A pad gently rubbed over the wound to remove loose slough and disrupt biofilm. Requires a debridement sponge or filament pad. |
| **Conservative sharp** | A minor surgical procedure using a scalpel or curette, performed by a clinician with appropriate education and skills. |
| **Surgical** | Performed in theatre under general anaesthetic. |

---

## Management

### 1. Autolytic Debridement

**For dry eschar or dry wounds with low exudate** — your doctor or nurse may use products that hydrate the eschar:

| Product type | Examples | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acrylics | Tegaderm acrylic absorbent clear dressing | Provides a moist, occlusive environment; absorbs low levels of exudate. Not for highly exudating wounds. |
| Hydrocolloids | DuoDerm, Tegaderm hydrocolloid, Comfeel, Hydrocoll | Provides a moist, occlusive environment; rehydrates wound and assists autolytic debridement. Not for highly exudating wounds. |
| Hydrogels | Solosite, Intrasite, DuoDerm hydroactive gel, Hydrosorb gel | Donates moisture to rehydrate the wound. Avoid on moderately or highly exudating wounds. |

**For moist necrosis, slough, or highly exudating wounds** — products that absorb exudate are used instead.

**If wound infection is suspected** — an antimicrobial cleanser and dressing will be used.

### 2. Enzymatic Debridement

If necrotic tissue is moist, your doctor or nurse may consider an enzymatic product (e.g., Hyalo Start) to help liquefy slough and necrosis.

### 3. Mechanical Debridement

- If some loose slough is present, it may be gently removed with moistened gauze
- If debris is present, debridement sponges, pads, or filament pads may be used to gently lift debris and disrupt biofilm

### 4. Conservative Sharp Debridement

Your doctor or nurse may use a scalpel or curette at the bedside. Local anaesthetic is typically considered. This is only performed by clinicians who are competent and confident to do so.

### 5. Surgical Debridement

For wounds requiring surgical debridement, your doctor will arrange a plastic surgery assessment.

---

*Always discuss wound care management with your doctor or nurse — the appropriate debridement method depends on your wound type, overall health, and treatment goals.*
