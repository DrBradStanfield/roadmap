# Pregnancy and Postnatal Care in a COVID-19 Patient

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the management of pregnancy and postnatal care when a patient has COVID-19 — including risk stratification, escalation triggers, and monitoring priorities.

---

## Background

Pregnancy combined with COVID-19 infection increases the risk of adverse outcomes for both mother and baby:

- Pregnant patients may be slow to recognise COVID-19 symptoms because fatigue, shortness of breath, and nausea can be mistaken for normal pregnancy symptoms.
- Pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state. COVID-19 can cause additional micro-thrombi, increasing the risk of placental insufficiency and venous thromboembolism (VTE), which may lead to fetal growth restriction (FGR), small for gestational age (SGA), and preterm birth.
- The Omicron variant is associated with adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes, particularly in unvaccinated pregnant women.
- Vaccination significantly reduces the risk: those who have received two doses and a booster are approximately 88% less likely to be hospitalised with the Omicron variant compared to those who are unvaccinated.
- COVID-19 in pregnancy is not reported to cause an increase in congenital anomalies. Vertical transmission is uncommon. No infant has been found to be infected through the breast milk of COVID-19-positive mothers.

## Red Flags — Escalation Triggers Requiring Urgent Assessment

Your doctor should be contacted urgently, or acute obstetric assessment sought, if any of the following are present:

### Obstetric-Related Features

- Signs of pre-eclampsia: blood pressure over 140/90, headache, abdominal pain, visual disturbance, or widespread swelling (oedema)
- Reduced fetal movements
- Signs of preterm labour: abdominal pain that is unresolved or increasing, regular or rhythmic, recurring back pain, or a sense of weight or bulging in the vagina
- Pre-labour or preterm rupture of membranes, or query of leaking fluid
- Vaginal bleeding or severe lower abdominal pain

### COVID-19-Related — Respiratory Features

- Oxygen saturation less than 94%
- Increasing shortness of breath
- Talking in single words or short sentences
- Pausing between sentences to catch breath
- Respiratory rate over 24 breaths per minute
- Profound exhaustion (where a pulse oximeter is not available to exclude silent hypoxia)
- Coughing up blood (haemoptysis)

### COVID-19-Related — Non-Respiratory Features

- Rapid deterioration of any sign or symptom
- Fever greater than 38°C
- New onset of confusion or drowsiness
- Unexplained heart rate over 100 beats per minute
- Dehydration — reduced oral intake and minimal urinary output over 12 hours
- Chest pain or breathing with concerning features
- Cold, clammy, mottled, or pale skin
- History suggestive of deep vein thrombosis (DVT) or VTE

### Postnatal Features

- Secondary postpartum haemorrhage
- Signs of mastitis: fever over 38°C, painful, red breast
- Signs of endometritis: fever, tender uterus, malodorous uterine discharge
- Urinary tract infection (UTI)
- Wound infection
- Signs of pre-eclampsia
- Signs of VTE

## Risk Factors for Pregnancy Complications

Your doctor will assess whether additional risk factors are present. These influence the level of monitoring and whether specialist obstetric input is needed.

**Maternal risk factors include:**

- Not up to date with COVID-19 vaccinations
- Pulmonary conditions: moderate-to-severe asthma, bronchiectasis, or chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
- Persistent moderate-to-severe hyperemesis — unable to tolerate adequate oral intake, or requiring more than two antiemetics and still symptomatic
- Advanced maternal age (over 40 years)
- BMI over 40
- Diabetes — pre-existing or gestational
- Chronic kidney disease (CKD) stage 3 or higher
- Known thrombophilia
- Previous or current DVT or pulmonary embolism
- Active or flaring autoimmune or inflammatory disease (e.g. inflammatory bowel disease or systemic lupus erythematosus)
- Hypertensive disease in pregnancy, including gestational hypertension and pre-eclampsia

**Fetal risk factors include:**

- Fetal growth restriction
- Abnormal Dopplers
- Fetal anomaly
- Multiple pregnancy (twins, triplets)

---

## VTE Risk Factors

Your doctor will also assess for specific VTE risk factors, which are relevant to decisions about anticoagulation prophylaxis:

- Previous VTE
- Hospitalisation
- High-risk thrombophilia: antithrombin deficiency, homozygous Factor V Leiden (FVL), homozygous prothrombin G20210A mutation, compound heterozygote FVL + prothrombin G20210A, or antiphospholipid syndrome
- Low-risk thrombophilia (e.g. heterozygous FVL or heterozygous prothrombin G20210A mutation) plus family history of VTE
- BMI over 35
- Medical co-morbidities: type 1 diabetes, heart disease, systemic lupus erythematosus, renal disease
- Age over 35 years
- Three or more children
- Smoking
- Gross varicose veins
- Current pre-eclampsia
- Multiple pregnancy
- IVF or assisted reproduction

If postpartum, additional risk factors include:

- Birth before 37 weeks
- Stillbirth
- Prolonged labour (more than 24 hours)
- Instrumental birth or caesarean section
- Postpartum haemorrhage greater than 1 litre

## Management

Assessment and management decisions are made by your doctor or specialist obstetric team. The considerations below reflect what your doctor may consider based on your individual risk profile.

### Monitoring During Pregnancy

Your doctor will assess your risk for pregnancy complications and monitor accordingly. If you have additional risk factors, your doctor may refer you for obstetric assessment to create an individualised care plan.

Key monitoring priorities include:

- Watching for signs of VTE and placental insufficiency
- Monitoring for worsening COVID-19 illness, which can affect pregnancy care planning and clinical responsibility
- If clinical responsibility is transferred to an obstetric team, they will manage ongoing obstetric care including ultrasound; the role of the lead maternity carer (LMC) will be agreed upon by those involved

### VTE Prophylaxis

Your doctor may consider VTE prophylaxis using a low molecular weight heparin (LMWH). If enoxaparin is prescribed, the dose is weight-adjusted:

| Weight (kg) | Enoxaparin dose (subcutaneous, once daily) |
|---|---|
| 50 or less | 20 mg |
| 51–90 | 40 mg |
| 91–130 | 60 mg |
| 131–170 | 80 mg |
| More than 170 | 0.8 mg/kg |

*Based on Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists Green-top Guideline No. 37a.*

### Postnatal Monitoring

- Mother and baby should receive the usual frequency of postnatal assessments.
- Infant assessment at each postnatal visit includes respiratory rate, heart rate, and signs of respiratory distress.

### Breastfeeding

- If already breastfeeding, patients are encouraged to continue.
- No infants have been found to be infected through the breast milk of COVID-19-positive mothers.

### Mental Health

If there are mental health concerns or social distress during pregnancy or postpartum, your doctor can discuss referral to appropriate support services.
