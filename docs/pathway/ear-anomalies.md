# Ear Anomalies

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of ear deformities and malformations, from complete absence to subtle asymmetries.

---

## Background

There is a wide range of ear deformities and malformations, from complete absence to subtle asymmetries. Prominent ears are the most common anomaly to present in general practice.

Severe malformations — such as microtia (congenital underdevelopment of the pinna) and atresia of the external auditory canal — are associated with middle ear and facial abnormalities.

Children and adults with prominent or deformed ears can attract unwanted attention and may be subject to teasing. Parents may request assessment of their child's ear anomaly before teasing becomes an issue, often around the time of starting primary school.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess:

- **Severity** — identifying severe malformations such as microtia and atresia of the external auditory canal
- **Prominent ears** — measuring how far the outer edge of the ear rims project from the mastoid skin; more than 30 mm is usually considered prominent
- **Hearing** — whether the child has had a newborn hearing screen
- **Psychological impact** — the effect the ear anomaly is having on the child or adult

---

## Management

Your doctor will manage the ear anomaly based on its type and severity.

- **If a baby with an ear anomaly has not had a newborn hearing screen,** your doctor will arrange for a hearing screen to be organised.
- **If a severe malformation (e.g. microtia or atresia) is present,** your doctor will refer early to plastic surgery and ENT/Otolaryngology specialists, regardless of age.
- **If a minor ear anomaly is present,** your doctor may refer to plastic surgery or ENT/Otolaryngology for assessment.
- **If prominent ears are present,** your doctor may refer to plastic surgery or ENT/Otolaryngology for assessment. Referral is often timed between ages 5 to 7 years, when ear growth is sufficient, cartilage is strong enough for surgical correction, and timing aligns with the start of primary school.
- **If unsure whether referral is appropriate,** your doctor may seek paediatric ENT/Otolaryngology advice before referring.

When referring, your doctor will typically include ENT and audiology history, clinical photographs where possible, and a questionnaire about the impact on daily life.
