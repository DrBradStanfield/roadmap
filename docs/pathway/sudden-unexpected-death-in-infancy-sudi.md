# Sudden Unexpected Death in Infancy (SUDI)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the prevention of sudden unexpected death in infancy (SUDI) — identifying risk factors and supporting safer sleep for babies.

---

## Risk Factors

Your doctor will ask about the following when assessing SUDI risk.

**Modifiable risk factors** (things that can be changed):

- Maternal smoking — the largest modifiable risk factor
- Prone (face-down) sleeping, bed-sharing, or other unsafe sleep situations
- Maternal or caregiver drug or alcohol use
- Formula feeding

**Non-modifiable risk factors** (background factors that increase vigilance):

- Living in socio-economically deprived circumstances
- Previous SUDI in the family
- Maternal age under 20 years
- Caregiver sleep deprivation
- Prematurity or small for gestational age baby
- Multiple birth

Your doctor may also ask specifically about unsafe sleep situations:

- Baby sleeping face-down (prone)
- Baby bed-sharing with an adult or child
- Baby sleeping on an adult bed
- Baby sleeping on a couch or car seat
- Makeshift sleep arrangements when away from home

---

## Background

SUDI (previously called SIDS or cot death) is the leading cause of preventable death for babies in New Zealand, occurring in approximately 0.92 per 1,000 live births.

Rates are significantly higher for:

- Māori babies — approximately 7 times higher
- Pacific babies — approximately 4 times higher
- All babies exposed to maternal antenatal smoking — approximately 6 times higher

When bed-sharing and maternal antenatal smoking occur together, there is a 32-fold increase in risk. Around 60% of accidental suffocations occur from overlay by another person, and 25% happen in makeshift bedding.

Other factors that increase SUDI risk include prone sleeping, formula feeding, prematurity, birth weight below 2,500 g at full term, multiple birth, young mother, caregiver sleep deprivation, and drug or alcohol use that impairs caregiving.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will typically assess SUDI risk at the first antenatal consultation and at the 6-week baby check. If any risk factors are identified, your doctor may use a Safe Sleep Calculator to formally score the level of risk.

---

## Management

### Safe Sleep — The PEPE Framework

Your doctor will discuss the **PEPE** message for safer infant sleep:

- **Place** — Put baby in their own baby bed in the same room as their parent or caregiver
- **Eliminate** — Remove smoking from pregnancy and protect baby with a smoke-free home and car
- **Position** — Lay baby flat on their back to sleep, with their face clear of bedding
- **Encourage** — Support exclusive breastfeeding and gentle handling of baby

### Managing Modifiable Risk Factors

Your doctor may consider the following based on your situation:

- Advising placement of baby in their own baby bed (flat on back, face clear) as the most effective way to reduce SUDI risk
- Arranging urgent support to find a suitable baby bed if one is not available
- Referring to a safe sleep coordinator if risk is extremely high
- Recommending paediatric assessment if there has been a previous SUDI in the family
- Providing referral for smoking cessation support
- Providing referral for drug and alcohol support if needed
- Supporting breastfeeding with lactation advice or referral if difficulties arise
- Discussing immunisation, which is associated with reduced SUDI risk

### Community Support

Your doctor may also consider referral to support services such as:

- Parenting and community support services
- Healthy Homes Initiative (free service to help families have warmer, drier, safer homes)
- Māori or Pacific health services where appropriate
- Community social work support
