# Shoulder Imaging in Adults

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when imaging is appropriate for shoulder pain or injury in adults, including which type of scan may be used and what the red flags are.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Medical Assessment

The following features require prompt medical review and should not be delayed to arrange imaging:

- Unexplained deformity or swelling
- Significant weakness not due to pain
- Suspected malignancy
- Fever, chills, or malaise (may suggest septic arthritis or osteomyelitis)
- Significant or unexplained sensory or motor deficit
- Pulmonary or vascular compromise

If septic arthritis or osteomyelitis is suspected, your doctor will arrange an urgent orthopaedic assessment — the patient may need acute hospital admission.

---

## When X-Ray May Be Appropriate

Your doctor may consider an X-ray if:

- There is a new, unexplained bony swelling or joint deformity (usually within 2 weeks)
- Shoulder pain is associated with any of the following:
  - Persistent deep pain unrelated to activity
  - Unexplained night pain
  - History of cancer known to spread to bone
  - Pain in a previously replaced joint (arthroplasty)
  - Pain that has not responded to 6 weeks of conservative management, including a trial of corticosteroid injection and/or physiotherapy
  - Undiagnosed shoulder pain lasting more than 6 weeks
- Known osteoarthritis with significant functional impairment, if orthopaedic assessment for surgery is being considered

Note: imaging is not required before a corticosteroid injection if a clinical diagnosis has already been made.

---

## When Ultrasound May Be Appropriate

Your doctor may consider an ultrasound if recommended by a secondary care clinician or radiologist. When ultrasound is requested, an X-ray is usually arranged at the same time (unless one has been done recently).

Some routine ultrasound requests may be deferred if radiology capacity is limited — your doctor will advise you on timing.

---

## MRI

Primary care doctors cannot directly request shoulder MRI scans. If an MRI is thought to be needed, your doctor will refer you to an orthopaedic specialist who can arrange this.

---

## Conditions Where Imaging May Not Be the First Step

For the following conditions, your doctor may refer you to the appropriate pathway rather than arrange imaging directly:

- **Frozen shoulder** (reduced passive range of motion)
- **Bursitis**
- **ACC-covered shoulder injury** (imaging arranged through ACC)
