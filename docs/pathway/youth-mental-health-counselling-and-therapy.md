# Youth Mental Health Counselling and Therapy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway is for children and young people where the primary concern is their general mental health. It covers counselling and therapy options, digital self-help tools, and crisis support resources.

- If there are severe mental health issues in a child or young person aged 18 years or younger, discuss with your doctor — urgent mental health assessment may be needed.
- Where the primary concern is alcohol or other drugs, your healthcare provider can refer to appropriate youth alcohol and drug support services.
- For sexuality or gender identity related issues, your doctor can direct you to relevant support services.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Help

If a young person is in immediate danger of harming themselves or others, call 111 or go to the nearest emergency department.

For urgent but non-emergency mental health support, crisis helplines are available 24/7 — see the Helplines section below.

## E-Therapy (Digital Therapy Tools)

When considering e-therapy, your healthcare provider will take into account the young person's reading ability, ability to concentrate, access to devices and internet, and home circumstances. They may recommend follow-up after 2 to 4 weeks to check progress. If there is no improvement or symptoms worsen, further clinical assessment should be sought.

### Monitored E-Therapy

These programmes work best when initiated with guidance from a healthcare provider:

#### SPARX

A free e-therapy tool developed in New Zealand for young people aged 12 to 19 years with mild to moderate depression. It can also help with stress and anxiety.

- Delivers cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) in a fantasy game format
- Includes core CBT strategies for managing depression and reducing anxiety
- 7 levels, each taking approximately 30 minutes to complete
- A virtual therapist ("The Guide") explains skills and how to apply them in real life
- Your healthcare provider may use the PHQ-9 (Modified for Teens) to assess suitability — SPARX is generally recommended for those who score 10 to 19
- Suitable for those aged 12 to 19 years with mild to moderate symptoms and access to broadband internet
- No more than 2 modules per week is recommended to allow time to practise skills
- Website: [SPARX](https://www.sparx.org.nz/)

#### Beating the Blues

An online CBT treatment programme for patients aged 18 years or older with depression or anxiety.

- Fully funded; requires a healthcare provider to register the patient
- 8 sessions, each approximately 50 minutes
- Practitioners receive a progress report after each session
- Website: [Beating the Blues](https://beatingtheblues.co.nz/)

#### Just a Thought

Free online CBT-based courses for people aged 16 years or older.

- Courses cover: Generalised Anxiety, Social Anxiety, Depression, Mixed Depression and Anxiety, OCD, Health Anxiety, Panic, Insomnia, Pregnancy and Postnatal Wellbeing, and more
- 4 to 6 modules per course, including information, activities, homework, and ongoing support resources
- Website: [Just a Thought](https://www.justathought.co.nz)

#### Youthline E-Therapy Package

A free texting-based programme for young people with mild to moderate anxiety and/or depression.

- Website: [Youthline E-Therapy](https://www.youthline.co.nz/text-counselling.html)

### Unmonitored Self-Help Tools

These resources can be accessed independently:

- **My Anxiety Plan (MAP) for Children and Teens** (Anxiety Canada) — CBT-based anxiety management programme: [maps.anxietycanada.com](https://maps.anxietycanada.com/courses/anxiety-plan-children-teens/)
- **Aunty Dee** — Structured problem-solving based on CBT, aimed at Māori and Pacific youth aged 14 to 25 years: [auntydee.co.nz](http://www.auntydee.co.nz/)
- **CALM** (Computer Assisted Learning for the Mind) — Audio modules covering meditation, mindfulness, anger management, and exam preparation: [calm.auckland.ac.nz](https://www.calm.auckland.ac.nz/20.html)
- **Centre for Clinical Interventions** — Free self-help workbooks covering assertiveness, depression, self-esteem, disordered eating, social anxiety: [cci.health.wa.gov.au](https://www.cci.health.wa.gov.au/Resources/Looking-After-Yourself)
- **The Low Down** — For youth aged 18 to 25 years with anxiety or depression; includes self-help resources, chat forum, and free contact with trained counsellors: [thelowdown.co.nz](https://www.thelowdown.co.nz/)
- **Small Steps** — Free personalised online programme for mental wellbeing skills, available in te reo Māori: [smallsteps.org.nz](https://www.smallsteps.org.nz/)
- **Youthline** — Resources for young people aged 12 to 24 years, their family, and supporters: [youthline.co.nz](https://youthline.co.nz/)
- **Mental Wealth** — Online mental health literacy education for young people, with a Pacific focus: [mentalwealth.nz](https://mentalwealth.nz/)
- **Mindshift** — CBT tools and strategies for managing anxiety, social anxiety, perfectionism, panic, and phobias

## Mental Health and Wellbeing Apps

Mobile apps can be useful for helping young people manage stress, depression, anxiety, and general mental health. Clinically reviewed options include:

- **Aroha Chatbot** — Free chatbot for youth aged 13 to 24 years, using Facebook Messenger to provide mental health activities
- **Bite Back** — Free online wellbeing and resilience programme for youth aged 13 to 16 years, with interactive activities and goal setting
- **Headstrong** — Free online chatbot courses developed by the University of Auckland for rangatahi; uses Te Whare Tapa Whā framework
- **Manaaki Ora** — Free app providing guidance and self-help tips for wellbeing, based on Te Whare Tapa Whā
- **Mood Tools** — Free app with mood tests, thought diary, and safety planning
- **Smiling Mind** — Free daily mindfulness and meditation guide for children and adults
- **The Village** — Free social networking app to connect with and check in on friends' mental health, developed by Starship
- **Whitu 7 Ways in 7 Days** — Free app teaching self-help skills for youth aged 16 to 25 years

Your healthcare provider can recommend the most appropriate app for a young person's situation. Healthify He Puna Waiora provides clinical reviews of many of these apps at [healthify.nz/apps](https://healthify.nz/apps/m/mental-health-and-wellbeing-apps-for-teenagers-and-young-people).

## Counselling and Therapy Options

Your healthcare provider can discuss the most appropriate pathway for counselling or therapy. Options may include:

- **Primary care-based support** — Your GP or practice team may be able to provide brief psychological intervention or refer to a Health Improvement Practitioner if one is integrated into your practice
- **ACC-funded counselling** — ACC can help fund counselling and therapy sessions following a physical injury or sexual abuse. Information is available at [acc.co.nz/counselling-therapy](https://www.acc.co.nz/im-injured/types-of-ongoing-support/counselling-therapy)
- **Private therapists** — A range of registered psychologists, counsellors, and psychotherapists are available privately. Professional directories are maintained by the New Zealand College of Clinical Psychologists, New Zealand Association of Counsellors, New Zealand Psychological Society, New Zealand Association of Psychotherapists, and the Royal Australian and New Zealand College of Psychiatrists
- **WINZ Disability Allowance** — Fees for counselling (or transport to counselling) directly related to a health condition or disability may be subsidised. Your doctor or nurse practitioner can complete the required Disability Certificate — Counselling form. Up to 10 sessions are available initially, with up to 20 further sessions possible if recommended by the primary care provider

### Support for Specific Concerns

- **Eating disorders** — The Eating Disorders Association of New Zealand (EDANZ) offers support for families and carers: [ed.org.nz](https://www.ed.org.nz/)
- **Gambling harm** — Safer Gambling Aotearoa provides free confidential support online, by phone, and in person: [safergambling.org.nz](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/help-and-support/services-near-you)
- **Bereavement by suicide** — Aoake te Rā provides free support and manaaki for individuals, families, and communities who have lost someone to suicide: [aoaketera.org.nz](https://www.aoaketera.org.nz/)
- **Grief, loss, and trauma** — Skylight Trust offers counselling services across New Zealand, specialising in grief, loss, and trauma: [skylight.org.nz](https://www.skylight.org.nz/)
