# Biceps Tendon Rupture

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of biceps tendon rupture in adults.

---

## Background

More than 90% of ruptures involve the proximal biceps tendon (long head of the biceps).

- **Proximal rupture** — more often associated with repetitive microtrauma (e.g. rotator cuff pathology). Usually treated conservatively.
- **Distal rupture** — usually traumatic (e.g. heavy lifting, tug of war). Usually requires surgical repair to restore supination strength.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

- Any trauma or repetitive strain
- Whether the injury occurred during weight lifting (more common in younger adults) or is associated with rotator cuff problems (more common in the elderly)

Physical examination typically includes:

- Looking for **deformity of the biceps muscle**:
  - Proximal rupture: biceps tendon appears bunched up in the lower arm — the classic **"Popeye" sign**
  - Distal rupture: painful, swollen elbow
- Checking for bruising along the forearm
- Assessing shoulder and elbow range of movement and strength
- Testing for **weakness or pain with resisted forearm supination** — the most sensitive clinical sign
- The **Hook Test** — palpating the biceps tendon at the elbow to detect a distal rupture

If the diagnosis is unclear, your doctor may arrange:

- X-ray of the shoulder
- Ultrasound of the shoulder

to distinguish between a proximal tendon rupture and rotator cuff pathology.

---

## Management

### Proximal Tendon Rupture

Your doctor may consider:

- **Conservative (non-operative) management** in most cases — this is the standard approach
- Physiotherapy is not usually required
- In patients **younger than 40 years who are physically active**, your doctor may refer for acute orthopaedic assessment to discuss surgical repair

### Distal Tendon Rupture

- Your doctor will refer for **acute orthopaedic assessment**, as operative repair is usually necessary to restore supination strength

---

## When Referral Is Needed

Your doctor will refer for urgent orthopaedic assessment if:

- Distal tendon rupture (any age)
- Proximal tendon rupture in a young, physically active patient
