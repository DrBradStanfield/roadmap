# Managing Swallowing Difficulties

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the management of swallowing difficulties (dysphagia) in adults aged 16 and over **once an underlying diagnosis has been established**. If the cause of swallowing difficulty has not yet been identified, a separate Dysphagia assessment pathway applies.

> **Note:** This information is for educational purposes. Always discuss your symptoms and any changes to your diet, medications, or care plan with your doctor or healthcare provider.

---

## Management Overview

The [International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative (IDDSI)](https://www.iddsi.org/) is the current standard framework for managing dysphagia. It defines food and liquid levels from 0 (thin) to 7 (regular), guiding safe texture choices.

---

## Specific Swallowing Difficulties

### Coughing when eating or drinking

**Features:**
- May be accompanied by weight loss or recurrent chest infections
- Possible causes include TIA, stroke, progressive neurological condition, medication change, COPD, tumour, or vocal cord dysfunction

**How doctors manage this:**
- Address contributing factors: time of day, fatigue, dentition, positioning, and dependent feeding
- Provide general swallowing strategies
  - If strategies are successful, continue monitoring in general practice
  - If unsuccessful, your doctor will refer for a speech language therapy assessment

---

### Possible aspiration pneumonia

**Features:**
- Chest infection not responding to standard management

**How doctors manage this:**
- If modified food and fluid textures were previously recommended by a speech language therapist, your doctor will check whether you are following those guidelines — if not, they will advise you to return to the modified diet
- If aspiration pneumonia is known and recurrent: safe swallowing strategies are offered, videofluoroscopy (an X-ray swallowing study) is arranged, and information on oral hygiene and saliva management is provided
- If aspiration pneumonia is a single episode: safe swallowing strategies and monitoring; if unexplained chest infections continue, videofluoroscopy is arranged

---

### Unacceptable reduction in quality of life

**Features:**
- Known aspiration or choking risk
- Not following recommended swallowing regime
- Patient wishes to eat and drink normally for quality of life, and the goal of care is **not** to minimise aspiration at all costs

**How doctors manage this:**
- Ensure the patient and everyone involved in their care understands the risks of aspiration or choking
- If more information is needed to make an informed decision, your doctor may arrange videofluoroscopy or a speech language therapy assessment
- Advance Care Planning (ACP) may be considered — a process of discussing and documenting your wishes about future care
- General swallowing strategies and oral hygiene information are offered to reduce risk as much as possible within the agreed plan

---

### Pain or significant discomfort on swallowing

**Features:**
- Pain present only when swallowing, not at other times

**How doctors manage this:**
- Consider progressing diet texture, for example:
  - Normal liquids → slightly thick, mildly thick, or moderately thick liquids
  - Liquidised food → puréed, minced, and moist foods
  - Soft and bite-sized food → regular, easy-to-chew food
- Your doctor may refer you to HealthInfo resources on dietary suggestions for swallowing difficulties
- A speech language therapy assessment may be requested

---

### Difficulty drinking or eating meals

**Features:**
- Unable to manage current diet texture
- Difficulty chewing, holding food in the mouth, needing to be fed, dribbling food or drink, taking a very long time to eat or drink, or changes in taste or appetite

**How doctors manage this:**
- Consider behavioural or mood cause
- Assess oral hygiene, dentition, and ability to chew
- If an oral health or dental problem is found, a dental or oral health assessment is arranged and information on denture care provided
- For other causes, speech language therapy assessment is requested

---

### Behavioural or cognitive difficulties with eating or drinking

**Features:**
- Holding food or drink in the mouth
- Forgetting to swallow
- Taking too large a mouthful
- Spitting food
- Not maintaining a safe eating position
- Refusing food or drink
- Specific aversions to particular foods or drinks

**How doctors manage this:**
- Address contributing factors: time of day, fatigue, dentition, positioning, dependent feeding, and environment (distractions, discomfort, seating)
- Information is provided on safe eating for people with cognitive impairment
- Speech language therapy assessment may be requested

---

### Difficulties swallowing medications

**How doctors manage this:**
- Consider hydration status, review current medications, and consider alternative formulations (e.g., liquid instead of tablet)
- Provide medication swallowing strategies (a hierarchy of techniques)

---

### Dry mouth or saliva problems

**How doctors manage this:**
- Consider hydration status and review medications as a contributing cause
- Provide saliva management strategies
- HealthInfo resources on oral hygiene and saliva management are offered for further guidance

---

## Progressing Towards Normal Liquids and Food

Your doctor may consider progressing your diet if you:
- Are managing thin or slightly thin liquids without coughing, choking, weight loss, or chest infections
- Want to try progressing
- Have had a recent neurological event (e.g., stroke) that may have improved

**The progression steps (IDDSI levels) are:**
1. Level 0 thin liquids and Level 1 slightly thick liquids
2. Level 3 moderately thick and Level 4 extremely thick liquids
3. Level 4 puréed diet
4. Level 5 minced and moist food
5. Level 6 soft and bite-sized diet
6. Level 7 easy-to-chew diet

Each step is trialled over 2 to 3 days.

**If progression is unsuccessful:**
- Stay at the manageable texture level
- Make an informed decision about accepting the risks of trying a higher texture
- Seek a speech language therapy assessment

---

## When to Request Speech Language Therapy

If swallowing difficulties cannot be managed in general practice, your doctor will refer you for a formal **speech language therapy assessment**. Speech language therapists are the specialists in swallowing function and can assess, advise on texture, and provide targeted rehabilitation.
