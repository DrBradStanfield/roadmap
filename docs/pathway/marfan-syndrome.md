# Marfan Syndrome

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the approach to suspected or confirmed Marfan syndrome — a heritable connective tissue disorder affecting the cardiovascular system, skeleton, and eyes.

---

## Emergency — Chest Pain

**Chest pain in any person with suspected or confirmed Marfan syndrome is a medical emergency.**

Call 111 or go to the emergency department immediately. There is a risk of aortic rupture, which can be life-threatening.

---

## Background

Marfan syndrome is an autosomal dominant condition, meaning:
- There is a 50:50 chance of inheriting it from an affected parent
- It affects males and females equally
- About 15% of cases arise as a new mutation with no family history

Prevalence is approximately 1 in 10,000.

The condition affects the **cardiovascular system** (aorta, heart valves), **skeleton** (bone overgrowth, joint laxity), and **eyes** (lens position, retina). Patients are often tall with long limbs and fingers.

---

## Who Should Be Assessed

**Most tall, thin individuals do not have Marfan syndrome** and do not need genetic testing or cardiac investigation.

Your doctor may suspect Marfan syndrome if you:
- Have a first-degree relative with confirmed Marfan syndrome, **or**
- Are tall with long fingers **and** at least one other characteristic feature (pigeon chest, joint hypermobility, lens problem, previous pneumothorax, heart murmur)

Clinical diagnosis is difficult to confirm in people younger than 20 years.

---

## Features Doctors Look For

### Ocular
- Myopia (short-sightedness)
- Displacement of the lens from the centre of the pupil (ectopia lentis) — in approximately 50% of patients
- Increased risk of retinal detachment, glaucoma, and early cataract

### Skeletal
- Unusually long, slender limbs and fingers
- Hollow chest (pectus excavatum) or pigeon chest (pectus carinatum)
- Scoliosis
- Joint hypermobility

### Cardiovascular (potentially life-threatening)
- Aortic dilation
- Aortic dissection or rupture
- Mitral valve prolapse

---

## Assessment

If Marfan syndrome is suspected, your doctor will:
- Draw a 3-generation family tree, noting any sudden unexplained deaths
- Arrange an **echocardiogram** (heart ultrasound) to assess the aorta

---

## Referral

If Marfan syndrome is suspected:
- Adults and those aged 15 and over: referral to genetic health services for counselling and discussion of genetic testing
- Children aged 14 and under: referral to paediatric medicine

If echocardiography shows aortic abnormalities: referral to cardiology.

---

## Patient Safety Advice

People with suspected or confirmed Marfan syndrome are advised to:
- **Seek immediate medical attention for any chest pain** — risk of aortic rupture
- Take extra care during exercise and pregnancy (higher cardiac output increases risk)
- Avoid contact sports or activities likely to cause joint injury
