# Abdominal X-ray (AXR)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when an abdominal X-ray (AXR) is appropriate, including accepted clinical indications, situations requiring emergency assessment, and conditions where alternative imaging is recommended.

> **Note:** Before requesting an abdominal X-ray, your healthcare provider will consider the potential radiation risks involved.

---

## Critical — Do Not Delay Acute Assessment for Imaging

### Adults

If an acute abdomen is suspected — including suspected bowel obstruction — your doctor will arrange urgent assessment by the relevant specialist. An X-ray should **not** delay this.

### Children (aged 14 years and younger)

Your doctor will arrange urgent paediatric assessment (do not delay for imaging) if any of the following apply:

- Acute abdominal pain requiring emergency assessment
- History of foreign body ingestion with any of the following **red flags**:
  - Suspected oesophageal obstruction (e.g. drooling or reluctance to swallow)
  - Known or suspected disc or button battery ingestion
  - Multiple magnets ingested, or both magnets and metal ingested
  - Sharp or large objects (more than 5 cm long and/or 2 cm wide)
  - Lead-based objects (can cause acute systemic lead absorption if they do not transit the stomach)

---

## Accepted Indications for AXR

### Adults

Your healthcare provider may recommend an abdominal X-ray in the following situations:

- **Constipation** — for diagnostic purposes when a patient history cannot be obtained (e.g. autism spectrum disorder, significant cognitive impairment, or very poor historian)
- **IUD (intrauterine device) issues:**
  - *Incomplete IUD removal* — a lower abdominal X-ray (pelvis only) may be requested if a pelvic ultrasound has been performed and the missing IUD part was not seen within the uterus
  - *IUD threads not visible* — a full abdominal X-ray including the pelvis may be requested if pregnancy has been excluded, a pelvic ultrasound has been performed, and the IUD was not located within the uterus
- **Renal tract stones** — a KUB (Kidneys, Ureters, and Bladder) X-ray may be used for follow-up of radio-opaque stones previously visible on X-ray or CT scout view. See the relevant renal colic/urinary tract stones pathway.
- **Persistent lower UTI or pyelonephritis** — if bacteria are confirmed on urine culture despite appropriate antibiotic treatment, your doctor may consider colonisation of a renal tract stone and request both pre- and post-void renal ultrasound and a KUB X-ray
- **AXR recommended by a specialist, radiologist, or radiology liaison** — for either acute (within 48 hours) or routine (within 6 weeks) time frames

### Children (aged 14 years and younger)

An AXR may be arranged if **all** of the following apply:

- Suspected ingestion of an unknown foreign body
- Child is asymptomatic with **no** red flags (see list above)
- The case has been discussed with a paediatric surgeon or radiology liaison, who has recommended imaging

When imaging is arranged, X-rays of the neck (front and lateral views), chest (front and lateral views), and abdomen are typically requested together.

An AXR recommended by a specialist or radiologist for acute or routine follow-up timing is also an accepted indication.

---

## Alternative Management Recommended

### Adults

- **Renal or ureteric stent** — if a patient presents with common stent symptoms or signs, your doctor may consider requesting a KUB X-ray to confirm stent position and exclude displacement.

  **Common stent symptoms and signs** (occur in 30–50% of patients with stents):
  - Flank pain, worsened by urination or strenuous exertion
  - Intermittent blood in the urine (haematuria) without clots
  - Urinary frequency and urgency
  - Red cells and white cells on urine dipstick are expected with a stent and do not necessarily indicate infection

- **Suspected colorectal cancer** — your doctor will follow the colorectal symptoms pathway rather than ordering an AXR

### Children (aged 14 years and younger)

- **Abdominal pain not thought to be caused by constipation** — your doctor will follow the chronic abdominal pain in children and youth pathway

---

## Situations Where AXR Is Not Routinely Funded

The following are generally not funded through public radiology services and may require private imaging if desired:

- Vague central abdominal pain
- Suspected constipation in patient groups other than those meeting the accepted criteria above
