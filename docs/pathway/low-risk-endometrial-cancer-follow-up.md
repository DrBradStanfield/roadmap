# Low-risk Endometrial Cancer Follow-up

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers general practice care for patients after treatment for low-risk endometrial cancer.

---

## Background

- Low-risk endometrial cancers (stage 1A, Grade G1 and G2) have a 5–10% risk of local recurrence.
- Local recurrences are most frequently at the vaginal vault and may present as bleeding or a visible abnormality.
- If detected early, isolated recurrences can usually be cured by radiotherapy with or without surgery.
- Treatment is usually total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy (in post-menopausal patients). In pre-menopausal patients, ovarian conservation is discussed on a case-by-case basis.

---

## Warning Signs — Contact Your Doctor Promptly

Between follow-up appointments, contact your GP or gynaecologist promptly if you notice:

- Any vaginal bleeding
- New pelvic pain
- Bowel or bladder dysfunction
- New or worsening leg swelling
- Any other new or concerning symptoms

Do not wait for your next planned appointment.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess you at approximately **6 months and 18 months** after treatment.

They will ask about:

- Vaginal bleeding, pain, bowel or bladder problems, sexual difficulties, or leg swelling
- Symptoms of oestrogen deficiency (e.g., hot flushes, vaginal dryness, mood changes)
- General psycho-social wellbeing, including relationships and mood

Physical examination typically includes:

- Feeling the neck and groin lymph nodes
- Abdominal, speculum, and bimanual pelvic examination
- Vault smears are not routinely needed unless there were smear abnormalities before hysterectomy

If a pelvic mass is suspected on examination, your doctor will arrange an urgent pelvic ultrasound.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following depending on findings:

- **Palpable lymph nodes:** Urgent gynaecology assessment. Fine needle aspiration may be considered while awaiting the appointment.
- **Suspected pelvic mass:** Urgent pelvic ultrasound. If a mass is confirmed, urgent gynaecology assessment.
- **New leg swelling (possible lymphoedema):** Gynaecology assessment to investigate for recurrence, plus lymphoedema assessment.
- **Psycho-social concerns:**
  - If the cancer diagnosis or treatment has put strain on a relationship, couples counselling may be helpful.
  - If sexual difficulties are a concern, referral to a sex therapy counsellor may be considered.
  - The Cancer Society information line is available for support: **0800 226 237**
- **Other concerning symptoms or signs:** Your doctor will seek specialist gynaecology advice.
- **Menopausal symptoms:**
  - For urogenital symptoms, vaginal oestrogen can be used. A non-hormonal alternative for vaginal dryness is a vaginal moisturiser such as Replens.
  - If systemic oestrogen is being considered, the benefits and risks will be discussed individually with you, and your doctor will seek gynaecology advice.
