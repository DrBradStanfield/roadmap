# Gender Diversity and Transgender Health

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the provision of gender-affirming healthcare for people of all ages and at all stages of their gender journey. **Any decisions about hormone therapy, puberty blockers, or surgery should be made with your doctor or specialist.**

---

## Important Update: Puberty Blockers (as of early 2026)

New regulations (November 2025) prohibit new prescriptions of GnRH analogues for puberty suppression in children and adolescents with gender incongruence or dysphoria, unless already prescribed before 19 December 2025.

However, the High Court (December 2025) declared the Crown should not enforce these regulations pending judicial review, and the Court of Appeal (February 2026) affirmed this. The judicial review is scheduled for May 2026.

What this means in practice:
- Regulations remain law but are not being enforced at this stage
- Those already on puberty blockers may remain on treatment with appropriate monitoring and review
- Continue referrals through established pathways and routine practice

## Key Terms

- **Transgender** — a person whose gender is different from their sex assigned at birth
- **Non-binary** — an umbrella term for someone whose gender is neither male nor female, or not exclusively one or the other
- **Cisgender (cis)** — a person whose gender aligns with their sex assigned at birth
- **Gender-fluid** — a person whose gender is not fixed and varies over time
- **Intersex** — a range of natural variations in the body related to sex characteristics (anatomy, reproductive organs, hormonal or chromosomal patterns). Intersex people may be any gender, including cisgender, transgender, or non-binary.
- **Takatāpui** — Māori term encompassing gay, lesbian, bisexual, transgender, intersex, or part of the rainbow community
- **Gender dysphoria** — the distress caused by a discrepancy between a person's gender and their sex assigned at birth. Not all transgender or gender diverse people experience gender dysphoria.
- **Gender euphoria** — the positive feelings associated with alignment between how a person sees their own gender and body, and how others see them
- **Gender incongruence** — a marked and persistent incongruence between an individual's experienced gender and their sex assigned at birth
- **Gender affirmation (transition)** — steps taken to live authentically and be affirmed in one's gender. There is no right or wrong way; not everyone desires medical or surgical intervention.
- **Gender expression** — the external presentation of one's gender (name, clothing, behaviour, hairstyle, voice)
- **Social affirmation** — changing gender expression in social situations to better align with one's gender (name and pronouns, clothing, hair, participating in life as one's true self)
- **E-GAHT** — estrogen-based gender-affirming hormone therapy (previously called feminising therapy)
- **T-GAHT** — testosterone-based gender-affirming hormone therapy (previously called masculinising therapy)

## Assessment — What Your Doctor Will Do

### Consultation Approach

Your doctor should apply extra sensitivity. Social stigma and discrimination, including within the healthcare system, is a recognised barrier to care and contributes to adverse health outcomes.

Your doctor should:
- Discuss confidentiality at the start: your information is kept private within the treating team unless there are safety concerns or you consent to sharing. Parents or caregivers do not have access to your health information.
- Ask about your name, pronouns, and whether you'd like your self-identified name and gender entered in your records
- See young patients alone for at least part of the consultation

### History

Your doctor will ask about:
- How you identify (gender, pronouns)
- Your gender journey and who is supporting you
- Whether you experience gender dysphoria and what the triggers are
- Your previous experiences with healthcare
- Your full medical history including all medications (prescribed and non-prescribed, including self-medicating with hormones)
- Drug and alcohol history
- Sexual health
- Mental health — anxiety, depression, eating disorders, self-harm, and past experience of abuse or bullying

**Mental health screening is important:** Gender diverse people are at higher risk of depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation. Your doctor should screen sensitively for these, including asking about suicidal thoughts. Asking about suicidal ideation does not create or worsen suicide risk.

**Physical examination:** Your doctor will measure height and weight. Routine examination of breasts and genitalia is not usually required and may be emotionally traumatising.

### Goals and Needs

Your doctor should discuss your individual transition goals without assuming you want to conform to binary gender norms. Goals may include:
- Support around social affirmation
- Family/whānau and peer support
- Menstrual cessation and contraception
- Gender-affirming hormone therapy (GAHT)
- Vocal therapy
- Gender-affirming surgery
- Psychological support

## Management

### Mental Health Support

If you are experiencing a mental health crisis, go to the emergency department or call 111. For non-urgent mental health support, your doctor can refer you to appropriate services.

### General Care

Your doctor may discuss:
- Lifestyle factors (smoking, weight, blood pressure, diabetes management) to reduce cardiovascular risks associated with hormone treatments
- Menstrual cessation options
- Contraception — note that testosterone is **not** a contraceptive
- Future fertility and gamete preservation
- Cancer screening for organs not surgically removed
- Work or school environment

**Menstrual cessation options your doctor may consider:**
- Combined oral contraceptive pill (if no contraindications to estrogen)
- Norethisterone 5 mg twice daily (does not provide contraception)
- Depo-Provera
- Mirena IUD
- Contraceptive implant

**Future fertility:**
- Fertility preservation is not required for GAHT but should be discussed before starting.
- E-GAHT (estrogen) may result in permanent loss of fertility.
- T-GAHT (testosterone) usually causes reversible ovarian suppression; egg harvesting is not required before starting, but patients who may wish to become pregnant in the future should be aware they will need to stop testosterone and may need fertility assistance.
- Funding for fertility preservation is available in New Zealand.

### Cancer Screening

Transgender and gender diverse people who have not had surgical removal of breasts, cervix, uterus, ovaries, prostate, or testicles remain at risk of cancer in those organs. Your doctor should ensure appropriate cancer screening recalls remain in place even if your gender markers are updated in medical records.

### Non-Medical Body Interventions

**Safe binding** (flattening breast tissue for a male-appearing chest):
- Bind for less than 8 hours a day to avoid skin irritation, tissue breakdown, back pain, and breathing problems
- Always remove the binder before sleep and exercise
- Never use duct tape or Ace bandages (can restrict breathing)
- Stop binding if experiencing pain
- Use a binder made specifically for this purpose

**Tucking** (pushing testicles up inside the body and pulling the penis back between the legs):
- Use tight-fitting underwear or surgical tape only (other tapes can tear skin)
- Cut pubic hair short to help with tape removal
- Spend some time each day without tucking to avoid chafing and sores

**Packing** (using a prosthetic penis/packer):
- May help reduce body dysphoria, aid urination while standing, or be used for sexual intercourse
- If used for sexual penetration, use a condom

**Padding** (undergarments to create appearance of larger breasts, hips, or buttocks):
- Safe alternative to silicone injections
- Products include padded underwear, padded bras, bras with pockets, silicone gel breast forms

## Gender-Affirming Hormone Therapy (GAHT)

**Discuss with your doctor.** GAHT is an important treatment option but requires assessment, baseline investigations, and ongoing monitoring.

Physical changes from hormonal treatment occur gradually over 1 to 2 years; the degree of change and timeline are highly variable.

### Precautions — Tell Your Doctor If You Have

- Current or recent smoking history
- Heart failure, cerebrovascular disease, coronary artery disease, or atrial fibrillation
- Personal or family history of blood clots (VTE)
- Cardiovascular risk factors: BMI >30, high cholesterol, high blood pressure
- Migraines
- History of hormone-sensitive cancers (breast, prostate, uterine, testicular)
- Sleep apnoea
- Intersex variations

### Baseline Tests Before Starting

Your doctor will arrange:
- Blood pressure, height, weight, BMI (no routine genital or breast examination needed)
- Blood tests appropriate to the type of GAHT planned (see below)

### E-GAHT (Estrogen-Based — Feminising)

**Starting options (your doctor will prescribe and titrate):**
- Anti-androgen first: cyproterone 12.5 mg daily, or spironolactone 50 to 100 mg daily
- Estradiol added: Progynova (oral), Estradot (patch), or Estrogel (gel)
- Use of these medications for gender affirmation is off-label
- If a GnRH blocker is needed (e.g., to prevent full pubertal changes), your doctor will arrange a referral to a specialist service

**Ongoing monitoring your doctor will do:**
- Blood pressure and BMI every 6 months
- Blood tests every 3 to 6 months for the first year, then at least annually: liver function, estradiol, testosterone levels, and others as indicated
- Biochemical targets: testosterone aim <2 nmol/L; estradiol target <700–750 pmol/L
- Watch for potential complications: blood clots (VTE), cardiovascular disease, liver dysfunction, gallstones, mood and libido changes, and (rarely) osteoporosis, breast cancer, or hyperprolactinaemia

Note: If you are on high cumulative doses of cyproterone, there is a small risk of meningioma. Your doctor should titrate to the lowest acceptable dose.

If you are over 40 or have blood clot risk factors, transdermal estrogen (patch or gel) has lower clot risk than oral estrogen.

### T-GAHT (Testosterone-Based — Masculinising)

**Starting options (your doctor will prescribe and titrate):**
- Sustanon 125 mg IM every 3 weeks (contraindicated if hypersensitivity to peanuts or soya; suitable for self-injection)
- Depo-Testosterone 50–100 mg IM every 2 weeks, or 20–30 mg SC weekly (suitable for self-injection)
- Reandron 500 mg IM every 10–12 weeks (not suitable for self-injection)
- Testogel 1.62% gel — 1 to 2 pumps applied to clean skin once a day in the morning
- Testosterone is **contraindicated in pregnancy**; testosterone is **not** a contraceptive

**Ongoing monitoring your doctor will do:**
- Blood pressure and BMI every 6 months
- Blood tests every 3 to 6 months for the first year, then at least annually: full blood count (checking for polycythaemia), liver function, and others as indicated
- Testosterone levels: aim for male reference range; timing of test depends on preparation used
- Periods usually stop 3 to 6 months after starting testosterone. If they don't, your doctor may add Depo-Provera or a Mirena IUD.
- Ultrasound to assess endometrial thickness if vaginal bleeding restarts
- Watch for potential complications: polycythaemia (high red cell count), adverse lipid profile, mood and libido changes, sleep apnoea, and small risks of liver dysfunction, insulin resistance, cardiovascular disease, endometrial hyperplasia, and osteoporosis

**If haematocrit is >0.52:** your doctor will reduce testosterone dose and/or refer to a specialist.

### Bone Density

If you have major risk factors for osteoporotic fracture (age ≥65 for women or ≥75 for men, BMI <20, family history of osteoporosis, current smoking, glucocorticoid use, early menopause, excessive alcohol use, history of falls, rheumatoid arthritis, history of eating disorders, certain medical conditions or medications), your doctor may arrange a DEXA bone density scan.

## Voice Therapy

Voice therapy can help achieve a more gender-neutral pitch and modify other communication aspects. Outcomes are variable. Voice surgery may be required for some patients.

If you are interested in voice therapy, discuss with your doctor. Publicly funded sessions may be available through an ENT/ORL service (for patients ≥16 years). Private voice therapy is also available; WINZ disability allowance funding may be accessible — your doctor can provide a medical certificate stating voice therapy is "essential medical treatment" for gender dysphoria.

---

## Gender-Affirming Surgery

Some gender-affirming surgery is publicly funded in New Zealand, including:
- Chest surgery: breast augmentation (feminising) or chest reconstruction (masculinising)
- Hysterectomy
- Salpingo-oophorectomy
- Orchidectomy

Surgery requires specialist assessment to determine readiness. Discuss with your doctor if this is a goal.

---

## Name and Gender Marker Changes

Your doctor can:
- Update your self-identified name and pronouns in your clinical records
- Help update your National Health Index (NHI) record to reflect your name and gender of choice — no proof of gender is required

It is important that cancer screening recalls are not removed when gender markers are updated in the medical records system.

---

## Support for Young People

Gender diverse young people may not have parental support, but this does not prevent them from receiving care. Your doctor should:
- Assess for risks around abuse, bullying, drug and alcohol use, sexual health, and mental health
- Provide assistance with family conflict
- Connect you with supportive peer groups if helpful
- Provide information and support to families and whānau

For young patients, medical intervention (e.g., puberty blockers) is never required before the onset of puberty.

---

## When to Seek Specialist Support

Your doctor may refer you to specialist transgender health services when:
- You need specialist support to explore gender identity
- You are considering puberty blockers
- You need support to start or monitor GAHT
- You are experiencing high levels of distress
- Your doctor is uncertain how to manage your situation
- You are seeking publicly funded gender-affirming surgery (adults ≥18 years)

If you are in a mental health crisis, contact your doctor urgently or call 111.

---

## Plan of Care

If you are under specialist services, a plan of care will be established for ongoing prescribing and monitoring. Refer back to the specialist if:
- Hormone targets cannot be reached or maintained
- You wish to discuss additional treatment options
- Complex health problems or complications develop
- Surgery is planned
