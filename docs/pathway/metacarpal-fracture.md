# Metacarpal Fracture

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and initial management of metacarpal fractures in adults.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history including:

- Most common causes: punch-related injuries, crush injuries, or a fall onto a clenched fist
- Whether any wound over the knuckle joints (metacarpophalangeal joints) may be from a "fight bite" — an injury from punching another person in the mouth

Physical examination typically includes:

- Looking for swelling, skin damage, and deformity
- Checking the range of movement of the hand, wrist joints, and fingers
- Checking for **rotational deformity**: your doctor will ask you to flex your fingers as fully as possible (even if painful) and observe whether the nail plates line up correctly, comparing with the other hand
- Checking for any shortening of the finger

A **hand X-ray** is arranged. The type of fracture (metacarpal head, neck, shaft, or base) determines management.

---

## Management

Your doctor will manage the fracture based on its type and severity. The general approach is:

### Multiple or Open Fractures

- If there are multiple metacarpal fractures of any type, or an open fracture, your doctor will arrange urgent hand surgery assessment.

### Metacarpal Head Fractures

- If undisplaced: your doctor may apply a moulded volar splint and buddy strap the fingers, then arrange follow-up with an orthopaedic specialist.
- For all other head fractures: urgent hand surgery assessment is arranged.

### Metacarpal Neck Fractures

Most commonly caused by a direct blow to the knuckles.

- Your doctor will check for rotational deformity.
- If not rotated and the angulation is within acceptable limits (less than 15° for index and middle fingers, less than 30° for ring and little fingers): your doctor may apply a moulded volar splint and buddy strap for 4 weeks, with orthopaedic follow-up.
- If the angulation is unacceptable: urgent hand surgery assessment is arranged.

**Boxer's fracture (neck of the 5th metacarpal):** If you can fully straighten the finger and there is no rotational deformity, your doctor may apply buddy strapping for 2–3 weeks without reduction. If reduction is needed, a moulded volar plaster cast is applied.

### Metacarpal Shaft Fractures

- If the fracture is stable and within acceptable angulation limits: your doctor may apply a moulded volar splint and buddy strap for 4–6 weeks.
- Acceptable limits: less than 10° angulation for index and middle fingers, less than 20° for ring and little fingers; up to 5 mm shortening for spiral fractures.
- If there are multiple fractures, significant shortening, or excessive angulation: urgent hand surgery assessment is arranged.
- Transverse fractures of the fourth metacarpal have a higher risk of shortening and may require hand surgery assessment.

### Metacarpal Base Fractures

- Your doctor will arrange a true lateral X-ray to check for joint dislocation (dorsal subluxation), which can easily be missed.
- If uncomplicated (index or middle finger base): your doctor may splint in a volar plaster cast, or splint and mobilise early.
- If complicated or associated with joint dislocation: urgent hand surgery assessment is arranged.
- For base of fifth metacarpal fractures: your doctor will check for subluxation of the joint. If undisplaced, a volar plaster splint is applied with urgent orthopaedic follow-up (usually within 1 week).

### Bennett Fracture (Base of Thumb)

- If intra-articular (fracture involving the joint surface): urgent hand surgery assessment is arranged. These fractures are unstable.
- If extra-articular:
  - Angulation more than 30°: urgent hand surgery assessment.
  - Angulation less than 30°: your doctor may reduce and apply a thumb spica cast, with non-urgent orthopaedic follow-up.

### Allied Health Support

Your doctor may consider:

- **Physiotherapy** if you are experiencing barriers to recovery, or have weakness, reduced range of movement, or significant pain
- **Hand therapy** if a custom thermoplastic splint would be helpful
- **Occupational therapy** if you are having difficulty with everyday activities such as self-care, household management, driving, or work
- **Work assessment and rehabilitation** if returning to work is a concern
