# Meningococcal Vaccination

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers meningococcal vaccination recommendations in New Zealand — who should be vaccinated, which vaccines are funded, and dosing schedules by age and risk group.

---

## Background

There are at least 12 groups of *Neisseria meningitidis*. Five groups cause most disease: B, A, C, Y, and W. In New Zealand, group B causes around 80% of meningococcal disease.

For the best protection, separate vaccinations against group B and groups A, C, Y, and W are recommended.

### Available vaccines

- **Group B:** Bexsero (4CMenB)
- **Groups A, C, Y, and W:**
  - MenQuadfi (licensed from age 12 months)
  - Nimenrix (licensed from age 6 weeks)

### Important note about Bexsero

Bexsero is a reactogenic vaccine and often causes fever above 38°C. For children under 2 years, three doses of prophylactic paracetamol (15 mg/kg) are recommended:

- First dose up to 30 minutes before vaccination, or at time of vaccination
- Second dose 4–6 hours after the first
- Third dose 4–6 hours after the second
- The child does not need to be woken after vaccination to receive paracetamol

## Funded Vaccination — National Immunisation Schedule

From September 2025, **Bexsero is fully funded** for all children under 5 years as part of the National Immunisation Schedule (primary course of up to 3 doses, depending on age at first dose).

Your doctor may consider the following funded schedules:

### Infants aged 6 weeks to under 12 months

| Indication | Dosage |
|---|---|
| National Immunisation Schedule | One dose of Bexsero at 3 and 5 months (or 8 weeks and 4 months), followed by a booster at 12 months. If high risk, two doses from 6 weeks (off-label, prescription required). |
| Previous meningococcal disease; close contact of a case (identified by Public Health) | Nimenrix two doses 8 weeks apart (from 6 weeks to under 6 months) or one dose then MenQuadfi at least 8 weeks later and after 12 months (from 6 months to under 12 months); plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart with booster from 12 months. |
| Post-haematopoietic stem cell transplant; immunosuppression >28 days | Nimenrix two doses 8 weeks apart, then MenQuadfi at least 6 months after second dose and after 12 months; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart with booster from 12 months. |
| Asplenia (functional or anatomic); splenectomy; solid organ transplant; complement deficiency; HIV positive | Nimenrix two doses 8 weeks apart, then MenQuadfi at least 6 months after second dose and after 12 months, with MenQuadfi boosters every 5 years; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart with booster from 12 months, then every 5 years. |

### Children aged 12 months to under 2 years

| Indication | Dosage |
|---|---|
| National Immunisation Schedule | Two doses of Bexsero 8 weeks apart, booster 12–23 months after the second primary dose. |
| Previous meningococcal disease; close contact of a case | One dose MenQuadfi; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart, then one dose 12–23 months later. |
| Post-haematopoietic stem cell transplant; immunosuppression >28 days | Two doses MenQuadfi 8–12 weeks apart (second dose off-license, prescription required); plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart, then one dose 12–23 months later. |
| Asplenia; splenectomy; solid organ transplant; complement deficiency; HIV positive | Two doses MenQuadfi 8–12 weeks apart (prescription required), booster after 3 years then every 5 years; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart, booster 12–23 months later, then every 5 years. |

### Children aged 2 years to under 7 years

| Indication | Dosage |
|---|---|
| National Immunisation Schedule | Two doses of Bexsero 8 weeks apart (for children aged 24–59 months at time of first dose). |
| Previous meningococcal disease; close contact of a case | One dose MenQuadfi; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart. |
| Post-haematopoietic stem cell transplant; immunosuppression >28 days | Two doses MenQuadfi 8–12 weeks apart (second dose off-license, prescription required); plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart. |
| Asplenia; splenectomy; solid organ transplant; complement deficiency; HIV positive | Two doses MenQuadfi 8–12 weeks apart (prescription required), booster after 3 years then every 5 years; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart with booster every 5 years. |

### Children aged 7 years to adults aged 18 years and older

| Indication | Dosage |
|---|---|
| Previous meningococcal disease; close contact of a case (identified by Public Health) | One dose MenQuadfi; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart. |
| Post-haematopoietic stem cell transplant; immunosuppression >28 days | Two doses MenQuadfi 8 weeks apart (second dose off-license, prescription required); plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart. |
| Asplenia; splenectomy; solid organ transplant; complement deficiency; HIV positive | Two doses MenQuadfi 8 weeks apart (prescription required), booster every 5 years; plus Bexsero two doses 8 weeks apart, booster every 5 years. |

### Teenagers and young adults in communal living settings (aged 13–25 years)

Individuals entering within the next 3 months, or in their first year of living in boarding school hostels, tertiary education halls of residence, military barracks, youth justice residences, or prisons:

- One dose of MenQuadfi
- Two doses of Bexsero 8 weeks apart

## Non-Funded Vaccination

Meningococcal vaccination is **recommended but not funded** for:

- All infants, children, adolescents, and young adults not covered by the National Immunisation Schedule
- Laboratory workers regularly handling meningococcal cultures
- Adolescents and young adults in communal or overcrowded accommodation not covered by funded vaccine
- Travellers to high-risk countries or those preparing for the Hajj

Your doctor may consider the following non-funded schedules:

| Age | Vaccine | Doses |
|---|---|---|
| 6 weeks to under 6 months | MenACWY (Nimenrix) | Two doses 8 weeks apart, plus booster from 12 months (at least 6 months after second dose) |
| 6 months to under 12 months | MenACWY (Nimenrix) | One dose, plus booster from 12 months (at least 8 weeks after first dose) |
| 12 months to under 5 years | MenACWY (MenQuadfi or Nimenrix) | One dose |
| 5 years to under 11 years | MenACWY (MenQuadfi or Nimenrix); MenB (Bexsero) | One dose MenQuadfi or Nimenrix; and two doses Bexsero 8 weeks apart (healthy children this age are at lowest risk outside epidemic situations) |
| 12 to under 16 years | MenB (Bexsero); MenACWY (MenQuadfi or Nimenrix) | Two doses Bexsero 8 weeks apart; one dose MenACWY, booster 5 years later if still at risk |
| 16 years and older | MenB (Bexsero); MenACWY (MenQuadfi or Nimenrix) | Two doses Bexsero 8 weeks apart; one dose MenACWY (no booster required) |
