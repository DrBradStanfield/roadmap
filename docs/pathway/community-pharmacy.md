# Community Pharmacy Services

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway outlines what community pharmacists in New Zealand can provide — including medicines and services that do not require a GP prescription.

---

## Medicines Pharmacists Can Supply Without a GP Prescription

Accredited community pharmacists can supply certain prescription and restricted medicines under defined criteria. These are not subsidised and costs vary between pharmacies.

### Trimethoprim (for urinary tract infections)

- Accredited pharmacists may supply a single course of 3 trimethoprim tablets for uncomplicated urinary tract infections in women aged 16 to 65 years.
- Cost typically $35–$50 (includes consultation and medication).
- Your doctor should be involved if symptoms are severe, recurrent, or do not resolve.

### Levonorgestrel Emergency Contraception (morning-after pill)

- Accredited pharmacists can supply levonorgestrel emergency contraception without a prescription.
- Funded; a prescription co-payment applies. Consultation fee varies ($35–$50).
- ECP-accredited pharmacists may also supply prochlorperazine for nausea associated with the ECP.

### Selected Oral Contraceptives

- Registered pharmacists who have completed oral contraceptive training can supply selected oral contraceptives (up to 6 months supply) without a doctor's prescription, provided the patient has been prescribed it within the last 3 years.
- Eligible contraceptives include:
  - Ethinylestradiol (35 micrograms or less) combined with levonorgestrel or norethisterone
  - Levonorgestrel, norethisterone, or desogestrel alone
- Requires an annual pharmacist consultation and a 3-yearly medical practitioner consultation.
- Not subsidised. Cost $35–$50 (includes consultation and medication).

### Sildenafil (for erectile dysfunction)

- Accredited pharmacists can sell sildenafil for treating erectile dysfunction in males aged 35 to 70 years.
- Maximum 12 tablets of 100 mg or less at a time.
- Full assessment required at first visit and every 12 months; briefer assessment for resupply within 12 months.
- Not subsidised. Your doctor may consider prescribing this on prescription if ongoing treatment is needed.

### Melatonin (for insomnia)

- Pharmacists can supply melatonin in modified-release form (Circadin, 2 mg or less) for treating primary insomnia.
- Restricted to adults aged 55 years and older, for up to 13 weeks.
- Not subsidised. Your doctor may consider other treatments or prescribe melatonin if extended use is needed.

### Chloramphenicol (for bacterial conjunctivitis)

- Community pharmacists may supply chloramphenicol eye drops or ointment for bacterial conjunctivitis.
- Not subsidised.

### Other Prescription Medicines Pharmacists May Supply

Under specific criteria, pharmacists can sell:

- **Calcipotriol (Daivonex) ointment** — for mild to moderate psoriasis previously diagnosed by a doctor
- **Adapalene cream or gel** — for comedo, papular, and pustular acne of the face, chest, or back
- **Famciclovir 500 mg** — 3-tablet pack for recurrent herpes labialis (cold sores)
- **Fluconazole 150 mg** — 1-tablet pack for vaginal candidiasis
- **Sumatriptan 50 mg** — up to 2 tablets for acute migraine with or without aura, in patients with a stable, well-established pattern of symptoms

These are not subsidised.

## Monitoring and Management Services

### Warfarin Monitoring

- Certain pharmacies provide point-of-care INR monitoring to support patients on warfarin.
- Accredited pharmacists perform INR testing and may adjust the warfarin dose according to a standing order from the patient's GP.
- Results are sent to the general practice in the same way as a laboratory result.
- Your doctor will set up this service and must remain involved in overall warfarin management.

### Gout Management (CPGMS)

- Accredited pharmacists provide serum urate point-of-care testing and dose escalation of allopurinol to reduce uric acid levels.
- Carried out according to a defined standing order from the primary care practitioner.
- Your doctor initiates allopurinol prescribing and the service supports ongoing dose optimisation.

### Medicines Use Review (MUR) / Medicines Management Service (MMS)

- Longer consultations to address intentional or unintentional non-adherence with medicines.
- Focuses on understanding medicines, setting goals, and improving health outcomes.
- Your doctor can refer patients by writing "Please consider for Medicines Adherence Support" on the next prescription.

### Medicines Adherence Support Service (MASS)

- For patients with long-term conditions who have identified medicine adherence problems.
- The pharmacist assesses eligibility and develops an agreed medicines adherence plan.
- Provided by all community pharmacies for regular patients.

## Other Pharmacy Services

### Smoking Cessation

- Some pharmacies provide funded smoking cessation support, including behavioural support and nicotine replacement therapy (NRT).
- Pharmacists can supply up to 3 months of NRT (nicotine patches, gum, lozenges) without a prescription.
- Your doctor may consider prescribing varenicline or bupropion for additional support.

### Vaccinations

Accredited pharmacist vaccinators can administer various vaccines, including:

- **Influenza** — funded for eligible patients (aged 65+, pregnant women, and those meeting specific criteria)
- **COVID-19** — funded
- **Measles, mumps, rubella (MMR)** — funded for eligible people aged 16–49 years
- **Diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis (Tdap/Boostrix)** — not funded; available to those aged 18+ (or pregnant women aged 13+)
- **Zoster vaccine (Zostavax)** — for shingles prevention in those aged 50+; not funded
- **Human papillomavirus (HPV)** — available
- **Meningococcal** — available for those aged 16+; not funded

### Hepatitis C Test and Treat

- Selected community pharmacies offer free Hepatitis C screening (HCV antibody fingerprick test, results in 5 minutes) for people aged 16+ who self-identify as at risk.
- Patients who test positive are managed by the pharmacist with further testing and support from a clinical nurse specialist.
- Those suitable for treatment may receive Maviret (glecaprevir/pibrentasvir) under a standing order.
- The patient's GP or nurse practitioner is informed of a positive test with patient consent.

### Opioid Substitution Treatment

- Community pharmacies can dispense methadone and buprenorphine with naloxone to opioid-dependent patients under prescriptions from authorised prescribers within specialist addiction services.
- Your doctor will coordinate with the specialist addiction service for prescribing.

### Medicines and Needle Disposal

- Any unwanted or expired medicines, needles, syringes, and lancets can be returned to the pharmacy for safe disposal at no charge.

### Compounded Oral Liquid Formulations

- Some pharmacies prepare compounded oral liquid medicines (e.g., for patients who cannot swallow tablets).
- Your doctor should prescribe according to standardised New Zealand formulations to avoid dosing errors.

## When to See Your Doctor

Pharmacy services complement but do not replace GP care. Discuss with your doctor if:

- Symptoms are severe, worsening, or do not respond to pharmacy treatment
- You need a prescription medicine not available through pharmacy supply
- You have multiple health conditions or take several medicines (potential interactions)
- You require ongoing management of a chronic condition
