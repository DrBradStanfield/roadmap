# Sexual Health in Youth

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how doctors approach sexual health discussions and assessments with young people, including STI screening, safety, and contraception.

---

## Background

Whether and how much to discuss sexual health varies between individual young people depending on their age, maturity, and reason for the consultation.

- Individual, peer, family, and cultural factors influence the nature and extent of sexual behaviour in adolescence. There is enormous variation within adolescent age groups in terms of knowledge and experience.
- Adolescent concerns about sexuality are usually to do with relationships and communication, rather than the risk of disease or pregnancy.
- Do not assume heterosexuality — some young people are attracted to people of the same sex or both sexes.

**Syphilis note:** There is an ongoing syphilis outbreak, with increased cases among young women (aged 15–44) and pregnant women. Routine syphilis and HIV testing is recommended for sexually active young people, especially women of reproductive age.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will approach this consultation in stages.

### Confidentiality

Your doctor will explain the limits of confidentiality upfront:

- Your information is kept private and confidential within the treating team, unless there are concerns for your safety or you consent to sharing it.
- Parents or caregivers do not have automatic access to your health information.
- If there are serious safety concerns, others may need to be involved — but your doctor will discuss this with you.

### General Questions (Younger Adolescents)

For younger adolescents presenting with an unrelated issue, your doctor may ask a few general questions to understand context. You do not have to answer anything you are not comfortable with. This may include:

- Whether you have ever had a sexual relationship.
- Questions about relationships and how you are feeling about them.
- Whether you have questions about attraction or sexuality — it is normal for some people to feel attracted to people of the same sex or both sexes.

### In-Depth Sexual History (If Presenting with a Sexual Health Issue)

If you are asking for an STI check or similar, your doctor will take a more detailed sexual history. This may include:

- Age of first intercourse.
- Number of partners, their gender, and duration of the relationship.
- Contraception and condom use.
- Any history of pregnancy, STIs, or current STI symptoms.
- Areas of risk — substance use, unsafe sex, or exploitative relationships.

### Sexual Safety and Consent

Your doctor may ask about sexual safety. This is a routine part of a sexual health check and not a sign that anything is assumed about your situation. Questions may include:

- Have you ever been touched in a sexual way that you did not ask for or like?
- Have you ever felt unsafe in a sexual relationship?
- Have you ever felt pressured into sex?
- Have you ever had sex in exchange for money, a gift, or another favour?

**Consent:** Consent means both people clearly say and show "yes." Anyone can change their mind at any time, and their partner must respect that. People cannot consent to sex if they are unconscious, drunk, or high on drugs. The legal age of consent in New Zealand is 16 years.

### STI Screening

Annual chlamydia testing is recommended for sexually active young people, regardless of symptoms. Your doctor may consider:

- A self-collected swab (females) or first void urine sample (males) for chlamydia and gonorrhoea.
- Syphilis and HIV blood testing, particularly for women of reproductive age.

STI risk is increased if you are under 25 years and in the past year have had more than two sexual partners, a new sexual partner in the last three months, an STI, or a sexual partner with an STI.

### HPV Vaccination

Your doctor will discuss HPV (human papillomavirus) vaccination, which protects against the most common cause of cervical cancer and other HPV-related conditions.

### Physical Examination

If a physical examination is needed:

- Your doctor will always offer a chaperone.
- They will explain what they are going to do before doing it.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider:

- **Contraception advice** — discussing options appropriate for your age and circumstances.
- **STI treatment** — if an infection is identified, your doctor will prescribe appropriate antibiotics or antiviral medication and advise on partner notification.
- **Chlamydia** — if detected, treatment is typically a single dose of azithromycin or a short course of doxycycline.
- **HPV vaccination** — if not yet completed, this can be arranged.
- **Safety planning** — if sexual safety concerns are identified, your doctor will discuss next steps with you and may refer to additional support services.
- **Referral** — for complex sexual health concerns or if sexual abuse is suspected, your doctor will arrange specialist assessment.
