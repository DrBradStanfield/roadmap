# Home Oxygen Therapy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) at home — who it is for, when it helps, and what assessment involves.

---

## What Is Home Oxygen Therapy?

The goal of oxygen therapy is to manage hypoxia (low blood oxygen) and improve longevity. When used for more than 16 hours per day in people with hypoxia, oxygen therapy can:

- Improve life expectancy
- Improve cardiac function
- Increase exercise capacity
- Improve quality of life and cognition

**Important:** Oxygen therapy is not an effective treatment for breathlessness (dyspnoea) on its own. Only a minority of people with hypoxia who receive home oxygen report a reduction in shortness of breath.

---

## Who May Benefit

Your doctor will assess whether you meet the criteria for long-term oxygen therapy. Assessment is only performed when your respiratory condition is stable — at least 6 weeks after any acute respiratory illness or hospital discharge.

People who benefit most:

- Those with a blood oxygen level (PaO2) below 7.3 kPa, which corresponds to an oxygen saturation below 88%
- Those with a PaO2 up to 8 kPa (oxygen saturation up to 91%) if there is also evidence of end organ damage, such as:
  - Haematocrit (red blood cell concentration) above 0.55
  - Signs of cor pulmonale (strain on the right side of the heart), shown on an ECG or echocardiogram

---

## Assessment Criteria

Your doctor may consider referring you for a domiciliary oxygen assessment if:

- You have a confirmed condition causing low oxygen levels, with a resting oxygen saturation below 90% when your condition is as well managed as possible
- You are not a current smoker, and if you previously smoked (including vaping), you have stopped for at least 6 weeks

For people with end-stage heart failure, your doctor will also need to ensure your heart failure is optimally managed and may seek advice from a cardiologist before proceeding.

For people in a palliative situation, additional considerations apply — your doctor will guide you through this.

---

## What Assessment Involves

An outpatient clinic appointment is arranged to:

- Measure an **arterial blood gas (ABG)** — a blood test that accurately measures oxygen and carbon dioxide levels
- Assess your overall suitability for long-term oxygen therapy at home

Your doctor will arrange this referral on your behalf.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider:

- Referring you for a formal domiciliary oxygen assessment if you meet the criteria above
- Optimising treatment of the underlying condition causing hypoxia before assessment
- If oxygen is prescribed, guidance on how many hours per day to use it (typically more than 16 hours for maximum benefit)
- For breathlessness without hypoxia, your doctor will explore other treatment pathways rather than oxygen
