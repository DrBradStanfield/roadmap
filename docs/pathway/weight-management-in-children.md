# Weight Management in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of overweight and obesity in children. Management is multifactorial and should be considered within the family context.

---

## Background

In New Zealand, 1 in 3 children are overweight or obese at the B4 School Check (aged 4 to 5 years). Overweight and obesity disproportionately affects Pacific and Māori children, and those from less affluent backgrounds.

Obesity tends to track from childhood into adulthood, with increasing likelihood if there is parental obesity or maternal smoking exposure in utero.

**Update (December 2025):** Semaglutide (Wegovy) is now available as an unfunded adjuvant treatment for obesity. It is approved for adolescents aged 12 years and older with obesity (BMI above the 98th percentile) and a body weight of 60 kg or more. Given the balance of risk, benefit, and limited long-term data in this age group, specialist paediatricians generally recommend use only in those with severe obesity at or above the 99th percentile.

## Red Flags — Discuss Urgently with Your Doctor

Seek prompt assessment if a child with obesity has:

- Symptoms of diabetes (polyuria, polydipsia, nocturia, secondary bed wetting)
- Significantly elevated blood pressure (above 95th percentile for age)
- HbA1c > 40 mmol/mol or fasting glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L
- Features suggesting an underlying medical cause (see Genetic or Endocrine Causes below)

## Assessment

Your healthcare provider will assess your child's weight status by:

### 1. Measuring Height, Weight, and BMI

Height and weight should be measured at all routine visits, especially once the regular Well Child programme finishes at age 5. BMI ranges for children differ from adult ranges — for example, a BMI of 19 is in the obesity range for a 4-year-old.

**Weight classification for children aged 5–15 years (BMI-for-age percentile):**

| BMI-for-age percentile | Weight status |
|---|---|
| ≤ 2% | Underweight for age |
| 2% to 91% | Healthy weight for age |
| 91% to 98% | Slightly unhealthy weight (overweight) |
| > 98% | Very unhealthy weight (obese) |

### 2. Family History

Your doctor will ask about family history of:

- Unhealthy weight in parents and siblings
- Premature cardiovascular disease (men < 55 years, women < 65 years)
- Dyslipidaemia
- Type 2 diabetes
- Hypertension
- Obstructive sleep apnoea

### 3. Weight-Related Health Problems

Your doctor will ask about:

- Type 2 diabetes symptoms
- Snoring or sleep apnoea
- Polyuria, polydipsia, nocturia, or secondary bed wetting
- Asthma, breathing difficulties, or exercise intolerance
- In girls: menstrual irregularities, hirsutism, or severe acne (may indicate PCOS)
- Joint problems (e.g., slipped femoral epiphysis)
- Psychosocial issues related to weight: isolation, bullying, depression, anxiety, low self-esteem, school avoidance
- Medications that may contribute to weight gain (e.g., antidepressants, risperidone, corticosteroids)

### 4. Food and Lifestyle History

Your doctor will ask about:

- **Diet:** Usual eating pattern, takeaways, portion sizes, sugar-sweetened drinks, fruit and vegetable intake
- **Physical activity:** Time spent outdoors, intensity of exercise
- **Sedentary activity:** Screen time (TV, games, smartphones, tablets); whether devices are in the bedroom
- **Sleep:** Whether the child has a regular bedtime and gets enough sleep

**Recommended sleep duration over 24 hours:**

| Age group | Recommended sleep |
|---|---|
| Infants | 12–15 hours |
| Toddlers | 11–14 hours |
| Preschoolers | 10–13 hours |
| 6–13 years | 9–11 hours |
| 14–17 years | 8–10 hours |

### 5. Physical Examination

Your healthcare provider will:

- Measure blood pressure using an appropriately sized cuff (a cuff that is too small gives a falsely high reading)
- Examine the nose and throat for causes of sleep apnoea
- Check the abdomen for hepatomegaly
- Perform an orthopaedic check (gait, hips, knees)
- Look for signs of genetic or endocrine causes (see below)

**Blood pressure screening values requiring further evaluation (mmHg):**

| Age (years) | Boys systolic | Boys diastolic | Girls systolic | Girls diastolic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 98 | 52 | 98 | 54 |
| 2 | 100 | 55 | 101 | 58 |
| 3 | 101 | 58 | 102 | 60 |
| 4 | 102 | 60 | 103 | 62 |
| 5 | 103 | 63 | 104 | 64 |
| 6 | 105 | 66 | 105 | 67 |
| 7 | 106 | 68 | 106 | 68 |
| 8 | 107 | 69 | 107 | 69 |
| 9 | 107 | 70 | 108 | 71 |
| 10 | 108 | 72 | 109 | 72 |
| 11 | 110 | 74 | 111 | 74 |
| 12 | 113 | 75 | 114 | 75 |
| ≥ 13 | 120 | 80 | 120 | 80 |

If the first BP reading is above the screening value, two additional readings are taken and averaged. If the averaged reading exceeds the screening limit, it is compared against the 95th percentile for age and height.

### 6. Genetic or Endocrine Causes

Your doctor will consider an underlying medical cause if the child has:

- Short stature or low height velocity
- Dysmorphic or syndromic features
- Delayed growth and sexual maturation
- Undescended testes
- Cushingoid features (e.g., buffalo hump, striae)
- **Acanthosis nigricans** — velvety, light-brown-to-black markings on the neck, underarms, or groin, suggesting insulin resistance
- Features of polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS)

## Investigations

Your healthcare provider may recommend the following investigations, depending on age and risk factors:

| Test | When | Abnormal result | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blood pressure | Age 5–10 years at initial assessment; age 10–15 years annually | > 95th percentile for age | Repeat to confirm; specialist assessment if persistently elevated |
| HbA1c or fasting glucose | Every 2 years from age 10 years | HbA1c > 40 mmol/mol; fasting glucose ≥ 7.0 mmol/L (diabetes), 5.5–6.9 mmol/L (borderline/IFG) | Specialist assessment; urgent if symptomatic |
| Urine protein:creatinine ratio | Annually if overweight/obese with hypertension, abnormal lipids, or abnormal HbA1c | Elevated result | Re-confirm with 3 consecutive early morning samples; discuss with specialist if persistently elevated |
| Lipids (fasting preferred) | Every 2 years from age 10 years | Total cholesterol > 5.2 or LDL > 3.4 mmol/L | Intensive lifestyle management; specialist assessment if remains elevated after 6 months |
| Liver function tests | Every 2 years from age 5 years | AST/ALT > 60 units/L | Intensive lifestyle management; repeat in 3 months. If > 100 units/L, specialist assessment |
| OSA questionnaire (for children who snore) | Every year from age 2 years | Positive result | Trial nasal steroids if rhinitis; specialist ENT assessment if positive |

*Note: HbA1c is not well validated in children and may be inaccurate in some conditions (e.g., haemoglobinopathy).*

## Management

### Starting the Conversation

Discussions about weight should be supportive, kind, and compassionate. Children from age 10–12 years can be included directly.

- The goal is framed as "achieving healthy growth," not losing weight
- Labelling words (obese, overweight, fat, chubby) are avoided
- If a child is not a healthy weight, the focus is on growing into their weight — **dieting is not recommended for children**

### Lifestyle Approach

Your doctor will recommend long-term behavioural changes:

- **Healthy sleep habits** — adequate sleep duration for the child's age
- **Healthy eating** — reduce sugar-sweetened drinks, improve meal patterns
- **Increased physical activity** — more time outdoors and active play
- **Less sedentary time** — limit screen time, remove devices from bedrooms

A goal-setting tool may be used to help children and families make sustainable changes.

### Monitoring

Your healthcare provider will recall your child every 3–6 months for:

- Height, weight, and BMI monitoring
- Repeat investigations where relevant
- Review of comorbidities and complications
- Discussion of lifestyle progress

---

## When Specialist Assessment May Be Recommended

Your doctor may refer for specialist (paediatric) assessment if:

- The child is in the very unhealthy weight range (obese) with a comorbidity
- Blood pressure is persistently > 95th percentile for age
- HbA1c > 40 mmol/mol or abnormal fasting glucose (urgent if symptomatic)
- Lipids remain significantly abnormal after 6 months of diet and exercise
- Liver enzymes (AST/ALT) remain > 60 units/L after 3 months of intensive lifestyle management
- A possible underlying medical cause is suspected (endocrine or syndromal)
- Urine protein:creatinine ratio remains persistently elevated
- Positive OSA questionnaire (ENT/otolaryngology referral)
- Psychological stressors are contributing to, or resulting from, the child's unhealthy weight (child and youth mental health referral)
