# Pilonidal Disease

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Pilonidal disease involves hairs burrowing into the natal cleft (the crease between the buttocks), leading to sinus formation, cyst development, or abscess. Management depends on the stage and severity.

---

## Background

- Pilonidal disease is caused by hairs burrowing into the natal cleft, forming a tract (pilonidal sinus). This may lead to a painless pilonidal cyst, which can become infected and form a pilonidal abscess.
- It typically affects young adults after puberty and is rare after age 40. It is more common in men than women.
- Risk factors include coarse or curly hair in the natal cleft, obesity, poor hygiene, prolonged sitting, and certain skin conditions.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

- Pain, swelling, and redness in the natal cleft area
- A painless lump (cyst)
- Systemic symptoms such as fever, malaise, or nausea
- Clear discharge (suggesting sinus formation)
- Pus or bloody discharge

Note: there may be no symptoms at all if only a sinus has formed.

Your doctor will examine for:

- A tender, swollen lump with surrounding redness (indicating abscess)
- Pus or bloody discharge
- Systemic signs such as fever or sepsis
- Visible or lumpy tracts in the skin (in chronic or recurrent disease)

---

## Management

Management depends on how the condition presents:

### Pilonidal Abscess (acute infected collection)

Your doctor will arrange urgent general surgery assessment for prompt surgical drainage. This is the definitive treatment for a pilonidal abscess.

### Chronic Pilonidal Disease (recurrent or persistent sinus)

Your doctor will refer for non-acute general surgery assessment for planned surgical management.

### Antibiotics

Antibiotics are not routinely used for pilonidal abscess (drainage is the treatment). Your doctor may consider antibiotics if there is:

- Cellulitis without abscess formation, or
- Systemic sepsis alongside abscess drainage, particularly in patients with prosthetic implants, heart valve disease, immunosuppression, or diabetes

If antibiotics are indicated, **amoxicillin + clavulanic acid (Augmentin)** is the preferred choice.

### Asymptomatic Pilonidal Disease

If your condition is not causing problems, active treatment is not required. Your doctor may advise:

- Good hygiene of the natal cleft area
- Avoiding tight clothing
- Maintaining a healthy weight
- Regular removal of hair from the cleft (e.g., shaving, laser, or depilatory cream)
- Smoking cessation, which supports healing and reduces risk of complications
