# IV Morphine for Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the safe administration of IV morphine in children and infants, including dosing, monitoring, and when to escalate care.

---

## Red Flags — Clinical Emergency

Stop giving morphine and treat as an emergency if:

- Respiratory depression in infants under 1 year of age
- Child is deeply sedated — AVPU score not responding to pain stimulus, or unresponsive
- Respiratory rate at or below minimum safe parameters

**Emergency response:** Stimulate the child, assist ventilation with bag, mask, and oxygen, and consider reversing morphine with naloxone. Arrange urgent transfer to hospital by ambulance.

---

## Background

IV morphine is used for significant pain in children when other routes are insufficient. It requires careful weight-based dosing, correct dilution, and close monitoring of sedation level and respiratory rate throughout.

Your doctor will assess whether IV morphine is appropriate based on the child's age, weight, alertness, and respiratory status before any doses are given.

---

## Assessment Before Each Dose

Your doctor checks three parameters before giving each increment:

**1. Pain score** — Pain must be greater than 4 out of 10 (on a standard numerical pain scale) to justify giving a dose.

**2. AVPU score** — A quick neurological assessment:
- **A**lert
- Responds to **V**oice
- Responds to **P**ain (purposeful or non-purposeful movement)
- **U**nresponsive

The child must be Alert or responding to Voice before a dose is given.

**3. Respiratory rate** — Must be above the minimum before each increment:
- Age 1–8 years: greater than 18 breaths per minute
- Age over 8 years: greater than 12 breaths per minute

---

## Management

### Dose

Your doctor may consider the following weight-based dosing:

- **Children 6 months and older:** 20 micrograms/kg per increment, up to a total of 100 micrograms/kg (0.1 mg/kg)
- **Infants under 6 months:** 10 micrograms/kg per increment, up to a total of 100 micrograms/kg (0.1 mg/kg)
- **Maximum dose (any age):** 10 mg (adult ceiling)

Doses are given in increments, not all at once. Your doctor waits 5 minutes between increments and reassesses pain, AVPU, and respiratory rate before giving the next increment.

### Dilution

Your doctor will prepare and check the syringe carefully, with a second professional verifying the dose and calculation.

**Children older than 6 months:**
Dilute 1 mL of 10 mg/mL morphine with 9 mL normal saline 0.9% → total 10 mL (1 mL = 1 mg = 1000 micrograms). Give in 20 micrograms/kg increments.

**Infants under 6 months:**
Prepare as above, then take 1 mL from that syringe and add a further 9 mL saline 0.9% → discard the first syringe (1 mL = 0.1 mg = 100 micrograms). Give in 10 micrograms/kg increments.

### Naloxone (reversal agent)

Naloxone must be available before morphine is given. It reverses morphine if the child becomes excessively sedated.

- Route: IV, IM, or subcutaneous
- Dose: 2 micrograms/kg
- Repeat every 1–2 minutes up to a maximum of five doses

### Monitoring

The child must be closely monitored throughout, with oxygen available. Children given opioids must be watched for respiratory depression. If ambulance transport is needed, monitoring continues during transfer.

### When to escalate

Your doctor will discuss with the acute paediatric registrar or paediatric anaesthetist if:

- AVPU or respiratory rate does not meet safe parameters but the child's pain score is still greater than 4 out of 10
- The child is not alert and/or not responding to voice and/or respiratory rate falls below guidelines — do not give further opioid increments; arrange urgent hospital transfer by ambulance
