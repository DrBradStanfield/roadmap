# Acute Neurosurgery Assessment

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers conditions that require emergency neurosurgical assessment. These are serious, potentially life-threatening situations. If you or someone you know has any of the conditions below with a rapidly deteriorating clinical condition, call 111 or go to the emergency department immediately.

---

## When Neurosurgery Assessment Is Needed

A rapidly deteriorating clinical condition combined with any of the following warrants urgent neurosurgical assessment:

- **Brain tumour** — imaging suggestive of a malignant tumour, or a large intracranial tumour
- **Intracranial bleeding** — large intracerebral or intracerebellar haematoma (bleeding into the brain)
- **Subarachnoid haemorrhage** — bleeding around the brain, often presenting as a sudden severe "thunderclap" headache
- **Acute hydrocephalus** — build-up of fluid in the brain, including in people with existing shunts
- **Cranial (head) trauma** — significant head injuries
- **Pituitary apoplexy or acute visual failure** — sudden deterioration related to a pituitary tumour causing loss of vision or hormonal collapse
- **Intracranial infection** — serious infection within the skull (e.g., brain abscess, meningitis with surgical component)
- **Spinal infection with neurological impairment** — spinal infection causing weakness, numbness, or loss of bladder/bowel control
- **Open or leaking congenital myelomeningocele** — a spinal birth defect requiring closure

**Important:** These conditions require emergency assessment. Your doctor will contact the on-call neurosurgical team directly and arrange hospital admission.

---

## What to Expect

Your doctor will assess the situation and, if warranted, contact the neurosurgical team for immediate advice and arrange urgent transfer to hospital. Do not wait for a scheduled appointment — these conditions are time-critical.

If you have spine trauma (injury to the spine from an accident or fall), this is managed by orthopaedics, not neurosurgery.

---

## When to Call 111

Call an ambulance immediately if:

- Sudden severe headache described as "the worst of your life" (possible subarachnoid haemorrhage)
- Sudden loss of consciousness or collapse
- Rapid neurological deterioration: sudden weakness, confusion, slurred speech, seizure
- Sudden loss of vision
- Signs of serious head injury after trauma

Do not drive yourself to hospital in these situations.
