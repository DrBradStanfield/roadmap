# Newborn Hearing Screening and Early Intervention

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the newborn hearing screening programme — what it involves, what the results mean, and what happens next if hearing loss is identified.

---

## Aims of the Programme

The Universal Newborn Hearing Screening and Early Intervention Programme aims to ensure:

- All eligible babies are screened (with parental consent) by **1 month of age**, either in hospital or in the community
- Babies referred from screening complete audiological diagnostic assessment by **3 months of age**
- All babies identified with hearing loss receive family-centred intervention services by **3 to 6 months of age**

---

## Screening Results

### Pass (Clear Response) — No Risk Factors

If your baby passes screening and has no risk factors for late-onset or progressive hearing loss, screening is complete.

### Pass (Clear Response) — With Risk Factors

If your baby passes screening but has one or more risk factors for progressive hearing loss, an audiology appointment will be arranged at the appropriate time for that risk factor.

### No Clear Response

A "no clear response" result leads to a referral to the audiology service by the hearing screening service. This does not necessarily mean your baby has hearing loss — further testing is needed to find out.

---

## Risk Factors for Late-Onset or Progressive Hearing Loss

Some babies who pass the initial screen still need follow-up audiology testing (typically otoacoustic emissions at 18 months) because of risk factors for hearing loss that can develop or worsen over time.

**Risk factors requiring OAE testing at 18 months:**

- Continuous ventilation for more than 5 days, including:
  - Intermittent positive pressure ventilation (IPPV) or high frequency ventilation (HFV)
  - Nitric oxide
  - Extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO)
  - Severe persistent pulmonary hypertension
  - *Note: CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure) is excluded from this category*
- Severe birth asphyxia (Sarnat stage 2 or 3, cooled)
- Brain haemorrhage (grade 4 with post-haemorrhagic hydrocephalus)
- Ototoxic medications at above-therapeutic levels (confirmed by blood test)
- Syndromes associated with hearing loss, including but not limited to:
  - Pierre Robin sequence
  - Stickler syndrome
  - Goldenhar syndrome
  - CHARGE syndrome
  - Waardenburg syndrome
  - Pendred syndrome
- Congenital toxoplasmosis
- Congenital rubella

**Risk factors requiring audiologist triage (case-by-case):**

- Atresia (ear canal malformation)
- Jaundice at or above transfusion levels — if the baby was already screened before jaundice reached this level, rescreening should be offered before considering audiology referral
- Head and brain trauma

**Risk factors with specific audiological pathways:**

- Down syndrome
- Cleft palate
- Congenital cytomegalovirus (CMV)
- Meningitis

---

## If Hearing Loss Is Identified

If audiology assessment results in a diagnosis of hearing loss, a multidisciplinary team will develop an action plan for the baby and family. The team typically includes:

- Audiologist
- Paediatrician
- Ministry of Education Advisor on Deaf Children
- Southern Cochlear Implant Programme
- Ear, nose, and throat (ENT) specialist
- Ophthalmologist
- Other providers as needed (e.g., speech-language therapist, geneticist)

Your doctor will coordinate referrals and support your family through this process.

---

## At the 6-Week Check

Your doctor will confirm whether the newborn hearing screening has been completed. If it has not been done, they can arrange for screening to be offered to you.
