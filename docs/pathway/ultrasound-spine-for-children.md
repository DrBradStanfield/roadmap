# Ultrasound Spine for Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers which children with sacral dimples or pits may need a spinal ultrasound, and what features prompt urgent assessment.

---

## Background

Sacral dimples and pits are common findings in newborns. Most are simple and require no investigation. However, certain features increase the likelihood of an underlying spinal abnormality (such as spinal dysraphism or tethered cord), and your doctor may recommend an ultrasound of the spine to look further.

Spinal ultrasound is only technically feasible in young infants — generally under 6 weeks of age. After this age, the spinal bones harden and ultrasound cannot adequately image the spinal cord; a different imaging method would be needed if investigation is still required.

---

## Features That May Prompt Urgent Investigation

Your doctor may consider referring for an urgent spinal ultrasound (typically within 2 weeks) if your baby is **younger than 6 weeks** and has a sacral dimple or pit with any of the following features:

- The base of the dimple cannot be seen
- The dimple is larger than 5 mm in diameter
- The dimple is located more than 2.5 cm above the anal margin
- The dimple is associated with cutaneous markings (birthmarks or skin discolouration), hairy patches, skin tags, or fatty lumps
- A duplicated (split) gluteal cleft
- More than one dimple

The presence of any of these features increases the chance that imaging will show something that needs to be followed up.

---

## When Imaging Is Less Likely to Be Needed

Your doctor may advise that imaging is not routinely needed if:

- The dimple is **simple and isolated** — meaning the base can be seen, the dimple is small, and it sits within the gluteal cleft and within 2.5 cm of the anal margin
- These simple dimples are very common and almost always have no underlying significance

If your baby is **older than 6 weeks**, ultrasound is not technically possible. If imaging is still considered necessary, your doctor will seek paediatric advice about alternative options (such as MRI).

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will examine the dimple or pit, considering:

- Location — how far above the anal margin, and whether it is within or outside the gluteal cleft
- Size — width of the dimple opening
- Whether the base is visible
- Surrounding skin changes — any birthmarks, hair tufts, skin tags, or skin-covered lumps over the lower spine

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following based on their findings:

- **Urgent ultrasound referral** — if one or more concerning features are present in a baby under 6 weeks old
- **Paediatric advice** — if the baby is older than 6 weeks, or if there are soft tissue masses or skin changes over the spine in the midline; a paediatrician will advise on the most appropriate next step
- **Reassurance and monitoring** — for simple isolated dimples within the gluteal cleft and within 2.5 cm of the anal margin, which are not associated with any other features; these do not typically require investigation

In some cases, families may choose to pursue privately funded imaging even when it does not meet criteria for publicly funded investigation — this is a decision to make with your doctor.
