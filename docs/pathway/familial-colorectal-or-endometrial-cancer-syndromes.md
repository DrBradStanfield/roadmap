# Familial Colorectal or Endometrial Cancer Syndromes

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers how doctors assess and manage patients who may have a hereditary risk of colorectal or endometrial cancer, and when referral for genetic assessment is appropriate.

> **Important (February 2025):** Referral criteria for Genetic Health Service NZ (GHSNZ) have changed due to resource pressure. GHSNZ now only offers appointments to patients who have a cancer meeting the threshold for genetic testing, or who have a first-degree relative with a confirmed cancer predisposition gene. Private genetic assessment is an option for those who meet criteria based on family history alone.

---

## Background

Around 10–15% of all colorectal cancer patients have a significant family history. Only about 5% of all colorectal cancer is caused by an identified inherited predisposition gene mutation.

### Familial Colorectal Cancer Syndromes

**Lynch syndrome**
- Caused by mutations in one of 5 genes: MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2, and EPCAM
- Colon cancer risk by age 70: 20–47% in male carriers; 10–37% in female carriers (depending on gene)
- Female carriers: up to 30% endometrial cancer risk and up to 15% ovarian cancer risk by age 70
- Small increased risk of other cancers (gastric, urothelial, small bowel)
- Autosomal dominant — each child of a carrier has a 1 in 2 chance of inheriting the mutation

**Familial adenomatous polyposis (FAP)**
- Caused by mutations in the APC gene
- Over 95% lifetime risk of colon cancer in carriers
- 1 in 5 people have a new mutation with no prior family history
- More than 100 polyps typically develop from around age 15; colon cancer usually develops between ages 20–40
- Increased risk of duodenal cancer, desmoid tumour, duodenal adenoma, gastric fundic gland polyps
- Surgery and regular surveillance can significantly reduce morbidity and mortality
- Autosomal dominant

**MutYH-associated polyposis (MAP)**
- Caused by mutations in the MutYH gene
- 80% risk of colon cancer by age 70 without intervention
- Autosomal recessive — usually no family history beyond the affected sibship

**Other rare conditions**
- Peutz-Jeghers syndrome (STK11 gene)
- Juvenile polyposis (BMPR1A or SMAD4 gene)

Endometrial carcinoma is the most common gynaecological tumour worldwide and can be the presenting malignancy indicating an undiagnosed hereditary syndrome.

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess your current symptoms first, then take a family history. Hereditary cancer risk assessment is done after any active symptoms are managed.

### Step 1 — Current Symptoms

If you have current symptoms, your doctor will manage those first:
- Colorectal symptoms → assessed via the Colorectal Symptoms pathway
- Abnormal uterine bleeding → assessed via the Abnormal Uterine Bleeding pathway

### Step 2 — Family Tree

Your doctor will sketch a 3-generation family tree documenting all cancers, ages at diagnosis, and which family members were affected.

### Step 3 — Criteria for Genetic Assessment (GHSNZ)

Your doctor will check whether you meet the following criteria for referral to GHSNZ:

- Blood relative of a person with a known cancer-associated gene mutation
- Personal history of:
  - Endometrial carcinoma where tumour testing detected microsatellite instability (MSI) or abnormal MMR immunohistochemistry
  - Sarcoma of the endometrium (e.g., leiomyosarcoma) diagnosed before age 60
  - Endometrial carcinoma with features of Cowden syndrome (PTEN hamartoma syndrome): macrocephaly, hamartomatous colorectal polyps, oral mucosal papillomatosis, thyroid cancer, or renal cancer
  - Endometrial carcinoma diagnosed before age 50
  - Endometrial carcinoma plus a second Lynch syndrome-associated cancer
  - Endometrial carcinoma plus 1 or more first- or second-degree relatives with colorectal or endometrial cancer, with at least one diagnosed before age 50
  - Endometrial carcinoma plus 2 or more first- or second-degree relatives with a Lynch syndrome-associated cancer (any age)

### Step 4 — Criteria for Familial Gastrointestinal Cancer Service (FGICS)

Your doctor will also check indications for referral to FGICS:

- Personal or family history of both colorectal and endometrial cancer in the same individual
- Personal history of:
  - 2 or more primary cancers, or 2 or more Lynch syndrome-related cancers in the same individual
  - Colorectal cancer plus family history of 2 or more relatives with a Lynch syndrome-associated cancer
- Family history of 2 or more first- or second-degree relatives with colorectal or endometrial cancer, with at least 1 diagnosed before age 50

## Management

All referrals require an attached family history sketch and cancer pedigree.

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Colorectal symptoms | Request colonoscopy via Colorectal Symptoms pathway |
| Endometrial symptoms | Follow Abnormal Uterine Bleeding pathway |
| Meets criteria for genetic assessment | Refer to GHSNZ (note Feb 2025 restrictions — consider private if family history only) |
| Meets FGICS criteria | Refer to Familial Gastrointestinal Cancer Service |
| Moderate bowel cancer risk | Request colonoscopy for surveillance |
| Low bowel cancer risk, aged 60–74 | Discuss National Bowel Cancer Screening Program (NBCSP) |
| At risk for endometrial cancer syndrome | Consider gynaecology oncology referral |
