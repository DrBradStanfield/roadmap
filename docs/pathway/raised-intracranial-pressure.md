# Raised Intracranial Pressure (Palliative Care)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of raised intracranial pressure (raised ICP) in adults with cancer or other life-limiting illness. It is intended to guide your doctor — always discuss your symptoms and options with them.

---

## Who This Applies To

This pathway is for adult palliative patients. Raised ICP in this context is most commonly caused by:

- Primary brain tumours (especially in acute or advanced stages)
- Brain metastases (particularly from breast, bowel, and lung cancer)
- Bleeding into primary or metastatic brain tumours

---

## Symptoms Your Doctor Will Look For

Your doctor will consider raised intracranial pressure if you develop any of these new symptoms:

- Headache
- Unexplained nausea or vomiting
- Uncoordinated movements or balance problems (ataxia)
- Drowsiness, confusion, disorientation, vagueness, or changes in behaviour

> Note: Symptoms often come on gradually.

---

## Signs Your Doctor Will Check

Your doctor will examine for:

- Confusion or other changes in level of consciousness
- Cranial nerve problems (e.g., double vision, facial weakness)
- Papilloedema (swelling at the back of the eye — seen on fundoscopy)
- Raised blood pressure with a wide pulse pressure (a late sign)

---

## Other Causes to Consider

Your doctor will also consider whether symptoms could be due to:

- **Hypercalcaemia** or other metabolic disturbances — these can cause similar neurological symptoms without actually raising intracranial pressure

---

## Goals of Care Discussion

An important part of assessment is understanding your goals and wishes:

- If you cannot communicate your wishes, your doctor will refer to your advance care planning documents, or consult your enduring power of attorney, family/whānau, or carers
- If you have declined suddenly from a well state, reversible causes will be investigated and treated if appropriate
- If your short-term prognosis is poor, comfort-focused care may be most appropriate
- Your doctor will discuss all of this openly with you and your family

---

## Investigations

**If you have new symptoms and brain cancer has not previously been diagnosed**, your doctor will:

- Arrange a **CT head scan** to confirm the diagnosis
- If you are already under an oncology service: request acute oncology assessment
- If not yet under oncology: request acute general medicine assessment

---

## Management

### 1. Corticosteroids (if brain metastases are known)

If you are known to have brain metastases and symptoms suggest raised ICP, your doctor will consider starting or increasing corticosteroids:

**Dexamethasone** is the preferred treatment:
- Dose: 8–16 mg immediately, given orally or by subcutaneous injection
  - If given subcutaneously: injected slowly (it can sting); if the volume is large (> 2 mL), given as two separate injections at different sites
- Improvement is expected within 12–48 hours
- If no improvement after 72 hours, dexamethasone will be stopped
- Once symptoms stabilise, the dose is tapered to the lowest effective dose to minimise side effects

**If dexamethasone is not available:**
- Prednisone 100 mg orally as a one-off, then switch to daily dexamethasone

**Important:**
- If you have been on high-dose dexamethasone for more than 2 weeks, it cannot be stopped suddenly — a gradual dose reduction is needed to avoid withdrawal symptoms (including agitation)
- In palliative care, doses, indications, and routes of administration may be used in ways not listed in the product labelling (unapproved use) — this is normal practice and will be discussed with you

Your doctor will also seek urgent specialist advice (oncology or palliative care).

### 2. If Corticosteroids Are Not Working or Not Appropriate

If corticosteroids are ineffective, not tolerated, or symptoms are progressing, your doctor will shift focus to **comfort care**. This is guided by the Last Days of Life pathway.

### 3. Supportive Measures (All Patients)

Regardless of other treatments, your doctor will address:

- **Pain** — opioids may be needed for headache or other pain
- **Anxiety, distress, or agitation** — benzodiazepines may be considered

---

## When Your Doctor Will Seek Specialist Input

Your doctor will seek advice from oncology or palliative care specialists in the following situations:

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| New symptoms, already under oncology | Acute oncology assessment |
| New symptoms, not yet under oncology | Acute general medicine assessment |
| Known brain metastases with raised ICP symptoms | Urgent oncology or palliative care advice |
| Symptoms persist or advice needed | Oncology or palliative care advice |
| Children under 16 | Children's palliative care referral |
