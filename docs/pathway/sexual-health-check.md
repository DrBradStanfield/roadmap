# Sexual Health Check

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the initial approach to sexual health screening in adults — determining who to test, what to test for, and how to manage results.

> This information is for general education. A sexual health check must be done with your doctor or a trained clinician. Do not attempt self-diagnosis or treatment without professional guidance.

---

## Who Should Have a Sexual Health Check

Your doctor will offer a sexual health check if you:

- Request one
- Are at increased risk of a sexually transmitted infection (STI) — higher-risk behaviours include multiple sexual contacts, a recent new partner, a partner with an STI, or being sexually active under age 30
- Have possible signs or symptoms of an STI
- Are pregnant
- Are having a pregnancy termination or intrauterine device (IUD) inserted
- Have had a non-consensual sexual encounter

**HIV and syphilis blood tests are always arranged as part of a sexual health check.**

There is currently an ongoing syphilis outbreak, and doctors are encouraged to routinely test for syphilis in women of reproductive age, including as part of antenatal care.

---

## Sexual History

Your doctor will ask questions about your sexual health. This is routine and confidential. Typical questions include:

- Any symptoms or concerns
- When you last had sex and with how many partners
- The gender of your sexual contacts
- Whether sex involves vaginal, oral, or anal sex
- Condom use
- Whether you or any partners have had an STI before
- Use of alcohol or drugs around sex

For patients under 18, your doctor will also assess maturity, capacity to consent, and whether there is any risk of an abusive relationship. They will explain the limits of confidentiality clearly.

---

## Testing by Patient Group

### Women

**If no symptoms (asymptomatic):**
- Self-collected vaginal swab (PCR/NAAT) or first-catch urine sample for chlamydia, gonorrhoea, and trichomonas (if higher risk)
- Anal swab for chlamydia/gonorrhoea if anal sex occurs
- Blood tests for HIV and syphilis

**If symptomatic:**
Your doctor will take a full history (discharge, bleeding, pain, genital sores, rashes) and examine you (including internal examination). Additional tests may include bacterial vaginosis culture, herpes swab, and hepatitis serology.

---

### Men Who Have Sex with Women

**If no symptoms:**
- First-catch urine for chlamydia and gonorrhoea
- Blood tests for HIV and syphilis

**If symptomatic:**
Your doctor will examine the genitals and arrange appropriate swabs and blood tests.

---

### Men Who Have Sex with Men (MSM)

Routine testing every 3 months is recommended for sexually active MSM, or annually if not sexually active or in a monogamous relationship.

Tests include:
- First-catch urine for chlamydia and gonorrhoea
- Throat swab for chlamydia and gonorrhoea
- Anal swab for chlamydia and gonorrhoea
- Blood tests for HIV and syphilis

Testing is recommended regardless of reported condom use, as asymptomatic infection is common.

---

### Transgender, Non-Binary, and Intersex Patients

Your doctor will offer screening based on your anatomy, sexual practices, and personal preference. Examinations and sample collection can be adapted to your needs (e.g., self-collection, smaller instruments).

---

### Patients Who Use Drugs

Alcohol and drug use can increase STI risk. Injecting drugs also increases the risk of blood-borne infections. Annual STI and blood tests are recommended; if you engage in chemsex (use of drugs like methamphetamine before/during sex), 3-monthly testing is recommended.

---

## Recommended Testing Intervals

If you are concerned about a specific recent sexual exposure:

- **Chlamydia, gonorrhoea, trichomoniasis:** test 2 weeks after the exposure
- **HIV and syphilis:** test 3 months after the exposure
- **High-risk HIV exposure:** test at 6 weeks and again at 3 months

---

## Management

### Risk Reduction

Your doctor will discuss:
- Cervical screening if due
- HIV pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) if you or your partner is MSM or at elevated risk
- Doxy-PEP (doxycycline taken within 72 hours after sexual exposure) for eligible patients — generally gay and bisexual men and transgender women who have had a recent syphilis or bacterial STI diagnosis. Your doctor will assess eligibility and arrange regular follow-ups.

### Immunisation

Your doctor can discuss:
- HPV vaccine (Gardasil 9): funded up to age 27; may benefit older patients (unfunded)
- Hepatitis B vaccine if you are not immune

### Treatment

If you have symptoms, a positive test result, or have been in contact with someone with an STI, your doctor will treat you according to the relevant condition pathway (e.g., chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis, trichomoniasis, herpes, HIV, pelvic inflammatory disease).
