# Specific Learning Difficulties Support

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the initial approach to specific learning difficulties in children — understanding the conditions and the pathways to assessment and support.

---

## Background

Specific learning difficulties encompass a wide range of processing disorders, including:

- **Dyslexia** — difficulty with reading, writing, and spelling
- **Dyspraxia / Developmental Coordination Disorder (DCD)** — difficulty with physical coordination and motor planning
- **Sensory Processing Disorder** — difficulty processing and responding to sensory information

Hearing and vision testing is recommended for all children with learning difficulties, as undetected sensory impairment can contribute to or worsen learning problems.

Learning disorders are mostly managed through the education sector. Families who present to their GP may need advice on how to access Ministry of Education (MoE) support, or may be seeking additional support beyond what the education system offers.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor may consider:

- Ruling out underlying medical causes (hearing loss, vision problems, developmental delay, intellectual disability)
- Referring for a developmental or paediatric assessment if intellectual disability or a significant neurodevelopmental concern is suspected
- Coordinating with the school — input from teachers (via a school questionnaire or report) is typically required before a specialist paediatric referral can be accepted

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider:

- Advising families to speak with the child's school principal, special needs education coordinator (SENCO), or resource teacher of learning and behaviour (RTLB) as the first step
- Referring for non-acute paediatric assessment if intellectual disability or complex developmental concerns are present — a completed teacher/school report is usually required for this referral
- Discussing private assessment options (e.g., educational psychologists, SPELD NZ) where families want diagnostic clarity or support beyond what the education system provides

Most intervention and ongoing support is delivered through the education sector rather than the health system.
