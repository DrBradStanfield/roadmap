# Myocarditis and Pericarditis After COVID-19 Vaccination

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of suspected myocarditis or pericarditis in someone who has recently received a COVID-19 vaccine. It also applies to suspected cases following mpox vaccination.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care Immediately

Call 111 or go to the emergency department immediately if any of the following are present:

- Severe chest pain or chest pressure
- Significant shortness of breath at rest
- Collapse, loss of consciousness, or syncope
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat with instability
- Signs of heart failure (severe breathlessness, low blood pressure)

Do not wait for a GP appointment if you have any of the above.

---

## Background

Myocarditis (inflammation of the heart muscle) and pericarditis (inflammation of the sac surrounding the heart) are rare but recognised complications after COVID-19 vaccination.

Key facts your doctor will consider:

- Symptoms usually appear within **1 to 5 days** of vaccination, though onset can range from 0 to 98 days (median 2 days)
- The risk is higher in **adolescents and younger males**, and decreases with age
- More likely to occur after the **second dose** of a COVID-19 vaccine
- Most cases are **mild and self-limiting**, but myocarditis carries a short-term risk of serious arrhythmia and heart failure, so hospital assessment is important
- For context: COVID-19 infection itself carries a substantially higher risk of myocarditis than vaccination

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess you for myocarditis and/or pericarditis if you develop cardiac symptoms within hours to days of a COVID-19 vaccination.

### Cardiac symptoms to look for

- Chest pain — often sharp and pleuritic (worsened by breathing in); in pericarditis, typically improved by sitting forward
- Chest pressure or discomfort
- Palpitations
- Sweating (diaphoresis)
- Shortness of breath — after exercise, at rest, or when lying down
- Fainting or near-fainting (syncope)

### Non-specific symptoms that may also suggest this diagnosis

- Fatigue
- Abdominal pain
- Dizziness
- Swelling (oedema)
- Cough

Your doctor will also consider other causes for your symptoms, including COVID-19 infection itself.

### Physical examination

Your doctor will check for signs of cardiac compromise, including:

- Pericardial friction rub
- Pulsus paradoxus
- Distant heart sounds
- Elevated heart rate (tachycardia)
- Elevated breathing rate (tachypnoea)
- Low blood pressure (hypotension)
- Raised jugular venous pressure
- Enlarged liver (hepatomegaly)

### ECG

Your doctor will perform a 12-lead ECG. They will look for:

**Pericarditis ECG features:**
- Widespread concave (saddle-shaped) ST-segment elevation
- ST-segment depression in lead aVR
- PR-segment depression (best seen in leads II and V3)

**Myocarditis ECG features:**
- Sinus tachycardia with ST or T-wave abnormalities (most common)
- Premature atrial or ventricular complexes
- Atrial or ventricular arrhythmias
- AV conduction delays or bundle branch block

### Blood tests

Your doctor will arrange blood tests, including:

- **Serum troponin** — a marker of heart muscle injury
- **C-reactive protein (CRP)** — a marker of inflammation
- COVID-19 test if clinically appropriate

---

## Management

Your doctor will determine the most appropriate setting for your care based on how unwell you are, your ECG findings, and your blood test results.

### When hospital assessment is needed

Your doctor will arrange urgent hospital assessment if any of the following apply:

- You are acutely unwell
- You have more than mild symptoms, or symptoms are worsening
- There are abnormal findings on physical examination (see above)
- ECG shows features suggestive of pericarditis or myocarditis
- Troponin is raised

### Community management (if safe to do so)

If you are not acutely unwell, have mild symptoms, a normal or near-normal ECG, and blood tests can be available within 12 hours, your doctor may manage your initial assessment in the community. In this case:

- Stay with family or friends — do not be alone
- **Avoid all exercise** until you have been reviewed
- **Call 111 immediately** if your symptoms worsen while waiting for results
- Your doctor will review you within 12 hours of your blood tests

### If blood tests are reassuring

If your ECG and blood tests do not suggest myocarditis or pericarditis, your doctor will advise:

- Return immediately if new or worsening symptoms develop, or if pain does not respond to simple pain relief (NSAIDs)
- Avoid high-intensity exercise or competitive sport until symptoms have fully resolved
- Return for review in 1 to 2 days if symptoms are ongoing

---

## Treatment

Your doctor may consider the following treatments depending on the diagnosis:

- **Pericarditis:** Usually managed with NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen) until symptoms settle, and/or **colchicine** (for adults) until reviewed by a cardiologist
- **Myocarditis:** Requires hospital assessment and monitoring until the condition is confirmed to be resolving. Treatment is directed by the hospital team

Outcomes are better the earlier treatment is started.

---

## After Hospital Discharge

If you have been treated in hospital for myocarditis or pericarditis, your doctor will:

- Advise you to **avoid high-intensity exercise** for as long as your specialist recommends — this is important to prevent complications
- Advise you to **defer any further doses** of COVID-19 vaccine until specialist advice has been obtained
- Continue treatment with NSAIDs and/or colchicine as prescribed until specialist review
- Seek specialist advice about the timing and type of any further COVID-19 vaccination

If you are unsure about returning to exercise after discharge, your doctor can seek advice from your own specialist.
