# Curly Toes

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of curly toes in children — a common condition that usually resolves on its own.

---

## Background

Curly toes are a common childhood condition present at birth, caused by a flexion deformity that leads to malrotation of the toes. The 3rd, 4th, and 5th toes are most commonly affected.

The condition is generally bilateral (affects both feet) and does not usually worsen without treatment. Spontaneous correction may occur by age 2 years.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

- The child's age
- Any history of trauma to the feet or toes
- Whether developmental milestones are being met appropriately
- Family history of curly toes

Physical examination typically includes:

- Looking at the typical appearance of the curly toes
- Measuring the child's weight, height, and percentile for age
- Examining the feet and toes for rigidity or other deformity
- Checking for symmetric toe deformity on the opposite foot
- Looking for callus or signs of pressure on the toes
- Checking for footwear problems

X-rays are not indicated for this condition.

---

## Management

Most cases of curly toes do not need treatment or referral.

Your doctor may consider:

- **Reassurance** — curly toes will not impede early development or stop a child from walking. Taping, orthotics, and physiotherapy are not effective for this condition.
- **Photographic diary** — parents may be encouraged to keep a photographic record of the child's toes from birth to track progress.
- **Private podiatry assessment** — if corns or callus are a concern, a podiatrist may assist with symptomatic relief (this service is not publicly funded).
- **Referral to paediatric orthopaedics** — your doctor may refer if:
  - The condition persists past age 3 years and is a concern to parents
  - The toe deformity cannot be passively corrected
  - Callus formation or trophic nail changes are occurring due to end bearing (weight-bearing on the end of the toe)
  - The child complains of pain or has difficulty walking

Surgery, if needed, is best performed when the child is around 4 years of age.
