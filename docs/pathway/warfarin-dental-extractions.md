# Warfarin — Dental Extractions

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Patients taking warfarin generally do not need to stop their anticoagulant before a dental extraction. Stopping warfarin carries a risk of thrombo-embolism. Extractions can usually proceed safely as long as the INR is below 3.5, using local haemostatic measures.

---

## Key Principle

**Do not stop warfarin before dental extractions.** The risk of a blood clot (thrombo-embolism) from stopping warfarin outweighs the bleeding risk of the procedure, provided local haemostatic measures are used.

---

## Before the Extraction

Your doctor or dentist will:

- Check your INR within 24 hours of the procedure
  - **INR < 3.5:** Extraction can proceed
  - **INR > 3.5:** Surgery should be deferred; your GP will be consulted
- Ask if you are also taking aspirin — if so, aspirin is usually stopped 7 days before the extraction (your GP coordinates this)
- Encourage good oral hygiene with chlorhexidine mouthwash and smoking cessation before the procedure

---

## During the Extraction

Your dentist will:

- Give local anaesthetic as normal — warfarin is not a reason to skip this
- Provide infective endocarditis prophylaxis if you need it (e.g., certain heart valve conditions)
- Remove excessive granulation tissue from the socket
- Irrigate the socket with tranexamic acid solution (500 mg tablet crushed in 10 mL water)
- Consider placing oxidised cellulose or gelatine foam in the socket to aid clotting
- Suture (stitch) the socket closed
- Have you bite on gauze soaked in tranexamic acid solution

---

## After the Extraction

Your dentist will prescribe **tranexamic acid 500 mg tablets** for home use:

- Dissolve one tablet in 10 mL of water
- Use as a gentle mouthwash — **do not swallow**
- Use up to 4 times per day for 2 to 5 days

**Medications to avoid after extraction:**

- **NSAIDs** (e.g., ibuprofen, diclofenac) — they increase bleeding risk and interact with warfarin
- **Metronidazole** (an antibiotic) — it significantly increases warfarin effect and bleeding risk

**If antibiotics are prescribed for another reason:** they can affect your INR. Have your INR rechecked when the course is finished.

**If bleeding starts:** bite firmly on a pressure pack of gauze soaked in tranexamic acid solution for 15 minutes continuously.

---

## When Referral May Be Needed

If you are on warfarin **and** have additional medical conditions that increase your bleeding risk, your dentist may refer you for specialist dental assessment rather than proceeding in a general dental setting. Conditions that may trigger referral include:

- Liver disease
- Renal (kidney) failure
- Thrombocytopenia (low platelet count)

**A history of uncomplicated heart valve replacement on long-term warfarin alone is not a reason for referral.**
