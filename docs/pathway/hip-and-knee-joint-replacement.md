# Hip and Knee Joint Replacement

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment of patients being considered for hip or knee replacement surgery, including what your doctor will evaluate and what factors may affect your suitability for surgery.

> **Always discuss your symptoms and any medication changes with your doctor.** This information is a clinical reference, not a substitute for individual medical advice.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will carry out a detailed assessment to identify patients with the greatest need, ability to benefit from surgery, and who are fit for the procedure.

### 1. Non-surgical treatments first

Before you are considered for joint replacement, your doctor will check that suitable non-surgical treatments have been properly tried, as set out in the osteoarthritis pathway.

### 2. Pain history

Your doctor will ask about:

- Where the pain is located and whether it radiates
- Whether onset was gradual or sudden
- Whether weight-bearing, twisting or impact makes it worse
- Whether rest relieves the pain
- Whether pain disturbs sleep
- Whether pain is intermittent or constant
- Pain intensity from 0 to 10, where 10 is the worst pain you have experienced

### 3. Functional limitations

Your doctor will assess how the joint affects your daily life, including:

- How far you can walk (in metres and in time)
- Restrictions on activities such as putting on shoes and socks, getting in and out of a car, and cutting toenails
- Whether the knee locks or feels unstable
- Whether not having surgery would have significant consequences. For example, you may lose your job, need to move into care, or need a wheelchair.
- Impact on personal care and safety, including risk of falls

### 4. Treatment history

Your doctor will ask about:

- Regular pain medications you have been using
- Use of walking aids
- Physiotherapy received
- Acupuncture

### 5. Physical examination

**Hip examination:** your doctor will look for:

- Antalgic or Trendelenburg limp
- Pain on palpation
- Range of motion in flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, and rotation

**Knee examination:** your doctor will look for:

- Antalgic limp
- Pain on palpation
- Range of flexion and extension, any deformity (valgus or varus), and flexion contracture

### 6. Investigations

Your doctor may arrange:

- Blood tests: FBC (full blood count), ferritin, CRP (inflammation marker), renal function, and HbA1c if you have diabetes
- X-ray of the affected hip or knee (taken within 6 months of referral), with specific views required for joint replacement surgery

### 7. Fitness for surgery

Your doctor will check for conditions that may affect your ability to have surgery safely, including:

- Active or chronic oral (dental) infection. You may be encouraged to see your own dentist before surgery.
- Local skin infections or ulcers
- Undiagnosed anaemia. This needs investigation before referral.
- Poorly controlled diabetes, heart or breathing conditions, or high blood pressure
- BMI greater than 40. Your doctor will need a recent BMI, measured within the last three months. People with a BMI of 40 or greater will be offered a first surgical assessment. They may be referred to a weight loss programme, with dietary and physical activity assessment and support, before being accepted for surgery.

**Conditions that may delay (defer) surgery:**

- Heart attack within the past 6 months
- Any open wound or ulcer
- Blood pressure poorly controlled (greater than 160/90)
- Poorly controlled diabetes
- Prostate problems (prostatism) that could lead to a failed trial of catheter removal after surgery
- Currently taking clopidogrel. Surgery is best deferred until the clopidogrel course is finished, because stopping it early increases the risk of heart problems around the time of surgery.

**Conditions that may mean surgery is not offered (decline):**

- Angina class 3 or 4 (symptoms with minimal exercise or at rest)
- Heart failure class 3 or 4 (symptoms with minimal exercise or at rest)
- Crescendo transient ischaemic attacks (TIAs)

### 8. Willingness to proceed

Your doctor will also ask whether you are willing to have surgery. They may use the NZ Risk Calculator as part of this discussion.

## Management

If your doctor considers you suitable for joint replacement, they will refer you for a non-acute orthopaedic assessment. This applies if you:

- Have not responded to medical (non-surgical) treatment
- Have severe functional limitations
- Are willing to consider surgery and are fit for it

**What happens after referral:**

Your doctor will include full examination findings in the referral. If you are accepted for assessment, you may be offered a First Specialist Assessment (FSA) with an orthopaedic surgeon.

After the FSA, you may:

- Be placed on the surgical waiting list and referred for a preoperative assessment of surgical and anaesthetic fitness
- Have further investigations or non-surgical treatments recommended before being placed on the waiting list

**After surgery:**

You will have follow-up from the orthopaedic surgeon or a physiotherapist.

- **Knee replacement:** After partial and total knee replacements, physiotherapy can help with mobilisation and knee strengthening
- **Hip replacement:** Physiotherapy is not normally required after a total hip replacement
- **Orthopaedic surgeon follow-up:** Usually at 6 weeks after surgery, and then as appropriate. This may change depending on your age, activity level, and any complications. Your surgeon will advise when they expect to see you again
- **Post-operative X-ray:** Your surgeon may arrange a check X-ray within 1 year of surgery. After that first check X-ray, further check X-rays are not needed unless you have symptoms

**If surgery is declined at any stage:**

Your doctor will explain that you may need to continue medical management and improve control of your other medical conditions, such as diabetes, anaemia, or obesity.
