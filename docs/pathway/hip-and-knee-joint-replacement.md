# Hip and Knee Joint Replacement

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment of patients being considered for hip or knee replacement surgery, including what your doctor will evaluate and what factors may affect your suitability for surgery.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will carry out a detailed assessment to identify patients with the greatest need, ability to benefit from surgery, and who are fit for the procedure.

### 1. Non-surgical treatments first

Before being considered for joint replacement, your doctor will confirm that appropriate non-surgical treatments have been adequately trialled, including physiotherapy, weight management, analgesics, and activity modification.

### 2. Pain history

Your doctor will ask about:

- Where the pain is located and whether it radiates
- Whether onset was gradual or sudden
- What makes it worse (weight-bearing, twisting, impact)
- Whether rest relieves the pain
- Whether pain disturbs sleep
- Whether pain is intermittent or constant
- Pain intensity rated 0–10 (where 10 is the worst pain you've experienced)

### 3. Functional limitations

Your doctor will assess how the joint affects your daily life, including:

- How far you can walk (in metres and time)
- Restrictions on activities such as putting on shoes and socks, getting in and out of a car, and cutting toenails
- Whether the knee locks or feels unstable (for knee problems)
- Whether surgery not being done would have significant consequences — for example, loss of employment, need to move into care, or needing a wheelchair
- Impact on personal care and safety, including risk of falls

### 4. Treatment history

Your doctor will ask about:

- Regular pain medications you have been using
- Use of walking aids
- Physiotherapy received
- Other treatments tried (e.g. acupuncture)

### 5. Physical examination

**Hip examination** — your doctor will look for:

- Antalgic or Trendelenburg limp
- Pain on palpation
- Range of motion in flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, and rotation

**Knee examination** — your doctor will look for:

- Antalgic limp
- Pain on palpation
- Range of flexion and extension, any deformity (valgus or varus), and flexion contracture

### 6. Investigations

Your doctor may arrange:

- Blood tests: FBC (full blood count), ferritin, CRP (inflammation marker), renal function, and HbA1c if you have diabetes
- X-ray of the affected hip or knee (taken within 6 months of referral), with specific views required for joint replacement planning

### 7. Fitness for surgery

Your doctor will check for conditions that may affect your ability to have surgery safely, including:

- Active or chronic oral (dental) infection — you may be advised to see a dentist before surgery
- Local skin infections or ulcers near the joint
- Undiagnosed anaemia — this needs investigation before referral
- Poorly controlled diabetes, heart or breathing conditions, or high blood pressure
- BMI greater than 40 — surgery may still be offered for assessment, but your doctor may refer you to a weight loss programme (including dietary and physical activity support) before surgery is confirmed

**Conditions that may delay (defer) surgery:**

- Heart attack within the past 6 months
- Any open wound or ulcer
- Blood pressure poorly controlled (greater than 160/90)
- Poorly controlled diabetes
- Significant prostate problems that could complicate catheter removal after surgery
- Currently taking clopidogrel — surgery is best deferred until the clopidogrel course is finished, as stopping it early increases the risk of cardiac events during or after surgery

**Conditions that may mean surgery is not offered (decline):**

- Angina class 3 or 4 (symptoms with minimal exercise or at rest)
- Heart failure class 3 or 4 (symptoms with minimal exercise or at rest)
- Crescendo transient ischaemic attacks (TIAs)

### 8. Willingness to proceed

Your doctor will also discuss whether you are willing to have surgery, including understanding the risks and recovery process.

## Management

If your doctor considers you suitable for joint replacement, they will refer you for a non-acute orthopaedic assessment. This applies if you:

- Have not responded to medical (non-surgical) treatment
- Have severe functional limitations
- Are willing to consider surgery and are assessed as fit for it

**What happens after referral:**

Your doctor will submit a full referral including examination findings. If accepted, you may be offered a First Specialist Assessment (FSA) with an orthopaedic surgeon.

After the FSA, the surgeon may:

- Place you on the surgical waiting list and arrange a preoperative assessment for surgical and anaesthetic fitness
- Recommend further investigations or non-surgical treatments before placing you on the waiting list

**After surgery:**

- **Knee replacement:** Physiotherapy is usually recommended to help with mobilisation and knee strengthening
- **Hip replacement:** Physiotherapy is not normally required after a total hip replacement
- **Orthopaedic surgeon follow-up:** Usually at 6 weeks after surgery, and then as appropriate depending on your age, activity level, and any complications. Your surgeon will advise when they expect to see you again
- **Post-operative X-ray:** Your surgeon may arrange a check X-ray within 1 year of surgery. Further X-rays are not routinely needed unless you develop symptoms

**If surgery is declined at any stage:**

Your doctor will discuss continuing with medical management and optimising control of any other medical conditions (such as diabetes, anaemia, or obesity) that may have contributed to the decision.
