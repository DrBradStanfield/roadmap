# Neck Lumps in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of neck lumps in children under 15 years of age. Most neck lumps in children are benign and do not require referral — your doctor will help determine if further investigation is needed.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent Medical Attention

Tell your doctor promptly or seek urgent care if:

- Thyroid nodule (rare, but often malignant)
- Rapid growth of a bulky, non-tender, asymmetrical neck node (may suggest lymphoma)
- Stridor (noisy breathing) or dysphonia (voice changes)
- Cold abscess (non-tender swelling with blue-purple skin discolouration — suggests atypical mycobacterium infection)

---

## Background

- Most neck lumps in children are due to benign reactive swelling of the lymph nodes in response to infection, and rarely need referral.
- Lumps that are asymmetrical, larger than 2 cm, or progressively enlarging are more likely to be significant.
- The majority of cystic neck lumps in children are congenital (present from birth) and include thyroglossal duct cysts, branchial arch cysts, dermoid cysts, and lymphatic malformations.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history including:

**About the lump:**
- When it appeared, how long it has been there
- Size and location
- Whether it has changed or fluctuated over time

**Associated symptoms:**
- Pain or tenderness, fever
- B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss) — these may suggest malignancy
- Pallor, bruising, or bone pain
- Recent viral illness, upper or lower respiratory tract infection, mouth ulcers, scalp or skin infections
- Voice changes or difficulty swallowing

**Other history:**
- Any previous malignancy
- Foreign travel
- Pets (especially cats — relevant for cat scratch disease)
- Contact with tuberculosis (TB): the child, a household member, or a carer who has lived more than six months in a high TB incidence country

Physical examination typically includes:

- Size, location, and character of the lump (cystic or solid, superficial or deep)
- Whether the lump moves on tongue protrusion or with swallowing
- Overlying skin changes
- Whether the lump is associated with a salivary gland (e.g. parotid)
- Other lumps or generalised lymph node swelling
- Check for pallor, bruising, or petechiae
- Temperature, height, and weight if indicated
- Evidence of infection (e.g. tonsillitis, abscess)
- Signs of systemic illness (e.g. enlarged liver or spleen)

> **Note:** Fine needle aspiration (FNA) is **not** arranged for children with neck lumps.

## Types of Neck Lumps

### Mid-line Neck Lumps

| Type | Features |
|------|----------|
| **Thyroglossal cyst** | Most common mid-line lump (80%). Usually in pre-school children. Appears over the hyoid bone and moves with swallowing or tongue protrusion. Early specialist referral recommended before infection occurs. If the cyst becomes infected, urgent assessment is required. |
| **Submental lymph node** | Usually just below the chin. Your doctor will check the mouth for a primary source of infection (e.g. mouth ulcer or dental infection). |
| **Dermoid cyst** | May be difficult to distinguish from a thyroglossal cyst without imaging. |
| **Ectopic thyroid** | Very rare. If present, it is likely the child's only thyroid tissue — it should not be removed. |
| **Thyroid nodule** | Rare, but can be malignant. Warrants urgent assessment. |

### Lateral (Side-of-Neck) Lumps

| Type | Features |
|------|----------|
| **Reactive hyperplasia** | Most common type. Bilateral, usually less than 2 cm. Occurs in response to viral or bacterial infection. No treatment required in most cases. |
| **Branchial cyst** | Second most common congenital neck mass. Found along the front border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle. Often presents with inflammation. |
| **Cervical abscess** | Usually in children under 2 years. Tender with fluid underneath, skin redness, fever. Requires urgent specialist assessment. |
| **Non-tuberculous mycobacterium (cold abscess)** | Painless swelling growing over 3–6 weeks. Pink-purple skin discolouration with underlying liquefaction when advanced. Usually in children aged 2–6 years. |
| **Bacterial cervical lymphadenitis** | Acute onset, usually unilateral, with fever and tender red skin. Caused mainly by *Staphylococcus aureus* or group A *Streptococcus*. |
| **Lymphoma** | Usually in children over 6 years. Rubbery, round, rapidly growing, non-tender nodes — especially with fever. Requires urgent assessment. |

## Differential Diagnosis for Persistent Lumps (>6 weeks)

Your doctor will consider:

- Periodontal (dental) infection
- Viral infections: Epstein-Barr virus (EBV), cytomegalovirus (CMV), toxoplasmosis, HIV
- Mycobacterium infection including cold abscess (atypical, non-tuberculous mycobacterium)
- Congenital cyst
- Lymphoma, leukaemia, or thyroid malignancy

---

## Investigations

For persistent neck lumps, your doctor may consider the following on a case-by-case basis:

**Blood tests:**
- Full blood count (FBC), CRP, electrolytes, and liver function tests (LFTs) if systemic disease is suspected
- Thyroid function tests (TFTs) if a thyroid origin is suspected
- Viral screen: EBV, CMV, toxoplasma, HIV, *Bartonella henselae* (cat scratch disease), mumps serology
- Tuberculosis testing (e.g. sputum acid-fast bacilli if coughing with TB contact history)

**Imaging:**
- Ultrasound may be considered if: the lump persists after 6 weeks or after antibiotic treatment; the lump is larger than 1.5 cm; there is a mid-line neck mass (excluding simple submental nodes less than 1.5 cm); or a vascular malformation is suspected
- Chest X-ray if TB is suspected

---

## Management

### Reactive Hyperplasia (most common)

The vast majority of neck lumps — especially with recent upper respiratory infection, less than 2 cm, less than 2 weeks duration, bilateral, and no skin changes — are reactive hyperplasia. No treatment is required. Your doctor will reassure the family and review in 6 weeks.

### Suspected Bacterial Cervical Lymphadenitis

Your doctor may prescribe antibiotics. Options include:

- **Cefalexin** — palatable suspension, well tolerated, funded. 25 mg/kg (up to 1 g) per dose orally every 8 hours for 7 days.
- **Flucloxacillin** — effective against *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Streptococcus pyogenes*. 12.5 mg/kg (maximum 500 mg) per dose orally every 6 hours for 7 days. Note: the suspension is unpalatable and must be taken on an empty stomach.
- **If penicillin allergy (immediate hypersensitivity):** Co-trimoxazole 24 mg/kg (maximum 960 mg) twice daily.

Your doctor will review after 48 hours. If there is no improvement, further specialist assessment will be arranged.

### Lymphoma (suspected)

Urgent paediatric medical assessment will be arranged.

### Tuberculosis (suspected)

Urgent paediatric medical assessment will be arranged.

### Cold Abscess (non-tuberculous mycobacterium)

Urgent specialist (ORL/head and neck surgery) assessment will be arranged.

### Neck Lump >2 cm with B Symptoms

Urgent ENT/otolaryngology assessment will be arranged. A lymph node larger than 2 cm is likely to require excision biopsy.

### Post-Infection Lymphadenopathy Persisting >6 Weeks

If the lymph nodes are receding, they are unlikely to be pathological. Your doctor will advise you to return if the picture changes. If they are not receding, non-urgent ENT/otolaryngology assessment will be arranged.

### Congenital Lumps and Other Neck Lumps

Non-urgent ENT/otolaryngology assessment will be arranged for:
- Non-infected thyroglossal cyst
- Thyroid nodule
- Dermoid cyst
- Branchial cyst
- Other cystic neck mass (e.g. lymphatic malformation)
- Mass associated with a salivary gland (e.g. parotid — marked urgent)

---

## Situations Requiring Urgent Specialist Assessment

Your doctor will arrange urgent assessment if:
- Quinsy, stridor, or persistent voice changes
- Cervical abscess
- Rapidly enlarging thyroid lump
- Infected thyroglossal cyst
- Cold abscess (atypical, non-tuberculous mycobacterium)
- Suspected lymphoma or tuberculosis
- Bacterial cervical lymphadenitis not responding to 48 hours of oral antibiotics
