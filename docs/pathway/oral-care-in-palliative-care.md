# Oral Care in Palliative Care

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of mouth problems in adults with advanced illness. Mouth problems are common in palliative care and cause significant morbidity and distress — prevention should be a priority. A healthy mouth has intact mucosa and is clean, moist, and pain-free. Your doctor or care team will guide management.

---

## Assessment

Your care team will assess the condition of your mouth, including:

- Dentition (broken teeth, ill-fitting dentures)
- Dry mouth (xerostomia)
- Pain
- Ulceration, with or without infection
- Dirty mouth (coated tongue, candida, oral debris)

They will also consider factors that may contribute to poor oral health, such as nutritional state, medications (e.g., anticholinergics causing dry mouth), mental and physical state, and pain.

---

## Management

### Maintaining a healthy mouth

Your doctor or care team may recommend the following routine for patients or carers:

- Brush teeth with a soft toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, and do oral care 4 times daily.
- Rinse with water after brushing.
- Use a mouthwash — acceptability is key; some trial and error is normal:
  - **Sodium bicarbonate mouthwash** (half a teaspoon baking soda dissolved in 200 mL lukewarm water, four times daily) — neutralises the oral environment; some find the taste unpleasant.
  - **Normal saline** (1 teaspoon salt in 500 mL lukewarm water) — mild antiseptic; may not be well tolerated with altered taste or nausea.
  - **Chlorhexidine mouthwash** (12-hourly, fully subsidised) — antibacterial, antifungal, antiplaque. Note: incompatible with nystatin — if using both, give nystatin at least 30 minutes after chlorhexidine.
  - **Hydrogen peroxide 3%** (dilute 30 mL in half a glass of warm water, rinse for 2–3 minutes, two to three times daily) — antimicrobial; some find the taste unpleasant or experience a burning sensation.
  - Over-the-counter mouthwashes are often too astringent and painful for sore mouths.
- Moisten the mouth with frequent sips of fluids or water sprays/atomisers.
- Stimulate saliva flow with sugar-free sweets, frozen pineapple pieces, ice blocks, or chewing gum.
- Moisturise lips with a lanolin-based preparation or lip balm.
- Arrange dental care if poor dentition is present.

### Dry mouth (xerostomia)

If dry mouth is present, your care team may:

- Treat underlying causes (e.g., dehydration, infection, anxiety).
- Review medications that may be contributing.
- Suggest: ice chips; frozen or crushed pineapple pieces; ice block on a stick; sugar-free sweets or chewing gum (sugar can make the mouth drier); diet drinks (low pH).
- Recommend moisturising lips regularly.
- Consider a saliva substitute (note: not funded).

### Dirty mouth and candida

If there is no candida, your care team will recommend regular cleaning (4 times daily), brushing the tongue, sodium bicarbonate mouthwash, and frozen pineapple pieces to suck.

If candida is present (white plaque or coating), your doctor will prescribe antifungal medication 4 times daily until 48 hours after clinical cure. Options include nystatin suspension, amphotericin lozenge, or miconazole oral gel. If these are not effective, your doctor may prescribe fluconazole 50 mg orally once a day for 5 days.

### Ulceration with infection

- If viral infection (herpes simplex or zoster) is suspected, your doctor will prescribe valaciclovir (preferred over aciclovir due to reduced pill burden). **Important:** dose must be reduced in kidney impairment; your doctor will check for this.
- If bacterial infection is likely, your doctor will seek adult palliative care support and advice.

### Painful mouth

Your doctor may prescribe topical analgesia:

- **Lignocaine viscous 2%** — Rinse and spit every 4 hours. Note: hot drinks may cause burns to the mouth due to temporary numbness.
- **Choline salicylate (Bonjela) gel** — Applied topically every 4 to 6 hours.
- **Benzydamine (Difflam) oral solution** — Diluted with water; rinse and spit every 4 hours.

If topical analgesia does not control the pain, your doctor may consider systemic opioids. If pain is due to broken teeth or ill-fitting dentures, dental care will be arranged.

### Oral care for a dying patient

Oral care is an important part of end of life care and should be done at least every 2 hours. The act of moistening the mouth is important — the fluid used matters less than keeping the mouth comfortable.

Family members may be taught to help with oral care if they wish, as this can reduce feelings of helplessness.

Recommendations:
- Use maxi swabs (oversize cotton buds). **Do not** use green sponge swabs (impregnated with sodium bicarbonate, unpleasant for the patient) or lemon and glycerine swabs (the acidity worsens mouth problems).
- Use familiar fluids to swab the mouth (tea, fruit juice, carbonated drinks, cooled soups, ice cream, yoghurt). Avoid iced water — it can shock a semi-conscious patient.
- If the mouth is painful or ulcerated, take care and use appropriate topical treatments.

Your doctor will ensure close collaboration between all palliative care providers and can seek adult palliative care support and advice for anticipatory planning, arranging a joint visit, or considering hospice admission.

---

## When to Contact Your Doctor or Care Team

Contact your doctor or care team if:

- Mouth pain is not controlled with topical treatments
- Candida infection does not respond to initial antifungal treatment
- There are signs of viral or bacterial infection in the mouth
- Oral problems are significantly affecting eating, drinking, or comfort
