# Home Oxygen

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This page covers when home oxygen therapy is appropriate, what the clinical criteria are, and what to expect from assessment.

---

## What Home Oxygen Is For

The goal of long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) is to manage hypoxia (low blood oxygen) and improve longevity. Home oxygen is indicated for the treatment of **hypoxia**, not breathlessness.

An important distinction:
- Oxygen therapy **can improve life expectancy, cardiac function, exercise capacity, quality of life, and cognition** when used for more than 16 hours per day in people with confirmed hypoxia.
- Oxygen therapy is **not an effective treatment for breathlessness (dyspnoea)** on its own. Only a minority of hypoxic patients report reduced breathlessness from oxygen therapy.

If breathlessness (without confirmed hypoxia) is the main issue, your doctor will follow a separate pathway.

---

## Who Benefits Most from LTOT

Assessment for LTOT must be done when your respiratory condition is stable — **at least 6 weeks** after any acute respiratory illness or hospital discharge.

**Most benefit:** Patients with a resting arterial oxygen level (PaO2) of **less than 7.3 kPa**, corresponding to oxygen saturation below 88%.

**May also benefit:** Patients with PaO2 up to 8 kPa (oxygen saturation up to 91%) if there is associated end-organ damage:
- Haematocrit (red blood cell concentration) over 0.55
- Evidence of cor pulmonale (right heart strain from lung disease) on ECG or echocardiogram

---

## Criteria for a Domiciliary Oxygen Assessment

Your doctor will check whether you meet the following criteria before referring for assessment:

- A confirmed condition causing hypoxia, with **resting O2 saturation below 90%** when your respiratory condition is optimally managed
- You are **not a current smoker**, and if you are an ex-smoker, you have quit for at least **6 weeks** (including vaping)

Additional criteria for palliative patients:
- Terminal illness with life expectancy less than 6 months
- Patient is aware of their palliative diagnosis
- Referral for palliative assessment or hospice is complete (or unsuitable)

If you have end-stage heart failure, your heart failure management must be optimised and cardiology approval obtained before referral for oxygen assessment.

---

## What the Assessment Involves

A specialist assessment involves an **arterial blood gas measurement** to accurately determine your blood oxygen levels and assess whether domiciliary LTOT is appropriate. This is an outpatient clinic appointment.

Your doctor will refer you to the appropriate respiratory service for this assessment. The decision about whether to prescribe home oxygen is made by the specialist team following this assessment.

Always discuss your symptoms and eligibility with your doctor before expecting a referral for home oxygen.
