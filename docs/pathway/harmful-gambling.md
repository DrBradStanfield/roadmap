# Harmful Gambling

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers patients at risk of, or experiencing, harmful gambling — and those affected by another person's gambling.

---

## Red Flags — Discuss with Your Doctor Urgently

Tell your doctor immediately if you are experiencing:

- Suicidal thoughts or poor mental well-being
- Domestic or family violence (as a victim or if you are concerned about your own behaviour)

---

## Background

Approximately one in five New Zealanders will experience harm in their lifetime due to their own, or someone else's, gambling. Almost 50% of that harm is related to low-risk gambling.

Gambling can affect physical, social, emotional, and mental health — for both the person gambling and those around them. It is associated with smoking, hazardous alcohol use, other drug use, and suicidality.

Long-term impacts can include gambling-related debt, relationship problems, loss of employment, and poor physical and mental wellbeing.

Gambling harm is strongly associated with domestic and family violence. Stigma is common — around 75% of people experiencing gambling harm do not seek formal counselling or support groups.

**Groups with higher rates of gambling harm:**
- Māori and Pacific people are 2.24 and 2.31 times more likely respectively to be moderate- or high-risk gamblers compared to non-Māori and non-Pacific people
- Asian communities have a 9.5 times greater chance of developing moderate-risk harmful gambling compared to the European/other population
- Almost 45% of youth aged 15–24 gambled in the last year
- Online gambling is increasing, with particular concerns about ease of access and targeting of young people

## Assessment

Your doctor may assess gambling harm using the following approach:

### Screening Questions (Brief Biosocial Gambling Screen)

Your doctor may ask:

- During the past 12 months, have you become restless, irritable, or anxious when trying to stop or cut down on gambling?
- During the past 12 months, have you tried to keep your friends or family from knowing how much you gambled?
- During the past 12 months, did you have such financial trouble as a result of your gambling that you had to get help with living expenses from family, friends, or welfare?

Answering yes to any one question indicates potential harmful gambling and the need for further evaluation.

You can also complete a self-assessment at [Test your Gambling](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/help-and-support/test-your-gambling) (Safer Gambling Aotearoa).

### Risk Factors Your Doctor Will Consider

- Accessibility of gambling
- Alcohol or other drug use
- Family history of gambling-related harm
- Previous experience of trauma
- Low socio-economic status
- High levels of psychological distress
- Life events such as bereavement, retirement, or return to work after injury

### Warning Signs of Gambling Harm

**Initial signs:**
- Less time or money for recreation and family
- Reduced savings
- Increased alcohol consumption
- Feelings of guilt or regret after gambling

**Advanced signs:**
- Relationship conflict
- Reduced work or study performance
- Financial difficulties
- Anger
- Feelings of shame and hopelessness

**Behavioural signs:**
- Spending a lot of time in gambling venues, on the computer, or on their phone
- Stopping activities previously enjoyed
- Changes in sleep, eating, or sex
- Neglecting self-care, work, school, or family responsibilities
- Increased alcohol or drug use

**Emotional signs:**
- Mood swings, anger, or frustration
- Unexplained stress, anxiety, or sleeplessness
- Boredom and restlessness
- Depression or suicidal thoughts

**Financial and legal signs:**
- Frequently seeking unexplained emergency financial help
- Ongoing financial problems that cannot be logically explained
- Bare house (very little food, no utilities, little furniture)
- Legal problems related to gambling

### Severity

Your doctor will assess severity based on functional impairment and distress:

- **Mild:** Minimal distress; able to maintain work commitments with little financial or relationship difficulty
- **Moderate:** Distress and impairment between mild and severe
- **Severe:** Marked depression or anxiety, significant suicidal ideation, relationship breakdown, inability to meet work or family commitments, debt, poverty, domestic violence

### Medication Review

Your doctor will review your medications — dopamine agonist medications used for Parkinson's disease, restless legs, or prolactinoma may trigger compulsive gambling. These include:

- Apomorphine (e.g. Apomine, Movapo)
- Cabergoline (e.g. Cabaser, Dostinex)
- Pramipexole (e.g. Sifrol, Simipex)
- Ropinirole (e.g. Appese, Repeve)

### Co-existing Conditions

Your doctor will also screen for conditions that commonly co-occur with gambling harm, including:

- Mental health conditions (depression, anxiety, OCD, ADHD, bipolar disorder, personality disorder)
- Substance use (alcohol, illicit drugs)
- Suicidality
- Domestic and family violence

## Management

### General Approach

Your doctor may use motivational interviewing to understand your readiness to change and tailor support accordingly.

**If you are not ready to stop gambling**, your doctor may:
- Discuss the possible harms from continued gambling and the benefits of stopping
- Suggest harm minimisation strategies:
  - Consider other activities — interests, family time, friendships, work
  - Set a limit on how much money you will spend
  - Set limits on the time you will spend gambling
  - Avoid borrowing money for gambling
  - Avoid mixing gambling with heavy drinking or drug use
- Connect you with free, confidential counselling via [Safer Gambling Aotearoa](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/help-and-support/services-near-you)
- Monitor your gambling harm at future appointments

**If you are thinking about reducing gambling**, your doctor may:
- Help you reflect on the pros and cons of continuing
- Explore barriers to reducing gambling
- Promote the idea that change is possible
- Connect you with free, confidential counselling and support
- Discuss harm minimisation strategies (see above)

**If you are ready to stop gambling**, your doctor may:
- Help you set realistic, time-limited, and measurable goals
- Provide information about maintaining change and avoiding triggers (people, locations, dates, emotions, stress, reminders of trauma)
- Advise on self-exclusion from gambling venues — see [Multi-Venue Exclusion (MVE)](https://multivenueexclusion.org.nz/) and [Safer Gambling Aotearoa – Staying in Control](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/safer-gambling/staying-in-control)
- For moderate to severe harm: support you to access free, confidential counselling via [Safer Gambling Aotearoa](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/help-and-support/services-near-you)
- For mild harm: advise monitoring your gambling and its financial, social, and emotional impact

### Medication

Medication is not first-line for harmful gambling. However, treating co-existing mental illness or substance use disorder with appropriate medication may reduce gambling harm.

Your doctor may consider naltrexone, which has been shown to be effective for people experiencing gambling-related harm. Note: naltrexone is not formally approved for this use, so prescribing would be off-label.

### Financial Concerns

If you have financial concerns related to gambling:
- Contact the **Gambling Helpline** on **0800-654-655**, or text **8006**
- For Asian language services: **Asian Family Services on 0800-862-342** (support available in 8 languages)
- The Gambling Debt Helpline offers free specialist gambling debt crisis counselling and practical support including budgeting, debt information, and safe communication with creditors

### Self-Exclusion

Your doctor will advise all patients to consider self-exclusion from gambling, including blocking access to online gaming and transactions. Multi-Venue Exclusion (MVE) allows you to self-exclude from multiple venues at once. Contact a gambling harm service to initiate this — see [multivenueexclusion.org.nz](https://multivenueexclusion.org.nz/).

### If You Are Affected by Someone Else's Gambling

Your doctor can:
- Provide information about harmful gambling and connect you with free, confidential counselling and support via [Safer Gambling Aotearoa](https://www.safergambling.org.nz/help-and-support/services-near-you)
- Advise on options for a third-party exclusion order — if you have evidence of gambling harm, a venue operator may monitor the person or issue an exclusion order for up to 2 years
- Manage any co-existing mental health conditions you may be experiencing (e.g. depression, anxiety)
- Support you if you have experienced domestic or family violence
