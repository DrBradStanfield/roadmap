# Previously Undisclosed Sexual Assault

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

An adult may disclose childhood abuse at any time, or after a doctor has asked about non-consenting sexual contact or unwanted touching.

---

## When Doctors Ask About Abuse History

Making enquiry about abuse a routine part of a health assessment can make these conversations easier for both the clinician and patient. Doctors may introduce the topic with a phrase such as: *"We routinely ask these questions as part of our health assessment."*

Situations where a doctor may ask include:

- As part of a routine sexual health history
- In patients with a strong negative reaction to pelvic examination
- In cases of severe chronic or unexplained pain, especially pelvic or gastrointestinal
- In patients with significant behavioural, drug, alcohol, or mental health problems
- When family violence is disclosed or suspected

Example questions a doctor may use:

- "Has anyone ever made you have sex when you did not want to, or in a way you did not want?"
- "Has anyone ever forced you to have sex?"
- "Have you ever had a sexual experience you did not want?"
- "Has anyone ever touched you in a way you did not like, or at a time when you did not want them to?"

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask:

- Whether past abuse or assault is still affecting your life
- Whether you would like help
- Whether you would like to involve the police

If there are medical concerns, your doctor may refer you for a specialist sexual assault medical and forensic assessment.

---

## Management Overview

A disclosure can take time to work through sensitively. If there are no medically or forensically urgent needs, your doctor may schedule an extended appointment.

### Emotional Support

Your doctor will:

- Listen to your account
- Acknowledge what you have shared
- Validate your experience — for example: *"This is not your fault"* and *"No one deserves to be treated like this"*

### ACC Sensitive Claims

New Zealand's ACC scheme covers mental health injuries arising from sexual abuse. Key points:

- Your GP can lodge an ACC sensitive claim on your behalf (ACC 45 form)
- While the claim is being assessed, you can access **Sensitive Claims Support Sessions** — up to 16 hours with an ACC-registered counsellor — to support your safety and wellbeing
- Until a claim is accepted, ACC will not pay entitlements for time off work
- The patient may also go directly to a counsellor, who can submit the claim

The [ACC Find Support website](https://www.findsupport.co.nz/) lists ACC-funded counsellors and psychologists and explains the support available.

### Crisis Support

Crisis support counsellors can:

- Provide support regardless of whether you choose to go to the police or not
- Offer 24-hour phone support for sexual assault
- Be available by phone during your GP consultation
- Attend a forensic medical examination or police interview with you
- Assist you to make contact with the police

Your doctor can help connect you with crisis support services in your area.

---

## Next Steps Your Doctor May Arrange

- Referral for **sexual assault medical and forensic assessment** if there are medical concerns
- Contact with police if you choose to involve them
- Referral to a **sexual assault community support service**
- An ACC sensitive claim and referral for counselling
- Longer-term psychological support through ACC-funded counselling
