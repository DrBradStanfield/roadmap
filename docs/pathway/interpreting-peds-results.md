# Interpreting PEDS Results

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway explains what PEDS (Parents' Evaluation of Developmental Status) results mean and what your doctor may do next.

---

## Background

Parents' Evaluation of Developmental Status (PEDS) is a tool used by clinicians to understand parental concerns about their child's development, typically as part of the B4 School Check. Your doctor or nurse will ask about any concerns you have, then score the results across five paths (A to E) based on the number and type of concerns identified.

---

## Assessment

If developmental concerns are found, your doctor will typically:

- Take a detailed history, including:
  - Family history
  - Pregnancy and birth history
  - Whether the child is already receiving therapy services
  - Any chronic illnesses
  - Social and environmental factors
- Review your child's development against age-appropriate milestones (usually for children around 4 years of age)
- Review results from the Strengths and Difficulties Questionnaire (SDQ) and any vision and hearing tests
- Discuss the results with you and note any concerns you raise

If speech and language delays or disorders are identified (including stuttering or voice difficulties), your doctor may:

- Check whether vision and hearing screening has been completed — this is important because hearing problems can cause speech delays
- Consider whether other developmental areas are also affected (e.g., social development)

---

## Management

Your doctor will manage the results based on the PEDS path:

### Path A — Two or More Predictive Concerns

Your doctor may:

- Review findings and discuss next steps with you
- Refer to the Ministry of Education Early Intervention Service if significant concerns are identified
- Consider referral for a non-acute paediatric medicine assessment if there are significant developmental concerns in more than one area
- Note: Children with an isolated expressive language delay (normal receptive language, no other concerns) may not need a Child Health Outpatient Department review, but your doctor will advise based on the full picture

### Path B — One Predictive Concern

Your doctor may:

- Review findings and discuss next steps with you
- Consider referral to the Ministry of Education Early Intervention Service depending on the nature and extent of concerns
- Consider referral to Child Development Therapy Services if there are significant motor concerns
- Consider referral for behavioural or parenting support if needed
- Suggest community organisations for additional family support

### Paths C, D, and E — Non-Predictive Concerns, Communication Difficulties, or No Concerns

Your doctor will follow the relevant PEDS path:

- **Path C:** Non-predictive concerns — monitoring and support as appropriate
- **Path D:** If there were difficulties communicating during the assessment (e.g., language barrier), your doctor may arrange for an interpreter or repeat the assessment with a caregiver who knows the child well
- **Path E:** No concerns identified — no further action required at this time

Some results may be a combination of paths (e.g., A and C, or B and C).
