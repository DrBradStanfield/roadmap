# Ankle Sprain

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the management of a diagnosed ankle sprain, including grading the injury, initial treatment, and when further assessment is needed.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will determine the grade of injury:

- **Grade 1** — Stretch of the ligament with minor or no tearing. Minimal pain, little or no joint instability.
- **Grade 2** — Partial ligament tear. Moderate pain and swelling, some joint instability (may be difficult to fully examine due to pain and swelling).
- **Grade 3** — Complete rupture of one or more ligaments. Significant swelling and bruising, joint instability, difficulty bearing weight.

---

## Management

All lateral ligament sprains are managed initially with compression, elevation, analgesia, physiotherapy, and early mobilisation.

### Grade 1 Sprain

Your doctor may consider:

- Double tubigrip and elevation to control swelling. A good quality compression bandage is preferred — crepe bandages only provide compression for about an hour. Remove at night.
- Review in 1 week if not improving.
- Taping for mobilisation; recovery checked by single leg hop and single leg squat.
- Return to full activity is expected in 2–3 weeks.

### Grade 2 Sprain

Your doctor may consider:

- Immobilisation using strapping or bracing, depending on the level of disability and swelling.
- A short leg cast or backslab for 1 week if needed.
- Review in 1 week if not improving.
- Taping for mobilisation; recovery checked by single leg hop and single leg squat.
- Physiotherapy assessment, particularly if you are in a manual or physical job, or if pain and swelling is severely limiting function.
- Return to full activity is expected in 2–3 weeks.

### Grade 3 Sprain

Your doctor may consider:

- Immobilisation in a non-weight-bearing cast or backslab for 1–3 weeks, followed by gradual remobilisation.
- Weekly review during recovery.
- Orthopaedic assessment if you are unable to bear any weight throughout the day after injury, if the injury appears severe, or if pain and swelling make assessment difficult — to exclude occult injuries such as an osteochondral fracture of the talar dome.
- Taping for mobilisation; physiotherapy-supervised rehabilitation is expected.
- Ongoing bracing or taping if needed to manage swelling and stability.
- Rehabilitation typically takes 8–12 weeks to regain range of motion, strength, and proprioception.
- Orthopaedic reconstruction may be considered if functional instability (giving way or feeling of instability) persists after rehabilitation.

### General

- If recovery takes longer than 4 weeks, your doctor may consider orthopaedic assessment for further investigation.
- Allied health support your doctor may consider:
  - **Physiotherapy** — if you need help with taping, pain and swelling is severely limiting function, or you have a manual or physical job.
  - **Podiatry** — if uncomfortable with weight-bearing; techniques such as padding, strapping, orthotics, or exercise prescription may help offload the injured area.
  - **Occupational therapy** — if experiencing difficulty with everyday activities such as self-care, household management, driving, or work.
  - **Work assessment and rehabilitation** — if returning to work or having difficulty sustaining work tasks.

---

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical assessment if:

- You are unable to bear any weight at all after the injury
- There is severe swelling, bruising, or deformity
- Pain appears excessive or out of proportion to the injury
- You suspect a fracture (Ottawa Ankle Rules: bone tenderness at specific points, or inability to bear weight)
- Recovery is not progressing as expected after 1 week
