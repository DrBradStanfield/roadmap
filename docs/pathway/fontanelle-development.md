# Fontanelle Development

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of fontanelle development concerns in infants, including when variation is normal and when further assessment is needed.

---

## Background

- At birth, babies have 6 fontanelles. The largest is the **anterior fontanelle**, which ranges in size from 0.6 to 4.7 cm at birth depending on ethnicity.
- The initial size of a fontanelle does not predict when it will close.
- The anterior fontanelle can enlarge in the first few months of life and tends to close earlier in boys than in girls.
- **Median age of closure is 13.8 months.** It is closed in 1% of normal infants by 3 months, and in 4% of normal infants it may still be open at 24 months.
- In the majority of infants, a larger or smaller fontanelle is a benign developmental matter requiring no investigation, treatment, or specialist referral.
- In rare cases, fontanelle changes can be associated with craniosynostosis, syndromes, or endocrine problems.
- Parents are sometimes concerned about "small" fontanelles or "early closure," and Plunket may also request medical review for this reason.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:

1. Take a general development and medical history, including:
   - Any developmental concerns from the caregiver
   - Check for appropriate developmental milestones

2. Examine the child, including:
   - Head shape, looking for abnormalities — including viewing the head from above
   - Head circumference, height, and weight to check for normal growth tracking along centiles over time
   - Other physical or behavioural abnormalities

3. Check that newborn screening (Guthrie card) was normal — this is usually in the child's Well Child book or available on Testsafe.

4. **Blood tests and X-rays are not necessary** for fontanelle concerns in an otherwise well child.

---

## Management

### If the child is well and development is normal

Your doctor will reassure you if all of the following are present:

- Growing normally (particularly head growth) over time
- Normal development for age
- Normal head shape for age
- No dysmorphic features
- Normal newborn screening result

**Reassurance points your doctor may share:**

- The fontanelle is an area on top of the skull where the skull bones meet.
- It can feel like a gap or a dent, and sometimes it is small and hard to feel.
- The fontanelle gets smaller as babies grow and eventually closes as the skull bones grow.
- Size varies a lot — some young babies have a large fontanelle, and some have a very small one.
- As long as the baby is growing normally, including head size, fontanelle size does not matter.
- Your doctor is happy to re-check your baby's head if you remain worried.

Head growth will continue to be monitored as part of the routine Well Child framework.

### If there are clinical concerns

Your doctor may refer for non-acute paediatric assessment if there is concern about any of the following:

- Growth over time, particularly head growth
- Developmental milestones
- Head shape for age
- Appearance, such as dysmorphic features
- Newborn screening results
