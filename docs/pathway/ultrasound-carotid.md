# Carotid Ultrasound

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when a carotid ultrasound is appropriate — mainly as part of the assessment following a suspected transient ischaemic attack (TIA).

---

## Red Flags — Do Not Delay for Imaging

Your doctor will arrange **emergency assessment** rather than carotid imaging if you have a suspected TIA **and** any of the following high-risk features:

- **ABCD2 score of 4 or higher** (see table below)
- Currently taking anticoagulants
- Known or new atrial fibrillation (AF), including paroxysmal AF
- Known carotid stenosis on the affected side
- Crescendo TIA — two or more suspected TIAs within the last seven days
- Unilateral arm weakness
- Neck or facial pain alongside the TIA (may indicate arterial dissection)

In these situations, your doctor will seek acute specialist assessment. Arranging imaging must not delay this.

---

## ABCD2 Score — Stroke Risk After TIA

Your doctor may calculate an ABCD2 score to estimate the risk of stroke in the seven days following a suspected TIA.

| Risk factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Age 60 years or older | 1 |
| Blood pressure 140/90 mmHg or higher | 1 |
| Unilateral weakness | 2 |
| Speech disturbance without weakness | 1 |
| Symptoms lasting 60 minutes or longer | 2 |
| Symptoms lasting 10–59 minutes | 1 |
| Diabetes present | 1 |

A score of 4 or higher, within seven days of the episode, is considered high risk.

---

## When Carotid Ultrasound May Be Appropriate

Your doctor may consider arranging an urgent carotid ultrasound (within 48 hours) if **all** of the following apply:

- The suspected TIA occurred within the last **2 weeks**
- Symptoms are consistent with an **anterior circulation TIA** (see below)
- You do **not** have any of the high-risk features listed above
- You do **not** have atrial fibrillation or symptoms of a posterior circulation TIA
- You are **not** taking anticoagulants
- You are a potential candidate for **carotid endarterectomy** (a surgical procedure to clear a blocked carotid artery)
- A neurologist or stroke specialist has recommended the scan

Your doctor will determine whether these criteria are met and will seek specialist input before arranging the scan.

---

## Anterior vs Posterior Circulation TIA

### Anterior circulation TIA

Anterior circulation TIAs involve the carotid artery and its branches. Symptoms may include:

- Weakness or sensory loss on one side of the face, arm, or leg
- Language difficulties — dysphasia (trouble finding words or understanding) or dysarthria (slurred speech)
- Monocular visual loss — temporary loss of vision in one eye (amaurosis fugax), usually lasting only a few minutes

### Posterior circulation TIA

Posterior circulation TIAs involve the vertebral and basilar arteries. Symptoms may include:

- Bilateral (both sides) motor or sensory deficits
- Cortical blindness
- Double vision (diplopia)
- Vertigo (though not usually in isolation)

Posterior circulation TIAs are not an indication for carotid ultrasound.

---

## When Carotid Ultrasound Is Not Publicly Funded

A publicly funded carotid ultrasound is not available for:

- Asymptomatic carotid bruits or incidental carotid artery calcification found on X-ray (no TIA symptoms)
- Screening for carotid artery abnormalities

If you fall into one of these categories, you may wish to discuss privately funded imaging with your doctor.
