# Bone Density Scan (DEXA)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways. Last reviewed: November 2022.*

> **Always discuss your symptoms and any medication changes with your doctor.** This information is a clinical reference, not a substitute for individual medical advice.

This pathway covers when a bone density scan (DEXA) is appropriate, how fracture risk is classified, and what happens next based on results.

---

## Fracture Risk Assessment

Before a DEXA scan is requested, fracture risk must be assessed using a fracture risk calculator: either [FRAX](https://frax.shef.ac.uk/FRAX/tool.aspx?country=23) or the [Garvan Fracture Risk Calculator](https://fractureriskcalculator.com.au/calculator/). If you have had a bone density scan before, your risk is reassessed the same way.

Risk is classified as:

### High Fracture Risk

10-year fracture risk by:
- **FRAX:** > 4.5% hip fracture, or > 30% major osteoporotic fracture
- **Garvan:** > 4.5% hip fracture, or > 45% all fractures

### Intermediate Fracture Risk

10-year fracture risk by:
- **FRAX:** 1.5 to 4.5% hip fracture, or 10 to 30% major osteoporotic fracture
- **Garvan:** 1.5 to 4.5% hip fracture, or 15 to 45% all fractures

### Low Fracture Risk

10-year fracture risk by:
- **FRAX:** < 1.5% hip fracture, or < 10% major osteoporotic fracture
- **Garvan:** < 1.5% hip fracture, or < 15% all fractures

---

## When a DEXA Scan Is Indicated

Your doctor may consider a DEXA scan if you have:

- **Intermediate fracture risk:** a scan measures your bone mineral density (BMD). Your doctor then recalculates your fracture risk with the BMD result to decide on treatment.
- **High fracture risk:** treatment without a DEXA scan may be appropriate. A scan may still be requested if it will help guide management. If unsure, your doctor may seek advice from an endocrinologist.

---

## When a DEXA Scan Is Generally Not Indicated

A DEXA scan is usually not needed if:

- Your fracture risk is **low**, with or without a report of osteopenia on a plain X-ray
- You are premenopausal or perimenopausal with no fracture risk factors
- You had premature menopause but have no other risk factors. Fracture risk assessment or a scan can usually be deferred until 15 to 20 years after menopause
- You are a younger woman with a history of breast cancer, now taking aromatase inhibitors, with no other risk factors. Assessment can usually be deferred until you are aged > 65 years
- You use Depo Provera (contraceptive injection)
- You have a spinal cord injury
- You are a young adult with an eating disorder
- You have unexplained musculoskeletal pain, periarticular or periprosthetic osteoporosis, or treated coeliac disease
- Your fracture risk is very low and knowing your bone density will not change management
- You have had bariatric surgery (a scan is not routinely needed afterwards)
- You use inhaled glucocorticoids (steroid inhalers)
- You are obese, at any age. Obese people are very unlikely to have low bone density, except with Cushing's syndrome. If unsure, your doctor may seek advice from an endocrinologist
- You already take bisphosphonates. Routine repeat scans are not indicated
- You have known low bone density or high fracture risk and meet the criteria for osteoporosis treatment, but are not being treated

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider treatment based on your fracture risk, recalculated with your BMD result if you have had a scan. With high fracture risk, treatment without a scan may be appropriate.

Treatment decisions are made by your doctor based on your overall fracture risk, bone density results, and other health factors.
