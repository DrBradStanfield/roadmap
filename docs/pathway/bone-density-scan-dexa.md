# Bone Density Scan (DEXA)

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when a bone density scan (DEXA) is appropriate, how fracture risk is classified, and what happens next based on results.

---

## Fracture Risk Assessment

Before a DEXA scan is requested, fracture risk must be assessed using a validated calculator — either [FRAX](https://frax.shef.ac.uk/FRAX/tool.aspx?country=23) or the [Garvan Fracture Risk Calculator](https://fractureriskcalculator.com.au/calculator/).

Risk is classified as:

### High Fracture Risk

10-year fracture risk by:
- **FRAX:** > 4.5% hip fracture, or > 30% major osteoporotic fracture
- **Garvan:** > 4.5% hip fracture, or > 45% all fractures

### Intermediate Fracture Risk

10-year fracture risk by:
- **FRAX:** 1.5–4.5% hip fracture, or 10–30% major osteoporotic fracture
- **Garvan:** 1.5–4.5% hip fracture, or 15–45% all fractures

### Low Fracture Risk

10-year fracture risk by:
- **FRAX:** < 1.5% hip fracture, or < 10% major osteoporotic fracture
- **Garvan:** < 1.5% hip fracture, or < 15% all fractures

---

## When a DEXA Scan Is Indicated

Your doctor may consider a DEXA scan if you have:

- **Intermediate fracture risk** — a scan is used to measure bone mineral density (BMD) and refine your fracture risk, which then guides treatment decisions.
- **High fracture risk** — treatment without a DEXA scan may be appropriate. A scan may still be requested if it will help guide management.

---

## When a DEXA Scan Is Generally Not Indicated

A DEXA scan is usually not needed if:

- Your fracture risk is **low** (including if plain X-ray has reported osteopenia)
- You are premenopausal or perimenopausal with no fracture risk factors
- You had premature menopause but have no other risk factors — assessment can usually be deferred until 15–20 years after menopause
- You are a younger woman with breast cancer on aromatase inhibitors but no other risk factors — assessment can usually be deferred until age > 65
- You use Depo Provera (contraceptive injection)
- You have a spinal cord injury
- You are a young adult with an eating disorder
- You have unexplained musculoskeletal pain, periarticular or periprosthetic osteoporosis, or treated coeliac disease
- You have recently had bariatric surgery (not routinely indicated)
- You use inhaled glucocorticoids only
- You are obese (very unlikely to have low bone density, unless Cushing's syndrome is suspected)
- You are already on bisphosphonates — routine repeat scans are not indicated
- You have known low bone density or high fracture risk but are not currently being treated

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following approaches depending on your fracture risk and BMD results:

- **Lifestyle:** Weight-bearing exercise, fall prevention, adequate calcium and vitamin D intake, smoking cessation
- **Medications for osteoporosis** may include:
  - **Bisphosphonates** (e.g., alendronate, risedronate, zoledronic acid) — commonly first-line
  - **Denosumab** — access has been widened under Pharmac special authority (updated June 2025); see your doctor for eligibility
  - **Raloxifene** (Evista) — note: being discontinued; stock expected to run out December 2025. Your doctor will discuss alternatives
  - Other agents depending on individual circumstances

Treatment decisions are made by your doctor based on your overall fracture risk, bone density results, and other health factors.
