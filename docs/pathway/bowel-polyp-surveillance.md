# Bowel Polyp Surveillance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers follow-up after bowel polyp removal for patients who are not regarded as high risk for colorectal cancer due to personal or family history or long-term inflammatory bowel disease.

---

## Background

Bowel polyps can be either hyperplastic or adenomatous. Only adenomas carry an increased risk for malignancy. Removing adenomas reduces the risk of colorectal cancer developing.

Adenomas include a subgroup of serrated lesions: traditional serrated adenomas (TSAs) and sessile serrated polyps (SSPs, also called sessile serrated adenomas or SSAs).

The risk of malignancy increases with:
- Tubulovillous or villous architecture
- Increasing numbers or size
- Presence of dysplasia

Some large (> 10 mm) SSPs may have previously been inadvertently classified as hyperplastic polyps.

---

## Who May Not Need Further Surveillance

- Patients aged > 75 years or with significant co-morbidities — your doctor will weigh whether the potential risks of continuing surveillance outweigh the benefits. In general, patients aged > 75 years are not offered further surveillance colonoscopy.
- If only small hyperplastic polyps (< 10 mm) are found, and they do not meet the criteria for serrated polyposis syndrome, no further surveillance is required.

---

## Surveillance Intervals

Your doctor determines the follow-up interval based on what was found at your colonoscopy — specifically the type, size, and number of polyps, and whether the colonoscopy was of high quality.

**High quality colonoscopy** means verified caecal intubation, good bowel preparation, and a withdrawal time of more than 6 minutes. Intervals may be shortened if the colonoscopy was suboptimal.

### Adenomas

| What was found | Recommended follow-up interval |
|---|---|
| More than 10 adenomas | 1 year |
| 5–10 adenomas; OR any adenoma ≥ 10 mm; OR tubulovillous/villous adenoma; OR adenoma with high grade dysplasia | 3 years |
| 3 or 4 adenomas, all < 10 mm | 5 years |
| 1 or 2 adenomas, all < 10 mm | 10 years (or next bowel screening programme, whichever comes first) |

### Sessile Serrated Polyps (SSPs)

| What was found | Recommended follow-up interval |
|---|---|
| Serrated polyposis syndrome (see below) | 1 year |
| 5 or more SSPs < 10 mm; OR any SSP ≥ 10 mm; OR SSP with dysplasia; OR traditional serrated adenoma; OR hyperplastic polyp ≥ 10 mm (if distinction from SSP uncertain) | 3 years |
| 1–4 SSPs < 10 mm; OR hyperplastic polyp ≥ 10 mm (if confident it is not an SSP) | 5 years |

> If a colonoscopy finds both adenomas (< 10 mm) and sessile serrated lesions (< 10 mm), count the total number and apply the follow-up interval for sessile serrated lesions.

*Source: Ministry of Health – Update on Polyp Surveillance Guidelines, 2020.*

---

## Serrated Polyposis Syndrome

This is a higher-risk condition defined as either:
- 5 or more serrated lesions proximal to the rectum, all ≥ 5 mm, with at least 2 ≥ 10 mm, OR
- More than 20 serrated lesions of any size throughout the large bowel, including at least 5 proximal to the rectum.

If you meet these criteria, your doctor will arrange more frequent surveillance (1-yearly colonoscopy) and may refer you for genetic assessment.

---

## Family History Considerations

If you have a category 2 or 3 family history of bowel cancer, your doctor will ensure surveillance occurs at intervals of 5 years or less — even if the most recent colonoscopy was normal or showed only low-risk polyps.

---

## Genetic Referral — When It May Be Recommended

Your doctor may refer you to a Familial Gastrointestinal Cancer Service for genetic assessment if you have had:
- 10 or more adenomas found at one colonoscopy (or at least 1 advanced adenoma if aged > 70 years)
- 5 or more advanced adenomas at one colonoscopy
- 20 or more adenomas in total across multiple procedures
- 10 or more cumulative adenomas if aged ≤ 30 years
- Serrated polyposis syndrome

An **advanced adenoma** is defined as ≥ 10 mm in size, or showing villous features or high grade dysplasia on pathology.

---

## Important Notes

- Surveillance intervals assume the colonoscopy was of high quality. Your doctor may recommend an earlier repeat if bowel preparation was poor or the procedure was incomplete.
- If your follow-up has not been arranged by a specialist service, or your polyp results are unknown, your doctor can refer you to a gastroenterologist.
- This pathway applies to average-risk surveillance after polyp removal. It does not apply if you have a strong personal or family history of colorectal cancer, or inflammatory bowel disease — those situations follow different protocols.

---

*This information is a general guide only. Your doctor will determine the appropriate surveillance interval and referral based on your individual pathology results, overall health, and clinical history.*
