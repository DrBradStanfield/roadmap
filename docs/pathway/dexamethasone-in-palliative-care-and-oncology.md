# Dexamethasone in Palliative Care and Oncology

> **Important:** This information is a summary of clinical guidance. Decisions about starting, adjusting, or stopping dexamethasone in palliative or oncology settings should be made by or in close consultation with your treating doctor, oncologist, or palliative care team. Never stop dexamethasone suddenly without medical advice.

---

## What is dexamethasone used for in cancer/palliative care?

Dexamethasone is a potent corticosteroid (steroid) used in cancer and palliative care for several purposes:

**Emergency indications (start immediately):**
- Malignant spinal cord compression
- Raised intracranial pressure due to brain tumours or cerebral metastases
- Superior vena cava obstruction (SVCO)
- Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting

**Symptomatic trials (cautious, short-term use):**
- Bowel obstruction
- Nausea and vomiting
- Anorexia and fatigue
- Dyspnoea (breathlessness)
- Pain (as an adjunct to regular pain medications)
- Dry cough (unresponsive to opioids)
- Pruritus (itch)
- Hiccups
- Sweating

For symptomatic indications other than brain tumours and chemotherapy-induced nausea, the evidence base is limited. Trials should be short (5–7 days) and stopped if there is little benefit.

---

## How does dexamethasone work?

Dexamethasone is a strong glucocorticoid — it works primarily as a potent anti-inflammatory. Its mineralocorticoid effect is minimal. It has a long biological half-life (36–54 hours), which means it stays active in the body for a prolonged period and adrenal suppression is more likely with extended use.

---

## Before starting: check HbA1c

All patients should have an HbA1c checked before starting dexamethasone, as steroid-induced diabetes is a significant risk.

---

## Emergency dosing

For malignant spinal cord compression, raised intracranial pressure, or SVCO:

**Dose: dexamethasone 16 mg** — given as:
- 16 mg orally, or
- 16 mg IV in 100 mL sodium chloride 0.9% over 30 minutes, or
- 16 mg subcutaneously (give slowly — it can sting; if >2 mL volume needed, split into two 8 mg subcutaneous boluses)

Repeat once daily in the morning.

Once acute symptoms stabilise, taper the dose over a short number of weeks to the lowest effective dose. Your doctor or palliative care/oncology team will guide the taper.

---

## Maintenance doses by symptom

All doses given **once daily in the morning** (reduces insomnia risk).

| Indication | Typical dose |
|---|---|
| Raised ICP / spinal cord compression / SVCO | Seek oncology or palliative care advice |
| Bowel obstruction | 4–8 mg SC in the morning |
| Nausea and vomiting | 4–8 mg orally or SC in the morning |
| Anorexia and fatigue | 2–4 mg/day (max 2 weeks; then seek palliative care advice) |
| Dry cough | 4–8 mg in the morning |
| Dyspnoea | 4–8 mg in the morning |
| Pruritus (itch) | 4–8 mg orally or SC in the morning |
| Hiccups | 4–8 mg orally in the morning |
| Pain (adjunct) | 8–16 mg in the morning initially, then reduce |
| Sweating | 4–8 mg in the morning (or prednisone 20–40 mg) |

**For all symptomatic trials:** Review at 5–7 days and stop if no significant benefit.

---

## Key safety rules

### Duration
- Courses under 3 weeks: lower risk of adrenal suppression.
- Courses over 3 weeks: adrenal suppression is likely — **do not stop suddenly.**
- When tapering doses above 0.75 mg/day after >3 weeks, reduce gradually.

### Steroid equivalents (reference)
- Dexamethasone 0.5 mg/day ≈ physiological dose (equivalent to prednisone 5 mg or hydrocortisone 20 mg/day)

### Avoid NSAIDs
Concurrent NSAIDs increase the risk of GI ulceration and bleeding 15-fold. Concurrent low-dose aspirin is safe. If the patient develops indigestion, consider a proton pump inhibitor (e.g. omeprazole 20 mg or pantoprazole 40 mg).

---

## Monitoring while on dexamethasone

- **Blood glucose:** Afternoon blood sugar level testing every 2 weeks (dexamethasone causes afternoon glucose rise). Sooner if symptomatic (fatigue, polyuria, polydipsia).
- **Mood:** Watch for anxiety, depression, psychosis — risk increases with dose.
- **Sleep:** Single morning dose preferred. Consider temazepam for insomnia if needed.
- **Muscle strength:** Watch for proximal myopathy (difficulty rising from chair, climbing stairs).
- **Infections:** Be alert for opportunistic infections (oral thrush, unusual presentations of sepsis). Clinical presentation may be masked.
- **Skin:** Fragility increases — advise protective measures.

---

## Side effects

**Short-term:**
- Insomnia (common at doses >4 mg/day)
- Mood changes: anxiety, depression, psychosis

**Longer-term:**
- Hyperglycaemia / steroid-induced diabetes
- Proximal myopathy
- Cushingoid body changes (may slowly reverse on stopping)
- Skin fragility
- Immunosuppression and increased infection risk
- Indigestion / GI ulceration (especially with NSAIDs)

---

## Adrenal insufficiency — what to know

When dexamethasone is used at doses above 0.75 mg/day for 3+ weeks, adrenal suppression is likely. The adrenal glands may not respond normally to stress.

**Symptoms of adrenal insufficiency:**
- Life-threatening: adrenal crisis — hypotension, collapse, death
- Non-life-threatening: nausea, vomiting, anorexia, lethargy, weight loss, fever, headache, hypotension

**When to suspect it:**
- Dose reduced below equivalent of prednisone 5 mg/day
- Patient is unwell, has had trauma, or has undergone surgery
- Doses missed for any reason

**Sick day rules** (for patients on ≤0.75 mg dexamethasone/day who have been on steroids >3 weeks):
- Mild illness or trauma: double the dose for 1–2 days, then return to normal maintenance
- Moderate/severe illness, significant vomiting or diarrhoea: seek medical advice — parenteral hydrocortisone 50 mg IV/IM every 6 hours may be needed; hospital admission may be required

---

## Reducing / stopping dexamethasone

**Less than 3 weeks' use:** No taper needed.

**More than 3 weeks' use at >0.75 mg/day:** Taper gradually.
- At high doses (e.g. 16 mg/day): taper over at least 1–2 weeks; longer if on steroids for months.
- Immunotherapy-related use: taper only under oncologist/haematologist guidance (typically 4–6 weeks).
- Reduce dose down to 0.5 mg, then consider stopping.
- If patient becomes unwell at 0.5 mg or on stopping, increase back to 1 mg and seek medical advice.
- Write the reduction plan down for the patient, carer, and other health professionals.
- Start reductions early in the week so medical help is accessible if needed.
- If patient cannot swallow or is approaching death: use subcutaneous doses each morning (equivalent to oral dose) to prevent symptomatic hypoadrenalism.

If difficulty tapering, seek endocrinology or palliative care advice.

---

## When to involve a specialist

Your doctor should seek oncology or palliative care advice if:
- Starting or tapering emergency dexamethasone for raised ICP, spinal cord compression, or SVCO
- Difficulty tapering steroids
- Unsure about managing adrenal insufficiency (endocrinology advice)
- Anorexia/fatigue treatment extends beyond 2 weeks
