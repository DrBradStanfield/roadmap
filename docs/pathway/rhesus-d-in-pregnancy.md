# Rhesus D in Pregnancy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when and how anti-D immunoglobulin is used during pregnancy to prevent Rhesus D sensitisation and protect future pregnancies.

---

## Background

Anti-D immunoglobulin (anti-D) is given to RhD negative women to prevent sensitisation to the RhD antigen during pregnancy and after birth (if the newborn is RhD positive). The goal is to reduce the risk of haemolytic disease of the newborn in future pregnancies.

Anti-D is given by intramuscular injection (or intravenously in some cases). It is always effective if given in adequate doses within 72 hours of a sensitising event or within 72 hours of delivery.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will first confirm whether you are RhD negative before prescribing anti-D. Anti-D is only indicated for RhD negative women.

**If your blood group is not known**, anti-D will not be given until an urgent maternal blood group and antibody screen is arranged — even if the 72-hour window is approaching or has passed.

### Sensitising Events — Less Than 12 Weeks Gestation

Anti-D is indicated for the following events before 12 weeks gestation:

- Chorionic villus sampling
- Completed spontaneous miscarriage
- Surgical or medical abortion (medical abortion between 10–12 weeks only)
- Ectopic pregnancy
- Molar pregnancy
- Uterine bleeding that is repeated, heavy, or associated with abdominal pain

Anti-D is not normally required following a threatened miscarriage in the first trimester. If the dates are uncertain and you are close to 12 weeks, your doctor may recommend anti-D.

### Sensitising Events — 12 Weeks or More Gestation

Anti-D is indicated for all potentially sensitising events from 12 weeks gestation (or when dates are uncertain), including:

- All vaginal bleeding (e.g., miscarriage, threatened miscarriage, antepartum haemorrhage)
- Ectopic pregnancy
- Molar pregnancy
- All invasive fetal procedures (chorionic villus sampling, amniocentesis, intrauterine blood sampling)
- External cephalic version (turning a breech baby), whether successful or not
- Abdominal trauma
- Medical or surgical abortion
- Intrauterine death or stillbirth

### Kleihauer Test

If you are more than 20 weeks gestation, your doctor may consider a Kleihauer test. This blood test measures the size of the feto-maternal haemorrhage (how much fetal blood has entered your circulation) and is used to confirm the correct dose of anti-D. Kleihauer testing is not performed before 20 weeks, as the fetal blood volume is small enough to be covered by the standard dose.

### Routine Antenatal Prophylaxis

Separate from sensitising events, RhD negative women routinely receive anti-D prophylaxis during pregnancy:

- **Two-dose schedule:** 28 weeks and 34 weeks gestation
- **Single-dose schedule:** 30 weeks gestation

---

## Management

**Anti-D is not required if you are confirmed RhD positive.**

Your doctor will manage anti-D based on your circumstances:

### After a Sensitising Event

Your doctor will prescribe anti-D immunoglobulin as soon as possible — always within 72 hours of the event.

**Dosing your doctor may consider:**

- Less than 12 weeks gestation (single pregnancy): 250 units
- 13–20 weeks gestation, or known twin/multiple pregnancy: 625 units
- 20 weeks or more gestation: 625 units, plus your doctor will seek obstetric advice and may arrange a Kleihauer test

If a Kleihauer test is performed and shows:
- Less than 6 mL feto-maternal haemorrhage: no additional dose needed
- 6–12 mL: your doctor may prescribe two doses of 625 units (total 1250 units)
- More than 12 mL: your doctor will seek advice from a blood bank transfusion medicine specialist

**For subsequent sensitising events:**

- If the previous anti-D dose was given 2 or more weeks ago, your doctor will offer a further dose
- If the previous dose was given less than 2 weeks ago, your doctor will seek obstetric advice (a Kleihauer test may be indicated)
- If bleeding continues intermittently after 12 weeks gestation, your doctor will seek obstetric advice

**Administration:** Anti-D immunoglobulin is given intramuscularly (not intravenously). You will be asked to provide written consent and remain on-site for 20 minutes after the injection.

### Routine Antenatal Prophylaxis

Your doctor will:

1. Arrange a maternal blood group and antibody screen before the 28-week dose (you do not need to wait for the result before anti-D is given)
2. Prescribe anti-D immunoglobulin:
   - Two-dose schedule: 625 units at 28 weeks and 625 units at 34 weeks
   - Single-dose schedule: 1250 units at 30 weeks

---

## When Your Doctor Will Seek Specialist Advice

Your doctor will seek acute obstetric advice or arrange an obstetric assessment if:

- You are more than 20 weeks gestation following a sensitising event (Kleihauer test may be needed)
- A further sensitising event occurs less than 2 weeks after a dose of anti-D
- Bleeding continues intermittently after 12 weeks gestation
- A sensitising event occurred more than 72 hours ago and you are RhD negative
- A Kleihauer test shows feto-maternal haemorrhage greater than 12 mL
