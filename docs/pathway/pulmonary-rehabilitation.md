# Pulmonary Rehabilitation

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

Pulmonary rehabilitation is a structured programme of exercise and education for people with chronic lung conditions who experience breathlessness on exertion. This page explains what it involves and whether it may be suitable for you.

---

## Who May Benefit

Your doctor may consider pulmonary rehabilitation if you have a confirmed chronic lung condition and are breathless with activity. Conditions that commonly qualify include:

- Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
- Bronchiectasis
- Interstitial lung disease
- Asthma (in some cases)

To be considered, you would generally need:

- A confirmed respiratory diagnosis (spirometry is usually required for COPD)
- An MMRC breathlessness score of 2–4 (breathless on moderate-to-minimal exertion)
- Reduced exercise tolerance or restriction in daily activities
- Sufficient mobility to participate in walking-based exercise
- Optimal medical management of your condition already in place
- Willingness to commit to the full programme (typically twice-weekly sessions for 8 weeks)

---

## When Pulmonary Rehabilitation May Not Be Appropriate

Your doctor will check that it is safe to proceed. Pulmonary rehabilitation is generally not recommended if you have:

- Unstable angina, uncontrolled hypertension, or uncontrolled diabetes
- Severe aortic stenosis
- Severe pulmonary hypertension
- A heart attack within the last 4 weeks
- Recent exertional syncope (fainting with exercise)
- A musculoskeletal or neurological condition that prevents exercise
- Severe cognitive impairment
- A progressive condition where palliative care is the focus

---

## What Pulmonary Rehabilitation Can Do

Evidence shows that pulmonary rehabilitation can:

- Reduce breathlessness, fatigue, anxiety, and depression
- Improve health-related quality of life
- Improve exercise capacity
- Enhance your sense of control over your condition
- Reduce hospitalisation rates

---

## What to Expect

Programmes are typically run by respiratory physiotherapists and respiratory nurses. Sessions are usually held twice a week for 8 weeks. Depending on your situation and location, options may include:

- In-person group sessions at a physiotherapy department or community location
- Home exercise programmes (self-directed or via video)
- Community physiotherapy input

Your doctor will refer you to the programme that best matches your condition severity, co-morbidities, oxygen needs, and location.

---

## How Your Doctor Can Help

If you think pulmonary rehabilitation may be right for you, speak with your GP. Your doctor will:

- Review your diagnosis and breathing test results
- Assess your breathlessness using the MMRC scale
- Check your medications are optimised
- Make a referral to the appropriate local programme if you meet the criteria

This is a doctor-led referral process — your GP is the right starting point for access.
