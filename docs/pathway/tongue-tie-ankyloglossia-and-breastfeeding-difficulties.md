# Tongue-tie (Ankyloglossia) and Breastfeeding Difficulties

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of tongue-tie in infants where breastfeeding difficulties are present.

---

## Background

Tongue-tie (ankyloglossia) occurs when the lingual frenulum — the tissue connecting the underside of the tongue to the floor of the mouth — is abnormally short or dense, limiting tongue movement.

- Up to 10% of infants have a tongue-tie, but the majority are able to breastfeed without difficulty.
- A short frenulum may cause damaged nipples in the mother due to the infant's inability to latch correctly.
- Whether and when to release a tongue-tie surgically is controversial — there is no clear international consensus on diagnosis, selection criteria, or outcomes.
- There is no published evidence that lip ties (labial or buccal ties) affect breastfeeding or speech.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will typically:

- Discuss your breastfeeding concerns in detail
- Examine the infant's mouth, looking at tongue mobility and whether a frenulum is present and limiting movement:
  - A frenulum beneath the tongue is a normal anatomical variant
  - Ankyloglossia is diagnosed when the frenulum is abnormally short or dense and limits the range of tongue movement
- Weigh the infant and plot on a growth chart to assess for weight loss or faltering growth

---

## When to Seek Urgent Care

Seek urgent medical attention if the infant shows signs of **dehydration** in association with weight loss or faltering growth. Your doctor may request an acute paediatric assessment.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following:

1. **Breastfeeding support first** — Most infants with tongue-tie do not need surgery unless it is clearly causing breastfeeding difficulties. Lactation support and reassurance are the first step.

2. **Lactation consultant or midwife assessment** — If tongue-tie appears to be contributing to breastfeeding difficulties, an assessment of the infant's tongue function is needed before any surgical decision:
   - Infants aged 6 weeks or under: your midwife (Lead Maternity Carer) can provide breastfeeding support, or referral to a community lactation consultant
   - Infants aged over 6 weeks: assessment by a lactation consultant or midwife credentialed in the Bristol Tongue Assessment Tool (BTAT) or equivalent tool is recommended before surgery is considered

3. **Surgical options** — If surgery is deemed appropriate, options include:
   - **Frenotomy** — dividing the frenulum, typically a quick procedure; may be performed with a "swaddle, sugar, snip" technique or scissor snip under local or general anaesthetic
   - **Frenectomy** — removal of the frenulum under general anaesthetic

4. **Informed decision-making** — There is no age cut-off for treatment; each case is assessed individually. Your doctor will ensure you understand the potential complications before any surgical decision is made.

---

## Potential Complications of Surgery

Tongue-tie release surgery carries some rare but serious risks:

- Bleeding
- Oral aversion
- Persistent pain

Hospital admission may be needed if complications occur. It is also important to note that in some cases, an underlying medical condition can be missed when tongue-tie is assumed to be the cause of feeding difficulties.
