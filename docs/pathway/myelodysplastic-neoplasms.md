# Myelodysplastic Neoplasms

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when to consider myelodysplastic neoplasms (MDS), how to assess a patient with suspected MDS, and when referral to haematology is needed.

---

## When to Suspect MDS

Your doctor may consider myelodysplastic neoplasms if suggestive features are seen on a full blood count (FBC). MDS is typically asymptomatic — it is often found incidentally on blood tests.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history asking about:

- Symptoms (fatigue, bruising, bleeding, recurrent infections)
- Potential risk factors for myelodysplastic neoplasms
- Other causes of low blood counts (cytopenias)

Physical examination focuses on:

- Mouth ulcers or gingivitis
- Signs of localised infection (e.g., cellulitis, pharyngitis)
- Enlarged lymph nodes, liver, or spleen (lymphadenopathy, hepatomegaly, splenomegaly)
- Signs of sepsis
- Signs of anaemia (pallor, fatigue)
- Petechiae and purpura (small skin bleeds suggesting low platelets)

Investigations will be arranged as part of the assessment.

---

## When Emergency Assessment Is Needed

Your doctor will arrange **urgent (acute) haematology assessment** if any of the following apply:

- Haemodynamically unstable
- Moderate to severe neutropenia with the patient being unwell or febrile
- Significant bleeding or bruising
- Symptomatic severe anaemia

If sepsis is suspected, this will be managed according to sepsis guidelines.

---

## When Non-Urgent Haematology Referral Is Needed

Your doctor will request **urgent non-acute haematology assessment** if:

- Rapidly progressing anaemia, thrombocytopenia, and/or neutropenia
- Blood count shows:
  - New circulating blasts
  - Haemoglobin less than 90 g/L
  - Neutrophil count less than 0.5 × 10⁹/L
  - Platelet count less than 50 × 10⁹/L

If MDS is suspected from clinical assessment, non-acute haematology assessment will be arranged for bone marrow examination, diagnosis, classification, and a management plan.

---

## After a Confirmed MDS Diagnosis

Once MDS is confirmed:

- **Specialist follow-up:** Your haematologist will plan regular reviews, which may occur with the haematologist or in primary care.
- **Immunisations:** Recommended vaccinations for people with MDS include:
  - Annual influenza vaccine
  - Pneumococcal vaccine
  - COVID-19 vaccine
  - Shingrix (recombinant shingles vaccine)

**Important notes on injections and vaccines:**

- There is an increased risk of bleeding from intramuscular (IM) injections in patients with low platelets — your doctor will manage this based on your platelet level.
- MDS is not typically a contraindication to live vaccines, but haematology advice should be sought before administering them.
- If platelets are severely low (less than 20 × 10⁹/L), haematology advice must be sought before giving Shingrix.

---

## Ongoing Monitoring

If you have confirmed MDS and are being reviewed, your doctor will seek prompt haematology advice if any of the following develop:

- New circulating blasts on blood count
- Haemoglobin falls below 90 g/L
- Platelets fall below 50 × 10⁹/L
- Neutrophils fall below 0.5 × 10⁹/L
