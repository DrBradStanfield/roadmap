# Infectious Gastroenteritis

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of acute infectious gastroenteritis — sudden onset diarrhoea, with or without vomiting, fever, or abdominal pain.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Urgent or Emergency Care

Contact your doctor urgently or go to the emergency department if you have:

- Bloody diarrhoea
- High fever with severe illness
- Signs of significant dehydration (not urinating, dizziness on standing, sunken eyes, dry mouth)
- Symptoms not improving after 7 days
- You are pregnant, immunocompromised, elderly, or a newborn/infant
- Severe abdominal pain

---

## Background

Acute gastroenteritis is most commonly caused by viral agents — particularly **norovirus** (all ages) and **rotavirus** (children). **Campylobacter** is the most common bacterial cause.

Other important causes include:

- **Clostridioides difficile (C. difficile)** — especially after antibiotic use, or in people over 65 recently discharged from hospital
- **Listeria monocytogenes** — particularly in pregnant women, neonates, the elderly, or the immunocompromised
- **Giardia, Cryptosporidium, Salmonella, Shigella, VTEC/STEC, Yersinia** — less common but important causes

Most cases are self-limiting. Viral gastroenteritis typically resolves within a few days without specific treatment.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess whether testing is needed. Testing is usually **not** required unless you:

- Have diarrhoea not resolving after 7 days
- Are severely unwell, febrile, or have bloody diarrhoea
- Are a food handler, childcare or healthcare worker, or attend childcare
- Are immunocompromised
- Have returned from overseas travel with poor water or sanitation

If testing is needed, your doctor will arrange a faecal (stool) sample. You will be given instructions on how to collect it correctly.

---

## Management

### General Approach

The mainstay of treatment for most acute gastroenteritis is **fluid and electrolyte replacement**. Your doctor may consider antibiotics only in specific situations.

- Stay well hydrated — sip fluids regularly. Oral rehydration solutions (e.g., Gastrolyte) are helpful, especially for children and the elderly.
- Avoid work, school, or preschool until you have been symptom-free for **48 hours**.

### Management by Organism

#### Campylobacter

- Antibiotics are rarely needed. Your doctor may consider them if you have bloody diarrhoea, fever, or symptoms lasting over a week.
- If antibiotics are used, your doctor may prescribe **erythromycin** (adults: 400 mg four times daily for 5 days; children: 10 mg/kg four times daily for 5 days).
- Exclude from work/school/preschool until symptom-free for 48 hours.
- If you are immunocompromised or diarrhoea lasts more than 10 days, your doctor will seek specialist infectious diseases advice.

#### Clostridioides difficile (C. difficile)

- Your doctor will stop any antibiotic that may have caused it.
- Anti-motility medications (e.g., loperamide) should be avoided.
- Proton-pump inhibitors may be stopped.
- If symptoms do not improve within 48 hours after stopping the causative antibiotic, your doctor may prescribe **metronidazole** (adults: 400 mg three times daily for 10 days; children: 10 mg/kg three times daily for 10 days).
- For more severe infection, specialist advice will be sought.

#### Cryptosporidium

- Antibiotics are not indicated.
- Exclude from work/school/preschool until symptom-free for 48 hours.
- Do not swim in public or private pools for at least **2 weeks** after symptoms resolve.
- If you are immunocompromised or have chronic diarrhoea, your doctor will seek specialist advice.

#### Giardia

- Your doctor will treat you (and any symptomatic contacts) with antibiotics.
- Your doctor may prescribe **ornidazole** (adults and children over 35 kg: 1.5 g single dose; children under 35 kg: 125 mg per 3 kg as a single dose) or **metronidazole** (adults: 400 mg three times daily for 5 days, or 2 g once daily for 3 days; children: 30 mg/kg/dose up to 2 g once daily for 3 days).
- You may remain infectious for several months after onset of symptoms.
- Do not swim in public or private pools until at least **2 weeks** after symptoms resolve.
- Ongoing diarrhoea after treatment may be due to temporary lactose intolerance — your doctor may suggest avoiding dairy for around a month.

#### Listeria

- Listeria can cause serious illness including meningitis, bacteraemia, or pregnancy complications.
- Your doctor will seek specialist infectious diseases advice for antibiotic treatment.

#### Norovirus

- No specific treatment. Rest and fluids.
- Exclude from work/school/preschool until symptom-free for 48 hours.

#### Rotavirus

- No specific treatment. Rest and fluids.
- Exclude from work/school/preschool until symptom-free for 48 hours.

#### Salmonella (non-typhoidal)

- Antibiotic treatment is usually **not** needed and may prolong the period you remain infectious.
- If you are immunocompromised or have severe disease, your doctor will seek specialist advice.
- Exclude from work/school/preschool until symptom-free for 48 hours.

#### Shigella

- Your doctor may consider antibiotics if you are at higher risk (e.g., food handler, healthcare worker, young child in care, or the illness is severe).
- Your doctor will wait for antibiotic sensitivity results and seek specialist infectious diseases advice before prescribing.
- Exclusion requirements depend on the specific Shigella species and will be guided by public health authorities.

#### Typhoid and Paratyphoid Fever

- Your doctor will seek specialist infectious diseases advice for antibiotic treatment.
- Exclude from work, school, or preschool until symptom-free for at least 48 hours.
- Public health authorities will follow up regarding contacts and clearance testing.

#### Verotoxin-producing E. coli (VTEC) / Shiga toxin-producing E. coli (STEC)

- **Antibiotics are contraindicated** — they may worsen outcomes.
- Can return to work, school, or preschool once symptom-free for 48 hours.
- In children, VTEC can occasionally cause a serious complication called **haemolytic uraemic syndrome (HUS)** — features include reduced urine output, pallor, and bruising. Seek urgent medical review if these develop.

#### Yersinia

- Antibiotics are only used in hospitalised patients.
- Exclude until symptom-free for 48 hours.
