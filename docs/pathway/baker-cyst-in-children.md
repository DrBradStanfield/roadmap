# Baker Cyst in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of Baker cysts (popliteal cysts) in children.

---

## Background

In children, Baker cysts (also known as popliteal cysts) are usually a primary process arising directly from the gastrocnemius-semimembranosus bursa. They do not communicate with the joint space and do not indicate any underlying knee pathology.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will examine the back of the knee, looking for:

- A painless, mobile swelling at the back of the knee
- Note: Baker cysts are not usually very painful — significant pain can suggest a different diagnosis

**Transillumination** may be used to help confirm the diagnosis:

- A torch or otoscope is placed against the skin in a darkened room
- A fluid-filled cyst will glow red; a solid lump will not

If there is any concern about the diagnosis, your doctor may consider an **ultrasound** to confirm whether it is a fluid-filled cyst. In most cases imaging is not needed, as solid tumours in children are rare.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider the following:

1. **Reassurance** — advise that the cyst often resolves on its own within 10 to 20 months, though some may take several years
2. **Pain relief** — if the cyst is causing discomfort
3. **Follow-up** — return if ongoing pain or the cyst is still present after 2 years
4. **Specialist referral** — if ultrasound results are unclear or show a solid mass, orthopaedic advice will be sought; if there are persistent painful symptoms with no solid mass on ultrasound, a non-acute paediatric orthopaedic assessment may be requested
