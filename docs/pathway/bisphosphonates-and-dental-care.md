# Bisphosphonates and Dental Care

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the dental considerations for patients taking bisphosphonates, including the risk of osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ) and how to manage dental care safely.

---

## Background

Bisphosphonates are medications commonly used to treat osteoporosis and certain cancers. A recognised but uncommon complication is **osteonecrosis of the jaw (ONJ)** — an area of necrotic (dying) bone in the jaw.

Key facts about ONJ risk:

- ONJ prevalence is approximately **5%** in patients with malignancy treated with **high-dose intravenous bisphosphonates**
- In osteoporosis, where doses are much lower, the risk is far smaller — approximately **1 in 10,000 to 1 in 100,000**
- Australian data estimates a **0.01% to 0.4%** chance of ONJ following tooth extraction in a low-risk patient taking oral bisphosphonates
- ONJ is often associated with poor oral hygiene
- It usually follows a dental extraction or bony surgery, but can occasionally occur spontaneously
- Risk increases with higher cumulative bisphosphonate dose
- Additional risk factors include smoking, immunosuppression (corticosteroids, diabetes), and advanced age

---

## Assessment

### Before Starting Bisphosphonates

Your doctor will recommend:

- An **oral health check** before starting bisphosphonate treatment
- Information about the small risk of oral side effects
- For patients starting bisphosphonates for **malignancy** (not osteoporosis): a pre-treatment dental assessment to stabilise oral health and control any dental infection before bisphosphonate therapy begins

---

## Management

### While Taking Bisphosphonates

Your doctor may advise:

- **Continue regular dental care** — ongoing preventive care and regular dental assessments with your general dentist should continue as normal; do not avoid dental care
- **Avoid extractions where possible** — when a tooth does need removing, it is better to do so earlier in treatment when the cumulative dose is lower
- **Do not routinely stop bisphosphonates** before an extraction — there is no sound or consistent evidence that stopping bisphosphonates reduces ONJ risk, due to their very long half-life in bone

### If ONJ Is Suspected

Seek medical or dental review if you notice exposed bone in the mouth, jaw pain, swelling, or non-healing sores after a dental procedure. Your doctor will arrange appropriate assessment.
