# Migrant and Refugee Services

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This page provides background on refugee, migrant, and asylum seeker categories in New Zealand, health screening people may have completed before arriving, and support services that may be helpful.

---

## Understanding the Categories

### Refugees and Former Refugees

A **refugee** (as defined by the 1951 Refugee Convention) is someone unable or unwilling to return to their country of origin due to a well-founded fear of persecution based on race, religion, nationality, membership of a particular social group, or political opinion.

People of refugee background may arrive in New Zealand under different pathways:

**Refugee Quota Programme (including family reunification)**
- Refugees referred by the United Nations Refugee Agency (UNHCR) for resettlement
- Arrive as **permanent residents**, eligible for all publicly funded health services
- Will have completed health screening before arrival (see below)

**Refugee Family Support Category (RFSC)**
- Sponsored by a family member who is a former refugee living in New Zealand
- Arrive as **permanent residents**, eligible for all publicly funded health services
- Will have completed a General Medical Certificate (GMC) but generally no refugee health screening

**Community Organisation Refugee Sponsorship (CORS) Programme**
- Sponsored by approved community organisations for a two-year settlement programme
- Arrive as **permanent residents**, eligible for all publicly funded health services
- Will have completed a GMC but generally no refugee health screening

Once arrived in New Zealand, people should be referred to as **"former refugees"**.

### Asylum Seekers and Convention Refugees

**Asylum seekers** are people already in New Zealand who fear returning to their own country and have applied to be recognised as refugees or protected persons. Once their Immigration New Zealand application has been received (confirmed by a letter from Immigration NZ), they are:
- Classified as a current asylum seeker
- Eligible for all publicly funded health services
- Granted temporary work visas allowing them to work or attend school while their application is being considered

Asylum seekers may or may not have had prior health assessments, depending on how they entered New Zealand.

If their claim is accepted, they become a **convention refugee** and are granted permanent residency.

### Migrants and Immigrants

- **Migrants** are people born overseas who have chosen to temporarily settle in New Zealand (visitor, student, temporary work, or work-to-residence visas)
- **Immigrants** are people born overseas who have permanently settled in New Zealand (permanent residency, pathway to citizenship)

Ethnicity is a measure of cultural affiliation — self-perceived — not race, ancestry, or citizenship. People can belong to more than one ethnic group.

## Health Screening Before Arrival

People arriving under the Refugee Quota Programme may have had the following assessments:

### Limited Medical Certificate (LMC)
- Compulsory for quota programme applicants
- Screens for five non-waiver conditions:
  - Currently on dialysis or needing dialysis in the next 5 years
  - Haemophilia
  - Condition requiring full-time care, support, or equipment
  - Active drug-sensitive tuberculosis
  - Current or history of drug-resistant tuberculosis
- People aged 11 and older also require a Chest X-ray Certificate

### Settlement Health Assessment (SHA)
- Voluntary, offered after visa approval
- Includes medical history and examination, blood tests (hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV, syphilis), and vaccinations

### Departure Health Check
- Voluntary, offered approximately 3 weeks before arrival
- Includes medical history, physical exam, mental health screening, tuberculosis screening, blood tests (FBC, ferritin, renal function, liver function, calcium, vitamin D; and if indicated, HbA1c and lipids), and vaccinations

### General Medical Certificate (GMC)
- Compulsory for Refugee Family Support and CORS arrivals
- Full immigration medical screening including medical history, physical examination, urinalysis (age 5+), blood tests (age 15+: FBC, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, HIV, syphilis, HbA1c)
- People aged 11 and older also require a Chest X-ray Certificate

## What to Expect in Primary Care

Your doctor may consider the following when you first register with a GP:

- Reviewing any health screening completed before your arrival
- Planning catch-up immunisations if needed
- Referrals to relevant services (maternity care, dental, social work, specialist)
- Refugee health screening if this has not already been done
- Management of any health conditions identified, which may need to start from the beginning if earlier screening was not completed
- Longer appointments when an interpreter is needed — interpreters should be arranged by the practice, not family members

Asylum seekers will generally not have had refugee health screening, so a more thorough initial assessment is likely.

## Support Services

### Mental Health and Wellbeing

- **[Refugees as Survivors New Zealand (RASNZ)](https://rasnz.co.nz/)** — Free non-acute mental health and wellbeing service for adults (18+) from a refugee background. Offers community and youth services. See their [referral forms](https://rasnz.co.nz/support-for-refugees-asylum-seekers/).

- **[New Zealand Ethnic Social Services (NZESS)](https://www.nzess.co.nz/)** — Social support including counselling, child support, parenting advice, advocacy, and family harm prevention for former refugees, asylum seekers, and migrant families. Multilingual staff (Persian, Farsi, Kurdish, Turkish, Arabic, Hindi).
  - Phone: (09) 834-6668 or 021-226-5666
  - Email: info@nzess.co.nz

- **[Sahaayta Counselling and Social Support](http://sahaayta.org.nz/)** — Counselling in 15 languages including English, Hindi, Somali, Arabic, Urdu, and others.
  - Phone: (09) 280-4064
  - Email: info@sahaayta.org.nz

- **[The Fatimah Foundation](https://fatimahfoundation.org.nz/)** — Social support services including counselling, food bank, and community programmes.
  - Phone: 0800-222-432
  - Email: info@fatimahfoundation.org.nz

### Refugee and Migrant Settlement Support

- **[Belong Aotearoa](https://www.belong.org.nz/)** — Settlement support for migrant, former refugee, and ethnic communities, including playgroups and employment guidance.

- **[Red Cross – Refugee Programmes](https://www.redcross.org.nz/about-us/what-we-do/in-new-zealand/supporting-refugees)** — Range of services including a Pathways to Employment programme and support for asylum seekers and convention refugees.

- **[Shakti](https://www.shakti.org.nz/)** — Serves migrant and refugee women of Asian, African and Middle Eastern origins. Includes a 24-hour domestic violence intervention service, women's refuge safehouse, and settlement support.
  - 24-hour crisis line: 0800-742-584 (0800-SHAKTI)

- **[New Settlers Family and Community Trust (NFACT)](https://www.nfact.co.nz/)** — Services for refugees and people from refugee-like backgrounds, including resettlement support, counselling, and resilience programmes.
  - Phone: 021-280-0515

- **[Asylum Seekers Support Trust (ASST)](https://asst.org.nz/)** — Support for asylum seekers including accommodation, information, and help accessing entitlements. [Refer online](https://asst.my.salesforce-sites.com/Clients/).

- **[Refugee Council of New Zealand](http://rc.org.nz/)** — Provides advice, information, and assistance to asylum seekers and refugees.
