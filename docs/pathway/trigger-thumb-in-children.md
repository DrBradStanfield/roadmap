# Trigger Thumb in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of trigger thumb in children, including two distinct conditions: classic trigger thumb and congenital clasped thumb.

---

## Background

Trigger thumb in children comprises two separate conditions:

- **Classic trigger thumb** — the thumb cannot be fully extended at the interphalangeal (IP) joint, with a palpable nodule in the tendon at the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint
- **Congenital clasped thumb** — the thumb appears permanently flexed and bent inward at the MCP joint

**Natural history of classic trigger thumb:**
- Up to two thirds of children with classic trigger thumb will improve in the first 4 years of life without any intervention
- If symptoms persist beyond age 4, around one third of those children are likely to need surgical treatment
- Classic trigger thumb does not impair use of the hand and does not require splinting or other active treatment in the meantime

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will examine the thumb to determine which condition is present:

1. **Classic trigger thumb** — thumb cannot be fully extended at the IP joint; a palpable nodule is felt in the tendon at the MCP joint
2. **Congenital clasped thumb** — thumb appears permanently flexed and adducted (drawn inward) at the MCP joint

In both conditions the thumb may appear dislocated, though X-rays are not usually helpful early on.

---

## Management

Management depends on which type of trigger thumb is present and the child's age:

- **Classic trigger thumb before age 4:** Your doctor will likely recommend watchful waiting, as many children improve on their own. No splinting or active treatment is required.
- **Classic trigger thumb persisting beyond age 4:** Your doctor may refer your child to a paediatric orthopaedic specialist for assessment. Surgical treatment may be considered if symptoms have not resolved.
- **Congenital clasped thumb:** Your doctor may refer your child to a paediatric orthopaedic specialist early. Splinting before age 6–8 months may avoid the need for later surgery.

If the diagnosis is uncertain, your doctor may photograph the thumb in the relaxed position and with the thumb stretched as far as possible to help with specialist assessment.

---

*This information is intended as general guidance. Always discuss your child's individual situation with your doctor before making decisions about treatment.*
