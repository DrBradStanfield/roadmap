# Scabies in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers diagnosis and management of scabies in children, including first-line topical treatment, household decontamination, and managing complications.

---

## Background

Scabies is caused by the scabies mite (*Sarcoptes scabiei*), which burrows into the skin surface and triggers an allergic reaction causing an intensely itchy rash. The mite does not bite. It cannot live on animals, nor survive in the environment for longer than 1 to 4 days.

- Transmission occurs through skin-to-skin contact, or from bedding or furniture
- Itching may occur anywhere from a few days to 6 weeks after infestation — it is typically worse when hot, in bed, or after a shower
- The rash may take 2 weeks to develop and does not usually affect the scalp or face, except in babies or debilitated patients
- Nodules may develop in the armpits, groins, or shaft of the penis
- Blisters, pustules, and secondary infection (impetigo, cellulitis) may occur
- Serious complications can include post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis and acute rheumatic fever

**Crusted (Norwegian) scabies** is a rare, very contagious variant involving thousands or millions of mites, resulting in a scaly generalised rash with very little itch. It is frequently misdiagnosed as psoriasis and most often affects immunocompromised individuals.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask about:

- Previous episodes and when the rash was first noticed
- History of eczema or other skin conditions in the child or family
- Allergies
- Any current medications or lotions applied to the skin
- Contact with other itchy people
- Other symptoms such as fever

**Key features your doctor will look for:**

- An intensely itchy rash made worse by heat (showering, being in bed)
- Contact with another itchy person
- Rash in typical scabies sites — mites are commonly found on wrists, hands, finger web spaces, outer elbows, abdomen, sides or soles of feet, and genitals in boys. The rash (an allergic reaction) can appear anywhere on the body.

**Physical examination:**

- Looking for a rash with scratch marks, papules, or nodules
- Using a low-level magnifier to look for burrows or tracks, especially on the wrist, hands, finger web spaces, outer elbow, abdomen, and genitals
- Dermoscopy, which can be diagnostic
- Scabies is usually diagnosed clinically — skin scraping for laboratory diagnosis has low sensitivity

**Differential diagnoses your doctor will consider:**

- Flea or bed bug bites
- Viral infections or eczema
- Contact dermatitis
- Allergic reactions (e.g., papular urticaria)
- Immunologically mediated diseases (e.g., bullous pemphigoid, pityriasis rosea)
- Crusted scabies (if there is a generalised scaly rash with very little itch)

**Risk factors for scabies:**

- Overcrowded housing
- Institutional settings (rest homes, hospitals)
- Immunocompromised patients

## Management

> This is a summary of how your doctor may approach treatment. Always follow your doctor's specific advice for your child's situation.

### Topical Treatment (First-Line)

Your doctor will likely recommend **5% permethrin lotion or cream**, applied on day 0 and again on day 7 (two doses total).

**All household members must be treated at the same time, regardless of whether they have symptoms.**

Amount of permethrin per application:
- Age 2 months to 1 year — one-eighth of a tube
- Age 1 to 5 years — one-quarter of a tube
- Age 5 to 12 years — half a tube
- Age over 12 years — one full tube

**Application instructions (treatment will not work if not applied correctly):**

- Apply 5% permethrin to the whole body, including scalp, neck, face, and ears. Ensure it is applied to the webs of fingers and toes, under fingernails and toenails (use a nail brush), in the belly button, genitals, and all skin folds.
- Leave on for at least 8 hours (up to 14 hours) before washing off. If any area (e.g., hands) is washed during this time, reapply immediately. Applying before bed reduces the chance of it being washed off.
- Repeat the treatment exactly 7 days later.

### Managing the Itch

Your doctor may consider:

- Oral antihistamine (age-appropriate, with attention to contraindications)
- Crotamiton cream 10% (e.g., Itch-Soothe) applied to itchy areas
- Calamine lotion

Note: itching may persist for weeks even after the mites are gone — this is normal and does not mean treatment has failed.

### Environmental Decontamination

Decontamination must happen the morning after everyone has been treated, and again after the second treatment. Your doctor will advise:

**Bedlinen, towels, soft toys, and clothing worn in the last 4 days — choose one option:**
- Hot wash at 50°C for at least 10 minutes
- Dryer at the hottest setting for at least 10 minutes
- Seal in a plastic bag for 4 days at room temperature
- Seal in a plastic bag and freeze overnight (below −20°C for more than 5 hours)

Mattresses and pillows do not usually need laundering — mites cannot pass through normal bedding. If a mattress is slept on directly (without sheets), wrap in plastic and seal for 4 days.

**Other steps:**
- Vacuum carpeted floors and upholstered furniture every day for 4 days
- Wash or seal children's car seat covers for 4 days
- Do not use pesticide sprays or fogs — they do not kill mites and may irritate skin
- Do not treat pets — they do not spread human scabies

### School and Childcare

The child should stay away from preschool or school for 24 hours after starting treatment.

### Follow-Up

- Wait at least 3 to 4 weeks after initial treatment before checking for new lesions — the rash often lasts more than 6 weeks, making cure difficult to assess early
- If symptoms persist, your doctor will check adherence to the full application process and whether all household members were treated
- If signs and symptoms persist 6 weeks after correct treatment, your doctor may seek specialist advice

### If There Is a Secondary Skin Infection (Cellulitis or Impetigo)

Your doctor may consider oral antibiotics. Options typically include:

- **Cefalexin** — palatable suspension, well tolerated, funded. 12.5–25 mg/kg twice daily (maximum 1 g per dose) for 5–7 days
- **Flucloxacillin** — very effective against *Staphylococcus aureus*. 12.5 mg/kg four times daily (maximum 500 mg per dose) for 7–10 days. Note: suspension is unpalatable and must be given on an empty stomach
- **If penicillin allergy:**
  - Erythromycin ethylsuccinate — 10–12.5 mg/kg four times daily (maximum 400 mg per dose)
  - Roxithromycin (for children over 12 who can swallow tablets) — 2.5–4 mg/kg twice daily, rounded to nearest 75 mg (maximum 150 mg per dose)
- **If confirmed MRSA:**
  - Co-trimoxazole — 24 mg/kg twice daily (maximum 960 mg per dose)

### Treatment-Resistant or Crusted Scabies

If suspected crusted scabies or resistance to repeated topical treatment, specialist advice will be sought before initiating further treatment. Your doctor may consider:

- **Oral ivermectin** (fully funded with Special Authority) — single oral dose of 200 micrograms/kg, rounded to nearest 3 mg. Not for use in children under 15 kg. Repeat doses are usually required.
- Combined oral and topical treatment with topical urea/salicylate

---

## When to Seek Further Medical Advice

Your doctor will seek specialist (paediatric) advice if:

- Crusted scabies is suspected
- Scabies is resistant to repeated topical treatment
- Secondary cellulitis or impetigo is not improving on antibiotics
- Signs and symptoms persist 6 weeks after correct treatment
