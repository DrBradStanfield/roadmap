# Fibromyalgia Diagnostic Criteria

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This resource explains the 2016 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria used to diagnose fibromyalgia. Your doctor will use these to assess whether your symptoms meet the diagnostic threshold.

---

## 1. Widespread Pain Index (WPI)

Your doctor will ask you to identify areas of pain felt in the **last week** across 19 body regions, grouped as:

**Right upper region:** jaw, shoulder girdle, upper arm, lower arm

**Left upper region:** jaw, shoulder girdle, upper arm, lower arm

**Axial region:** neck, upper back, lower back, chest, abdomen

**Right lower region:** hip (buttock/trochanter), upper leg, lower leg

**Left lower region:** hip (buttock/trochanter), upper leg, lower leg

*Note: Jaw, chest, and abdomen are counted in the WPI but are marked in italics in the original tool — they are NOT included in the "generalised pain" criterion (see Section 4).*

The total WPI score is between **0 and 19**.

---

## 2. Symptom Severity Score (SSS)

Your doctor will rate the severity of three symptoms over the past week on a scale of **0 to 3**:

| Symptom | 0 = No problem | 1 = Slight/mild | 2 = Moderate | 3 = Severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fatigue | | | | |
| Waking unrefreshed | | | | |
| Cognitive symptoms | | | | |

Additionally, each of the following somatic symptoms present in the past week scores 1 point:
- Headaches
- Pain or cramps in the lower abdomen
- Depression

The total SSS score is between **0 and 12**.

## 4. Diagnostic Criteria

Fibromyalgia can be diagnosed if all three of the following conditions are met:

1. **WPI ≥ 7 and SSS ≥ 5**; OR **WPI between 4 and 6 and SSS ≥ 9**

2. **Generalised pain** — pain in at least 4 out of 5 body regions as indicated in the WPI (not including jaw, chest, or abdomen)

3. **Symptoms present for at least 3 months**

If all three criteria are met, fibromyalgia can be diagnosed even if other conditions also contribute to symptoms. (Note: this differs from the 2010 criteria, which required that no other diagnosis explain the findings.)
