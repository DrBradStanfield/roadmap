# Abnormal Bleeding or Bruising

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of abnormal bleeding or bruising in adults — helping determine whether there is an underlying bleeding disorder and what to do about it.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

Go to the emergency department urgently if:

- Acute active bleeding that cannot be controlled
- Acute onset of widespread petechiae or purpura (small red or purple spots on the skin)

---

## Background

Bleeding disorders fall into two main categories:

**Coagulation disorders** (reduced blood clotting):
- Haemophilia
- Von Willebrand's disease (vWD)

**Platelet or blood vessel disorders** (increased bleeding tendency):
- Thrombocytopenia from idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP)
- Severe thrombocytosis (platelet count > 1000 × 10⁹/L)
- Myelodysplastic syndrome
- Medications (aspirin, NSAIDs, anticoagulants, corticosteroids, omega-3, garlic supplements)
- Liver disease (e.g., cirrhosis)

---

## Assessment

Abnormal bleeding or bruising is a common presentation. Your doctor will take a detailed history and arrange appropriate blood tests to rule out an underlying bleeding disorder.

### History

Your doctor will ask about:

**Sites of bleeding** — distinguishing between:
- *Mucocutaneous bleeding:* skin bleeding/bruising, nosebleeds, gum bleeding, heavy periods, gastrointestinal bleeding, respiratory tract bleeding
- *Internal bleeding:* haematoma (deep tissue bruising), joint bleeding (haemarthrosis), intracranial haemorrhage

Bleeding at multiple sites, especially internal bleeding, is more likely to indicate a significant bleeding disorder than a single mucocutaneous site.

**Other key history:**
- Previous or recurrent bleeding — e.g., after tooth extraction, childbirth, surgery, or events requiring emergency treatment, transfusion, or iron replacement (more likely to represent a significant disorder)
- Onset — lifelong symptoms suggest an inherited tendency; recent onset suggests an acquired cause
- Family history of bleeding disorders
- Alcohol intake
- Weight loss or malaise (may suggest a systemic disorder)

**Medications** — including over-the-counter and herbal products. Most commonly implicated:
- Aspirin
- NSAIDs (e.g., ibuprofen, naproxen)
- Anticoagulants and antiplatelet medications
- Prednisone or other corticosteroids
- Omega-3 supplements, high-dose garlic, and other complementary medications

### Examination

Your doctor will examine:
- Skin, palate, and gums for bruising, purpura, and petechiae
- Lymph nodes, and check for enlarged liver or spleen

### Criteria for Significant Mucocutaneous Bleeding

Investigation is recommended if you meet the criteria for significant mucocutaneous bleeding. This requires at least one of:
- Two or more symptoms (without a blood transfusion history)
- One symptom that required blood transfusion
- One symptom recurring on three or more distinct occasions

**Symptoms that count toward this threshold:**
- Nosebleeds: two or more episodes not stopped by brief compression, or one requiring transfusion
- Unexplained skin bruising or haemorrhage requiring medical attention
- Wound bleeding lasting 15 minutes or longer, or recurring within 7 days of injury
- Oral bleeding requiring medical attention (e.g., gum bleeding, lip or tongue bites)
- Spontaneous gastrointestinal bleeding causing anaemia or requiring medical attention (not explained by ulcers or portal hypertension)
- Prolonged bleeding after tooth extraction or oral surgery requiring medical attention
- Heavy periods causing anaemia or requiring medical treatment (not explained by uterine structural problems)
- Bleeding from other mucous membrane sites (eye, ear, respiratory tract, genitourinary tract) requiring medical treatment

---

## Investigations

If you meet the criteria above, your doctor will arrange two sets of blood tests:

**Standard laboratory (e.g., community collection):**
- Full blood count (FBC) and blood film
- Liver function tests (LFT) and renal function
- Coagulation screen: prothrombin ratio (INR) and APTT

**Hospital laboratory** — for platelet function assay (PFA-100):
- PFA-100 screens for von Willebrand's disease and platelet dysfunction
- Certain medications must be stopped at least 10 days before this test to avoid a false abnormal result: aspirin, NSAIDs, anticoagulants, antiplatelet agents, corticosteroids, omega-3, and garlic supplements

**If PFA-100 is abnormal** (epinephrine and/or ADP results) and you have a lifelong bleeding history, your doctor will also arrange:
- Von Willebrand factor (vWF) studies
- Blood group (if not already known)

---

## Management

### Emergency

- **Acute bleeding or acute onset of widespread petechiae or purpura** → emergency department assessment urgently.

### Known Inherited Bleeding Disorder (e.g., Haemophilia, vWD)

If you have a known inherited bleeding disorder and present with minor bleeding or bruising, your doctor may consider community management. However:
- They will seek advice from the regional haemophilia service before starting any treatment.
- If tranexamic acid is used, standard dosing is:
  - *Dental bleeding:* tranexamic acid syrup 500 mg/5 mL — 5–10 mL every 6 hours for 7 days, held in the mouth for ≥2 minutes before swallowing; or crushed tablets used as a mouthwash
  - *Other mild bleeding or bruising:* tranexamic acid 1 g tablets three times a day for 7 days

### No Known Bleeding Disorder — Normal Blood Tests

If you have a single site of mucocutaneous bleeding and your platelet count and coagulation screen are normal, this makes a significant bleeding disorder unlikely. Your doctor will:
- Reassure you
- Advise avoiding NSAIDs (including in over-the-counter products)
- Consider appropriate management or referral for the specific symptom (e.g., heavy periods, recurrent nosebleeds)

### Abnormal Platelet Count

If your platelet count is abnormal, your doctor will follow the relevant pathway:
- High platelets → Thrombocytosis pathway
- Low platelets → Thrombocytopenia pathway

### Abnormal Test Results (PFA-100, Coagulation, or vWF)

Your doctor will refer you for specialist haematology assessment.

### High Suspicion of Haemophilia, vWD, or Other Rare Inherited Disorder

Your doctor will seek advice from the regional haemophilia service.

---

## When Your Doctor Will Refer You

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Acute active bleeding or acute widespread petechiae/purpura | Emergency department |
| Known inherited bleeding disorder with minor bleeding or bruising | Haematology service advice first |
| Suspected haemophilia, vWD, or rare inherited disorder | Haematology service advice |
| Abnormal PFA-100, coagulation profile, or vWF | Non-acute haematology referral |
| Abnormal platelet count | Thrombocytosis or Thrombocytopenia pathway |
