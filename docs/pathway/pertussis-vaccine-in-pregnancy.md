# Pertussis Vaccine in Pregnancy

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the recommended approach to pertussis (whooping cough) vaccination during pregnancy, including timing, rationale, and protection of the newborn infant.

---

## Background

- Pertussis is a highly infectious bacterial infection caused by *Bordetella pertussis* affecting the respiratory tract.
- Infants aged 11 months and younger are at greatest risk for severe pertussis — the younger the infant, the greater the risk.
- Pertussis-containing vaccine (also known as Tdap and Boostrix) is recommended as a single dose between 16 and 32 weeks gestation in **every pregnancy**.
- Maternal pertussis antibody levels peak two weeks after vaccination. Active transport of antibodies to the fetus is greatest from around 30 weeks gestation onwards.
- Vaccination in pregnancy is more than 90% effective in preventing whooping cough in infants, and more effective than vaccinating the mother postpartum.

**Note:** A national pertussis outbreak was declared in New Zealand in November 2024 and remains in place as of March 2026.

---

## Management

Your doctor or midwife will typically follow this approach:

1. **At first contact during pregnancy**, a recall is created for pertussis vaccination at 16 weeks.
2. **Funded pertussis vaccine (Boostrix/Tdap)** is administered from 16 to 32 weeks in every pregnancy, regardless of previous immunisation history, to provide maximal protection to the infant.
3. **From 13 to 15 weeks**, your doctor may consider early vaccination if you are high risk or travelling.
4. **If vaccine not given by 32 weeks**, it can still be given at any time up to delivery — however, the baby may not be fully protected as it takes two weeks for peak antibody response.
5. **If not given antenatally**, self-funded pertussis vaccination for the mother as soon as possible postpartum is recommended. This reduces the mother's risk and provides some indirect protection to the infant.

---

## Household Contacts and Close Contacts

Your doctor may also recommend vaccination for people in close contact with your new baby:

- All children in the household should be up to date with their pertussis vaccinations.
- Parents or primary caregivers of infants who were not exposed to maternal vaccination at least 14 days before birth **and** have been admitted to a neonatal intensive care unit or specialist care baby unit for more than 3 days — these caregivers are eligible for funded vaccination.
- Carers and adults in the household who have not received a pertussis booster within the past 10 years — this is usually self-funded.
- All caregivers of infants born at less than 32 weeks' gestation.
- Healthcare professionals in contact with infants are recommended a booster at 5–10 year intervals — usually self-funded unless covered by their employer.
