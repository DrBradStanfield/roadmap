# Toe Injuries

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of toe injuries, including fractures.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will ask how the injury happened. Toe injuries are commonly caused by a heavy object dropped onto the foot or by stubbing the toe against an immovable object.

Your doctor may arrange a foot X-ray if:

- There is a visible deformity of the toe
- The injury involves the hallux (great toe)

---

## Management

Most toe fractures heal with very little treatment.

Your doctor may consider:

- **Pain relief and RICE** (rest, ice, compression, elevation)
- **Buddy strapping** — taping the injured toe to the adjacent toe with a gauze pad between them, to provide support and comfort
- **Crutches** if needed for mobility
- Advising comfortable, well-supported footwear for getting around

### Management by injury type

**Displaced toe joint (especially little toe with significant deformity):**
Your doctor may reduce the fracture under a local anaesthetic nerve block.

**Obvious deformity with fracture into a joint on X-ray:**
Your doctor will arrange urgent orthopaedic assessment, as surgery may be required.

**Hallux (great toe) fracture or mallet toe:**
Management is the same for a proximal phalanx fracture and a mallet toe (avulsion of the extensor tendon from the distal phalanx).

- Your doctor may apply a toe spica — a foot back slab cast moulded around the toe for protection
- If the fracture is displaced and involves a joint, urgent orthopaedic assessment will be arranged
- Follow-up at 1 week to check for any change in position (repeat X-ray) and change the plaster
- Follow-up at 3 weeks for plaster removal

**Lesser toe fracture (undisplaced):**
Buddy strapping for comfort is usually sufficient.

**Lesser toe fracture (displaced, involving a joint):**
Your doctor will arrange orthopaedic assessment.

### Allied health support

Your doctor may refer you to additional services if:

- You have associated hypersensitivity, a functional deficit, or need a splint — physiotherapy (hand therapy)
- You are uncomfortable with weight-bearing — podiatry assessment (techniques may include padding, strapping, orthotics, or exercise)
- You are having difficulty with everyday activities (self-care, household tasks, driving, work, study, or leisure) — occupational therapy
- You are having difficulty returning to work or sustaining work tasks — work assessment and rehabilitation

---

## When Orthopaedic Referral May Be Needed

Your doctor may refer you for orthopaedic assessment if:

- The fracture is displaced and involves a joint, or causes a mallet toe
- There is obvious deformity and the X-ray shows fracture into any joints (surgery may be required)
- At 1-week follow-up, the fracture has become displaced

Your doctor may also consider referral to:

- Physiotherapy (orthopaedic or musculoskeletal) if you are having barriers to recovery, strength or range-of-motion deficits, proprioceptive or functional problems, or significant pain
- Podiatry if you are uncomfortable with weight-bearing
- Occupational therapy if you are having difficulty with everyday activities
- Work assessment and rehabilitation if you are struggling to return to work
