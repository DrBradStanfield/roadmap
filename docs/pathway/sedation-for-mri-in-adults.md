# Sedation for MRI in Adults

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers when and how sedation may be used for adults who experience significant anxiety or claustrophobia that could prevent a successful MRI scan.

---

## Background

Around 1 in 100 patients will need sedation due to significant anxiety or claustrophobia during an MRI scan. Many patients manage well with reassurance and practical strategies — sedation is not always necessary.

Things that may help without medication:
- Different MRI scanners have different sized tunnels — some provide more space
- For limb, pelvic, or lumbar spine imaging, the head often remains outside the scanner
- Patients are given headphones and a choice of music
- A mirror inside the scanner lets patients see the technologists throughout
- Technologists will talk to patients during the scan and respond to any concerns
- Patients have a button to press at any time if they need to pause or ask questions
- Scans typically take 30–45 minutes, though some take as little as 8–15 minutes

Your doctor will discuss these options with you before considering medication.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will assess whether anxiety is likely to compromise the scan, and check your overall health before prescribing sedation.

They will specifically check for **contraindications to sedation**, including:
- Respiratory depression
- Marked neuromuscular respiratory weakness (including myasthenia gravis)
- Acute pulmonary insufficiency
- Sleep apnoea syndrome

They will also consider:
- Liver or kidney impairment
- Other health conditions
- Pregnancy status
- Current medications

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider sedation if you have a history of a previous failed MRI scan, or experience severe claustrophobia. Patients with moderate anxiety do not usually require sedation — having a support person present may be enough.

### Sedation medication

If your doctor decides sedation is appropriate, they may prescribe **lorazepam (a short-acting benzodiazepine)**:
- Standard dose: **1 mg** (one tablet) for an average adult (around 70 kg)
- For a very anxious or larger patient: up to **2 mg** (two tablets) may be considered
- Dose adjustments your doctor will consider:
  - **Elderly or debilitated:** lower dose may be used due to increased risk of breathing effects
  - **Liver or severe kidney impairment:** dose may be the same, but the effect will last longer
  - **Pregnancy:** benzodiazepines are generally avoided unless no safer alternative exists

### Important instructions if you are prescribed lorazepam

Your doctor will advise you to:
- Let the MRI department know in advance that you are anxious and that your GP has prescribed an oral sedative
- Confirm with the MRI department how early you need to arrive to allow time for the medication to take effect
- Collect the prescription from the pharmacy **before** your radiology appointment
- **Do not take the medication before checking in** — you cannot be consented for the MRI after taking it. You will be told when to take it once you have checked in
- **Arrange a support person to drive you home** — it is not safe to drive after taking this medication
