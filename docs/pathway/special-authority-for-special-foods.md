# Special Authority for Special Foods

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers eligibility for subsidised standard oral nutritional supplements (known as "Special Foods") through PHARMAC in New Zealand. To access a subsidised supplement, a Special Authority is required. Your doctor or a registered dietitian can apply on your behalf.

---

## Background

Standard oral nutritional supplements — such as Fortisip or Ensure — require a PHARMAC Special Authority to be dispensed at a subsidised cost. Not everyone qualifies; there are specific medical and nutritional criteria that must be met. Your doctor will assess whether you meet these criteria.

---

## Eligibility Criteria

Your doctor may consider applying for a Special Authority if you have **both**:

1. A qualifying medical condition (see list below), **and**
2. Evidence of malnourishment after completing a 4-week Food First programme, with a nutrition goal set (e.g., a target weight or BMI)

### Qualifying Medical Conditions

Your doctor may consider this pathway if you have any of the following:

- Nasogastric or other feeding tube
- Malignancy-related malnutrition
- Bone marrow transplant
- Temporomandibular joint surgery
- Cystic fibrosis
- Liver disease
- Chronic renal failure
- Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD)
- COPD with hypercapnia
- Short bowel syndrome
- Bowel fistula
- Severe chronic neurological condition
- Severe hyperemesis gravidarum
- Multiple births where nutritional needs are not being met (under obstetric care)
- Epidermolysis bullosa
- AIDS (CD4 count < 200 cells/mm²)
- Chronic pancreatitis

---

## Malnourishment — Definition

Your doctor will assess whether you meet the clinical definition of malnourishment:

- BMI < 18.5 kg/m²
- Unintentional weight loss > 10% within the last 3–6 months
- BMI < 20 kg/m² **and** unintentional weight loss > 5% within the last 3–6 months

---

## Food First Approach

Before a subsidised supplement is considered, a 4-week Food First programme is usually trialled. This involves increasing nutritional intake through diet alone:

- Eating more frequently — adding snacks between meals
- Using high-energy foods such as milkshakes, full-fat milk, butter, cream, cheese, and sugar
- Healthy fat sources: margarine, avocado, nuts, peanut butter, oil, and cheese
- Trialling over-the-counter supplements (e.g., Complan, Vitaplan) before prescription products

If after 4 weeks nutritional goals have not been met, your doctor may consider applying for a subsidised supplement.
