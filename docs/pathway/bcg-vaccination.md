# BCG Vaccination

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers BCG vaccination eligibility, contraindications, and aftercare for infants and children under 5 years at increased risk of tuberculosis (TB).

---

## Background

The Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine has been used since the 1920s. It is a live vaccine containing an attenuated strain of *Mycobacterium bovis*.

BCG vaccination has a protective effectiveness of 80–90% against serious extrapulmonary TB disease in children, including miliary TB and TB meningitis. It does not have a significant effect on overall TB rates in the population.

BCG vaccination is recommended and funded for children younger than 5 years who are at increased risk of acquiring TB. Vaccination before age 6 months is recommended.

---

## Who Is Eligible

Infants and children younger than 5 years are eligible if they:

- Will be living in a household with a person who has current TB or a past history of TB
- Have one or both parents, household members, or carers who — within the last 5 years — have lived for 6 months or longer in a country with a high TB incidence (rate of 40 or more per 100,000)
- Will be living for 3 months or longer in a country with a TB rate of 40 or more per 100,000 during their first five years, and are likely to be exposed to those with TB

Your doctor will check eligibility at newborn visits, the 6-week baby check, and in children under 5 years.

---

## Contraindications

BCG is a live vaccine and should **not** be given to:

- Babies or children who are immunosuppressed, including those on steroids or radiotherapy, or with malignant conditions, HIV, or another immune-compromising disease
- Babies whose mothers received biologic agents (e.g. infliximab, adalimumab) during pregnancy

Before vaccination, your doctor will check the newborn screen to exclude severe combined immunodeficiency.

If there is a possibility the child may become immunosuppressed within the next 6 months (e.g. pending organ transplant), your doctor will discuss timing with a specialist paediatrician before recommending vaccination.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will:

1. Check eligibility based on household TB contacts and travel or residency history
2. Review contraindications, including maternal biologic agent use in pregnancy
3. Recommend vaccination as early as possible, and ideally before 6 months of age

**If the child is older than 6 months:** A Mantoux (tuberculin skin) test is required before vaccination, to check for prior TB exposure. The test is placed at a laboratory and read 3 days later. BCG is given at the reading appointment if the Mantoux is negative.

---

## Management

### Eligible babies and children under 5 years

Your doctor may advise:

- Arranging BCG vaccination as early as possible, ideally before 6 months of age
- Families can request a BCG appointment online or by phoning **0800 367 224 (0800 FOR BCG)**

### BCG aftercare

After vaccination, advise families to:

- Keep the injection site clean and dry
- Avoid occlusive coverings such as sticking plasters — gauze can be used to allow airflow
- Avoid applying ointments, oils, or herbal preparations to the site
- Avoid squeezing, scratching, rubbing, or massaging the site — this reduces the risk of secondary infection or scarring

Post-BCG skin lesions can be significant but do not usually require medical treatment (e.g. lancing, antibiotics).

### Children aged 5 years or older

BCG vaccination is not funded or available for children aged 5 years or older, due to global supply constraints. Discuss with your doctor if you have concerns.
