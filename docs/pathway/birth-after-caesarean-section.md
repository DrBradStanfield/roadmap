# Birth after Caesarean Section

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers birth planning for women who have had a previous caesarean section — specifically, how to approach the choice between a vaginal birth after caesarean (VBAC) and an elective repeat caesarean section (ERCS).

---

## Background

Many women who have previously given birth by caesarean section are able to give birth vaginally in a subsequent pregnancy. The two main options are:

- **Trial of labour (TOL) / VBAC** — planning for a vaginal birth, with the understanding that it may not always be possible
- **Elective repeat caesarean section (ERCS)** — planning a scheduled caesarean

There is no need to make a final decision in the first trimester. The goal is to have an informed, documented discussion early in pregnancy so that a care plan is in place well before 36 weeks.

---

## Assessment and Planning

Your doctor or Lead Maternity Carer (LMC) will:

- Discuss both birth options with you early in pregnancy
- Provide accurate information about the benefits and risks of each approach
- Assess whether any additional pregnancy or delivery concerns are present
- Arrange an obstetric review if needed — all women referred are seen by an obstetrician for discussion and a birth plan by 36 weeks gestation

---

## Benefits and Risks Discussion

It is important that women have a detailed, documented discussion about the benefits and risks of trial of labour versus elective caesarean section before making a decision.

Key considerations your doctor will cover include:

- Your individual obstetric history (reason for the previous caesarean, how it was performed)
- Any additional pregnancy complications or risk factors
- Your preferences and circumstances

Women who have had **one straightforward caesarean section** (for example, an elective caesarean for a breech-presenting baby) and wish to attempt a trial of labour may not require a formal obstetric clinic appointment, provided their LMC has fully informed them of the relevant benefits and risks.

If there are **other pregnancy or delivery concerns** in addition to the previous caesarean, obstetric assessment is required.

---

## Management

Your doctor may consider:

- **Trial of labour (VBAC):** Appropriate for many women with one previous caesarean and no other significant risk factors. Requires ongoing monitoring during labour.
- **Elective repeat caesarean section (ERCS):** Planned surgical delivery — appropriate when VBAC carries higher risk, or based on the woman's informed preference.
- **Obstetric referral:** Recommended when additional risk factors are present, or when the woman or her LMC would benefit from specialist input on the birth plan.

All management decisions should be made in partnership between the woman, her LMC, and — where appropriate — an obstetrician.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Help

Contact your LMC or go to the maternity unit urgently if during pregnancy or labour you experience:

- Severe abdominal pain (which may indicate uterine rupture — a rare but serious complication)
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Reduced fetal movements
- Any sudden change in your condition
