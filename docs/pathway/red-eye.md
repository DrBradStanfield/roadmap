# Red Eye

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways. Last reviewed: June 2019.*

Most people with a red eye have a relatively minor problem that rarely affects vision much. Causes that threaten sight almost always come with reduced vision.

> **Always discuss your symptoms and any medication changes with your doctor.** This information is a clinical reference, not a substitute for individual medical advice.

---

## When to Seek Urgent Medical Attention

**A red eye with any of these needs same-day hospital eye assessment. Call 111 or go to the emergency department if:**
- Your vision is reduced.
- There is a white or cloudy spot on the clear front of the eye (the cornea).
- The pain is severe and constant (it stops you sleeping) and bright light is very uncomfortable.
- You wear contact lenses and your eye is red.
- Something has pierced or cut into the eye.
- A chemical has got into the eye. Alkaline chemicals are worse than acids.

---

## Background

- Sight-threatening causes of red eye almost always come with reduced vision.
- Most people with a red eye have relatively minor problems that rarely affect vision significantly.
- Red eyes on both sides are common and seldom have a sight-threatening cause.

---

## Assessment

### History

Your doctor will ask about:
- How long the eye has been red, and whether it is one eye, both eyes, or one after the other.
- Any change in vision.
- Discharge from the eye.
- Exposure to chemicals, other irritants, or eye drops.
- Previous eye problems.
- Contact lens use.
- Recent injury:
  - A definite injury from a pointed object or a flying fragment, even a very small one, may need referral. An example is pain while hammering without safety goggles.
  - Many people link their symptoms to a minor knock that happened by chance. If the event sounds minor, or there is no sign of a scratch, your doctor will look for other causes.
- Light sensitivity and eye pain:
  - Severe, constant, aching pain is typical of serious problems. Sensitivity to light as well makes an important cause more likely.
  - Sharp, brief, gritty pain points to irritation of the eye surface. If this is marked, the pain can be constant and light may be mildly uncomfortable.
  - Pain that goes away with numbing (anaesthetic) eye drops points to a problem on the surface of the eye.

### Examination

Your doctor will examine the eye carefully.

### Causes to consider

Your doctor will consider whether the red eye affects one eye, both eyes suddenly, or both eyes over a long time. They may also consider wider (systemic) disease, for example:
- **Autoimmune disease**, including causes such as Reiter's syndrome, which can lead to scleritis or uveitis.
- **Thyroid eye disease**, even early in an overactive thyroid (thyrotoxicosis):
  - It affects about 20 to 40% of people with Graves' disease.
  - Smoking is the main risk factor that can be changed.
  - It usually happens alongside the overactive thyroid, but may come before or after the diagnosis.
  - Significant, sight-threatening thyroid eye disease is rare. It may occur without the eyes bulging forward. It can threaten vision by pressing on the optic nerve behind the eye, or by causing an ulcer on the cornea.

---

## Management

- If red flags are present, your doctor will arrange same-day hospital eye assessment.
- Otherwise, your doctor will manage the most likely diagnosis. This may include:
  - Allergic conjunctivitis
  - Infective conjunctivitis
  - Infectious keratitis
  - Corneal ulcers
  - Acute angle-closure glaucoma
  - Herpes simplex keratitis (dendritic ulcer)
  - Herpes zoster ophthalmicus
  - Iritis or uveitis
  - Scleritis
- If the cause is unclear, your doctor may review you again in 24 hours to check for a condition that is getting worse.

### Simple treatment

**Your doctor may suggest simple symptom relief, such as lubricant eye drops and cool compresses, if:**
- Your vision is normal.
- The outside of the eye looks normal on examination.
- There are no red flags.

Lubricant drops include:
- Hypromellose 0.3% with dextran 0.1% eye drops (funded).
- Polyvinyl alcohol 1.4% or 3% eye drops (funded).
- Hypromellose 0.5% eye drops (partially funded).

Many causes of red eye settle on their own and are only partly helped by medicines.

---

## When to See a Specialist

Depending on the diagnosis, your doctor may:
- Arrange same-day hospital eye assessment.
- Refer you for a non-urgent eye specialist (ophthalmology) assessment.
- Seek advice from an eye specialist.

---

## Key Concepts for Patients

- Most red eyes are caused by minor problems, and many settle on their own.
- A red eye with reduced vision, severe pain, sensitivity to light, contact lens wear, or an eye injury is a warning sign. Call 111 or go to the emergency department.
- If the cause is unclear, your doctor may check the eye again in 24 hours.
- Always discuss any new symptoms or medication questions with your doctor before making changes.
