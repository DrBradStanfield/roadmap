# Inguinal Hernia in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the assessment and management of suspected inguinal hernia in children, including when emergency care is needed and what to expect after surgery.

---

## Red Flags — Seek Emergency Care

Take your child to the emergency department immediately if the hernia is:

- Tender and cannot be pushed back in (irreducible)
- Associated with skin changes over the lump — redness, discolouration, inflammation, or the surrounding tissue feels woody or rubbery
- Accompanied by the child being unwell, with:
  - Abdominal distension
  - Signs of systemic illness
  - Signs of poor circulation (haemodynamic compromise)

These features may indicate **strangulation** — where the blood supply to the contents of the hernia is cut off — which is a surgical emergency.

---

## Assessment

Your doctor will take a history and examine your child. Key points:

- A history of a groin lump is taken seriously — if a parent has seen a lump and points to the correct place, this is generally considered accurate even if the lump is not visible at the time of the appointment
- Your doctor will examine the groin to confirm whether an inguinal hernia is present
- A reducible inguinal hernia may appear as an intermittent or reducible mass over the inguinal canal or in the scrotum, and is sometimes only seen when the child cries or strains
- Your doctor will assess whether the hernia shows any signs of strangulation (see Red Flags above)
- **No ultrasound is required** to diagnose an inguinal hernia in children

### Differential Diagnosis

Your doctor will consider other causes of a groin lump:

- **Undescended testis** — the testis should be felt separately from the lump, and the scrotum on that side will feel empty
- **Encysted hydrocele of the cord** — rare; irreducible and non-tender; may need referral to confirm the diagnosis
- **Lymph node** — typically positioned more to the side and below the inguinal ligament, and may be associated with signs of infection elsewhere

---

## Management

### Referral

All children with a suspected inguinal hernia are referred to a paediatric surgeon, as surgery (herniotomy) is the definitive treatment. Your doctor will arrange this referral.

If there are signs of strangulation, emergency surgical assessment is arranged immediately.

### After Surgery (Herniotomy)

Your doctor will follow up with your child 2 to 3 weeks after surgery to:

- Check wound healing
- Check the position of the testes

Return to your doctor sooner if you have concerns about:

- The appearance of the scar
- The position of the testes
- Signs that the hernia may have come back (recurrence)
