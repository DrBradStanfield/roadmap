# Antenatal Genetic Screening

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the routine genetic screening offered to all pregnant women in New Zealand — what conditions are screened for, which tests are done at different stages of pregnancy, and when further assessment is needed.

---

## What Is Screened For

Antenatal genetic screening is routinely offered to all pregnant women in New Zealand. The conditions screened for include:

- **Down syndrome (trisomy 21)** — occurs in approximately 1 in 700 births
- **Edwards' syndrome (trisomy 18)** — occurs in approximately 1 in 6,000 births
- **Patau syndrome (trisomy 13)** — occurs in approximately 1 in 21,700 births
- **Neural tube defects** (90% are spina bifida or anencephaly) and some rare metabolic and genetic disorders — occurs in approximately 1 in 1,000 births

Screening is not diagnostic — it estimates the likelihood of these conditions. Further testing is needed to confirm a diagnosis.

---

## Screening Tests by Gestational Age

Your doctor or midwife (LMC) will arrange screening based on how many weeks pregnant you are. If your LMC has not yet organised these, your GP may arrange them.

### Before 14 Weeks — First Trimester Screening (MSS1)

For pregnancies up to 13 weeks and 6 days, combined first trimester screening is offered:

- **Blood test (serum markers):** Taken between 9 and 13 weeks (optimal timing: 9 to 11 weeks). Uses a specific request form for antenatal screening.
- **Nuchal translucency (NT) ultrasound scan:** Performed between 11 weeks 2 days and 13 weeks 6 days (optimal timing: 12 weeks). A standard radiology referral is used.

Together, these give an estimate of the risk of chromosomal abnormalities. MSS1 detects 75–90% of Down syndrome cases.

The advantage of first trimester screening is that if a high-risk result is returned, decisions can be made earlier in the pregnancy.

**Cost:** The blood test is publicly funded. Some ultrasound providers charge a co-payment for the NT scan.

### 14–20 Weeks — Second Trimester Screening (MSS2)

For pregnancies between 14 and 20 weeks (optimal: 14–18 weeks), a maternal serum blood test is offered if first trimester screening was not completed.

- MSS2 detects approximately 80% of Down syndrome cases.
- The patient's age, weight, and dates must be documented on the request form.

### Ultrasound Scanning

- **11–14 week scan:** Detects anencephaly (98% detection rate) as part of the nuchal translucency assessment.
- **18–22 week scan:** Detects open spina bifida (90% detection rate) and other structural anomalies.

### Non-Invasive Prenatal Screening (NIPS) — From 10 Weeks

NIPS is a blood test that analyses fetal cell-free DNA to screen for chromosomal conditions (trisomy 13, 18, 21, sex chromosome differences):

- Available from 10 weeks gestation
- Sensitivity and specificity of approximately 99% (in a woman with 1% baseline risk); about 50% of positive NIPS results will be confirmed as true positives on diagnostic testing
- **Not publicly funded** — available privately
- A false-positive result is possible; an abnormal result usually requires a follow-up invasive test (e.g., amniocentesis) for confirmation
- Even if NIPS has been done, the 11–14 week nuchal translucency scan is still recommended to check normal anatomy for gestation

## When Further Assessment Is Needed

Your doctor or midwife will arrange an urgent referral to the **Maternal Fetal Medicine Unit** if:

- Screening results show an increased risk of aneuploidy or neural tube defect
- Nuchal translucency measurement is 3.5 mm or greater, or any structural anomaly is suspected on ultrasound
- You have previously been pregnant with, or given birth to, a child with a chromosomal anomaly, significant physical condition, or learning disability
- There is a family history of a genetic condition

The Maternal Fetal Medicine Unit will discuss further diagnostic testing options with you and your partner, including risks, benefits, and all considerations.

### Further Diagnostic Tests (Arranged by Specialist)

If indicated, the Maternal Fetal Medicine Unit may offer:

- **Chorionic villus sampling (CVS):** Performed at 11–14 weeks gestation. Microarray karyotype results available in 10–14 days.
- **Amniocentesis:** From 15 weeks gestation. Microarray karyotype results available in 10–14 days.
- **FISH testing:** For higher-risk cases of common aneuploidies (trisomy 13, 18, 21, sex chromosomes); results available in 2–3 working days.

These are invasive tests that carry a small risk of miscarriage. Your specialist team will explain all options.

---

## What to Discuss With Your Doctor

Screening is offered but not mandatory. Your doctor or midwife should discuss:

- What each test is for and what the results mean
- Your right to decline screening
- What a high-risk result would mean and what options would be available
- Informed consent before any screening is carried out
- How results will be shared between your GP and LMC

Always discuss your results and next steps with your doctor or midwife — this pathway provides background information only, not personal medical advice.
