# Infliximab Shared Care Guidance

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway supports GPs managing patients on infliximab following specialist initiation. Infliximab is a biologic medicine — your doctor will manage it in partnership with the specialist who started it.

---

## Stop the Drug Urgently — Contact Your Doctor

Stop infliximab and seek urgent specialist advice if any of the following occur:

- Signs of heart failure
- Hypersensitivity reaction (rash, anaphylaxis)
- New or worsening neurological symptoms
- Gastrointestinal haemorrhage
- Cough, weight loss, or fever (possible tuberculosis reactivation)
- Signs of serious or life-threatening infection

---

## Background

Infliximab is a tumour necrosis factor (TNF) blocker — a biologic that reduces inflammation by targeting TNF-alpha.

**Indications** (specialist-initiated, with Pharmac special authority):
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Ankylosing spondylitis
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Pulmonary sarcoidosis
- Chronic and severe ocular inflammation
- Behcet's disease
- Inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease, ulcerative colitis)
- Severe chronic plaque psoriasis

**Pre-treatment safety checks completed by the specialist:**
- Chest X-ray within 6 months (to exclude latent tuberculosis)
- TB test: QuantiFERON-TB Gold or Mantoux
- Hepatitis B and C serology (and possibly HIV and varicella zoster)
- Exclusion of seizure history
- Assessment of prior malignancy and heart failure (stable treated heart failure is not an absolute contraindication but requires close monitoring)
- Personal and family history of demyelinating disease assessed

## Ongoing Management in Primary Care

### Dosing

Your doctor will administer infliximab as an intravenous (IV) infusion under specialist or hospital oversight:

- Dose: 3–5 mg/kg IV every 6–8 weeks depending on the underlying condition
- Response generally occurs within 6 weeks to 6 months
- May be used as long-term therapy

### Monitoring

Your doctor will review you regularly. Specific routine blood monitoring is not required for infliximab alone, but:

- If taking other medications alongside infliximab, monitoring will follow those medicines' requirements
- Women and people with a cervix aged 25–70 years will be recalled for **cervical screening every 3 years** due to the increased risk of HPV infection from immunosuppression
  - If aged 20–24, sexually active, and on immunosuppressants for more than 5 years, a single HPV test may be considered
  - All those with HPV detected (any type) should be referred to colposcopy

## Adverse Effects

| Adverse effect | What to watch for | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Infection** | Increased risk of serious or life-threatening infection; typical signs such as fever may be masked | Treat infection; stop drug; consult specialist; low threshold for hospital admission |
| **Hypersensitivity** | Rash, anaphylaxis | Oral antihistamine; stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Tuberculosis (TB)** | Reactivation of latent TB — cough, weight loss, fever | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Neurological** | Reactivation of demyelinating disorders (e.g. multiple sclerosis symptoms) | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Malignancy** | Increased risk of lymphoma | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Heart failure** | New or worsening heart failure | Stop drug; consult specialist |
| **Blood abnormalities** | Thrombocytopenia, leukopenia — sore throat, bruising, bleeding | Consult specialist |

For a full list, see the NZ Formulary (infliximab) or Medsafe.

## Vaccinations

**Avoid live vaccines** during infliximab treatment. Examples include:
- MMR (measles, mumps, rubella)
- Oral rotavirus
- Oral polio
- Oral typhoid
- BCG (tuberculosis)
- Varicella zoster (chickenpox, Zostavax)
- Yellow fever

For specific guidance on vaccinations for patients on immune-modifying therapy, your doctor may consult the Immunisation Advisory Centre or Starship immunology guidelines.

---

## Special Situations

### Surgery

Infliximab should be discontinued 4–6 weeks before an operation and restarted once wounds have healed satisfactorily (approximately 2 weeks post-operatively). Your doctor will coordinate timing with the prescribing specialist or surgeon.

### Pregnancy

- Advise reliable contraception during therapy and for 6 months after completing treatment
- Infliximab should only be used in pregnancy if essential
- Your doctor will discuss risks and benefits with you and the prescribing specialist before any planned pregnancy
