# Pre-pregnancy Diabetes Planning

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers pre-pregnancy planning for people with existing type 1 or type 2 diabetes. Good metabolic control before and around the time of conception is critical for reducing risks to both mother and baby.

---

## Why Planning Matters

The risk of pregnancy complications — including miscarriage, congenital malformations, and perinatal mortality — is proportional to the level of glycaemic control in the first trimester, reflected by the HbA1c. Pre-conception counselling and planning have been shown to reduce the risk of these outcomes.

**Adverse pregnancy outcomes associated with poor glycaemic control:**
- Large or small for gestational age baby
- Maternal hypertensive complications
- Preterm birth
- Caesarean section
- Birth trauma
- NICU admission
- Neonatal hypoglycaemia
- Intrauterine fetal demise
- Early pregnancy loss

**Congenital malformations associated with poor glycaemic control:**
- Congenital heart defects
- Anencephaly
- Microcephaly
- Neural tube defects

The risk is directly proportional to the HbA1c during the first 10 weeks of pregnancy.

---

## Glycaemic Targets Before Conception

Your doctor will work with you to achieve glycaemic control before you conceive. Continue contraception until your HbA1c reaches the target.

**HbA1c target:** Less than 48 mmol/mol (without causing significant hypoglycaemia). Any reduction towards this target is likely to reduce risk — your doctor will reassure you that progress matters even if the target isn't reached immediately.

**Consider monthly HbA1c** to monitor progress towards target.

### Blood Glucose Monitoring Targets

If using **self-monitoring (finger stick):**
- Pre-meal: less than 6.0 mmol/L
- Post-meal: less than 8.5 mmol/L (1 hour after meal) or less than 7.5 mmol/L (2 hours after meal)
- Provided these can be achieved without causing significant hypoglycaemia

If using **continuous glucose monitoring (CGM):**
- Target range: 3.5 to 7.8 mmol/L
- Time in range: more than 70% (more than 16.8 hours/day)
- Time below range: less than 4% (less than 1 hour/day) at less than 3.5 mmol/L; less than 1% (less than 15 minutes/day) at less than 3.0 mmol/L
- Glycaemic variability: 36% or less

## Medications to Review

Your doctor should review your medications as soon as pregnancy is being actively considered:

- **Metformin:** Safe to continue.
- **Other glucose-lowering therapies:** Consider switching to insulin before conception if possible. Metformin and insulin are the only currently known safe glucose-lowering therapies during pregnancy. Otherwise, your doctor will switch you once pregnancy is confirmed.
- **Statins:** Stop before conception — these are teratogenic.
- **ACE inhibitors and ARBs:** Stop before conception — these are teratogenic.
- **Hypertension:** If you have high blood pressure, your doctor will follow a specific pathway for hypertension in pregnancy.
- **Diabetic kidney disease:** If you have this, your doctor will seek specialist diabetes advice about the timing of stopping statins, ACE inhibitors, and ARBs.

---

## Supplements to Start

**Folate:** 5 mg once daily (high dose — higher than standard). Start at least **three months before conception**.

**Iodine:** 150 micrograms once daily. Start at least **one month before conception**.

**Vitamin D:** Your doctor may prescribe vitamin D supplementation if you have risk factors for deficiency, including:
- Darker skin tone
- Limited sun exposure (for cultural, medical, or personal reasons)
- Living south of Nelson-Marlborough during winter or spring (for pregnant people)
- Malabsorption conditions (coeliac disease, IBD, previous bariatric surgery)
- Obesity, or chronic liver or renal disease
- Taking certain medications (anticonvulsants, antiretrovirals, tuberculosis treatment, long-term steroids)

---

## Pre-conception Check-ups Your Doctor Will Arrange

- **Diabetes review:** Weight, BMI, glycaemic control, medications, and screening for diabetes complications
- **Retinal screening:** Ensure this is up-to-date before conception
- **Dentist appointment:** Diabetes is associated with higher rates of gum (periodontal) disease, which itself is linked to adverse pregnancy outcomes

---

## Lifestyle

Your doctor will discuss the following as part of pre-conception preparation:

**Weight:** If overweight, aim for 5–10% weight loss. In type 2 diabetes, 10–15% loss may achieve remission — though this may not be possible if diabetes is longstanding.

**Diet:** Optimal dietary management is essential. Dietitian referral may be recommended, especially when starting insulin therapy.

**Physical activity:**
- 150 minutes of moderate-to-high intensity aerobic exercise per week, spread over at least 3 days
- Resistance exercise at least twice per week
- Avoid sitting for longer than 30 minutes at a time
- Even a brisk 5–6 minute daily walk is associated with an additional 4 years of life

**Sleep:** Aim for 6–8 hours per night. Sleep disorders are common in type 2 diabetes — over 50% of people with type 2 diabetes have obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA), and treating OSA significantly improves glucose levels.

**Smoking:** Your doctor can provide cessation support.

**Alcohol:** Reduce or eliminate intake during the pre-conception period.

---

## Once Pregnant

Contact your doctor as soon as you know you are pregnant. Your doctor will arrange an **early obstetric assessment** (referral to a specialist maternity service).

---

## Note for People with Diabetes Who Are Partners

Pre-conception glycaemic control in men with diabetes is also considered important, though the pathway focuses primarily on the person who will be pregnant.
