# Urinary Continence Questionnaire

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This questionnaire helps identify whether urinary incontinence is present and what type it is. Discuss your answers with your healthcare provider to guide further assessment and management.

---

## Screening Questionnaire

Answer the following three questions about the last 3 months:

**Question A**

Have you leaked urine (even a small amount)?

- If **No** — incontinence is unlikely; no further questions needed.
- If **Yes** — continue to Questions B and C.

---

**Question B**

Did you leak urine in any of the following situations? *(Select all that apply)*

1. When performing physical activity — such as coughing, sneezing, lifting, or exercise
2. When you had the urge or feeling that you needed to empty your bladder, but couldn't get to the toilet fast enough
3. Without physical activity and without a sense of urgency

---

**Question C**

When did you leak urine **most often**? *(Select only one)*

1. When performing some physical activity, such as coughing, sneezing, lifting, or exercise
2. When you had the urge or feeling that you needed to empty your bladder, but you couldn't get to the toilet fast enough
3. Without physical activity and without a sense of urgency
4. About equally as often with physical activity as with a sense of urgency

---

## Interpretation

The type of urinary incontinence is determined by the answer to **Question C**:

| Answer to C | Incontinence Type |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 — Most often with physical activity | Stress-only or stress-predominant urinary incontinence |
| 2 — Most often with urgency | Urge-only or urge-predominant urinary incontinence |
| 3 — Without activity or urgency | Incontinence due to other causes |
| 4 — Equally with activity and urgency | Mixed urinary incontinence |

---

## What This Means

Your healthcare provider may recommend further assessment and management based on the type identified:

- **Stress incontinence** — leakage triggered by physical activity, coughing, or sneezing. Often related to pelvic floor weakness. Your doctor may discuss pelvic floor exercises (supervised physiotherapy is most effective), lifestyle factors, or specialist referral.
- **Urge incontinence** — a strong, sudden urge to urinate that is difficult to defer. Your healthcare provider may discuss bladder retraining, lifestyle changes, or medication options.
- **Mixed incontinence** — features of both stress and urge. Management typically addresses the most bothersome component first.
- **Other causes** — incontinence without clear triggering factors may warrant further investigation to exclude overflow incontinence, neurological causes, or other underlying conditions.

---

## When to Seek Further Assessment

Discuss with your doctor promptly if you experience:

- Blood in the urine (haematuria)
- Pain or burning with urination
- New or sudden-onset incontinence, particularly in older adults
- Symptoms of pelvic organ prolapse
- Incontinence affecting quality of life or daily activities
