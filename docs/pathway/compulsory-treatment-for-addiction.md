# Compulsory Treatment for Addiction

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers the legal framework for compulsory addiction treatment in New Zealand under the Substance Addiction (Compulsory Assessment and Treatment) Act 2017. This is a last-resort option for people with severe substance addiction who cannot consent to treatment. Always discuss with your doctor.

---

## Important — When to Seek Emergency Help

Go to the emergency department or call 111 if the person is:

- Acutely unwell, collapsed, or at imminent risk of harm
- Showing acute complications of intoxication or withdrawal (e.g., seizures, confusion, severe agitation)
- Suicidal

Compulsory addiction treatment is **not** for acutely unwell patients — emergency or mental health crisis services are needed first.

---

## Background

The Substance Addiction (Compulsory Assessment and Treatment) Act 2017 allows a person to receive compulsory treatment if they:

- Have a **severe substance addiction** that poses a serious danger to their health or safety, and
- Have **severely impaired capacity** to make decisions about their own treatment

It should only be used as a **last resort** if voluntary treatment is unlikely to be effective.

The goals of compulsory treatment are to:

- Protect the person from harm
- Allow their addiction to be assessed
- Stabilise their health, including through medically managed withdrawal
- Protect and enhance their dignity and restore their capacity to make informed decisions
- Facilitate continued voluntary treatment after the compulsory period ends

The compulsory status lasts a **maximum of 8 weeks (56 days)**, which may be extended by a further 8 weeks if the person also has a brain injury.

---

## Who It Applies To — Criteria

Your doctor will assess whether all of the following criteria are met:

### 1. Severe Substance Addiction

Defined as a continuous or intermittent condition of compulsive substance use, characterised by at least **2** of the following:

- Neuro-adaptation to the substance (tolerance or withdrawal)
- Craving for the substance
- Unsuccessful efforts to control use
- Continued use despite harmful consequences

### 2. Severely Impaired Capacity

The person must be unable to make a legally effective decision about their own addiction treatment. Your doctor will assess whether the person can:

- Understand information relevant to the decision
- Retain that information
- Use or weigh that information to make a decision
- Communicate their decision

Note: Capacity assessment is difficult during intoxication or acute withdrawal and will be re-assessed by a specialist.

### 3. Serious Danger to Health or Safety

The addiction must pose a serious danger to the person's health or safety and seriously diminish their ability to care for themselves. Your doctor will consider:

- The nature and magnitude of the harm
- How imminent and frequent the harm is
- Situational circumstances affecting likelihood of harm

### 4. Appropriate Treatment Must Be Available

A specialist treatment centre must have availability. Treatment centres have a limited number of beds, so there can be a significant delay between application and admission.

---

## How the Process Works

This is a specialist-led process managed by Community Drug and Alcohol Services (CADS) and is not something families or individuals can initiate alone. Your doctor will:

1. Assess whether the criteria above are met
2. Consult with CADS (Community Drugs and Alcohol Service) for advice and to discuss the case
3. If proceeding, complete an application for assessment form and a medical certificate
4. CADS will allocate an authorised officer (a trained health professional) to carry out a full assessment

If the assessment confirms the criteria are met and a bed is available:

- An approved specialist completes a specialist assessment
- A compulsory treatment certificate is signed
- The person is admitted for withdrawal management
- A responsible clinician prepares a treatment plan and arranges transfer to the treatment centre
- The court reviews and may confirm the compulsory status at a hearing

At any point, the person may choose voluntary treatment — in which case compulsory treatment is not pursued.

---

## Voluntary Treatment Is Always Preferred

Compulsory treatment is a last resort. Your doctor may first discuss:

- Community drug and alcohol services
- Voluntary detoxification programmes
- Medication-assisted treatment (e.g., methadone, buprenorphine for opioid dependence)
- Residential rehabilitation
