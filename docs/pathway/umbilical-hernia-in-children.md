# Umbilical Hernia in Children

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

This pathway covers umbilical hernia in children — what it is, what to expect, and when specialist assessment is needed.

---

## Background

An umbilical hernia is a bulge or protrusion at the belly button caused by a weakness in the abdominal wall.

Key points your doctor will explain:

- The majority of umbilical hernias close on their own by age 3 years.
- Unlike inguinal (groin) hernias, umbilical hernias virtually never strangulate (become trapped and cut off from blood supply), so watchful waiting is usually safe.

---

## When to Seek Specialist Assessment

Your doctor may refer your child to a paediatric surgeon if:

- Your child is **aged 3 years or older** and the hernia has not closed, or
- Your child is **younger than 3 years** and any of the following apply:
  - There is uncertainty about the diagnosis
  - Parents would like a second opinion
  - The hernia appears to be causing discomfort (this is very rare)

If you are unsure whether your child meets these criteria, discuss with your doctor.
