# Radionuclide Thyroid Scan — Adult

*Source: Auckland Region HealthPathways*

A radionuclide (nuclear medicine) thyroid scan uses a small amount of radioactive tracer to show how the thyroid gland is functioning. It is used to investigate certain causes of thyroid problems. This scan is not a routine investigation — it is ordered by a doctor based on specific criteria, often after discussion with an endocrinologist.

---

## When This Scan May Be Considered

Your doctor may consider ordering this scan if you have:

- A **palpable thyroid nodule** with a suppressed TSH — your doctor will usually discuss this with an endocrinologist first
- An **incidentally found thyroid nodule** (discovered on imaging done for another reason) with a suppressed TSH — again, specialist discussion is typical
- **Thyrotoxicosis** where the diagnosis is unclear (for example, not a typical presentation of Graves' disease) — this must be discussed with and approved by an endocrinologist before the scan is ordered

Your doctor may also order this scan on the direct advice of an endocrinologist.

---

## Contraindications

This scan should not be done in certain situations:

- **Pregnancy** — if there is any doubt, a urine pregnancy test should be done before proceeding. In rare cases where the scan is considered essential during pregnancy, this requires special authorisation from a Nuclear Medicine Physician.
- **Breastfeeding** — breastfeeding needs to be interrupted for 24 hours after the scan, and expressed milk stored during this period should be discarded (feeds stored beforehand for 48 hours can be used).
- **Amiodarone use** — if you take amiodarone (a heart rhythm medication), this can significantly affect the scan result. Your doctor will need to seek advice from a Nuclear Medicine Physician before proceeding.

---

## Medications and Other Considerations Before the Scan

If this scan is being planned, your doctor will check whether you have recently had:

- **Thyroxine (levothyroxine)** — should not have been taken for **4 weeks** before the scan
- **Kelp tablets** (iodine-containing supplements) — should not have been taken for **2 months** before the scan
- **CT scan with contrast (iodinated contrast dye)** — should not have been performed in the **6 weeks** before the scan

**Carbimazole / neomercazole** (antithyroid medications used in thyrotoxicosis) do **not** need to be stopped before the scan.
