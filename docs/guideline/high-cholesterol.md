# High Cholesterol: LDL, ApoB, Statins and Lowering It

*This guide educates; it is not personal medical advice. Your own doctor knows your history; if this guide and your Health Roadmap plan disagree, the plan wins.*

## In short

- LDL particles cause the plaque behind most heart attacks and strokes. Genetics, cohorts and drug trials show cause, not just association [Borén 2020].
- ApoB counts those particles directly. It is the best single blood marker, then non-HDL cholesterol, then LDL cholesterol [Sniderman 2019].
- Lowering LDL lowers risk in proportion to how far it falls: each 1 mmol/L (39 mg/dL) drop is linked to about 20% fewer cardiovascular events [ESC/EAS 2019].
- Diet helps but has a ceiling. Statins are the first medicine; ezetimibe, bempedoic acid and PCSK9 drugs add more lowering.
- Most muscle aches people feel on a statin also happen on a placebo [CTT 2022; Howard 2021].
- Test Lp(a) once. It is mostly inherited and adds risk at any LDL [Kronenberg 2022].
- Brad's view: aim low and start early. The plan's targets are ApoB below 0.5 g/L, LDL below 1.4 mmol/L and non-HDL below 1.6 mmol/L, lower than most guidelines set for people without heart disease (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

## Get help now

- Chest pain, tightness or squeezing, especially if it spreads to your arm, neck or jaw, or comes with breathlessness, sweating or nausea: call your emergency number now (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Do not drive yourself [NHS heart attack].
- Signs of stroke such as face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech, even if they pass within minutes: call your emergency number now (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) [NHS stroke].
- Sudden, severe belly pain that spreads to your back, especially if your triglycerides are known to be very high: call your emergency number or go to the emergency department now (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). This can be acute pancreatitis [NHS pancreatitis].

## What it is

Cholesterol is a fat your body needs for cell walls and hormones. In the blood it travels inside particles called lipoproteins.

- **LDL, VLDL, remnants and Lp(a)** each carry one ApoB protein. They can enter the artery wall and get stuck there, which starts plaque [Borén 2020; Sniderman 2019].
- **HDL** carries no ApoB, which is why non-HDL cholesterol leaves it out [Sniderman 2019].
- **Triglycerides** are a different fat, carried mostly in VLDL. Very high levels raise the risk of pancreatitis [Nordestgaard 2016].

"High cholesterol" usually means too many ApoB particles. It causes no symptoms, and plaque can grow silently for decades [Ibanez 2021]. In the PESA study of 4,184 middle-aged adults without symptoms, about half of those with no standard risk factors already had plaque, and the odds rose with LDL [Fernández-Friera 2017].

**Common causes.** Guidelines first rule out other causes: heavy drinking, uncontrolled diabetes, an underactive thyroid, liver disease and nephrotic syndrome [NICE NG238].

**Familial hypercholesterolaemia (FH)** is an inherited condition that keeps LDL high from birth. About 1 in 311 people have it [Hu 2020]. An LDL above 5 mmol/L (190 mg/dL) should prompt the question [Nordestgaard 2016]. Other clues are fatty lumps on the tendons (xanthomas), and heart disease or sudden cardiac death at a young age in you or a close relative [ESC/EAS 2019]. If you have FH, ask about testing your close relatives, best done through a lipid clinic [ESC/EAS 2019].

## Your numbers

These are the plan's lines, in mmol/L and g/L with mg/dL beside them. A lipid panel does not need fasting for most people; a fasting test may help if non-fasting triglycerides are above 5 mmol/L [Nordestgaard 2016].

**The plan uses one marker, the best you have:** ApoB first, then non-HDL, then LDL. Non-HDL is total cholesterol minus HDL.

**On-treatment targets** (the value should be below these):

| Marker | Target |
|---|---|
| ApoB | below 0.5 g/L (50 mg/dL) |
| LDL cholesterol | below 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL) |
| Non-HDL cholesterol | below 1.6 mmol/L (62 mg/dL) |

The LDL and non-HDL targets are also the plan's "Optimal" line. A value at the target counts as above it.

**Bands above the target:**

| Marker | Borderline from | High from | Very high from |
|---|---|---|---|
| ApoB | 0.5 g/L (50 mg/dL) | 0.7 g/L (70 mg/dL) | 1.0 g/L (100 mg/dL) |
| LDL | 3.36 mmol/L (130 mg/dL) | 4.14 mmol/L (160 mg/dL) | 4.91 mmol/L (190 mg/dL) |
| Non-HDL | 4.14 mmol/L (160 mg/dL) | 4.91 mmol/L (190 mg/dL) | 5.69 mmol/L (220 mg/dL) |
| Total cholesterol | 5.17 mmol/L (200 mg/dL) | 6.21 mmol/L (240 mg/dL) | none |

Between the target and Borderline, LDL and non-HDL read "Above optimal"; ApoB has no such band.

**HDL:** low below 1.03 mmol/L (40 mg/dL) in men and below 1.29 mmol/L (50 mg/dL) in women. With no sex on record the plan grades no HDL.

**Triglycerides:** borderline from 1.69 mmol/L (150 mg/dL), high from 2.26 mmol/L (200 mg/dL), very high from 5.65 mmol/L (500 mg/dL). The plan marks very high triglycerides urgent because of the risk of pancreatitis.

**Lp(a):** normal below 75 nmol/L (about 30 mg/dL); borderline 75 to 125 nmol/L; elevated from 125 nmol/L (about 50 mg/dL). NZ, Australian and UK labs often print mg/L, a tenth of mg/dL. Mass-to-particle conversion is approximate.

## What helps

These steps work with medicines, not instead of them.

- **Swap saturated fat for unsaturated fat.** Butter, fatty meat and coconut oil for olive oil, nuts, seeds and oily fish. In a Cochrane review, cutting saturated fat lowered combined cardiovascular events (risk ratio 0.83), with little or no effect on deaths [Hooper 2020]. See the diet guideline.
- **Eat the cholesterol-lowering foods together.** In a controlled feeding trial, a "portfolio" of plant sterols, soy protein, viscous fibre and almonds lowered LDL by 28.6%, against 30.9% with lovastatin 20 mg [Jenkins 2003]. Eaten at home for six months, the drop was about 13% to 14% [Jenkins 2011].
- **Soluble fibre.** Psyllium at a median of about 10 g a day lowered LDL by 0.33 mmol/L and ApoB by 0.05 g/L across 28 trials [Jovanovski 2018]. Oats, beans and lentils add more.
- **Move, and stop smoking.** Guidelines encourage physical activity and quitting smoking for everyone, whatever their risk [Nelson 2024]. See the exercise guideline.
- **For high triglycerides,** weight loss, less alcohol and better blood sugar control can bring levels down a long way [NZ Ministry of Health 2018].

**Supplements.** Plant sterols at about 2 g a day lower LDL by about 9% [Demonty 2009], but have no outcome trial, and NICE does not advise them to prevent heart disease [NICE NG238]. Never take red yeast rice with a statin: it can hold monacolin K, the same molecule as lovastatin, in unknown amounts, with the same side effects and interactions [NCCIH red yeast rice]. Brad's entries cover cholesterol-lowering supplements, psyllium, red yeast rice and berberine.

## Medicines

Doses below are from US prescribing information; your doctor sets yours.

### Statins (first line)

Statins block cholesterol production in the liver. In 26 trials, each 1 mmol/L drop in LDL lowered major vascular events (rate ratio 0.78) and all-cause deaths by 10%, with no rise in cancer [CTT 2010].

- Common choices: atorvastatin calcium 10 to 80 mg once daily [Lipitor label]; rosuvastatin calcium 5 to 40 mg once daily [Crestor label]; pravastatin, pitavastatin, simvastatin.
- Each dose step adds less LDL lowering than the first [Law 2003]; the most intensive regimens carry slightly more muscle symptoms [CTT 2022].

**Muscle symptoms.** In 19 double-blind trials, muscle pain or weakness was reported by 27.1% of people on a statin and 26.6% on placebo. Only about 1 in 15 first-year reports were caused by the statin, with no clear excess after year 1 [CTT 2022]. In the SAMSON trial, 60 people who had quit statins took statin, placebo and empty months in random order: symptoms were the same on statin and placebo [Howard 2021]. The labels list age 65 or over, an underactive thyroid, kidney impairment, some interacting drugs and higher doses as risk factors, and advise reporting unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness promptly [Crestor label; Lipitor label]. If symptoms persist, options include a lower dose, a different statin, or stopping and trying again once symptoms settle [NZ Ministry of Health 2018].

**Diabetes.** Statins raise the chance of type 2 diabetes a little (odds ratio 1.09, about one extra case per 255 people treated for four years), far less than the heart attacks they prevent [Sattar 2010].

**Interactions.** Large amounts of grapefruit juice (over 1.2 litres a day) and several drugs raise statin levels [Lipitor label]; gemfibrozil, ciclosporin and several antivirals raise rosuvastatin levels [Crestor label]. Tell your pharmacist everything you take.

### Ezetimibe

Ezetimibe blocks cholesterol absorption in the gut. The dose is 10 mg once daily [Zetia label]. Added to simvastatin after a recent heart attack or unstable angina, it lowered LDL to 1.4 mmol/L and reduced the main composite outcome over seven years (32.7% against 34.7%) [IMPROVE-IT]. A moderate-dose statin plus ezetimibe was as good as a high-dose statin over three years, got more people under 1.8 mmol/L, and fewer stopped or cut the dose for side effects (4.8% against 8.2%) [RACING 2022].

### Bempedoic acid

Bempedoic acid blocks cholesterol production earlier in the pathway than statins. The dose is 180 mg once daily [Nexletol label]. In people who could not take statins, it lowered major cardiovascular events (11.7% against 13.3%) over about 40 months, with more gout and gallstones [CLEAR Outcomes]. The label warns of raised uric acid and tendon rupture, and limits simvastatin to 20 mg and pravastatin to 40 mg alongside it [Nexletol label].

### PCSK9 drugs

- **Evolocumab** and alirocumab are injections. Evolocumab is 140 mg every 2 weeks or 420 mg once a month [Repatha label]. On a statin, in people with heart disease, it cut LDL by 59% to a median 0.78 mmol/L and lowered events (9.8% against 11.3%) with no rise in diabetes or memory problems [FOURIER]. Very low LDL looks safe short term; long-term safety, especially outside high-risk groups, is less studied [Karagiannis 2021]. In people with atherosclerosis or diabetes but no previous heart attack or stroke, it lowered first major events over five years (6.2% against 8.0%) [VESALIUS-CV].
- **Inclisiran** is an injection given at 0 and 3 months, then every 6 months (284 mg) [Leqvio label]. It lowered LDL by about half in two trials [ORION-10 and ORION-11]. Those trials measured LDL, not heart attacks.
- **Enlicitide** is a PCSK9 tablet, approved in the US in July 2026. It lowers LDL about as much as the injections but is not yet shown to prevent heart attacks ([Lp(a) post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/lpa-cholesterol-study-shocks-cardiologists-now-what)).

### Triglyceride medicines

Levels of 11 mmol/L or more may need treatment whatever the overall risk, because of pancreatitis [NZ Ministry of Health 2018].

- **Fibrates** lower triglycerides. Limited trial evidence suggests they may reduce heart events [NZ Ministry of Health 2018], but in 10,497 people with type 2 diabetes, pemafibrate cut triglycerides by about a quarter and did not lower events [PROMINENT].
- **Icosapent ethyl**, a purified prescription omega-3 (EPA), lowered events in people on a statin with heart disease or diabetes and triglycerides of 1.52 to 5.63 mmol/L (17.2% against 22.0% over about five years). More were admitted to hospital with atrial fibrillation (3.1% against 2.1%) [REDUCE-IT].
- **Fish oil supplements** did not lower major cardiovascular events, the main outcome, in two large trials [VITAL; ASCEND], and they raise the risk of atrial fibrillation, more so above 1 g a day [Gencer 2021]. See Brad's [omega-3 entry](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/omega-3-fish-oil-benefits-forms-dosing-and-side-effects).

## Brad's view (reviewed 2 Nov 2026)

**Targets.** The targets in "Your numbers" are Brad's. The ApoB target, lower than the ESC/EAS 2019 goal of below 65 mg/dL for very high risk, rests on the Ez-PAVE trial and the PESA study (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). He aims for an LDL below 55 mg/dL himself because PESA showed plaque building even at an LDL of 60 mg/dL ([statin panic post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-30-year-statin-panic-is-finally-over)). With his patients he discusses aiming for 55 rather than 70, and he notes Ez-PAVE's limits: it ran only in South Korea, and only 60.8% of the intensive group reached the target ([cholesterol war post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-cholesterol-war-is-over-heres-who-won)).

**Measure ApoB.** Brad would rather people knew their ApoB than their LDL, because ApoB is a particle count and the count does the damage ([heart attacks post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/soon-well-forget-what-heart-attacks-are)).

**The order of medicines.** Brad's steps are a low-dose water-soluble statin (pravastatin 20 mg, rosuvastatin 5 mg or pitavastatin 1 to 2 mg), then ezetimibe, then a higher statin dose or a stronger statin, then a PCSK9 inhibitor; never skip ezetimibe to go straight to a high dose. Statin not tolerated: PCSK9 inhibitor, ezetimibe, bempedoic acid, never with a statin (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). He holds that a low-dose statin plus ezetimibe beats a high-dose statin alone, with fewer side effects (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). He takes a low-dose statin himself because most of the lowering comes at low doses while muscle risk climbs at high doses ([statin panic post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-30-year-statin-panic-is-finally-over)): pravastatin 20 mg at night plus ezetimibe 10 mg, though young and healthy, to prevent plaque as he ages ([ezetimibe post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/ezetimibe-is-the-most-underrated-weapon-against-heart-disease)).

**Side effects.** Brad's reading is that most statin muscle symptoms are nocebo, true muscle injury is rare, fewer heart attacks outweigh the small diabetes risk for people at raised risk, and the biggest risk is stopping a statin, or never starting, because of something read online ([statin panic post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-30-year-statin-panic-is-finally-over)). Rare muscle damage: stop the statin, move to a PCSK9 inhibitor. No statins in pregnancy (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

**Diet first, never diet alone.** He advises most patients to follow a Mediterranean diet, and notes that diet's LDL lowering has a ceiling that leaves a gap for most people at meaningful risk ([statin panic post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-30-year-statin-panic-is-finally-over)). The plan's food card lists oats, walnuts, almonds, ground flaxseed, edamame and extra-virgin olive oil, plus psyllium husk, and it stays on alongside medicines (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

**Lp(a).** The plan follows the 2026 ACC/AHA advice to measure Lp(a) at least once in all adults and, when it is high, drives every other risk factor down: lipids, blood pressure, blood sugar and weight (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). Brad aims for ApoB below 0.5 g/L in that case and is quicker to add a PCSK9 inhibitor, which is not licensed to lower Lp(a) ([Lp(a) post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/lpa-cholesterol-study-shocks-cardiologists-now-what)).

**Calcium scores.** If your risk factors are already controlled, Brad sees no reason for a coronary calcium score, because it would not change anything, and guidelines say not to rescan once treating ([heart attacks post](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/soon-well-forget-what-heart-attacks-are)).

**When every step is taken.** Above target with every step answered: no further card. Brad's reason: nothing else can clinically be done (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

## Who needs extra care

- **Pregnancy and planning pregnancy.** In 2021 the FDA removed its strongest warning against statins in pregnancy but still advises most people to stop once pregnant; exposure before pregnancy is known is unlikely to harm the baby [FDA 2021]. NICE is stricter: stop statins 3 months before trying to conceive [NICE NG238]. Talk to your doctor before trying.
- **Breastfeeding.** US labels advise against breastfeeding on a statin [Crestor label; Lipitor label]. Ask your doctor whether to pause the statin or keep it and not breastfeed [FDA 2021].
- **Reduced kidney function.** Kidney impairment raises the risk of statin muscle problems, and the rosuvastatin label limits the dose in severe impairment [Crestor label]. Ask about the right statin and dose.
- **People of Asian ancestry** reach higher rosuvastatin levels; the label advises starting at 5 mg once daily [Crestor label].
- **Age 75 and over.** Statins lower major vascular events at every age, but the direct evidence is thinner after 75 in people without known artery disease [CTT 2019]. The USPSTF calls the evidence insufficient for starting one after 75 without heart disease [USPSTF 2022]. In STAREE, in 9,971 people aged 70 or over without heart disease, diabetes or dementia, atorvastatin 40 mg cut major events by 30% over about six years but did not lengthen disability-free survival [STAREE 2026]. Frailty, other illnesses and other medicines shape the choice.
- **Under 18.** The Health Roadmap plan is for adults (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). A child or teenager with high cholesterol, or a family history of FH, should see their doctor.

## When to see your doctor

**Same day (in the UK, NHS 111):**
- Severe muscle pain, cramps or weakness on a statin, especially with a fever or red or brown urine. This can be muscle damage [NHS atorvastatin].
- Yellow skin or eyes on a statin [NHS atorvastatin].
- Triglycerides of 5.65 mmol/L (500 mg/dL) or more. The plan marks this urgent and says it needs immediate medical attention (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

**Book a visit:**
- You have never had a lipid panel, or not for five years, or never had ApoB or Lp(a) measured (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).
- Your LDL is 4.91 mmol/L (190 mg/dL) or more, or a close relative had a heart attack or stroke young. Ask about FH.
- You are on treatment and above target, or a side effect makes you want to stop. Do not stop on your own; there are options.
- You are planning a pregnancy while on a statin.
- Your total cholesterol is above 9.0 mmol/L or your non-HDL above 7.5 mmol/L. Ask about a lipid specialist [NICE NG238].

**Tests before a statin.** NICE checks lipids, kidney and liver tests (ALT or AST) and diabetes status, thyroid tests if you have thyroid symptoms, and creatine kinase (a muscle enzyme) only after unexplained muscle symptoms [NICE NG238].

**Recheck.** NICE rechecks lipids and liver tests 2 to 3 months after starting or changing treatment, and liver tests again at 12 months [NICE NG238]. New Zealand: a non-fasting check every 6 to 12 months until on target, then yearly [NZ Ministry of Health 2018].

## Where guidelines differ

Brad's plan uses one set of targets for every adult on treatment. Most guidelines tie treatment and target to calculated risk.

| | Risk tool | Start a statin (no heart disease) | LDL goal, no heart disease | LDL goal with heart disease |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Brad's plan** | None; uses the lipid marker | Suggests a statin when the marker is at or above target | Below 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL); ApoB below 0.5 g/L | Same |
| **New Zealand** (2018) | 5-year risk calculator | Strongly advised at 15% or more; discuss at 5% to 15%; also if total to HDL ratio is 8 or more | 40% or more reduction (5% to 15% risk); below 1.8 mmol/L at 15% or more | No set goal; notes targets of about 1.6 to 1.8 mmol/L in use |
| **Australia** (2023) | Aus CVD Risk calculator, 5-year | Prescribe at 10% or more; consider at 5% to under 10% | Not set | Not covered |
| **United States** (ACC/AHA 2026; USPSTF 2022) | 10-year risk | USPSTF: ages 40 to 75 with a risk factor and risk of 10% or more; selectively at 7.5% to under 10% | Below 70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L) at high risk; below 100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L) at borderline or intermediate risk | Very high risk: below 55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L), non-HDL below 85 mg/dL |
| **United Kingdom** (NICE 2023) | QRISK3, 10-year | Atorvastatin at 10% or more | More than 40% fall in non-HDL | LDL 2.0 mmol/L or less, or non-HDL 2.6 mmol/L or less |

Sources: NZ Ministry of Health 2018; Nelson 2024; ACC/AHA 2026; USPSTF 2022; NICE NG238.

**Other differences:**
- **Europe** (ESC/EAS 2019) sets LDL below 1.4 mmol/L with a 50% fall at very high risk and below 1.8 mmol/L at high risk. Its ApoB goals are below 65, 80 and 100 mg/dL at very high, high and moderate risk [ESC/EAS 2019].
- **Lower LDL targets.** In Ez-PAVE, 3,048 people with heart disease aimed for LDL below 55 or below 70 mg/dL; three-year events were 6.6% against 9.7% [Ez-PAVE].
- **ApoB.** ACC/AHA 2026 suggests checking ApoB once LDL and non-HDL goals are met [ACC/AHA 2026]. Brad uses ApoB first.
- **Statin dose.** NICE offers atorvastatin 80 mg to people with heart disease [NICE NG238]; Brad starts low.
- **Statin not tolerated.** NICE offers ezetimibe instead of a statin, then bempedoic acid with ezetimibe if that is not enough [NICE NG238]. Brad's plan puts a PCSK9 inhibitor first. The PCSK9 outcome trials were run on top of a statin [FOURIER]; bempedoic acid's was not [CLEAR Outcomes].
- **Very high triglycerides.** The plan calls 5.65 mmol/L (500 mg/dL) urgent. ESC/EAS puts clinically significant pancreatitis risk above 10 mmol/L, though it can occur at 5 to 10 [ESC/EAS 2019]. NICE seeks specialist advice if a repeat fasting level stays above 10, and urgent review above 20 [NICE NG238]. New Zealand acts at 11 [NZ Ministry of Health 2018].
- **Calcium score.** ACC/AHA uses it when the decision to treat is uncertain [ACC/AHA 2026]. A score of 0 may allow waiting, but not in smokers, people with diabetes or a strong family history of early heart disease. An LDL of 4.9 mmol/L (190 mg/dL) or more, as in FH, calls for a statin whatever the score [ACC/AHA 2018].

## Common questions

**My report says my LDL is normal, but the plan says "Above optimal". Who is right?** Both, by different rules. A European consensus advises labs to flag LDL from 3 mmol/L (115 mg/dL) [Nordestgaard 2016]. The plan's line is its treatment target of 1.4 mmol/L (55 mg/dL), set lower on purpose (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

**Rosuvastatin or pitavastatin?** Both are on Brad's list of low-dose starting statins; pitavastatin 1 to 4 mg lowers LDL about 30% to 40%, while rosuvastatin runs from about 40% at 5 mg to about 63% at 40 mg (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).

**Will a statin damage my memory?** A meta-analysis of 25 placebo-controlled trials found no link to cognitive impairment [Ott 2015].

**My LDL rose on a weight-loss medicine. What now?** Ask your doctor. The plan judges it against the same target, whatever the cause (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026).
