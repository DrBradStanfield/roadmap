## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. Baobab fruit pulp is a dried powder that is roughly half fiber [56], and the USDA lists 44.5 g of fiber, 173.2 mg of vitamin C and 2189 mg of potassium per 100 g of baobab powder [50]. In single-meal trials, a baobab extract drink (18.5 g or 37 g in 250 mL of water) taken with white bread [10], or a baobab drink after a glucose load in healthy adults [39], lowered the rise in blood glucose, while baobab baked into white bread did not [40]. One trial found less hunger after a baobab smoothie but no change in how much people ate at the next meal [22], and a 12-week trial in Kenyan schoolchildren found no significant effect on hemoglobin or iron status [43]. The EU authorized the pulp as a novel food ingredient in 2008 [7], the US FDA had no questions about its GRAS notice in 2009 [8], and eating a lot of it may loosen the stools [56].

## Key safety points

- Baobab lowered the rise in blood glucose in single-meal trials [10][39], so it could add to the effect of diabetes medicines. If you take them, ask your doctor first and watch for signs of low blood sugar. If someone with diabetes is unconscious, having a seizure, or cannot swallow safely, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Do not give food or drink by mouth.
- Baobab powder is high in potassium [50]. In chronic kidney disease, or with ACE inhibitors, ARBs or potassium-sparing diuretics, extra potassium can cause high blood potassium, so ask your doctor first [54].
- Anaphylaxis to baobab fruit has been reported [47]. For trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.
- People who form calcium oxalate kidney stones are advised to avoid oxalate-rich foods [36], and reported oxalate levels in baobab pulp vary widely [56].
- Eating a lot of baobab may loosen the stools [56].

## 1. Overview

Baobab (*Adansonia digitata* L., family Malvaceae) is a tree whose fruits and leaves are eaten throughout the year in rural West Africa [14][49]. The fruit pulp dries naturally inside the hard shell, so it needs no further drying before it is milled into powder [56].

A survey in Benin, Mali and Senegal recorded 300 traditional uses of baobab across 11 ethnic groups [49]. In many African countries, parts of the plant have been used for hydration, fever, parasites and cough, and to treat diarrhea and dysentery [41].

In 2008, the European Commission authorized baobab dried fruit pulp as a novel food ingredient, after the UK food assessment body concluded that it was safe at the proposed use levels [7]. In 2009, the FDA closed GRAS notice 273 with no questions; the notice covered use at up to 10 percent in blended fruit drinks, 15 percent in fruit cereal bars, and from 0.5 up to 3.8 percent in other foods such as grain products, juices, milk products and candies [8].

Baobab pulp is sold for its fiber, vitamin C, minerals and polyphenols [41]. Its extracts show antioxidant activity in laboratory tests [6][14].

### Nutritional Composition

Reported nutrient contents of baobab pulp vary widely between studies [4][20]. The USDA record for baobab powder gives these values per 100 g [50]:

- **Energy:** 250 kcal
- **Total fiber:** 44.5 g
- **Vitamin C:** 173.2 mg
- **Calcium:** 342 mg
- **Potassium:** 2189 mg
- **Iron:** 8.42 mg
- **Magnesium:** 158 mg
- **Phosphorus:** 47 mg
- **Zinc:** 0.92 mg
- **Protein:** 3.69 g
- **Fat:** 0.47 g
- **Carbohydrates:** 79.65 g (including fiber)

The main polyphenols in the pulp are procyanidins and flavonol glycosides, led by tiliroside [14]. In a study of 14 wild fruits from Burkina Faso, baobab had the highest phenolic and flavonoid content after *Detarium microcarpum* [6].

The pulp analyzed for the GRAS notice had less vitamin C than the USDA record, at 74.1 to 76.2 mg per 100 g [56].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Available Forms

Baobab is primarily available in the following forms:

- **Dried fruit pulp powder:** The most common and most studied form. The pulp is removed from the shell and seeds, then milled [7]. The human trials used baobab extract or powder dissolved in water or a smoothie [10][22][39].

- **Capsules and tablets:** Containing dried fruit pulp powder in encapsulated form.

- **Fruit juice and beverages:** Baobab pulp dissolved or suspended in liquid.

- **Baobab seed oil:** The seeds and kernels are rich in fat, at 11.6 to 33.3 g and 18.9 to 34.7 g per 100 g dry weight [4].

- **Baobab leaf powder:** The leaves are particularly rich in calcium (307 to 2640 mg per 100 g dry weight) and contain good quality protein [4].

### Bioavailability Considerations

**Fiber:** In the pulp analyzed for the GRAS notice, pectin (a soluble fiber) made up 30.9 g of the fiber, with the rest insoluble [56]. Soluble fibers that form a gel in the small bowel can lower cholesterol and improve blood glucose control [23].

**Vitamin C:** Vitamin C enhances the absorption of nonheme iron [55].

**Polyphenols:** A 2009 review called for research on the digestibility and bioavailability of baobab products [4].

**Minerals:** In a cell model of digestion, iron from baobab fruit pulp had the highest percentage bioavailability of the plant foods tested, and baobab, unlike fenugreek and moringa, did not block iron uptake from iron salts [44].

**Iron bioavailability specifically:** Adding baobab pulp to pearl millet at 10 parts per 100 parts raised the iron and zinc released in a laboratory digestion test [45].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Antioxidant Activity

Extracts of baobab pulp show antioxidant activity in standard laboratory tests (DPPH, FRAP and ABTS) [6][14][39]. A 2009 review found more antioxidant activity in the pulp than in the leaves [4]. Extracts from 6 locations in Africa were all good sources of polyphenols [10].

### Glycemic Response and Blood Sugar

**White bread with a baobab drink (Coe et al., 2013):** Volunteers drank baobab fruit extract at a low dose (18.5 g) or a high dose (37 g) in 250 mL of water with white bread. Both doses significantly lowered the glycemic response, but neither changed satiety or energy expenditure [10]. In the laboratory, baobab extract added to white bread at 1.88% reduced rapidly digestible starch [10].

**Baobab baked into bread (Coe and Ryan, 2016):** In a crossover trial in 13 volunteers, white bread made with 1.88% baobab extract did not lower the glycemic response or hunger. It did lower the insulin response, so less insulin was needed for the same blood glucose response [40].

**Baobab after a glucose drink (Rita et al., 2022):** In a randomized trial in 31 healthy adults, those who drank 250 mL of baobab extract after a glucose tolerance test had a lower glucose area under the curve (p = 0.012) and a lower peak glucose (p = 0.029) than controls [39].

**Polyphenol-mediated mechanisms:** In a laboratory test, baobab pulp extracts blocked alpha-glucosidase, an enzyme that breaks down starch, more strongly than the diabetes drug acarbose [14].

**Practical significance:** The trials above measured single meals [10][39][40]. They show a short-term effect only; long-term blood glucose control was not tested.

### Prebiotic and Gut Health Effects

Baobab powder has 44.5 g of fiber per 100 g [50].

**Prebiotic potential (in vitro):** In 48-hour fermentations with stool from three adult donors, baobab pulp powder raised acetate (+18.4 mM) and propionate (+5.5 mM), and butyrate to a lesser extent [42]. Bacteroidetes rose in all donors, while rises in Bifidobacteria and Lactobacilli varied by donor [42].

**Comparison with established prebiotics:** The authors judged its prebiotic potential promising, including in comparison with inulin [42].

**Human data on gut health:** No published randomized controlled trial has specifically examined the prebiotic effects of baobab supplementation on the human gut microbiome. A placebo-controlled trial of 16 g of baobab powder a day for 45 days in adults with obesity is planned, with gut permeability and the microbiome among its outcomes [48].

### Iron Absorption and Anemia

Baobab fruit pulp contains vitamin C, which enhances the absorption of non-heme iron [43].

**Trial in Kenyan schoolchildren (Evang et al., 2021):** In a single-blind randomized trial, children aged 6 to 12 years with hemoglobin less than 12.2 g/dl drank a baobab pulp drink (n = 29) or a drink of equal energy without baobab (n = 29) daily for 12 weeks. Hemoglobin, ferritin and soluble transferrin receptor did not differ significantly between the groups [43].

**Population-level relevance:** A cluster randomized trial in Ghana plans to test a pearl millet drink fortified with baobab pulp in 258 adolescent girls with anemia [57].

**Limitation:** No RCT has demonstrated that regular baobab supplementation improves hemoglobin levels, ferritin stores, or clinical anemia outcomes over time.

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects

Several studies have reported anti-inflammatory and pain-relieving activity of baobab [41].

**Human evidence:** No published human clinical trial has specifically measured anti-inflammatory biomarkers (CRP, IL-6, TNF-alpha) before and after baobab supplementation.

### Cardiovascular Health

**Blood pressure (theoretical):** Baobab powder has 2189 mg of potassium per 100 g [50]. Higher potassium intake is associated with lower blood pressure in adults, which in turn influences the risk of stroke and coronary heart disease [31].

**Human cardiovascular data:** No published RCT has examined the effect of baobab supplementation on blood pressure, lipid profiles, or cardiovascular outcomes in humans. A planned trial in adults with obesity will measure blood lipids and blood pressure [48].

### Weight Management and Satiety

**Human study (Garvey et al., 2017):** In a single-blind crossover trial in 20 healthy adults, a smoothie with 15 g of baobab extract reduced self-rated hunger compared with a control smoothie (p < 0.05). Calorie intake at a later meal, where people ate as much as they wanted, did not differ [22].

**Other trials:** Baobab drinks taken with white bread did not change satiety [10], and baobab baked into bread did not reduce hunger [40].

### Antimicrobial Activity

Several studies have reported antimicrobial activity of baobab [41].

**Traditional anti-diarrheal use:** Baobab has been used to treat diarrhea and dysentery in many African countries [41]. A 1997 trial in Senegal compared a rehydration solution made from baobab fruit (n = 82) with the WHO standard solution (n = 79) in children aged 6 months or older with acute diarrhea and mild to moderate dehydration. The WHO solution did better on duration of diarrhea and weight gain, but the difference was not statistically significant [34]. The authors recommended the baobab solution for home use on nutritional, economic and cultural grounds [34].

### Hepatoprotective (Liver-Protective) Effects

**Animal evidence:** In rats, a methanol extract of baobab fruit pulp given before acetaminophen (paracetamol) significantly reduced the disturbance in liver function tests [46].

**Human evidence:** No published clinical trial has examined the hepatoprotective effects of baobab supplementation in humans.

### Skin Health

**Vitamin C and collagen:** Vitamin C is required to make collagen [55]. However, these are general vitamin C effects, not specific to baobab.

**Human evidence:** No published study has specifically examined the effects of oral baobab supplementation on skin health outcomes (wrinkles, hydration, elasticity) in humans.

## 4. Recommended Dosing

No optimal therapeutic dose of baobab has been established from clinical research. The amounts below come from the regulatory notices and the trials.

### Notified food-use levels

These are the use levels in GRAS notice 273, which FDA did not question [8].

- **Blended fruit drinks:** Up to 10 percent baobab dried fruit pulp [8]
- **Fruit cereal bars:** Up to 15 percent baobab dried fruit pulp [8]
- **Other foods:** From 0.5 up to 3.8 percent in grain products, juices, milk products, beverages and candies [8]
- **General food use:** The EU decision authorizes the pulp as a novel food ingredient and lists no use levels [7]

### As a Dietary Supplement

- **Doses used in glycemic response studies:** 18.5 g or 37 g of baobab extract in 250 mL of water [10], or 250 mL of baobab extract [39]
- **Dose used in the satiety study:** 15 g of baobab extract in a smoothie [22]
- **Dose in a planned trial:** 16 g of baobab powder a day for 45 days [48]

### Fiber Considerations

Baobab powder has 44.5 g of fiber per 100 g [50]. For reference, recommended fiber intakes are 25 g a day for adult women and 38 g a day for adult men [23].

### Practical Usage Tips

- **Mixing:** The powder can be stirred into water, smoothies, yogurt or porridge.
- **Timing for blood sugar effects:** In the trials, baobab was taken with white bread or right after a glucose drink [10][39].
- **Start low and increase gradually:** As with any high-fiber food, starting with a small amount and increasing slowly may limit bloating, gas and loose stools.

### Vitamin C Contribution

The USDA lists 173.2 mg of vitamin C per 100 g of baobab powder [50], and the pulp analyzed for the GRAS notice had 74.1 to 76.2 mg [56]. The US Daily Value for vitamin C is 90 mg [55]. Reported nutrient values vary widely within each fruit species [20], so checking the label or certificate of analysis is advisable.

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety

Baobab fruits are eaten throughout the year in rural West Africa [49]. The UK food assessment body judged the pulp safe at the proposed use levels before the EU authorized it in 2008 [7], and the FDA had no questions about its GRAS notice in 2009 [8].

### Common Side Effects

**Gastrointestinal effects:** In a survey reported in the GRAS notice, one respondent said that eating too much baobab pulp could loosen the stools, which resolved without treatment once the person stopped [56]. The notice put the fiber content of the pulp at roughly 50% by weight [56]. Starting with a small amount and increasing gradually may help.

### Contaminants

**Cyanide:** In samples tested for the GRAS notice, cyanide was below the detection limit of 0.5 mg/100 g, while an earlier study reported 4.9 mg/100 g of fruit pulp [56]. The notice concluded that the pulp is expected to be safe at the anticipated intake [56].

**Oxalic acid:** Reported oxalic acid levels in baobab pulp vary. One study using titration reported levels as high as 9.5% by weight of fruit pulp, by a titration method the notice considers inaccurate, but the pulp in the GRAS notice, tested by HPLC, was below the detection limit of 400 mg/kg [56]. People who form calcium oxalate kidney stones are advised to avoid oxalate-rich foods [36].

**Heavy metals and mycotoxins:** The GRAS notice lists pesticide residues, heavy metals and mycotoxins as possible contaminants of the pulp [56]. Commercial products sourced from reputable suppliers should be tested for contaminants.

### Allergic Potential

Anaphylaxis to baobab fruit has been described in a published case report [47]. For trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

### Pregnancy and Lactation

There is insufficient clinical data on the safety of baobab supplementation during pregnancy and lactation. Given the lack of clinical safety data, pregnant and nursing women should consult their healthcare provider before using baobab supplements (as opposed to occasional dietary consumption).

### Kidney Disease

Individuals with kidney disease should be cautious with baobab supplementation due to:
- **High potassium content:** Baobab powder has 2189 mg of potassium per 100 g [50]. In chronic kidney disease, even dietary potassium intakes below the adequate intake can cause high blood potassium [54].
- **Oxalate content:** Reported oxalic acid levels vary widely [56], and people who form calcium oxalate stones are advised to avoid oxalate-rich foods [36].
- Consult a healthcare provider before use if you have chronic kidney disease.

## 6. Drug Interactions

No specific drug interactions have been formally studied or reported for baobab fruit pulp. However, several theoretical interactions warrant caution based on its composition:

### Fiber-Related Interactions

Baobab pulp is roughly half fiber by weight [56], but no study has tested whether it changes how medicines are absorbed.

### Blood Sugar-Lowering Interaction

Because baobab lowered the glycemic response in single-meal trials [10][39], people taking insulin or other glucose-lowering medicines should check their blood glucose more carefully when starting baobab, as there is a theoretical risk of low blood sugar. If someone with diabetes is unconscious, having a seizure, or cannot swallow safely, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Do not give food or drink by mouth.

### Iron Absorption Interaction

Vitamin C enhances nonheme iron absorption [55]. In hereditary hemochromatosis, long-term high doses of vitamin C could worsen iron overload and cause tissue damage [55], so people with this condition should ask their doctor before taking baobab.

### Potassium-Related Interactions

The high potassium content of baobab is relevant for individuals taking:
- **ACE inhibitors / ARBs** (benazepril, losartan): these medicines reduce potassium excretion in the urine, which can lead to high blood potassium [54]
- **Potassium-sparing diuretics** (spironolactone, amiloride): these reduce potassium excretion in the urine and can cause high blood potassium [54]

### General Recommendation

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 7. Dietary Sources

The European Commission has authorized imports of baobab fruit pulp as a novel food [49]. Internationally, it is available in the following forms:

### Baobab Fruit Pulp Powder

The most accessible form worldwide. The dried pulp is milled and separated into coarse and fine lots [7]. It can be:
- Mixed into water, juice, or milk
- Added to smoothies and protein shakes
- Stirred into yogurt, oatmeal, or breakfast cereal
- Added to energy bars and ball recipes

### In Traditional African Cuisine

In rural West Africa, baobab is used extensively for subsistence [49]:

- **Fruit pulp and leaves:** Eaten throughout the year [49].
- **Leaves:** Particularly rich in calcium, with good quality protein [4].
- **Seeds:** The whole seeds and kernels have a relatively high fat content [4].

### Sustainability Considerations

A 2010 study warned that exporting baobab fruits could reduce nutritional intake in rural West Africa and change power relations and access rights [49]. The authors suggested capacity building and certification to support a sustainable and ethical trade [49].

## 8. Comparison with Established Fiber and Vitamin C Sources

### Fiber Comparison (per 100 g)

Per 100 g, baobab powder has 44.5 g of fiber, while raw orange, banana and blueberries have 2.4 to 2.6 g [50][51][52][53]. No trial has compared baobab with fiber supplements such as psyllium.

### Vitamin C Comparison (per 40 g of pulp)

A 2009 review found that 40 g of baobab pulp covers 84 to more than 100% of the recommended daily intake of vitamin C for pregnant women aged 19 to 30 years [4].
