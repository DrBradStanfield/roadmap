## Current view (reviewed 25 October 2026)

Brad does not see apple cider vinegar as a dependable solution for weight loss or blood sugar control: there might be a small effect in certain short-term trials, but the research is limited and sometimes flawed ([Brad, 12 Feb 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/do-not-use-apple-cider-vinegar-to-lose-weight-use-this-instead)). A meta-analysis reported a small drop in fasting glucose, but many of its trials were not properly blinded, so he rates the effect on fasting blood sugar as uncertain ([Brad, 12 Feb 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/do-not-use-apple-cider-vinegar-to-lose-weight-use-this-instead)). In a blinded Japanese trial of diluted apple cider vinegar taken twice a day, the low-dose group lost an average of 2.6 pounds and the high-dose group about 4.2 pounds after three months ([Brad, 12 Feb 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/do-not-use-apple-cider-vinegar-to-lose-weight-use-this-instead); Kondo 2009 [30]). He does not take it himself and does not think the results are worth seeking out; enjoy it on a salad as part of a healthy diet ([Brad, 6 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/benefits-apple-cider-vinegar)). If you have a stomach ulcer, esophageal damage or other digestive problems, the acid can make them worse, which is why studies use a very diluted drink, typically a tablespoon at most in an 8 oz glass of water ([Brad, 6 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/benefits-apple-cider-vinegar)). Used in moderation it won't hurt you, though it can damage tooth enamel, and relying on it as your primary strategy will likely leave you disappointed ([Brad, 12 Feb 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/do-not-use-apple-cider-vinegar-to-lose-weight-use-this-instead)).

## Key safety points

- In people with type 1 diabetes and gastroparesis, apple cider vinegar slowed stomach emptying even further, which may upset blood sugar control [10]. If you take insulin or other diabetes medicines, check with your doctor first.
- Very large amounts were linked to low potassium and osteoporosis in one case report [62]. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.
- Case reports link it to acute pancreatitis [16] and to liver injury after years of use [61]. For sudden severe upper belly pain with nausea or vomiting, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.
- Apple cider vinegar tablets have injured the esophagus, and tested tablet brands varied widely in acid content and label claims [15].
- Regular drinking can erode tooth enamel [57][68]. Dilute it and rinse your mouth with water afterwards [67].
- On the skin it can cause chemical burns [63][69], and dilute soaks irritated the skin of most people in a small trial [50].
- It can cause indigestion or an upset stomach [67].

## 1. Overview

Apple cider vinegar (ACV) is made in two fermentation steps: yeast turn the sugars in apple juice or cider into alcohol, then bacteria turn the alcohol into acetic acid [67]. Most products are between 4 and 7% acetic acid [67].

ACV is mostly water and acetic acid, with trace minerals and flavor compounds carried over from the apples [67].

Vinegar has a long medicinal history. Hippocrates (c. 420 BC) used it to manage wounds, and before modern diabetes drugs, people with diabetes drank vinegar "teas" [21].

Most research looks at blood sugar and weight. In cultured intestinal cells, acetic acid lowered the activity of the enzymes that split sugars such as sucrose and maltose [8]. In small human studies, vinegar slowed stomach emptying after a meal [10][11]. A 2020 systematic review judged the evidence on health effects insufficient because of a lack of high-quality research, and called for large, long-term trials with a low risk of bias [70].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

ACV is sold as a liquid and as tablets, capsules and gummies. Brad notes that most studies use a very diluted liquid [67].

### Liquid Apple Cider Vinegar

The trials in this article gave diluted liquid vinegar, usually with or after meals [20][26][30].

Unfiltered products keep the mother, and filtered products remove it [67]. Some products claim that only vinegar with the mother has benefits [67].

The active ingredient in the trials is acetic acid, which every vinegar contains [67].

### Apple Cider Vinegar Pills, Capsules, and Gummies

ACV is also sold as tablets, capsules and gummies.

**Acetic acid variability:** When researchers tested eight ACV tablet products, tablet size, pH, acid content and label claims varied widely between brands, and they doubted that some even contained ACV [15].

**Safety concerns:** The same tablet study began after a reported case of esophageal injury from an ACV tablet [15]. Acute pancreatitis followed 30 days of a weight-loss gummy whose main ingredient was 1,000 mg of ACV, in a woman aged 84 [16].

### Comparison Table

| Form | Acetic Acid Content | Clinical Evidence | Key Advantages | Key Disadvantages |
|------|:---:|:---:|------|------|
| Liquid (filtered) | 4 to 7% for most products | Used in the trials here | Form used in trials | Taste, tooth enamel erosion, stomach and esophageal irritation |
| Liquid (unfiltered, with mother) | 4 to 7% for most products | Not tested | None shown | Taste, tooth enamel erosion |
| Pills/Tablets | Varies widely between brands | Not tested in the trials here | No taste | Unreliable acid content, esophageal injury reported |
| Gummies | Unknown | No trials | Palatable | One pancreatitis case report |

### Comparison to Other Vinegars

Whether apple cider vinegar does anything that red wine, balsamic or white vinegar would not has not been tested; the trials point to acetic acid, which all vinegars contain [67]. In a Swedish study of 12 healthy volunteers, vinegar providing 18, 23 or 28 mmol of acetic acid lowered blood sugar and insulin after a bread meal, and the higher the dose, the lower the responses [20].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Blood Sugar Control

Liquid vinegar taken with a meal may blunt the blood sugar rise after it. Evidence for longer-term effects in diabetes is mixed and comes from small trials of uneven quality [17][67].

**Mechanism:** In cultured intestinal cells, acetic acid lowered the activity of sucrase, maltase, lactase and other enzymes that split sugars [8]. In healthy volunteers, vinegar with a starchy meal lowered blood sugar and insulin responses, probably by slowing stomach emptying [11].

**In healthy adults without diabetes:**

A 2006 review of vinegar's medicinal uses focused on trials of its effect on blood sugar [21].

- **Johnston et al. (2005), n=11 healthy adults:** In a randomized crossover study, vinegar or peanut products taken with a bagel and juice meal cut the 60-minute blood sugar response by about 55% [22].

- **Ostman et al. (2005), n=12 healthy adults:** Vinegar with white bread lowered blood sugar and insulin responses and increased fullness, with larger effects at higher acetic acid doses [20].

- **Brad's summary:** It's possible that for people without diabetes, vinegar might reduce blood sugar spikes after eating [67].

**In people with insulin resistance:**

- **Johnston et al. (2004):** A study tested vinegar with a high-carbohydrate meal in people with insulin resistance or type 2 diabetes and measured insulin sensitivity [23].

**In people with type 2 diabetes:**

- **White et al. (2007):** A trial in adults with well-controlled type 2 diabetes tested vinegar taken at bedtime and measured waking blood sugar [24].

- **Gheflati et al. (2019), n=70 adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood lipids (Iran):** 20 ml of apple vinegar per day for 8 weeks lowered fasting blood sugar by an average of 10.16 mg/dl, a significant difference from the control group. Insulin and insulin resistance measures fell in both groups, and blood pressure did not change [26].

**Meta-analyses and systematic reviews:**

A 2021 meta-analysis of nine studies (10 study arms) found that ACV lowered fasting plasma glucose by 7.97 mg/dL and HbA1c by 0.50, with no significant effect on fasting insulin or insulin resistance. The fall in fasting glucose was significant in studies lasting more than 8 weeks, and ACV appeared to raise fasting glucose in apparently healthy participants [17]. Brad notes that of the nine studies, one was not randomized and only three were blinded, so the data may not be reliable [67].

**Synthesis:** Vinegar taken with a carbohydrate meal lowers the blood sugar rise in small studies of healthy people [20][22]. Effects on fasting blood sugar and HbA1c in diabetes are small and come from trials of uneven quality [17][67].

### Weight Loss

Evidence for weight loss comes from a few small trials, and one of them was later retracted [30][32].

**Meta-analysis of RCTs:**

- **Castagna et al. (2025):** This meta-analysis included the later-retracted Lebanese trial, so its pooled results cannot be relied on [12][32].

**Individual RCTs:**

- **Kondo et al. (2009), obese adults (Japan):** In a double-blind trial, participants drank 500 ml a day of a drink containing 15 ml of vinegar (750 mg acetic acid), 30 ml of vinegar (1,500 mg acetic acid) or no vinegar for 12 weeks. Weight, BMI, triglycerides, waist size and visceral fat were significantly lower in both vinegar groups than with placebo [30].

- **Abou-Khalil et al. (2024, RETRACTED):** This trial in Lebanese adolescents and young adults reported weight loss with ACV. It was retracted in 2025, and its results should not be relied on [32].

**Synthesis:** ACV may produce modest weight loss, based on one 12-week trial [30]. Brad judges the data too limited and inconsistent for a strong recommendation [66]. He also notes that the appetite effects in these studies seem short-term, and very few long-term studies exist [67].

### Lipid Profile (Cholesterol and Triglycerides)

Several of the trials above measured blood lipids as secondary outcomes.

- **Kondo et al. (2009):** Triglycerides were significantly lower in both vinegar groups than with placebo after 12 weeks [30].

- **2021 meta-analysis:** ACV lowered total cholesterol by 6.06 mg/dL, with no significant effect on LDL or HDL cholesterol. Total cholesterol and triglycerides fell in people with type 2 diabetes, at 15 mL/day or less, and with use for more than 8 weeks [17].

No trial in this article was designed with lipids as its main outcome.

### Blood Pressure

- **Gheflati et al. (2019):** In adults with type 2 diabetes and high blood lipids, 20 ml of apple vinegar per day for 8 weeks had no meaningful effect on blood pressure [26].

No trial in this article studied blood pressure as its main outcome.

### Depression and Mood

Brad notes animal studies suggesting acetic acid might change how the body processes amino acids used to make mood-regulating neurotransmitters, but no study has proven a link between vinegar and these neurotransmitters [67].

- **Johnston et al. (2021), healthy college students:** For 4 weeks, 14 took 1.5 g of acetic acid a day as liquid vinegar and 11 took 0.015 g of acetic acid a day as a pill. Depression scores improved more in the vinegar group, and urine tests showed changes in glycine, serine and threonine metabolism [35].

The study was small, and the pill control did not match the vinegar drink, so the results need confirmation [35].

### Digestion and Acid Reflux

Social media claims that ACV eases bloating, gas and heartburn. Brad notes that very little research has tested this, and that as an acid, vinegar can make stomach and esophagus problems worse [67].

### Balancing pH

ACV is sometimes promoted to "balance" the body's pH. Blood pH is tightly regulated by the body's organs, with the kidneys playing a central role, and no evidence shows that ACV changes it or that a more acidic pH would help [38][67].

### Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS)

- **Wu et al. (2013), pilot study, n=7 women with PCOS:** A drink containing 15 g of apple vinegar daily for 90 to 110 days lowered insulin resistance in six women, and four of the seven had ovulatory periods within 40 days [42].

This is preliminary evidence from a very small, uncontrolled study.

### Antimicrobial Properties

ACV inhibits microbes in laboratory tests.

- **Yagnik et al. (2018), lab study:** Diluted ACV inhibited the growth of *E. coli*, *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Candida albicans*: *E. coli* was inhibited even at a 1/50 dilution, while *C. albicans* needed a 1/2 dilution [45].

No evidence shows that drinking or gargling ACV prevents or treats colds, flu, COVID-19 or other illness [67].

### Ear Infections

- **Aminifarshidmehr (1996), chronic otitis media:** Rinsing the ear with 2% acetic acid three times a week for up to 3 weeks, without antibiotics, cleared the discharge in 55 of 96 patients (57%). The study had no control group [47].

- **Jung et al. (2002), granular myringitis:** All 15 patients treated with dilute vinegar irrigation had a dry ear within three weeks, compared with two-thirds of 15 patients given antibiotic drops. Vinegar caused ear canal irritation with pain and dizziness [48].

- **Yamano et al. (2015) and Jinn et al. (2001), animal studies:** Acetic acid in the middle ear of guinea pigs dulled hearing-nerve responses at pH 5.0 or lower, more so with stronger acid and longer exposure [71], and it was the most toxic of several ear drops to isolated cochlear hair cells from chinchillas [72]. These findings may not apply to people, but ask a doctor before putting vinegar in your ear.

### Topical Uses

**Home skin treatments:** Vinegar can cause chemical burns: an adolescent was burned after following an internet protocol for removing moles with ACV [69].

**Eczema:** In a 14-day study of 22 people (11 with atopic dermatitis and 11 without), soaking one forearm in dilute ACV (0.5% acetic acid) for 10 minutes a day did not improve the skin barrier compared with water, and 72.7% (16/22) reported mild side effects that improved after stopping [50].

### Safer Grilling

Vinegar may reduce polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), compounds that can cause cancer and form when meat is charred.

- **Cordeiro et al. (2020), pork study:** Spraying pork loin with vinegar before charcoal grilling reduced PAH formation. Elderberry vinegar cut PAHs the most (82%), with white wine vinegar next at 79%, cider and red wine vinegars at 66% and a raspberry fruit vinegar at 55% [51].

### Cleaning Produce

- **Kilonzo-Nthenge et al. (2006):** Soaking lettuce in a 5% vinegar solution before rinsing removed no more surface bacteria than soaking it in cold tap water. The authors advised rubbing or brushing produce under cold running water [52].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

Brad does not recommend taking ACV as a supplement [67]. The amounts below are what trials used, not a recommendation.

### For Blood Sugar Control

- **With a meal:** 18 to 28 mmol of acetic acid as vinegar with a bread meal lowered the blood sugar rise in healthy volunteers [20].
- **Longer term:** 20 ml of apple vinegar per day for 8 weeks lowered fasting blood sugar in type 2 diabetes [26]. In the 2021 meta-analysis, the fall in fasting glucose was significant in studies lasting more than 8 weeks, and ACV appeared to raise fasting glucose in apparently healthy participants [17].

### For Weight Loss

- **Kondo et al. (2009):** 500 ml daily of a drink containing 15 ml or 30 ml of vinegar, for 12 weeks [30].

### General Dosing Principles

| Indication | Dose | How to Take | Duration in Studies |
|-----------|:---:|------|:---:|
| Post-meal blood sugar | 18, 23 or 28 mmol acetic acid | With a bread meal [20] | One meal |
| Fasting blood sugar | 20 ml apple vinegar per day | Daily [26] | 8 weeks |
| Weight loss | 15 ml or 30 ml vinegar a day | In 500 ml of drink daily [30] | 12 weeks |
| Mood | 1.5 g acetic acid a day | As liquid vinegar [35] | 4 weeks |

### Important Practical Notes

- **Always dilute:** Studies use a very diluted drink, typically a tablespoon at most of ACV in an 8 oz glass of water [67].
- **Protect tooth enamel:** Rinse your mouth with water after drinking any vinegar [67].
- **If you take medicines for diabetes**, check with your doctor before starting, because vinegar lowers blood sugar after meals [22].

## 5. Dietary Sources

ACV is a food, not a nutrient you need.

### Culinary Applications

Brad's suggestion is to enjoy it on a salad as part of a healthy diet [67].

### Nutritional Content per Tablespoon (15 mL)

In the Japanese weight trial, 15 ml of vinegar held 750 mg of acetic acid [30].

## 6. Safety and Side Effects

A 2020 systematic review judged that the risk of side effects seems small when apple vinegar is taken in recommended amounts and in recommended ways [70].

### Gastrointestinal Effects

- **Indigestion and upset stomach:** ACV can cause indigestion or an upset stomach [67].
- **Delayed gastric emptying:** In 10 people with type 1 diabetes and gastroparesis, 30 ml of ACV slowed stomach emptying further (median emptying rate 17% with vinegar against 27% without), which may harm blood sugar control [10].
- **Acid reflux/GERD:** If you have a stomach ulcer, esophageal damage or other digestive problems, the acid can make them worse [67].

### Dental Erosion

Regularly drinking ACV can erode tooth enamel [57][67].

- **Gambon et al. (2012), case report:** A 15-year-old girl developed erosive tooth wear from drinking a glass of ACV daily to lose weight [57].
- **Willershausen et al. (2014), lab study:** Several vinegars released minerals from human tooth enamel, with losses of about 20% for the most erosive [68].

**Mitigation strategies:** Dilute it and rinse your mouth with water after drinking it [67].

### Esophageal Injury

- **Hill et al. (2005):** After a reported case of esophageal injury from an ACV tablet, researchers tested eight tablet products and found wide variation in size, pH, acid content and label claims [15].

### Pancreatitis

- **Alvarez et al. (2024), case report:** A woman aged 84 went to the emergency department with sudden upper belly pain, nausea and vomiting after 30 days of a weight-loss gummy whose main ingredient was 1,000 mg of ACV. Her acute pancreatitis was possibly linked to the supplement [16].

For sudden severe upper belly pain with nausea or vomiting, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

### Liver Injury

- **Andonie et al. (2025), case report:** A man aged 60 developed jaundice, pale stools and dark urine after four years of drinking about 30 cc of ACV after meals three times a week. His liver tests improved after he stopped [61].

If your skin or eyes turn yellow, stop taking it and see your doctor.

### Hypokalemia (Low Potassium)

- **Lhotta et al. (1998), case report:** Low potassium, high renin levels and osteoporosis were reported in a patient who drank large amounts of cider vinegar [62].

### Skin Burns

- **Bunick et al. (2012) and Feldstein et al. (2015), case reports:** Topical ACV caused chemical burns, including in an adolescent following an internet protocol for removing moles [63][69].
- Avoid putting ACV on the skin or on wounds [63][69].

### Vinegar Eels

The FDA treats vinegar that contains vinegar eels as adulterated [3].

### Special Populations

- **Type 1 diabetes with gastroparesis:** ACV may slow stomach emptying further and upset blood sugar control [10].
- **Diabetes medicines:** If you take insulin or other diabetes medicines, check with your doctor first.
- **Stomach ulcer, esophageal damage or other digestive problems:** The acid can make these worse [67].
- **Kidney disease:** Ask your doctor before taking ACV regularly.
- **Pregnancy:** Ask your doctor before taking more than food amounts.

## 7. Drug Interactions

Cautions about these medicines rest on two findings: vinegar lowers blood sugar after meals [22], and large amounts were linked to low potassium in one case report [62].

| Medication | Interaction | Clinical Concern |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Insulin and oral diabetes medicines** | Vinegar lowers blood sugar after meals [22] | Blood sugar may drop lower than expected |
| **Diuretics** (furosemide, hydrochlorothiazide) | Some diuretics also lower potassium | Low potassium may be more likely |
| **Digoxin** | Low potassium can increase digoxin toxicity | Higher risk of digoxin toxicity |

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.
