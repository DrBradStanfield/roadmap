## Key safety points

- If you take blood-pressure medicines or have low blood pressure, check with your doctor first. In a 2025 meta-analysis, taurine lowered systolic blood pressure by 4.38 mmHg and diastolic by 2.54 mmHg [1]. The effects may add together.
- If you take insulin or other glucose-lowering medicines, check your blood sugar more closely when you start, because taurine may lower fasting blood sugar [1][2].
- If you have leukaemia or another blood cancer, talk to your doctor before taking taurine: a 2025 study in mice and in patient leukaemia cells found that aggressive myeloid leukaemias depend on taurine [31]. In the same study, taurine supplements sped up myeloid leukaemia in mice, and the authors suggest carefully weighing the benefits of supplemental taurine in people with leukaemia [31]. Brad notes that the human studies so far show no known risks from taurine supplements [40].
- Safety evidence is strongest for intakes up to 3 g a day. Higher intakes have been tested without harm, but the data are not enough for a confident conclusion on long-term safety [32].
- Trials and meta-analyses have reported no significant adverse effects from taurine [2][4][9]. In one cirrhosis trial, mild adverse events occurred in 11.8% of people taking taurine [20].

## 1. Overview

Taurine is a sulfur-containing amino acid [1][5]. The heart holds about 100 times more taurine than blood plasma [15].

The body makes some of its own taurine [12], and meat and dairy foods supply more. Plant foods supply none [13]. In one small study, vegan men, whose diets supplied a negligible amount of taurine, had lower plasma taurine levels than non-vegetarian men [12]. The study did not test whether the lower levels affect health.

Some animals, such as cats, make little taurine and can develop heart disease and blindness on a diet that lacks it. In people, a link between low dietary taurine and heart muscle disease is not yet clear [15].

A 2023 study (Singh) found lower blood taurine in older mice, monkeys and people [10]. A 2025 study (Fernandez) that measured blood taurine in three groups of people, and in monkeys and mice, both over time and across age groups, found that levels rose or stayed the same with age [11]. In the 2023 study, taurine lengthened life span in worms and mice, and its authors said human trials were still needed [10].

In the liver, taurine is joined to bile acids. In a trial in chronic hepatitis, taking taurine raised the levels of these taurine-conjugated bile acids [17].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Blood Pressure

Taking taurine may lower blood pressure by a few mmHg on average [1][2][4]. In a trial in people with prehypertension, the effect was clearest in those with high-normal blood pressure [5].

**Prehypertension trial:** A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study assigned 120 people with prehypertension to taurine (1.6 g per day) or placebo for 12 weeks [5]. Mean clinic systolic blood pressure fell by 7.2 mmHg with taurine and 2.6 mmHg with placebo; diastolic fell by 4.7 and 1.3 mmHg (Sun et al., *Hypertension*, 2016) [5].

**Type 2 diabetes trial:** In a randomized, double-blind trial in people with type 2 diabetes (82 on taurine, 83 on placebo), taurine at 2.4 g a day for 12 weeks lowered systolic blood pressure and improved blood-vessel function and arterial stiffness compared with placebo (Li et al., *iScience*, 2025) [6].

**Meta-analysis of blood pressure trials:** A meta-analysis of seven studies (103 participants of varying age and health) found that taurine at 1 to 6 g a day, taken for 1 day to 12 weeks, lowered both systolic and diastolic pressure by about 3 mmHg on average, with no adverse events reported (Waldron et al., *Current Hypertension Reports*, 2018) [4].

**Meta-analysis of 25 trials (2024):** A 2024 meta-analysis of 25 randomized controlled trials (1,024 participants) found significant falls in both systolic and diastolic blood pressure (Tzang et al., *Nutr Diabetes*, 2024) [2].

**Meta-analysis of 34 trials (2025):** A larger meta-analysis of 34 randomized controlled trials, published online in 2025, found that systolic blood pressure fell by 4.38 mmHg and diastolic by 2.54 mmHg (Nie et al., *Nutr Rev*) [1].

**Clinical significance:** Average falls in the meta-analyses were smaller than in the prehypertension trial [1][4][5]. If you have low blood pressure or take blood-pressure medicines, check with your doctor before starting taurine, because the effects may add together.

### Congestive Heart Failure

The evidence on taurine in heart failure is mixed, and most trials are small.

**2024 meta-analysis:** A meta-analysis of 20 randomized controlled trials (808 participants) found that taurine raised left ventricular ejection fraction and improved heart failure class (NYHA); the ejection-fraction gain was clearest in people with heart failure. It reported no significant adverse effects (Tzang et al., *Nutr J*, 2024) [3].

**2022 systematic review:** A review of eleven studies in heart failure, using 500 mg to 6 g per day, rated only one as high quality. Pooled results for ejection fraction and stroke volume were not significant, no significant safety concerns were reported, and the authors called for a formal clinical trial (McGurk et al., *Wellcome Open Res*, 2022) [9].

**CHF trial:** A double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study added taurine to standard treatment in 14 patients with CHF for 4 weeks. Compared with placebo, taurine improved heart failure severity (NYHA functional class), crackles in the lungs, and chest X-ray findings. No patient worsened during taurine, but four did during placebo (Azuma et al., *Clin Cardiol*, 1985) [7].

**Comparison with CoQ10:** A very small study compared taurine (3 g a day) with coenzyme Q10 (30 mg a day) in 17 patients with CHF [8]. After 6 weeks, taurine improved systolic left ventricular function and CoQ10 did not (Azuma et al., *Jpn Circ J*, 1992) [8].

**Context:** The two early trials were small and came from the same research group [7][8]. Taurine is not licensed as a medical treatment in Europe or the United States [9], and it should not replace standard heart failure medicines.

### Lipid Metabolism and Cholesterol

**Human evidence:** The 2024 meta-analysis by Tzang et al. found that taurine lowered triglycerides by 18.315 mg/dL but did not significantly change HDL cholesterol [2]. The 2025 meta-analysis of 34 trials found lower triglycerides (−14.42 mg/dL), total cholesterol (−12.41 mg/dL) and LDL cholesterol (−5.08 mg/dL) [1].

### Diabetes and Metabolic Syndrome

Supplementation may lower fasting blood glucose.

**2024 meta-analysis:** The meta-analysis by Tzang et al. (*Nutr Diabetes*, 2024) of randomized controlled trials found that taurine supplementation reduced fasting blood glucose, blood pressure, and triglyceride levels, with a dose-dependent fall in fasting glucose. Doses ranged from 0.5 to 6 g/day, with follow-up periods between 5 and 365 days [2].

**2025 meta-analysis:** The 2025 meta-analysis of 34 randomized controlled trials found that taurine lowered fasting blood sugar by 5.90 mg/dL, HbA1c by 0.21% and the insulin-resistance index HOMA-IR by 0.57 [1]. In its dose-response analysis, a daily dose of 1.5 to 3.0 g was more effective, and 8 weeks or more gave larger improvements in blood sugar and blood fats [1].

**Clinical context:** Tzang et al. concluded that taurine may be a useful dietary addition for people at risk of, or already living with, metabolic syndrome [2].

### Liver Diseases

Taurine has been studied in several liver-related conditions.

**Liver enzymes:** In the 2025 meta-analysis, taurine lowered the liver enzymes AST (by 9.65 U/L) and ALT (by 8.26 U/L) [1].

**Cirrhosis-related fatigue:** In an open-label trial, 220 people with decompensated cirrhosis were randomized to 1 g a day of taurine plus standard care, or standard care alone, for 12 weeks. Taurine did not improve fatigue overall. A possible benefit in people without anaemia came from an after-the-fact subgroup analysis, which the authors called hypothesis-generating (Sasidharan et al., *Hepatol Commun*, 2026) [20].

**Cirrhosis-related muscle cramps:** A crossover trial tested taurine in people with chronic liver disease who had three or more muscle cramps a week, with 4 weeks on each arm. Compared with placebo, 2 g of taurine a day meant seven fewer cramps per fortnight, 89 minutes less cramping per fortnight and severity 1.4 units lower on a Likert scale. No side effects were reported with taurine (Vidot et al., *Aliment Pharmacol Ther*, 2018) [19].

**Chronic hepatitis:** In a double-blind trial in 24 people with chronic hepatitis, taurine at 1.5 g a day did not lower liver enzymes (Podda et al., *Gastroenterology*, 1990) [17].

**Liver injury and ammonia:** In rats with chronic or acute liver injury, high-dose taurine injections limited the rise in blood and brain ammonia (Heidari et al., *Tox Reports*, 2016) [18].

### Exercise Performance and Recovery

Taurine is frequently included in pre-workout and muscle supplements, but the evidence for ergogenic effects is mixed.

**Systematic review (positive):** A meta-analysis of ten studies of taurine alone, at 1 to 6 g a day, found that it improved endurance exercise performance, including in time-to-exhaustion trials (Waldron et al., *Sports Med*, 2018) [23]. In the Waldron meta-analysis, single doses worked as well as supplementation over several days [23].

**Systematic review (mixed):** A 2021 review of 19 exercise studies found that limited and varied findings do not allow firm conclusions about taurine for exercise performance or recovery (Kurtz et al., *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*, 2021) [24].

**Negative evidence:** In a small 2026 crossover trial in 16 physically active young adults, 1 g of taurine 1 hour before an exercise test did not significantly change aerobic capacity or energy metabolism compared with placebo (Júnior et al., *Res Q Exerc Sport*, 2026) [22]. Similarly, in trained cyclists, 1.66 g of taurine before exercise did not improve time-trial performance, though it slightly raised fat burning (Rutherford et al., *Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab*, 2010) [21].

**Taurine plus BCAAs for recovery:** In a small controlled trial, untrained men took 2 g of taurine plus 3.2 g of branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) three times a day, for two weeks before and three days after hard eccentric arm exercise. The combination lowered delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS), along with markers of muscle damage, compared with placebo (Ra et al., *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*, 2013) [25].

**High-dose taurine for muscle recovery:** In a small crossover trial, ten fit men took taurine in morning and evening doses over three days after hard biceps exercise, and it may help them regain eccentric strength. Only peak eccentric torque improved significantly, and creatine kinase, a marker of muscle damage, did not differ from placebo. The dose was 100 mg/kg of body weight a day (McLeay et al., *Antioxidants (Basel)*, 2017) [26].

### Aging and Longevity

Interest in taurine and aging grew after a 2023 paper by Singh et al. in *Science*.

**The Singh et al. Science paper (2023):** Taurine supplements increased health span (the period of healthy living) in worms, mice and monkeys, and life span in worms and mice (Singh et al., *Science*, 2023) [10]. In taurine-treated mice, median life span rose by 10 to 12% [39]. The authors also reported that blood taurine was lower at older ages in mice, monkeys and people, and one author is named as an inventor on provisional patent applications filed by Columbia University [10].

**Mechanisms identified:** In these animal studies, taurine reduced cellular senescence, protected against telomerase deficiency, suppressed mitochondrial dysfunction, decreased DNA damage, and attenuated inflammaging (age-related inflammation) [10].

**The Fernandez et al. Science paper (2025):** Researchers at the US National Institute on Aging measured blood taurine in three groups of people in different countries, and in monkeys and mice, both in the same individuals over time and across age groups. Levels rose or stayed the same with age, and links between taurine and age-related changes in movement and energy balance varied widely. The authors concluded that changes in blood taurine are not a universal feature of aging (Fernandez et al., *Science*, 2025) [11].

**Human healthspan markers:** While no human lifespan data exist, the 2024 meta-analysis by Tzang et al. of 25 RCTs (1,024 participants, doses 0.5 to 6 g/day) found that taurine supplementation improved metabolic markers in adults, including reductions in blood pressure, fasting glucose, and triglycerides. These are risk factors for cardiovascular disease and diabetes [2]. The 2025 meta-analysis also found lower C-reactive protein and other markers of inflammation and oxidative stress [1].

**DNA and cellular protection:** In one lab study, taurine reduced DNA damage in human blood cells exposed to the toxic metal hexavalent chromium; this has not been tested in people (Husain et al., *Amino Acids*, 2020) [27].

**Limitations:** The Singh authors said clinical trials in humans are needed to test whether taurine deficiency drives aging in people [10]. The animal data are interesting, but the gap between animal lifespan extension and human evidence remains large [10]. The 2025 Fernandez study undercut the idea that low taurine drives aging in people [11].

### Neurological and Cognitive Function

**Cognition:** A 2025 meta-analysis of 7 randomized controlled trials (402 people) found that taurine, alone or with exercise training, did not significantly change cognitive scores (Cao et al., *Int J Food Sci Nutr*, 2025) [28].

**Dementia risk:** In the Framingham Offspring Cohort, adults without dementia had blood taurine measured and were followed for new cases of dementia. Higher blood taurine was linked to a lower risk of dementia (hazard ratio 0.74), which the authors called a suggestive association [29]. An observational link like this does not show that taurine supplements prevent dementia.

**MELAS:** In an open-label trial without a placebo group, 10 people with MELAS, a rare inherited mitochondrial disease, took 9 g or 12 g a day of taurine for 52 weeks. Stroke-like episodes stopped completely in 60%, and the yearly relapse rate fell from 2.22 to 0.72 (Ohsawa et al., *J Neurol Neurosurg Psychiatry*, 2019) [30].

### Retinal and Sensory Health

Children on long-term intravenous nutrition without taurine had low plasma taurine and abnormal retinal tests; adding taurine to the drip restored plasma levels in four children, and retinal tests became normal in three of them [16]. There is no good public evidence on taurine supplements for eye health or cataracts in people who eat a normal diet.

### Sleep and Relaxation

There is no good public evidence on taurine as a sleep aid.

### Gut Microbiota

There is no good public evidence on taurine and gut bacteria yet.

### Cancer and Immune Implications

A 2025 study in *Nature* identified taurine as a key regulator of myeloid cancers [31]. The researchers worked in mice and in leukaemia cells from patients with acute myeloid leukaemia (AML): aggressive myeloid leukaemias depended on taurine made by bone-marrow cells, and blocking the taurine transporter slowed leukaemia growth in mice [31]. In the same study, taurine supplements sped up myeloid leukaemia in mice, and the authors suggest carefully weighing the benefits of supplemental taurine in people with leukaemia [31]. Brad covered this study after it caused confusion online [40]. He described it as a study of existing cancer cells, compared the result with glucose or NAD, which can also speed cancer growth in a dish, and said that is very different from the effects in the human body [40]. His conclusion was that from the human studies so far there are no known risks with supplementation, though better data could change that [40]. If you have leukaemia or another blood cancer, talk to your doctor before taking taurine. There is no good public evidence on taurine and immune function.

### Other Proposed Uses

In the 2023 Singh study, lower blood taurine levels in people went with several age-related diseases; that link does not show that taurine supplements treat them [10].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### General Dosing Guidelines

A 2008 risk assessment could not set a tolerable upper intake level for taurine by the usual method, because human trials showed no consistent pattern of harm; it set an observed safe level instead, for intakes up to 3 g a day [32]. Doses in clinical trials have varied widely depending on the condition [1][2][4][9][19][23][25][26][39]:

| Indication | Dose Range | Duration | Evidence Level |
|-----------|-----------|----------|---------------|
| Blood pressure reduction | 1 to 6 g/day | 1 day to 12 weeks | Moderate (multiple RCTs, meta-analyses) |
| Cardiometabolic risk factors | 0.5 to 6 g/day; best results at 1.5 to 3.0 g | between 5 and 365 days | Moderate (two meta-analyses) |
| Heart failure | 500 mg to 6 g per day | 2 to 48 weeks | Low (small studies; reviews disagree) |
| Exercise performance | 1 to 6 g/day | Single dose or several days | Low-Moderate (mixed results) |
| Post-exercise recovery | 2 g three times daily (with BCAAs), or a weight-based dose | 72 hours after, to two weeks before plus three days after exercise | Low-Moderate (small studies) |
| Cirrhosis-related cramps | 2 g/day | 4 weeks | Moderate (single crossover RCT) |
| General supplementation | 1 to 3 g | Ongoing | Low (Brad's view on safety, not efficacy trials) |

### Practical Dosing Notes

- **Combining with BCAAs:** For muscle recovery, 2 g taurine plus 3.2 g BCAAs three times a day reduced soreness and muscle damage compared with placebo [25].
- **Duration:** In the 2025 meta-analysis, 8 weeks or more gave larger improvements in blood sugar and blood fats, while under 8 weeks was best for blood pressure and inflammation [1].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety Profile

Taurine appears to be well tolerated: meta-analyses of trials reported no significant adverse effects [2][3][4][9].

### Regulatory Safety Assessments

- **Shao et al. (2008):** A safety review found strong evidence for the absence of adverse effects from supplemental taurine at intakes up to 3 g/day [32]. Much higher intakes have been tested without adverse effects and may be safe, but the data are not sufficient for a confident conclusion of long-term safety [32].
- **High-dose trial:** In the MELAS trial, 10 people took 9 g or 12 g a day for 52 weeks, and no severe adverse events were linked to taurine [30].

### Side Effects

Taurine is generally well tolerated at typical supplemental doses [2][19]:

- **Gastrointestinal:** The cramp trial in chronic liver disease reported no side effects [19].
- **Mild adverse events:** In a 12-week cirrhosis trial of 1 g a day, adverse events occurred in 11.8% of people taking taurine, and all were mild [20].
- **Blood pressure lowering:** Taurine may lower blood pressure by a few mmHg on average [1][4]. That could be a problem if you have low blood pressure or take blood-pressure medicines.

### Special Populations

- **Pregnant and nursing women:** High-dose supplementation has not been studied in pregnancy or breastfeeding. Consult a healthcare provider before use.
- **Leukaemia and other blood cancers:** A 2025 study found that aggressive myeloid leukaemias depend on taurine in mice and in patient leukaemia cells [31]. If you have leukaemia or another blood cancer, talk to your doctor before taking taurine.

### Taurine in Energy Drinks: Safety Considerations

A 2022 risk assessment estimated that energy drinks supply 1,000 to 2,000 mg of taurine, and that drinking more than 500 mL would give intakes above the reference value it used [33]. Energy drinks also contain caffeine [33].

## 6. Drug Interactions

Interaction studies with specific medicines are lacking, but taurine's effects on blood pressure and blood sugar warrant attention:

### Blood Pressure Medications

In a 2018 meta-analysis, taurine at 1 to 6 g a day lowered blood pressure by about 3 mmHg on average [4]. This effect may add to that of prescription blood-pressure medicines and could cause low blood pressure. Individuals taking antihypertensive drugs should monitor blood pressure when initiating taurine supplementation and inform their healthcare provider.

### Lithium and Mood Stabilizers

There is no good public evidence on taurine with lithium or other mood stabilizers. Check with your doctor before combining them.

### Caffeine

Energy drinks often pair taurine with caffeine [33]. A 2025 network meta-analysis of twelve studies found the combination improved anaerobic capacity and reaction time more than either alone, with no difference in heart rate; it was not designed as a safety study (Deng et al., *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*, 2025) [34].

### Antidiabetic Medications

Taurine supplementation may reduce fasting blood glucose [1][2]. Individuals taking insulin or oral hypoglycemic agents should monitor blood glucose more closely when starting taurine, as additive blood sugar-lowering effects are theoretically possible.

### General Recommendation

Because taurine may lower blood pressure and blood sugar, if you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 7. Dietary Sources

Taurine is found predominantly in animal-derived foods.

### Food Sources

In a study that measured many foods, taurine was found in meats, dairy products and infant formula, but not in plant foods [13].

### Key Points About Dietary Taurine

- **Vegan status:** Vegans can have lower plasma taurine levels than non-vegetarians [12]. In one small study, 12 vegan men had lower plasma taurine (μmol/L) than 14 non-vegetarian men: 45 vs 58 [12]
- **Building blocks:** In the vegan study, intake of the sulfur-containing amino acids methionine and cystine, and of vitamin B6, was marginal to adequate, while taurine intake was negligible; the authors attributed the lower plasma taurine to the lack of dietary taurine [12]
- **Another vegan study:** Urinary taurine in vegans was less than half that of meat eaters, but plasma and breast-milk levels were only slightly lower [14]

### Taurine for Vegetarians and Vegans

Vegans have lower plasma taurine levels than non-vegetarians [12][14]. The studies did not test whether this affects health, and plant foods supply no taurine [13]. On a plant-based diet, supplements are the only dietary source.

## 8. Taurine for Pets

### Cats

The link between low taurine and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) in cats was first reported in 1987: cats with low plasma taurine had heart muscle failure, and taurine supplements returned their heart function to normal (Pion et al., *Science*, 1987) [36].

### Dogs

DCM linked to diet also occurs in dogs; a 2018 review set out what was known (Freeman et al., *J Am Vet Med Assoc*, 2018) [38]. In 24 golden retrievers with low taurine and DCM, all but one had eaten grain-free or legume-rich diets, and all but one improved after a diet change plus taurine [37]. If you think your dog may have taurine deficiency or heart disease, ask your vet about testing and dosing.
