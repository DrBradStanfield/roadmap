## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. Flaxseed oil supplies alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), a plant omega-3, but little of it becomes EPA and DHA: NIH calls the conversion very limited, with reported rates of less than 15% [50]. Flaxseed oil did not lower blood pressure in adults whose readings were high-normal or at stage I hypertension [17], while 30 g of milled whole flaxseed each day did in people with peripheral artery disease [14]. Evening primrose and borage oils, sources of the omega-6 fat GLA, did not help eczema in a Cochrane review [25]. If you take a blood thinner, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk with your doctor before using flaxseed oil [2].

## Key safety points

- Flaxseed oil might interact with anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs, and in a small trial it reduced platelet clumping [2][44]. Evening primrose oil may increase bleeding in people taking warfarin [25]. If you take a blood thinner, talk with your doctor first.
- Some studies suggest flaxseed is possibly unsafe in pregnancy, though the evidence is not conclusive, and little is known about flaxseed oil in pregnancy or breastfeeding [2]. In one study, evening primrose oil taken late in pregnancy may have been linked to more labour complications [58].
- Anaphylaxis after eating linseed (flaxseed) has been reported [38][65]. For trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
- Do not eat raw or unripe flaxseeds; they may contain potentially toxic compounds [2].
- Some studies linked higher ALA intake with prostate cancer, but later pooled analyses did not confirm it; men with or at risk of prostate cancer should talk to their doctor [35][61].
- Higher doses of flaxseed oil supplements may cause bloating, fullness and diarrhea [2].

## 1. Overview

Flaxseed oil and the omega-6-rich seed oils (borage, evening primrose, hemp and black currant) are plant sources of polyunsaturated fat. Flaxseed oil supplies alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), an omega-3 fat. Evening primrose and borage oils are taken for their gamma-linolenic acid (GLA), an omega-6 fat [25].

ALA is the only essential omega-3, so the Adequate Intakes for age 1 and older apply to ALA alone [50]. Plant oils that contain it include flaxseed (linseed, *Linum usitatissimum*), soybean and canola oils [50].

The body can turn some ALA into EPA and then DHA, mainly in the liver, but the conversion is very limited, with reported rates of less than 15% [50]. One review put it at less than 5 to 10% for EPA and 2 to 5% for DHA [3]. In a tracer study in six young men, labelled ALA became EPA and DPA, but no labelled DHA appeared at any point up to 21 days [4]. In the EPIC-Norfolk cohort, the estimated ratio of long-chain omega-3 product to ALA precursor was greater in women than in men, and greater in people who did not eat fish, which may mean more conversion [5]. In the Adventist Health Study-2, mean omega-3 index values were 3.7 in vegans and 3.9 in lacto-ovo vegetarians, against 5.7 in pesco-vegetarians and 5.0 in non-vegetarians; the authors judged algae-derived EPA and DHA supplements the most reasonable way for vegans and vegetarians to raise them [6].

GLA is found in human milk and in a few seed oils, and people usually take it as a supplement [12].

Flaxseed has a long history of traditional use. It was used to make linen in ancient Egypt, in stews in Ethiopia and as a laxative in ancient Greece, and European settlers in North America made poultices from it for cuts and burns [2]. Today flaxseed and flaxseed oil supplements are promoted for the heart, brain and immune system [2].

Whole flaxseed contains omega-3 fats, lignans and fiber [14]. Flaxseed oil is the pressed fat. Effects of the seed do not always carry over to the oil (see Whole Flaxseed vs. Flaxseed Oil).

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### ALA Sources

Among the foods NIH lists, flaxseed oil has the most ALA per serving, ahead of chia seeds and walnuts (see Dietary Sources) [50].

In the NuEva study, 168 adults on Western, flexitarian, vegetarian or vegan diets followed nutrient-optimized menu plans and took flaxseed oil providing 3 g of ALA daily for 9 months [9]. Red blood cell EPA, DPA and DHA rose in every diet group. People with low baseline EPA had larger rises than those with higher EPA: EPA +62.9% vs. +12.9%, DPA +41.9% vs. +22.3% and DHA +27.0% vs. +7.6% [9]. The study was not randomized.

In two randomized trials, 8 weeks of echium oil (5 g ALA plus 2 g stearidonic acid a day) left blood EPA and DPA about 25% higher than linseed oil with 5 g ALA. Neither oil maintained blood DHA in people who ate no fish or seafood [11].

**Stearidonic acid (SDA)**, found in echium oil, is converted to EPA more readily than ALA [11]. In 11 people with high triglycerides, 15 g of echium oil daily for 4 weeks raised EPA in plasma and neutrophils and lowered serum triglycerides by 21% [10]. In a larger trial, 17 g of echium oil daily (2 g SDA) for 8 weeks raised EPA and DPA but not DHA, and raised them less than fish oil with 1.9 g EPA. Compared with baseline, echium oil lowered total and LDL cholesterol and triglycerides, but also HDL cholesterol, while fish oil lowered only triglycerides [59].

### GLA Sources

The GLA trials in this article used evening primrose oil, borage oil, or GLA as the free fatty acid [21][24][55].

**GLA Metabolism:** The body turns GLA into DGLA (dihomo-gamma-linolenic acid) and then into arachidonic acid. Metabolites of DGLA and of arachidonic acid have opposing effects on inflammation, and how much of each a person makes depends partly on variants in the FADS genes. These factors may explain the mixed results of GLA trials in inflammatory conditions [12].

### Product Considerations

Polyunsaturated oils break down with heat, light and air, so keep them sealed, cool and dark. For independent testing of specific products, see consumerlab.com or Labdoor.

### Whole Flaxseed vs. Flaxseed Oil

Milled flaxseed brings fiber and lignans as well as ALA: in a 6-month trial giving 30 g of milled flaxseed each day, plasma ALA and enterolignans rose 2- to 50-fold [14]. Two findings suggest the seed does things the oil does not. Milled flaxseed lowered blood pressure in that trial, while refined flaxseed oil did not in another [14][17]. Flaxseed mucilage helped weight loss, while flaxseed oil supplements do not seem to help [2].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Cardiovascular Health — ALA and Flaxseed

#### Cholesterol and Lipid Profile

In a 2004 meta-analysis of cohort studies, high ALA intake was linked to less fatal heart disease (combined relative risk 0.79, 95% CI 0.60 to 1.04, not statistically significant), and three open-label trials also suggested ALA may protect against heart disease [35].

In a randomized, double-blind crossover trial in 15 Japanese men, 10 g of flaxseed oil (5.49 g ALA) taken at dinner each day for 12 weeks lowered small dense LDL cholesterol, which corn oil did not [13]. Apolipoprotein B was also lower in the flaxseed oil period at 12 weeks, and the fall in small dense LDL was marked in men with triglycerides over 100 mg/dl [13].

In healthy adults, 6 g of ALA a day for 4 weeks, from ground flaxseed or flaxseed oil, did not change total, LDL or HDL cholesterol. Of the two age groups (18-29 or 45-69 years), only the younger saw triglycerides fall [15]. A 2009 meta-analysis of 28 randomized trials found that whole flaxseed lowered total and LDL cholesterol by 0.21 and 0.16 mmol/L, but flaxseed oil did not; the falls were larger in women, particularly after menopause, and in people with high starting cholesterol [63].

#### Blood Pressure

**Flaxseed oil alone** did not lower blood pressure in a 12-week trial in 59 overweight or obese adults whose readings were high-normal or at stage I hypertension. They took 10 g of flaxseed oil (refined and cold-pressed) or high-oleic sunflower oil each day, and 24-hour blood pressure did not differ significantly between the groups [17].

**Whole milled flaxseed,** in contrast, did lower blood pressure. In a 6-month trial in 110 people with peripheral artery disease, foods providing 30 g of milled flaxseed a day left systolic pressure about 10 mm Hg below placebo and diastolic about 7 mm Hg below [14]. In people who entered with systolic pressure ≥140 mm Hg, systolic fell 15 mm Hg and diastolic 7 mm Hg [14]. The authors called it one of the most potent antihypertensive effects achieved by a dietary intervention [14]. About 75% of participants had high blood pressure [18].

A later analysis of the same trial suggests that ALA in the seed may have lowered blood pressure by inhibiting the enzyme soluble epoxide hydrolase, which changed blood levels of oxylipins that regulate vascular tone [18]. In a 12-week trial in 112 people with hypertension, 30 g of flaxseed each day gave the largest falls in blood pressure and total cholesterol of the groups tested [48].

#### Omega-3 Status in Vegans and Vegetarians

Flaxseed oil can raise long-chain omega-3 levels: in the NuEva study, 3 g of ALA daily for 9 months raised them in every diet group, most in people starting with low EPA [9]. It does not fully make up for a diet without fish. Neither linseed oil nor echium oil kept blood DHA steady in people who ate no fish [11], and the Adventist study authors judged algae-derived EPA and DHA supplements the most reasonable way for vegans and vegetarians to raise their omega-3 index [6].

### Gestational Diabetes

In pregnant people with gestational diabetes, some research suggests that flaxseed oil supplements containing ALA might improve fasting measures and insulin resistance [2]. In a randomized, double-blind trial in 60 women with gestational diabetes, 2 × 1000 mg a day of flaxseed oil capsules (400 mg ALA in each) for 6 weeks lowered fasting plasma glucose, insulin and insulin resistance compared with placebo [19]. The journal later published an expression of concern about this trial [60].

### Weight Management

One clinical trial suggests that flaxseed mucilage powder mixed with water might support weight loss in adults with overweight or obesity, but flaxseed lignan extract and flaxseed oil supplements do not seem to help [2]. In that 12-week trial, 108 people on a calorie-reduced diet lost 4.96 ± 1.89 kg on the high dose and 3.70 ± 2.57 kg on the low dose of a proprietary mucilage (IQP-LU-104), against 1.33 ± 2.05 kg on placebo [20].

### ADHD

In a small pilot study in children with ADHD, an ALA-rich supplement of flax oil with an antioxidant (vitamin C) emulsion raised red blood cell fatty acids and lowered hyperactivity scores [51]. It tested a combination, so the result cannot be credited to flax oil alone.

### Dry Eye and Sjogren's Syndrome

In a randomized trial in 38 women with Sjogren's syndrome and dry eye, flaxseed oil capsules at 1 g/day or 2 g/day for 180 days improved symptoms and reduced inflammation of the eye surface compared with placebo [52].

### Bipolar Disorder

In a randomized trial in 51 children and adolescents aged 6-17 years with bipolar disorder, flax oil taken over 16 weeks did not improve the main mania, depression or global ratings compared with an olive oil placebo [53]. The authors favoured fish oil, citing varied conversion of ALA to EPA and DHA and the number of capsules needed [53].

### Acne — GLA

In a 10-week randomized trial in 45 people whose acne was mild to moderate, one group took borage oil providing 400 mg of GLA a day, one took an omega-3 supplement with 2,000 mg of EPA and DHA, and one was a control group [21]. After 10 weeks, inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesions had decreased significantly in both supplement groups, and no severe side effects were reported [21].

### Diabetic Neuropathy — GLA

The American Academy of Neurology's 2022 guideline update on painful diabetic neuropathy recommends tricyclic antidepressants, SNRIs, gabapentinoids and sodium channel blockers; GLA is not among them [22].

### Cyclic Mastalgia (Breast Pain) — GLA

In a 6-month randomized trial in 85 women with cyclical breast pain, evening primrose oil (3,000 mg per day), vitamin E, or both improved worst pain from baseline while placebo did not. Compared directly with placebo, the decreases were not statistically significant, and only 41 women completed the study [24]. In a later randomized trial in 126 premenopausal women, evening primrose oil (1000 mg twice daily) reduced breast pain more than placebo, and it worked best combined with vitamin E [64].

### Eczema — GLA

A Cochrane review of 27 studies (1596 participants) found that oral evening primrose oil and borage oil did not improve eczema more than placebo, and concluded they are not effective treatments [25].

### Rheumatoid Arthritis — GLA

In a 24-week trial in 37 people with active rheumatoid arthritis, 1.4 g/d of GLA in borage seed oil reduced tender and swollen joints, while placebo did not [54]. A second trial in 56 people gave 2.8 gm/day of GLA as the free fatty acid for 6 months; meaningful responses were seen in 14 of 22 patients on GLA and 4 of 19 on placebo [55]. Both groups of authors noted that GLA is usually taken in far lower doses than they used and should not be viewed as therapy for any disease [54][55].

### Raynaud's Phenomenon — GLA

In a small double-blind study of Raynaud's phenomenon, 11 people took evening primrose oil (12 capsules a day) for 8 weeks and 10 took placebo. As the weather worsened, the placebo group had significantly more attacks, but blood flow measures did not change in either group [56].

### Dry Eye Syndrome — GLA

In a 45-day randomized trial in 26 people with dry eye and inflammation of the eye surface, tablets of 28.5 mg linoleic acid with 15 mg GLA, taken twice a day with tear substitutes, improved symptoms and reduced inflammation compared with placebo [26].

In a 6-month trial in 38 women past menopause who had dry eye, a supplement combining GLA with omega-3 fats improved irritation symptoms and kept the corneal surface smoother than placebo [27]. It was a combination, so the result cannot be credited to GLA alone.

### Hair Loss — GLA / Black Currant Oil

In laboratory tests, GLA was the most potent of the fatty acids tested at inhibiting 5-alpha-reductase, the enzyme also blocked by finasteride (Propecia, Proscar), a hair loss drug [28]. In ovariectomized rats, a diet with 3% of an anthocyanin-rich blackcurrant extract (not the seed oil) for three months increased hairs by 0.5 per follicular unit [29]. Both findings come from laboratory and animal work, not human trials.

### The Omega-6 Controversy

A common worry is that diets high in omega-6 fats, mainly linoleic acid (LA), promote inflammation. Studies in healthy adults have found that eating more LA or arachidonic acid does not raise most inflammatory markers [30]. A meta-analysis of 30 randomized trials (1377 subjects) found no significant effect of more LA on inflammatory markers, including C-reactive protein (CRP), though larger increases in LA might raise CRP [31]. A high omega-6 diet may still blunt the anti-inflammatory effects of omega-3 fats [30].

Genes may matter. In a 4-week trial in 62 men, the responses of CRP and fasting glucose to an LA-enriched diet differed by FADS1 genotype [32]. Larger trials are needed before diet advice is tailored to genotype [32].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

Limited amounts of flaxseed oil supplements may be safe for short-term use, up to 6 months [2]. Higher doses of flaxseed or flaxseed oil supplements may cause bloating, fullness and diarrhea [2]. In eczema trials, evening primrose and borage oils had the same mild, short-lived side effects as placebo, mainly gastrointestinal [25].

### Raw and Unripe Flaxseeds

Do not eat raw or unripe flaxseeds; they may contain potentially toxic compounds [2].

### Seizure Risk with GLA

Older drug references listed epilepsy as a reason for caution with evening primrose oil, based on two reports from the early 1980s; a 2007 review judged the link spurious [57]. Mention it to your doctor if you have epilepsy.

### Borage Plant — Pyrrolizidine Alkaloids

Borage leaves and roots hold pyrrolizidine alkaloids, which may harm the liver and may cause cancer.

### Prostate Cancer Risk — ALA

A 2004 meta-analysis of observational studies found a higher risk of prostate cancer in men with a high intake or blood level of ALA (combined relative risk 1.70, 95% CI 1.12 to 2.58). The authors called the link a concern that warrants further study [35]. A 2009 meta-analysis of 8 case-control and 8 prospective studies found a weak link (RR 1.20, 95% CI 1.01 to 1.43) and no association after adjustment for publication bias (RR 0.96, 95% CI 0.79 to 1.17) [61]. A 2015 meta-analysis of prospective studies found dietary ALA inversely associated with prostate cancer risk, a result dominated by a single study [62]. If you have or are at risk of prostate cancer, talk to your doctor before taking flaxseed oil.

### Cataract Risk — ALA

In 440 women from the Nurses' Health Study, nuclear density (clouding) of the eye lens rose 16% more over five years in the highest quarter of ALA intake than in the lowest [36]. This was a single observational study.

### Allergic Reactions

Anaphylaxis after eating linseed (flaxseed) has been reported [38][65]. For trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Individuals with known flaxseed allergy should avoid both flaxseed and flaxseed oil products.

### Fish Allergy Concerns with Combination Products

Some products blend seed oils with fish oil. If you are allergic to fish or shellfish, read the label and take care with these products.

### Pregnancy and Nursing

Use these products cautiously during pregnancy and breastfeeding. Some studies of flaxseed in pregnancy suggest it is possibly unsafe, but the evidence is not conclusive, and little is known about flaxseed oil in pregnancy or about either while breastfeeding [2]. In a retrospective study of 54 women who took evening primrose oil from the 37th week and 54 who did not, the oil did not shorten pregnancy or labour and may be associated with more prolonged rupture of membranes, oxytocin augmentation, arrest of descent and vacuum extraction [58].

### THC in Hemp Products

Hemp seed and hemp seed oil may contain traces of delta-9-THC. In three consumer brands of hemp seeds (Canada's limit is 10 μg of THC per gram), concentrations were almost always above the limit and as high as 1250% of it [40]. One volunteer who took cold-pressed hemp seed oil twice a day for 4 1/2 days (135 mL total) tested positive on a standard workplace urine drug test [41]. In a later study, none of the adults who took 0.45 mg of THC a day in hemp oil screened positive, and the authors judged the THC levels then typical in Canadian hemp products low enough to prevent confirmed positives [42].

## 6. Drug Interactions

### Anticoagulant and Antiplatelet Medications

There are theoretical reasons to suspect that flaxseed or flaxseed oil might interact with anticoagulant or antiplatelet drugs [2]. In a small study in healthy young men, 40 g of flaxseed oil a day for 23 days reduced collagen-induced platelet aggregation [44]. GLA oils may act the same way: evening primrose oil may increase bleeding in people taking warfarin [25], and blood tests showed some antiplatelet effects in a Raynaud's study [56].

If you take a blood thinner such as warfarin (Coumadin), aspirin, apixaban (Eliquis) or rivaroxaban (Xarelto), talk with your doctor before using flaxseed or flaxseed oil [2]. If you have hemophilia, use these products only with your doctor's guidance, and stop them before surgery.

### General Drug Interaction Considerations

If you take any medicine, talk with your health care provider before using flaxseed or flaxseed oil; some herbs and medicines interact in harmful ways [2].

### Seizure Medications

If you take anti-seizure medicine, see Seizure Risk with GLA.
