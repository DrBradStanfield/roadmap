## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. In the stomach, alginates form a floating gel "raft" that holds back reflux of acid, pepsin and bile instead of lowering acid production [1][2]. A 2017 meta-analysis found alginates raised the odds of GERD symptom resolution compared with placebo or antacids (OR 4.42), but a 2020 meta-analysis found no statistically significant pooled difference, so the size of the benefit is uncertain [3][4]. In one trial, 10 mL four times daily was non-inferior to omeprazole 20 mg a day for reaching a first heartburn-free day in moderate episodic heartburn, though omeprazole gave more heartburn-free days in the first week [5]. Many alginate products contain sodium, potassium or calcium and can reduce the absorption of other medicines, so people on a low-salt diet, with kidney problems or on regular medicines should check with a doctor or pharmacist first [2].

## Key safety points

- Alginate products can reduce the absorption of other medicines. The UK label advises a 2-hour gap, especially from tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, iron, thyroid hormones, chloroquine, bisphosphonates and estramustine [2]. In rats, sodium alginate lowered carbamazepine blood levels [6].
- Do not use them if you are allergic to alginate or to the parabens some liquids contain. Anaphylaxis is very rare but has been reported [2][7].
- Some liquids are high in sodium: the maximum daily dose of one UK liquid supplies 23.14% of the WHO daily sodium limit. Ask your doctor first if you have heart failure, kidney disease, high blood calcium or calcium kidney stones [2].
- Children under 12 should take them only on medical advice. In infants, an alginate formula did not reduce reflux events [2][8].
- If heartburn has not improved after 7 days, see your doctor [2].
- Side effects are uncommon: diarrhea, nausea and vomiting [2].

## 1. Overview

Alginates are indigestible polysaccharides extracted from brown seaweed [9][10]. Food makers use them as stabilizers, thickeners and emulsifiers [9]. In medicine, they treat the symptoms of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD): heartburn, acid regurgitation and indigestion [2]. Rather than reducing acid production, they form a gel "raft" that floats on the stomach contents and holds back reflux of acid, pepsin and bile into the esophagus [1][2]. The label states that the mechanism is physical and does not depend on absorption into the bloodstream [2].

Alginate-raft products, most often sold as Gaviscon, have been marketed worldwide for decades [1]. The raft settles over the "acid pocket", an unbuffered pool of acid that floats on top of a meal and causes reflux after eating [11]. Imaging studies show the raft can neutralize or displace this pocket (see Acid Pocket Targeting below) [11][12].

The main evidence on symptoms comes from two meta-analyses that disagree. A 2017 analysis found alginates more effective than placebo or antacids; a 2020 analysis found no statistically significant pooled difference (see Evidence for Benefits) [3][4]. Alginate is also an indigestible fiber, and a 2005 review set out to gauge its potential as a dietary fiber supplement [9].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Chemical Forms

Alginates are sold as alginic acid or as its salts. The EU authorizes alginic acid and its sodium, potassium, ammonium and calcium salts as food additives [13], and magnesium alginate has been tested for throat reflux [14]. Sodium alginate is the active ingredient of Gaviscon Advance liquid, at 1,000 mg per 10 mL [2].

Products differ in the minerals they add. One UK sodium alginate liquid contains 57.85 mg of sodium per 5 mL, and its label calls it high in sodium [2]. The same liquid also contains potassium and calcium carbonate (see Sodium Content below) [2].

### Delivery Forms

Trials have tested alginates as liquids and as chewable tablets:

- **Liquid suspensions** are the most studied form. Gaviscon Advance contains sodium alginate 1,000 mg and potassium hydrogen carbonate 200 mg in each 10 mL dose [2]. Trial doses ranged from 10 mL to 30 mL (see Recommended Dosing).
- **Chewable tablets**: in a 7-day trial of 424 adults, alginate-antacid chewable tablets relieved reflux symptoms better than placebo [15].

### Key Formulation Components

Most raft-forming products contain sodium or potassium bicarbonate. In stomach acid, the alginate precipitates into a gel, and the bicarbonate turns into carbon dioxide, which is trapped in the gel and makes it float like a raft on water [1].

Raft strength depends on how much carbon dioxide is trapped, the molecular properties of the alginate, and whether the product contains aluminum or calcium [1]. In a lab comparison of liquid products, those with low acid-neutralizing capacity and a calcium source formed the strongest rafts, and stronger rafts resisted reflux better [16].

### Mechanism of Raft Formation

The raft often forms within a few seconds of a dose and can stay in the stomach for several hours [1]. The UK label says the raft has a near-neutral pH and impedes reflux for up to 4 hours [2]. Lab work suggests the raft also traps bile and pepsin (see Pepsin and Bile Acid Binding) [2][17].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### GERD Symptom Control -- Meta-analyses and Systematic Reviews

The 2017 meta-analysis by Leiman and colleagues searched PubMed/MEDLINE, Embase and the Cochrane library through October 2015. Of 665 studies found, 15 met the criteria and 14 were pooled, with 2,095 participants [3].

- **Alginate vs. placebo or antacids:** alginates raised the odds of symptom resolution (OR 4.42; 95% CI 2.45 to 7.97), with moderate heterogeneity between trials (I2 = 71%) [3].
- **Alginate vs. PPIs or H2 blockers:** alginates looked less effective, but the pooled estimate was not statistically significant (OR 0.58; 95% CI 0.27 to 1.22) [3].
- **Conclusion:** alginates are more effective than placebo or antacids for GERD symptoms [3].

A 2020 meta-analysis by Zhao and colleagues, covering trials from 2000 to mid-2020, included 11 randomized trials and reached a less certain result [4]. Read one by one, four trials favored alginates over placebo or antacids. Pooled, there was no statistically significant difference for heartburn, regurgitation or dyspepsia, and no difference between alginate plus a PPI and a PPI alone [4]. The authors concluded that alginates may be more effective than placebo or antacids, and that adverse events were no more common than with placebo or PPIs [4]. The two meta-analyses therefore conflict on how firm the benefit over placebo is.

A 2024 meta-analysis of 4 randomized trials (608 patients) compared a PPI plus alginate with a PPI alone. Heartburn and reflux scores improved more with the combination, but no difference was statistically significant, and adverse events were not increased (OR 0.93; 95% CI 0.58 to 1.47) [18].

### Alginate vs. Omeprazole (PPIs) -- The GOOD Trial

The GOOD trial was a 14-day multicenter randomized double-blind double-dummy non-inferiority trial in general practice [5]. Adults with heartburn on 2 to 6 days a week took Gaviscon 10 mL four times daily or omeprazole 20 mg daily. Of 278 patients recruited, 120 in the Gaviscon group and 121 in the omeprazole group entered the per-protocol analysis [5].

- **Primary endpoint:** mean time to the first 24-hour heartburn-free period was 2.0 days for both (2.0 +/- 2.2 for Gaviscon vs. 2.0 +/- 2.3 for omeprazole; P = 0.93), meeting the non-inferiority criterion [5].
- **Secondary endpoints:** omeprazole gave more heartburn-free days by day 7 (3.7 +/- 2.3 vs. 3.1 +/- 2.1; P = 0.02), and overall quality of pain relief was slightly in its favor (P = 0.049) [5].
- **Safety:** tolerance and safety were good and comparable in both groups [5].
- **Conclusion:** Gaviscon was non-inferior to omeprazole for reaching a 24-hour heartburn-free period in moderate episodic heartburn [5].

### Alginate vs. Antacids

Giannini and colleagues (2006) compared sodium alginate with the antacid magaldrate in 203 adults with heartburn or acid regurgitation [19]. After a run-in, 191 patients were randomized to 14 days of either drug, four doses a day.

- **Speed of action:** relief within 30 minutes was more frequent with alginate (49.4% vs. 40.4%; P = 0.0074) [19].
- **Duration and overall effect:** alginate showed only a trend toward longer relief (median 16.5 vs. 12.7 hours) and a greater sum of symptom intensity difference (median 40.0 vs. 31.0) [19].
- **Symptom resolution:** symptoms disappeared completely in 81.6% of the alginate group and 73.9% of the magaldrate group [19].
- **Conclusion:** sodium alginate was faster than magaldrate, with a tendency toward longer action and higher efficacy [19].

In a double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 424 adults with GERD symptoms took alginate-antacid chewable tablets or placebo for 7 days. More patients on the tablets met the symptom endpoint (47.8% vs. 33.2%; odds ratio 1.85), and adverse events did not differ notably [15].

### Acid Pocket Targeting

**Rohof et al. (2013):** 16 patients with symptomatic GERD and large hiatal hernias were randomized to a radiolabeled alginate-antacid (Gaviscon Double Action Liquid, n = 8) or an antacid (n = 8) after a standard meal [11].

- The alginate-antacid label localized to the acid pocket [11].
- Acid reflux episodes were fewer with the alginate-antacid (3.5 vs. 15; P = .03) [11].
- Time to the first acid reflux was longer with the alginate-antacid (63 vs. 14 minutes; P = .01) [11].
- The acid pocket sat below the diaphragm in 71% of the alginate group and 21% of the antacid group, but this difference was not statistically significant (P = .08) [11].

**Kwiatek et al. (2011):** in 10 symptomatic GERD patients, a 20 mL dose of Gaviscon Double Action Liquid taken after a high-fat meal neutralized the acidified segment near the esophagogastric junction in 6 of 8 subjects. Junction length was unchanged, so the effect was likely due to the raft displacing stomach contents [12].

**Deraman et al. (2020):** 26 obese adults (mean BMI 32.8) took a single dose of Gaviscon Advance or a non-alginate antacid after a 10 pm supper. Gaviscon Advance suppressed the acid pocket better than the antacid (all P < 0.04), though not the lower esophagus [20].

### Pepsin and Bile Acid Binding

Reflux carries pepsin and bile acids as well as acid, and these non-acid parts can damage the esophagus [17].

**Strugala et al. (2009):** in lab models, Gaviscon Advance inhibited pepsin activity in a dose-dependent way, beyond what its acid neutralization explains. It slowed the diffusion of pepsin and bile acids across a membrane and removed both from a simulated reflux event, with capacity left for several more events [17].

**Samuels et al. (2022):** in cultured human esophageal and vocal cord cells, two alginate liquids prevented the barrier damage that pepsin and acid caused, and did so better than a matched placebo [21].

Both are lab studies, and the second had authors from the product's maker [21].

### Non-Erosive Reflux Disease (NERD)

NERD is the most common form of GERD, and people with NERD respond less often to PPIs than those with erosive esophagitis [22].

**Manabe et al. (2012):** 76 Japanese patients with NERD took omeprazole 20 mg once daily, either with sodium alginate 30 mL four times a day (n = 36) or alone (n = 40), for 4 weeks [22].

- Complete resolution of heartburn for at least 7 days in a row was more common with the combination (56.7% vs. 25.7%) [22].
- One patient on the combination had mild diarrhea [22].

**Savarino et al. (2012):** in an open-label study of 40 patients with erosive or non-erosive reflux disease, 10 mL of an alginate compound (Faringel) was tested for 1 hour after a reflux-provoking meal [23].

- Esophageal acid exposure time and the number of acid refluxes fell (P < 0.001) [23].
- Non-acid reflux events rose (P < 0.01) [23].
- Reflux reaching the upper esophagus fell in both positions tested (60% vs. 32% and 64% vs. 35%; P < 0.001) [23].
- Heartburn was controlled, but regurgitation did not improve significantly. Two patients reported nausea and bloating [23].

### PPI-Refractory GERD

Many reflux patients remain symptomatic on a standard dose of PPI [24].

**Reimer et al. (2016):** in a randomized double-blind trial, 136 patients with persistent symptoms on a once-daily PPI added Gaviscon Advance 10 mL four times a day or placebo for 7 days [24].

- The reflux score improved more with Gaviscon Advance (mean -5.0 vs. -3.5; P = 0.03) [24].
- Nights with symptoms fell from 3.9 to 2.2 with Gaviscon Advance and from 3.6 to 3.0 with placebo (P < 0.01) [24].

In NERD, adding sodium alginate to omeprazole roughly doubled complete heartburn resolution (see above) [22]. The 2020 and 2024 meta-analyses found no significant pooled benefit from adding alginate to a PPI, so this evidence is mixed [4][18].

### Laryngopharyngeal Reflux (LPR)

LPR is the backflow of stomach contents into the throat. It may contribute to hoarseness, vocal fatigue, cough, globus and chronic throat clearing [25]. Most placebo-controlled trials of PPIs for LPR have shown no significant benefit in symptom scores [25].

**Pizzorni et al. (2022):** in a non-inferiority randomized trial, 50 patients with LPR took magnesium alginate (Gastrotuss, 20 mL three times daily) or omeprazole 20 mg once daily for 2 months [14].

- Symptom (RSI) and sign (RFS) scores fell significantly in both groups, and the differences between groups were not significant [14].
- Alginate was non-inferior to omeprazole and may be an alternative to PPIs for LPR [14].

**McGlashan et al. (2009):** in an open-label pilot study, 49 patients with LPR were randomized to Gaviscon Advance 10 mL four times daily after meals and at bedtime (24 patients) or no treatment (25 patients) [25].

- Symptom scores were better with treatment at 2 months (11.2 vs. 16.8; P = 0.005) and 6 months (11.2 vs. 18.3; P = 0.008) [25].
- Sign scores were better at 6 months (7.1 vs. 9.5; P = 0.005) [25].

**Mathew and Shilpa (2022):** 100 adults with LPR in rural south India took pantoprazole 40 mg twice a day, with or without alginate 500 mg/10 mL twice a day, for 8 weeks. Symptoms and signs improved more with the alginate [26].

**Systematic review (2025):** a review of 16 studies, 12 of them clinical studies with 994 people, concluded that alginates help in LPR both alone and with PPIs. Only four of the studies were randomized experiments, and the authors called for larger randomized trials [27].

### Nighttime and Post-Meal Reflux

Reflux after a late meal comes from the acid pocket, and the risk is higher with obesity [20]. In the Deraman trial, a single dose of Gaviscon Advance after a 10 pm supper reduced the percentage of time with esophageal pH below 4, symptom frequency and symptom scores on day 2 compared with day 1 (all P < 0.05); the antacid did not [20].

In the Reimer trial, adding Gaviscon Advance to a PPI cut the number of nights with symptoms more than placebo did (see above) [24]. The UK label doses alginate after meals and at bedtime and says the raft impedes reflux for up to 4 hours [2].

### Pregnancy

About two-thirds of pregnant women have heartburn [28]. In some countries, PPIs and H2 blockers are not allowed in pregnancy, or only late in it [29]. The 2022 ACG guideline recommends lifestyle changes first; when they fail, antacids, alginates and sucralfate are the first-line drugs [28].

**Strugala et al. (2012):** 144 pregnant women with heartburn were recruited to an open-label study of Liquid Gaviscon without a placebo group [30].

- Treatment was rated a success in 91% of patients by the investigator and 90% by the patients [30].
- Very few adverse events were judged related to the medicine, and serum sodium did not change [30].

**Meteerattanapipat and Phupong (2017):** in a randomized double-blind trial, one hundred pregnant women at less than 36 weeks took an alginate or a magnesium-aluminium antacid gel [31].

- The alginate did no better than the antacid: heartburn frequency improved in 80% vs. 88% (p = 0.275) [31].
- Side effects, pregnancy outcomes and newborn outcomes did not differ [31].

**Quartarone (2013):** a systematic review found no particular risks to mother or baby from alginates given in any trimester. The author worked for a company that sells an alginate product [29].

The UK label states that studies in more than 500 pregnant women and post-marketing data show no malformations or toxicity to the fetus or newborn, and that Gaviscon can be used in pregnancy, if clinically needed, and while breastfeeding [2].

### Alginate as Dietary Fiber

As an indigestible polysaccharide, alginate can be viewed as a dietary fiber [9]. A 2005 review examined its fiber-like effects on intestinal absorption and the colon [9]. Large amounts of alginate eaten with a meal can reduce the absorption of some carotenoids (see Effects on Nutrient Absorption) [32].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Effective Dose Range

The UK label dose of Gaviscon Advance for adults and children 12 years and over is 5 to 10 mL after meals and at bedtime [2]. A 10 mL dose of Gaviscon Advance contains 1,000 mg of sodium alginate [2]. Trials used 10 mL four times daily up to 30 mL four times daily, and one trial used an alginate at 500 mg per 10 mL [5][22][26].

### Dosing by Indication

| Use | Dose tested | Result |
|-----|-------------|--------|
| Moderate episodic heartburn | Gaviscon 10 mL four times daily for 14 days | Non-inferior to omeprazole 20 mg a day [5] |
| Symptoms despite a once-daily PPI | Gaviscon Advance 10 mL four times a day added to the PPI | Reflux score better than placebo (P = 0.03) [24] |
| NERD on omeprazole | Sodium alginate 30 mL four times a day added to omeprazole 20 mg | Complete heartburn resolution 56.7% vs. 25.7% [22] |
| Laryngopharyngeal reflux | Magnesium alginate 20 mL three times daily for 2 months | Non-inferior to omeprazole 20 mg once daily [14] |
| Laryngopharyngeal reflux | Gaviscon Advance 10 mL four times daily after meals and at bedtime | Better than no treatment in an open-label trial [25] |
| Pregnancy heartburn | Liquid Gaviscon, open-label | Investigator-rated success in 91% [30] |

### Timing Guidelines

- **After meals and at bedtime:** this is the label timing [2]. After a meal, the raft forms on top of the stomach contents, where the acid pocket sits [1][11].
- **Children:** under 12 years, only on medical advice. No dose change is needed for older adults or people with liver impairment [2].
- **Other medicines:** leave a 2-hour gap between an alginate product and other medicines, including iron [2].
- **Review:** if symptoms do not improve after 7 days, see your doctor [2].

### How to Read a Supplement Label

Labels may list alginic acid or sodium alginate, often with a bicarbonate or calcium carbonate. Gaviscon Advance, for example, lists sodium alginate 1,000 mg and potassium hydrogen carbonate 200 mg per 10 mL [2]. The 2022 ACG guideline notes that an alginic acid preparation made in the United Kingdom has shown potential efficacy, but the alginate content of products sold in other countries varies [28]. Choose a product that an independent lab has tested.

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety Profile

Alginates are generally well tolerated. The 2020 meta-analysis found the risk of adverse events no greater than with placebo or PPIs, and the 2024 meta-analysis found that adding alginate to a PPI did not increase adverse events [4][18]. In the GOOD trial, tolerance and safety matched omeprazole, and in a 424-patient placebo-controlled trial, adverse events did not differ notably [5][15]. Alginate acts physically and does not depend on absorption into the bloodstream [2].

### Common Side Effects

The UK label lists diarrhea, nausea and vomiting as uncommon [2]. In trials, one patient had mild diarrhea and two reported nausea and bloating [22][23]. An overdose is likely to cause only minor abdominal discomfort [2].

### Sodium Content

Products made with sodium alginate add sodium to the diet. One UK liquid contains 57.85 mg of sodium per 5 mL, 2.9% of the WHO recommended maximum daily intake, and its maximum daily dose supplies 23.14% [2]. The label calls it high in sodium and asks people on a low-salt diet, for example in some cases of heart failure or kidney disease, to take this into account [2].

The same liquid contains 1.0 mmol (39.06 mg) of potassium per 5 mL, which matters for people with reduced kidney function or on a controlled potassium diet [2]. Each 10 mL also contains 200 mg (2.0 mmol) of calcium carbonate, so care is needed with high blood calcium, nephrocalcinosis or recurrent calcium kidney stones [2].

In a study of 144 pregnant women, serum sodium did not change during treatment [30].

### Effects on Nutrient Absorption

Alginate, like other soluble fibers, can reduce the absorption of some nutrients when taken with food:

- **Carotenoids:** in 6 healthy women given fiber at 0.15 g per kg of body weight with a meal, pectin, guar and alginate cut beta-carotene absorption by 33-43% on average. All the fibers tested cut lycopene and lutein absorption by 40-74% [32].
- **Calcium, iron and zinc:** in a lab model of infant formula, 2 g of alginic acid per 100 mL lowered calcium availability from 13.3% to 5.3%, but raised iron availability from 1.28% to 6.05% and zinc from 6.7% to 10.2% [33].

These studies gave alginate with a meal or mixed into formula. Whether label doses taken after meals affect nutrients has not been studied.

### Allergic Reactions

Allergic reactions to alginate are rare but reported:

- **Occupational exposure:** Henderson et al. (1984) surveyed a British alginate factory and found lung hypersensitivity to seaweed dust in 7% of the workforce, and antibodies to sodium alginate and seaweed extracts in 4.5% [34]. Challenge testing showed immediate airway narrowing followed by a later loss of lung volume. These workers inhaled seaweed dust and alginate at work.
- **Medical and dental materials:** alginate has caused anaphylaxis from a wound dressing and fatal anaphylaxis from a dental impression paste [35][36].
- **Oral products:** a 2001 case report described IgE-mediated anaphylaxis to an antacid [7]. The UK label lists anaphylactic reactions, hives and bronchospasm as very rare, and warns that its paraben preservatives may cause allergic reactions, possibly delayed [2].
- **Possible anti-allergic effects:** in rodent and cell studies, alginic acid reduced anaphylaxis and histamine release, and alginate reduced allergic reactions in a mouse model of egg allergy. These findings have not been tested in people [37][10].

### Special Populations

**Who should not use them:** people with known or suspected hypersensitivity to the active substances or to any excipient, including the parabens methyl and propyl parahydroxybenzoate [2].

**Pregnancy and lactation:** the UK label allows use in pregnancy, if clinically needed, and while breastfeeding [2]. An open-label study found serum sodium unchanged, and a randomized trial found no difference from an antacid in pregnancy or newborn outcomes [30][31].

**Children:** under 12, use only on medical advice [2]. In 20 infants given Gaviscon Infant or placebo in milk, reflux events per hour did not differ (1.58 vs. 1.68). Only reflux height was marginally lower, and the authors questioned any clinical benefit [8]. The EU food safety panel advised that infants' and young children's exposure from food additives should stay below the therapeutic doses at which side effects could occur [13].

**Older adults:** the label requires no dose change [2].

**Kidney disease:** use with caution because of the sodium, potassium and calcium in some products (see Sodium Content) [2].

## 6. Drug Interactions

Alginate products can reduce the absorption of medicines taken at the same time. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Medications Requiring Separation

The UK label advises a 2-hour gap between Gaviscon and other medicines, especially [2]:

- Tetracycline antibiotics
- Fluoroquinolone antibiotics
- Iron salts
- Thyroid hormones
- Chloroquine
- Bisphosphonates
- Estramustine

A review by authors from SmithKline Beecham Consumer Health Care reported that Gaviscon is compatible with, and does not interfere with, the acid-suppressing drug cimetidine [1].

### Drug Absorption Study

**Nagai et al. (2019):** in rats, sodium alginate given just before or just after carbamazepine lowered its blood levels at every sampling point. The drop was larger when the alginate came just after the drug. In a lab test, sodium alginate also bound carbamazepine [6]. The authors advised care with the timing of carbamazepine and alginate-thickened tube feeds.

### Practical Recommendation

Take alginate products 2 hours apart from other medicines [2]. Because alginates are usually taken after meals and at bedtime, and many medicines are taken in the morning or with meals, adjusting the timing of one or both usually achieves this.

## 7. Dietary Sources

### Brown Seaweed Species

Alginate is chemically extracted from brown seaweed [34][37]. One study used alginate from the kelp *Laminaria japonica* [10].

### Food Additive Use

In the EU, alginic acid and its sodium, potassium, ammonium and calcium salts are authorized food additives E 400 to E 404 [13]. In 2017 the European Food Safety Authority found no need for a numerical acceptable daily intake and no safety concern at the reported uses [13]. Food makers use alginate as a stabilizer, thickener or emulsifier [9].

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**How do alginates differ from antacids?**
Antacids neutralize stomach acid. Alginates form a floating raft on the stomach contents that physically holds back reflux [1]. The raft forms within seconds, so relief starts about as fast as with an antacid, and it can stay in the stomach for several hours [1]. Some alginate products also contain antacid components [1].

**Are alginates as effective as PPIs?**
In the GOOD trial, Gaviscon was non-inferior to omeprazole 20 mg for reaching a 24-hour heartburn-free period in moderate episodic heartburn, though omeprazole gave more heartburn-free days by day 7 [5]. A meta-analysis found alginates less effective than PPIs or H2 blockers, but not significantly [3]. The 2022 ACG guideline states that PPIs remain the medical treatment of choice for GERD [28].

**Can I take alginates together with PPIs?**
Yes; the UK label allows use alongside acid-suppressing therapy [2]. Adding Gaviscon Advance to a once-daily PPI improved reflux scores more than placebo (P = 0.03) [24], and adding sodium alginate to omeprazole in NERD raised complete heartburn resolution from 25.7% to 56.7% [22]. Two meta-analyses, though, found no statistically significant pooled benefit from the combination [4][18].

**When should I take alginate?**
After meals and at bedtime, as the label directs [2]. After a meal, the raft forms on top of the stomach contents over the acid pocket [11].

**Is alginate safe during pregnancy?**
The UK label allows use in pregnancy, if clinically needed, based on studies in more than 500 pregnant women [2]. An open-label study of 144 pregnant women rated treatment a success in 91% [30]. In a randomized trial, an alginate worked no better than an antacid gel, and outcomes for mothers and babies did not differ [31].

**How long can I take alginate?**
The UK label says symptoms that do not improve after 7 days should be reviewed by a doctor [2]. One throat-reflux trial followed patients for 6 months [25].

**Does alginate interfere with my medications?**
It can. Leave 2 hours between an alginate product and other medicines, especially tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones, iron, thyroid hormones, chloroquine, bisphosphonates and estramustine [2].

**Are there different types of alginate supplements?**
Yes. They come as alginic acid or as sodium, potassium, calcium or magnesium salts, and as liquids or chewable tablets [13][14][15]. Sodium alginate liquids can be high in sodium, so check the label if you are on a low-salt diet [2].
