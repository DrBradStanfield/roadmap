## Current view (reviewed 25 October 2026)

Brad is not convinced by the evidence for L-theanine's benefits (Brad's current guidance, 25 Oct 2026). He does not take it himself: the anti-ageing claims rest on studies in roundworms, rat cells, rats and mice, and until effects show up in randomised controlled trials in people, they are not meaningful to him ([Brad, 4 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/theanine-anti-aging-longevity)). In 2024 he wrote that the claimed effects on mood, anxiety and stress had not shown up in most human studies, though one two-month trial in 60 patients with schizophrenia showed some benefit ([Brad, 4 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/theanine-anti-aging-longevity)). Supplements are often 200 to 500 mg, and at that level L-theanine does not appear to have significant side effects ([Brad, 4 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/theanine-anti-aging-longevity)). If you want some theanine anyway, he sees tea in moderation as an acceptable source, drunk only in the first four or so hours after waking because of its caffeine ([Brad, 4 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/theanine-anti-aging-longevity)). He cautions that L-theanine could interact badly with other body systems or with medicines, and he would not recommend an untested supplement, let alone a megadose, without good reason ([Brad, 4 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/theanine-anti-aging-longevity)).

## Key safety points

- In rats, L-theanine taken with the sedative midazolam had an additive or synergistic effect on anxiety and movement [21]. If you take benzodiazepines or other sedatives, talk to your doctor first.
- In mice, theanine raised doxorubicin levels in tumours; in the same work it also strengthened other chemotherapy drugs [20]. If you are having chemotherapy, talk to your oncologist first.
- L-theanine blunted rises in blood pressure from caffeine, and from mental stress in people whose blood pressure rose most, in small trials [11][18]. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.
- Trials of 400 mg a day for six to eight weeks found it well tolerated, including in boys aged 8 to 12 years with ADHD [15][17].
- In a 28-day trial in adults with moderate stress, the group taking 400 mg of L-theanine a day had less time asleep on an activity tracker, though the authors reported less light sleep and better sleep quality [7].
- L-theanine alone raised headache ratings in one crossover trial [12].

## 1. Overview

L-theanine (gamma-glutamylethylamide) is an amino acid found mainly in the tea plant, *Camellia sinensis*, the source of green, black, white and oolong tea, and in a few mushrooms of the genus *Boletus* [1][2]. The body does not use it to build protein [2]. It resembles the brain chemical glutamate and is thought to block glutamate from binding to its receptors in the brain [5][18].

Laboratory and animal work suggests L-theanine reaches the brain and acts on several signalling systems [1][2]. In a small trial, 50 mg raised alpha brain wave activity more than placebo, a change the authors link with a relaxed mind without drowsiness [4]. That finding drives interest in L-theanine as a way to feel calmer without sedation.

Human trials are small and short, and their results are mixed. L-theanine did no better than placebo for anxiety in people with generalized anxiety disorder [16], and perceived stress fell by similar amounts with L-theanine and placebo in healthy adults [7]. One trial in schizophrenia found modest gains when it was added to antipsychotic treatment [17].

People take it alone for relaxation or with caffeine for focus, though in one trial it left the jitteriness caffeine causes unchanged [11].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Branded Forms of L-Theanine

Some trials name the branded form they used: Suntheanine in a trial in boys with ADHD [15], and AlphaWave L-Theanine in two stress trials [6][7].

### Absorption and Pharmacokinetics

In a crossover study of 12 healthy adults, 100 mg of L-theanine, given as capsules or as green tea was absorbed quickly, with similar results for both forms [8]. Blood levels peaked 0.8 hours after intake of 100 mg [8]. In three people given 50 to 200 mg by capsule, total blood exposure rose with the dose [8]. The body appears to break L-theanine down into ethylamine and glutamic acid, which pass into the urine [8].

### Delivery Forms

Supplements come in several formats:

- **Capsules and tablets:** the most common form.
- **Powder:** often dissolved in water, much as L-theanine is taken in tea.
- **Chewable tablets and gummies:** a trial in boys with ADHD used chewable tablets of 100 mg each [15]. Dose accuracy may vary between products.
- **Beverages:** added to some functional drinks, often with caffeine.
- **Tea:** the original dietary source. Content varies by tea and preparation (see Section 7: Dietary Sources).

### L-Theanine and Caffeine Combinations

L-theanine is often sold with caffeine, on the idea that it softens caffeine's stimulating effects. In a trial of 48 healthy adults, 200 mg of theanine taken with 250 mg of caffeine blunted caffeine's rise in blood pressure but did not significantly change its effects on alertness, jitteriness or other aspects of mood [11].

In a crossover trial, 250 mg of L-theanine alone raised headache ratings, while L-theanine taken with 150 mg of caffeine lowered headache and tiredness ratings and improved several attention measures [12]. These are effects of the combination and cannot be credited to L-theanine alone.

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Stress and Anxiety in Healthy Adults

Results in healthy adults are mixed, and the trials are small.

**Positive findings:**

In a crossover trial in 12 people, L-theanine reduced heart rate and salivary immunoglobulin A responses to a mental arithmetic stress task compared with placebo [5].

In a crossover trial in moderately stressed adults, one 200 mg dose of AlphaWave L-Theanine raised frontal and whole-scalp alpha brain wave power 3 hours after the dose more than placebo [6]. Salivary cortisol also fell more than with placebo 1 hour after the dose [6].

**Mixed and negative findings:**

In 16 healthy volunteers, L-theanine 200 mg showed some relaxing effect at rest, but neither it nor 1 mg of the benzodiazepine alprazolam reduced anxiety during an experimentally induced anxiety task [13]. Any calming effect may be limited to resting conditions [13].

In a 28-day trial in 30 moderately stressed adults, people took AlphaWave L-Theanine (400 mg of L-theanine a day) or placebo [7]. Perceived stress scores fell by similar amounts in both groups (17.98% and 17.88% at 28 days), and salivary cortisol changes did not differ between groups [7].

**Summary:** One small trial found lower stress responses [5] and one found brain wave and cortisol changes after a single dose [6], but L-theanine did not beat placebo for anxiety under induced stress [13] or for perceived stress in a four-week trial [7].

### Sleep

L-theanine is widely sold as a sleep aid, but trial results are mixed.

**Healthy adults:**

In a four-week crossover trial in 30 adults (average age 48.3) without major psychiatric illness, L-theanine 200 mg a day lowered Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index scores [14]. Compared with placebo, it improved the subscores for sleep latency, sleep disturbance and use of sleep medication [14].

**Adverse sleep findings:**

In the 28-day AlphaWave trial, the L-theanine group had less time asleep after 28 days, though the authors also reported less light sleep than placebo and better sleep quality [7].

**Boys with ADHD:**

In a six-week trial in 98 boys aged 8 to 12 years with ADHD, 400 mg a day of Suntheanine raised sleep percentage and sleep efficiency on wrist actigraphy compared with placebo [15]. Time to fall asleep did not change [15]. Some boys were also taking stimulant medicine, and the trial balanced them between groups [15].

**Summary:** One small trial in healthy adults found better sleep scores [14], one found less time asleep [7], one in generalized anxiety disorder found better sleep satisfaction but not less insomnia [16], and one in boys with ADHD found better sleep efficiency but not faster sleep onset [15]. The trials are small and short, and their results are mixed.

### Cognitive Function and Memory

The evidence for L-theanine as a cognitive aid in healthy adults is weak and mixed.

**Small acute effects on attention:**

In a crossover trial in 32 healthy young adults, single doses of L-theanine sped simple reaction times compared with placebo, following 100 mg (16.3 ms) and 200 mg (16.9 ms) [9]. No dose improved sustained attention or inhibitory control compared with placebo, and 400 mg did not improve reaction time [9].

**Healthy adults, four weeks:**

In the four-week trial of 200 mg a day, verbal fluency and executive function scores improved after L-theanine [14]. Compared with placebo, verbal fluency improved only in people with lower starting scores [14].

**Chronic supplementation:**

In the 28-day AlphaWave trial, Stroop test reaction times improved after 14 days with L-theanine but only after 28 days with placebo [7].

**Alpha brain waves:**

EEG studies show L-theanine increases alpha brain wave activity. In one trial, 50 mg raised alpha activity more than placebo, measured at 45, 60, 75, 90 and 105 minutes after the dose [4]. Alpha activity is associated with being relaxed yet alert, but these changes have not translated consistently into better scores on cognitive tests.

**Summary:** L-theanine raises alpha brain waves, but trials show at most small, inconsistent effects on attention and other cognitive tests in healthy adults [4][7][9][14].

### ADHD (Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder)

Some people use L-theanine as an add-on treatment for ADHD.

The six-week trial in boys with ADHD described under Sleep measured sleep, not core ADHD symptoms [15]. At 400 mg a day, L-theanine improved sleep percentage and sleep efficiency but not time to fall asleep, and it was well tolerated with no significant adverse events [15].

**Summary:** L-theanine may improve some measures of sleep in boys with ADHD. Evidence is limited to a single trial that measured sleep, not attention or hyperactivity [15].

### Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

In an 8-week trial in 46 people with generalized anxiety disorder who stayed on their antidepressants, adding 450 to 900 mg a day of L-theanine was no better than placebo for anxiety or insomnia severity, and it had no effect on cognitive tests [16]. People taking L-theanine did report greater sleep satisfaction [16].

This trial used higher doses than most others and studied people with a diagnosed anxiety disorder, so its negative result is relevant to anyone weighing L-theanine against prescription anxiety treatment.

**Summary:** L-theanine at doses up to 900 mg/day did not reduce anxiety in people with diagnosed generalized anxiety disorder.

### Schizophrenia and Schizoaffective Disorder

The strongest psychiatric evidence for L-theanine comes from one trial in schizophrenia.

In an 8-week trial in 60 people with chronic schizophrenia or schizoaffective disorder already on antipsychotics, adding 400 mg a day of L-theanine reduced anxiety, positive symptoms and general psychopathology scores compared with placebo [17]. Only 40 patients completed the trial [17]. Effect sizes were modest to moderate, and negative symptoms, cognitive tests and quality of life did not change [17].

L-theanine was used alongside antipsychotics, not instead of them, and the authors call for longer studies [17].

**Summary:** Adding 400 mg a day of L-theanine to antipsychotic treatment may ease anxiety and positive symptoms in schizophrenia, based on a single trial [17].

### Blood Pressure and Caffeine Interaction

L-theanine may blunt rises in blood pressure caused by caffeine. In the trial of 48 healthy adults, 200 mg of theanine reduced the rise in blood pressure from 250 mg of caffeine but did not change alertness or jitteriness [11]. Theanine also slowed overall reaction time on one task [11].

In a crossover trial in 14 people, L-theanine reduced blood pressure rises during mental tasks in those whose blood pressure rose most, and it lowered tension-anxiety scores compared with placebo [18]. This possible blood pressure effect is the reason for caution with blood pressure medicines (see Section 6: Drug Interactions).

**Summary:** L-theanine may reduce blood pressure rises from caffeine or mental stress, but it does not appear to blunt caffeine's effect on alertness. Any effect on blood pressure at rest is not well characterized.

### Anti-Tumor Properties (Preclinical Only)

In tumour cells grown in the lab, theanine reduced the outflow of the chemotherapy drug doxorubicin from the cells [19]. In mice with ovarian sarcoma, theanine raised doxorubicin levels in the tumour but not in the liver or heart, strengthened its effect on tumour growth, and tended to normalise markers of its side effects [20]. Theanine also strengthened other anthracyclines, cisplatin and irinotecan in that work [20].

These results come from cells and mice only and say nothing yet about cancer in people.

**Summary:** Preclinical data only. No human evidence for anti-cancer effects.

### Fatigue

Evidence on L-theanine alone for fatigue is lacking. In one crossover trial, caffeine and the caffeine and L-theanine combination eased self-rated mental fatigue, results that cannot be credited to L-theanine alone [12].

### Immune Function

L-theanine is partly broken down into ethylamine [8]. Ethylamine is an antigen that primes gamma-delta T cells, part of the innate immune system, and in one study drinking tea primed these cells to respond to bacteria [25].

However, this research is primarily mechanistic. No controlled clinical trials have demonstrated that L-theanine supplementation meaningfully improves immune function outcomes such as infection rates, illness duration, or vaccine response in humans.

**Summary:** A plausible mechanism exists via ethylamine and gamma-delta T cell priming, but clinical evidence for immune enhancement is absent.

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Dosing by Indication

Doses tested in the trials above:

| Indication | Dose | Frequency | Result | Source |
|-----------|------|-----------|--------|--------|
| Alpha brain wave activity | 50 mg | Single dose | More alpha activity than placebo | Nobre et al., 2008 [4] |
| Acute stress (brain waves, cortisol) | 200 mg | Single dose | More alpha power and lower cortisol than placebo | Evans et al., 2021 [6] |
| Anticipatory anxiety | 200 mg | Single dose | Some relaxation at rest; no effect under induced anxiety | Lu et al., 2004 [13] |
| Stress-related symptoms and sleep | 200 mg | Daily for 4 weeks | Better sleep subscores than placebo | Hidese et al., 2019 [14] |
| Sleep in boys with ADHD | 400 mg | Daily for 6 weeks | Better sleep efficiency than placebo | Lyon et al., 2011 [15] |
| Schizophrenia symptoms (add-on) | 400 mg | Daily for 8 weeks | Less anxiety and fewer positive symptoms than placebo | Ritsner et al., 2011 [17] |
| Generalized anxiety disorder (add-on) | 450 to 900 mg | Daily for 8 weeks | No better than placebo for anxiety | Sarris et al., 2019 [16] |
| Perceived stress | 400 mg | Daily for 28 days | Similar to placebo; less time asleep | Moulin et al., 2024 [7] |
| Simple reaction time | 100, 200 and 400 mg | Single dose | Faster at 100 and 200 mg only | Dassanayake et al., 2023 [9] |
| Caffeine-induced blood pressure rise | 200 mg with 250 mg caffeine | Single dose | Smaller rise than caffeine alone | Rogers et al., 2008 [11] |

### Practical Dosing Recommendations

**Doses in trials:** Most trials above used 200 to 400 mg a day [14][15][17].

**Timing:** Blood levels peak 0.8 hours after a dose [8], and trials measured effects from 45 minutes to 3 hours after a single dose [4][6].

**With caffeine:** The blood pressure trial used 200 mg of theanine with 250 mg of caffeine [11].

**Upper range in trials:** The highest dose in these trials was 900 mg a day, in the generalized anxiety disorder trial, and it was no better than placebo for anxiety [16].

**Important context:** Benefits in healthy adults are not established, so expect modest effects at best.

### How to Read an L-Theanine Label

L-theanine supplement labels are generally straightforward because the compound is not chelated to a mineral (unlike, for example, magnesium glycinate, where the elemental magnesium content differs from the compound weight). When a label states "200 mg L-theanine," it means 200 mg of the amino acid itself.

Key things to check:

- **L-theanine vs D-theanine:** Look for "L-theanine," the form found in tea and used in the trials above.
- **Branded forms:** Some products name a branded form, such as Suntheanine or AlphaWave, which trials have used [6][15].
- **Other ingredients:** Some products combine L-theanine with caffeine, magnesium, GABA, or melatonin. Be aware of all active ingredients and their individual dose contributions. Results from combination products cannot be credited to L-theanine alone.

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety Profile

Trials of up to 8 weeks report L-theanine as well tolerated. At 400 mg a day it was reported as safe and well tolerated in schizophrenia [17] and caused no significant adverse events in boys with ADHD [15]. One 200 mg dose and 28 days of 400 mg a day were reported as safe in healthy adults [6][7].

### Reported Side Effects

**Headaches:** In a crossover trial, 250 mg of L-theanine alone raised headache ratings, while headache ratings fell when it was taken with 150 mg of caffeine [12].

**Children with tics:** In the ADHD trial, one boy who already had facial tics developed a new, very subtle facial tic within four days of starting L-theanine; it stopped after he left the study, and the supervising physician judged it unlikely to be related to L-theanine [15].

**Possible sleep disruption:** In the 28-day trial, the group taking 400 mg of L-theanine a day had less time asleep after 28 days [7]. This is worth knowing if you take L-theanine for sleep.

### Pregnancy and Lactation

There are no clinical trials of L-theanine supplementation in pregnant or lactating women. Supplement doses are many times the amount in a cup of black tea, about 20 mg [4]. Pregnant and breastfeeding women should ask their doctor first before using L-theanine supplements.

### Children

In a six-week trial, boys aged 8 to 12 years with ADHD took 400 mg a day without significant adverse events [15]. L-theanine supplements are not recommended for children without medical supervision.

### Long-Term Safety

The trials in this review lasted 8 weeks at most [16][17]. While the compound has a long history of dietary consumption via tea, the long-term safety of supplement doses is not established.

### Toxicology Data

Reviews of acute and chronic toxicity tests describe L-theanine as generally safe, even in large amounts, though they note that clear evidence on its safety is still lacking [2].

## 6. Drug Interactions

### Blood Pressure Medications

L-theanine blunted rises in blood pressure caused by caffeine [11] and by mental tasks in people whose blood pressure rose most [18]. Whether it adds to the effect of blood pressure medicines is unknown. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Benzodiazepines and Sedatives

In a study in rats, L-theanine combined with the sedative midazolam had an additive or synergistic effect, lowering anxiety-like behaviour and both fine and basic movement [21]. The finding comes from animals, but the combination could increase sedation, so talk to your doctor before taking L-theanine with benzodiazepines (such as diazepam, lorazepam or alprazolam) or other sedatives.

### Stimulant Medications

In the trial in boys with ADHD, some were taking stimulant medicine, and L-theanine was well tolerated overall [15]. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Chemotherapy Drugs

In mice, theanine raised doxorubicin levels in tumours and strengthened the effect of doxorubicin and other chemotherapy drugs, including cisplatin and irinotecan [20]. The findings come from animals, but changing how chemotherapy drugs act could have serious consequences, so talk to your oncologist before taking L-theanine.

### Summary of Drug Interaction Risk

| Drug Category | Interaction Type | Risk Level | Recommendation |
|--------------|-----------------|-----------|----------------|
| Blood pressure medicines | Possible added blood pressure lowering | Unknown (blood pressure effect seen in small trials) | Check with your doctor or pharmacist |
| Benzodiazepines and other sedatives | Possible added sedation | Unknown (animal data only) | Talk to your doctor first |
| Stimulant medicines | Some boys in one trial took stimulants; well tolerated | Unknown (limited data) | Check with your doctor or pharmacist |
| Caffeine | Blunts caffeine's blood pressure rise; no effect on alertness | Studied together in trials | Commonly combined |
| Chemotherapy drugs | May alter drug effects | Unknown (preclinical only) | Talk to your oncologist first |

## 7. Dietary Sources

### L-Theanine in Tea

Tea (*Camellia sinensis*) is the main dietary source of L-theanine. In 37 commercial teas, the mean L-theanine content of white, green, oolong and black tea leaves was 6.26, 6.56, 6.09, and 5.13 mg/g [24]. Pu-erh tea contained almost none [24]. A cup of black tea holds about 20 mg [4].

### Factors Affecting L-Theanine Content in Tea

Content varies widely even within one type of tea. In the 37-tea analysis, L-theanine levels were too variable to tell white, green, oolong and black teas apart [24].

### Tea vs Supplements

A cup of black tea provides roughly 20 mg [4], while most trials above used 200 to 400 mg a day [14][17]. Reaching trial doses from tea alone would take many cups, which also brings caffeine and other tea compounds.

### Mushroom Sources

Certain species of *Boletus* mushrooms (e.g., *Boletus badius*, bay bolete) contain L-theanine, although at lower concentrations than tea. Mushrooms are not a practical dietary source for meaningful L-theanine intake [1][2].
