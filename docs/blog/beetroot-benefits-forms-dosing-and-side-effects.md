## Current view (reviewed 9 November 2026)

Brad does not recommend a nitric oxide supplement, beetroot powder included; for nitric oxide, his advice is to eat nitrate-rich vegetables such as rocket (arugula), beetroot, spinach, chard and lettuce (Brad's current guidance, 10 Aug 2026).

His key study gave people with high blood pressure beetroot juice with about 397 mg of nitrate a day, which lowered blood pressure by around 8 points; in another trial, a week of daily doses of beetroot juice containing 378 mg of nitrate improved exercise endurance in older people with heart failure ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)).

He warns that nitrate content varies widely: one analysis of 24 beetroot products found only 5 with at least 300 mg of nitrate per serving, and he says a pill cannot carry that much ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)).

Beets are one of the highest food sources of oxalate, which can lead to kidney stones in susceptible people ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)).

His own choice is a rocket salad: rocket is low in oxalate, and about 63 grams gives the 300 mg nitrate level ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)).

## Key safety points

- Beetroot nitrate can lower blood pressure [1]. If you take blood pressure medicines or drugs such as sildenafil, ask your doctor first, and never stop a prescribed medicine on your own.
- Methemoglobinemia is mostly caused by drugs such as dapsone and benzocaine [2]. If you take these, or already eat a lot of nitrate, ask your doctor before taking concentrated beetroot products.
- Nitrate poisoning is still reported in babies fed home-made vegetable foods [63]. Do not give beetroot supplements or concentrated beetroot juice to infants or young children.
- Beets raise urinary oxalate [4], and people with chronic kidney disease may need to limit potassium [64]. If you have had kidney stones or have kidney disease, ask your doctor first.
- Two trainees had hypoxic events during breath-hold swims after taking beetroot powder [5]. If you free dive or train with breath-holds, it may be safer to skip beetroot supplements beforehand.
- Drinking beetroot juice may easily push nitrate intake above the acceptable daily intake [6]. Trials disagree on whether this raises N-nitroso compounds [7][8].
- Red or pink urine after beetroot is reported in 10-14% of people [9]. Nitrate made headache worse at simulated altitude [10].

## 1. Overview

Beetroot (*Beta vulgaris*) is a root vegetable rich in inorganic nitrate [11]. The body can turn nitrate into nitric oxide (NO) by a route separate from the classical L-arginine pathway, and this route matters most when oxygen is low [12]. Absorbed nitrate is concentrated by the salivary glands and secreted in saliva, where bacteria on the tongue reduce it to nitrite [13][14]. Swallowed nitrite forms NO in the acidic stomach [13].

In healthy volunteers, the largest fall in blood pressure, 10.4/8 mm Hg, came about 3 hours after 500 mL of beetroot juice, in step with the peak rise in plasma nitrite [15]. Blocking the conversion of nitrate to nitrite in the mouth prevented both the nitrite rise and the fall in blood pressure [15].

Trials often give doses in millimoles: 1 mmol of nitrate is 62 mg [16]. Approximately 80% of dietary nitrate comes from vegetables [17]. Drinks made from rocket or spinach raised plasma nitrate as much as beetroot juice at the same nitrate dose [18].

Beetroot also contains red pigments, the betacyanins, which pass into the urine of some people [9].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

Beetroot is sold as juice, concentrated juice shots, powders, capsules, extracts and chews. These products differ widely in how much nitrate they deliver.

### Comparison of Forms

| Form | What testing and trials show |
|------|------|
| Juice and concentrated shots | The form used in most trials [19][1][20]. Nitrate varied about 50-fold between 24 juice products, and only a few reliably gave at least 5 mmol per serving [11]. |
| Powders, capsules and tablets | A serving of fresh beetroot gave more nitrate than most daily doses; the best product gave the highest dose (169 mg/daily dose) [21]. |
| Standardized beetroot extract | Trials used 20 g daily of one extract [22], containing ∼548 mg/8.8 mmol nitrate [23]. |
| Chews | There is no good public evidence on their nitrate content yet. |
| Whole vegetables | Rocket and spinach drinks raised plasma nitrate as much as beetroot juice at the same nitrate dose [18]. |

### Key Considerations for Form Selection

Juice and concentrated juice are the forms most trials used, including the early exercise trials and the 4-week trial in people with hypertension [19][1]. The number that matters is nitrate, and labels often leave it out: most commercial beetroot juices do not state their nitrate content [24], and 64% of beetroot supplements tested in one study did not meet all labelling rules [21]. A 600 mg beetroot extract made no meaningful difference to recovery after exercise in healthy young men [25]. Choose a product that an independent lab has tested.

### Storage and Nitrate Preservation

Home-made beetroot juice lost all its nitrate after 3 days at 20°C [24]. Keeping it at 4°C or -20°C reduced that loss by 78% and 82% [24]. Adding lemon juice (5%) protected the nitrate for 3 days at 20°C [24].

### Processing Methods and Nitrate Retention

There is no good public evidence on how processing methods change nitrate content yet.

### Optimizing the Nitrate-to-Nitric-Oxide Conversion

The conversion of nitrate to nitric oxide depends on bacteria in the mouth. Two factors affect it:

- **Antibacterial mouthwash:** rinsing with a chlorhexidine mouthwash before a nitrate dose abolished nitrite formation in saliva and markedly blunted the rise in plasma nitrite [26]. A week of daily use reduced oral nitrite production by 90% and raised blood pressure in healthy people [14]; in treated hypertension, using it over 3 days raised systolic pressure by 2.3 mm Hg [27].
- **Timing:** blood pressure fell most about 3 hours after a dose, when plasma nitrite peaked [15]. Cyclists took their dose about 2.5 h before a time trial [28].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Blood Pressure

Nitrate lowers blood pressure through nitrite made by mouth bacteria [15][14]. Results differ by who is studied, the dose and the length of the trial.

#### In Adults Without High Blood Pressure

A 2013 meta-analysis pooled crossover trials with a total of 254 participants, lasting 2 h to 15 d [29]. Nitrate and beetroot juice lowered systolic blood pressure by 4.4 mm Hg; the 1.1 mm Hg fall in diastolic pressure was not statistically significant [29]. Higher daily nitrate doses were linked to larger falls in systolic pressure [29].

Single trials, from longest to shortest:

- In older adults, 20 g daily of beetroot extract for 12 weeks was well tolerated and did not change blood pressure [22].
- In a 2-week trial in young and older adults, nitrate-rich beetroot juice lowered mean arterial pressure by 4 mmHg in the older group, whose baseline pressure was higher, but not in the young [32].
- In people with type 2 diabetes, 250ml of beetroot juice a day (7.5mmol of nitrate) for 2 weeks did not lower 24-hour blood pressure or improve endothelial function or insulin sensitivity [30].
- One 500 g dose of beetroot and apple juice in healthy adults gave only a trend overall, but systolic pressure in men fell by 4 - 5 mmHg at 6 hours [31].

#### In People With High Blood Pressure

Trials in people with high blood pressure disagree. From the largest to the smallest:

- In a one-week factorial trial in 90 people with hypertension, beetroot juice with nitrate (13 mmol/day) made no difference to blood pressure, while cutting sodium lowered systolic pressure by 7 mmHg [34].
- In 68 patients with hypertension, half untreated and half on medicines, 250 mL daily of beetroot juice for 4 weeks lowered clinic blood pressure by 7.7/2.4 mm Hg, 24-hour pressure by 7.7/5.2 mm Hg and home pressure by 8.1/3.8 mm Hg, with no change on nitrate-free juice [1].
- In Tanzanian adults aged 50-70 y with raised blood pressure, beetroot juice with ∼400 mg nitrate a day for 60 d lowered 24-hour systolic pressure by 10.8 mm Hg, against 0.3 mm Hg on placebo [33]. Adding ∼5 mg folic acid gave a smaller fall of 6.1 mm Hg [33].
- In people with treated hypertension (n = 27), 1 week of nitrate-rich beetroot juice raised plasma nitrite 3-fold but did not lower home or 24-hour blood pressure [35].

#### During Exercise

In 12 healthy, older (60-70 yr) adults, 3 days of concentrated beetroot juice (2 × 70 ml/day, ∼9.6 mmol/day of nitrate) lowered resting blood pressure: systolic 115 vs. 120 and diastolic 70 vs. 73 on placebo, in mmHg [36]. Walking performance did not change [36]. The trial measured resting pressure, not pressure during exercise.

#### Broader Context: Systematic Reviews

Pooled trials show a modest fall in systolic pressure [29]. A review of trials in older adults found that half reported better blood pressure or blood flow and half did not [37]. In people already on blood pressure medicines, results are inconsistent: one trial found sustained falls [1], another found none [35]. In a head-to-head trial, cutting sodium lowered blood pressure and beetroot juice did not [34]. Beetroot does not replace blood pressure medicines. Never stop or change a prescribed medicine without your doctor.

### Exercise Performance

Nitrate can reduce the oxygen cost of submaximal exercise and may improve muscle power [38][19]. Benefits are smaller in highly trained athletes [39][40].

The International Olympic Committee consensus lists nitrate among the few performance supplements with good evidence [41]. The Australian Institute of Sport places dietary nitrate and beetroot juice in Group A, its category for strong scientific evidence in specific situations in sport [42].

#### Dose-Response Relationship

In pooled blood pressure trials, higher daily nitrate doses were linked to larger falls in systolic pressure [29]. In a test of 6.4, 12.8 and 19.2 mmol of nitrate, plasma nitrite did not rise further above 6.4 mmol, resting blood pressure fell only after 19.2 mmol, and 12.8 mmol or more was needed to raise knee-extensor torque [43]. At least 5 mmol per serving seems to be the minimal dose that improves exercise performance in most people [11].

#### In Younger, Healthy Active Adults

A 2013 meta-analysis of 17 studies in healthy people, using 300 to 600 mg nitrate from a single dose to 15 days of regular intake, found a moderate benefit for time-to-exhaustion tests and a small, non-significant benefit for time trials [44]. Benefits showed more often in inactive to recreationally active people and after several days of loading [44].

In healthy men, 500 ml/day of beetroot juice for 6 consecutive days raised plasma nitrite, lowered systolic blood pressure, cut the oxygen cost of moderate exercise and extended time to exhaustion [19].

In trained male cyclists, 140 ml/d of concentrated beetroot juice (about 8 mmol/d nitrate) for 6 d lowered submaximal oxygen uptake and improved 10-km time-trial time (953±18 vs. 965±18 s) [20]. In club-level cyclists, one 0.5 L dose (about 6.2 mmol of nitrate) taken 2.5 h before improved 4-km performance by 2.8% and 16.1-km performance by 2.7% [28].

**Sex differences:** in young women and men, 3 days of beetroot juice lowered oxygen use during moderate exercise by about 5% and extended time to exhaustion by about 15% in men, but did not help women [45].

**Muscle strength:** in 11 healthy participants, 12.8 mmol and 19.2 mmol of nitrate raised torque over the first 10 muscle contractions of a knee-extension test, while 6.4 mmol improved the speed of force development [43].

#### In Older Adults

**No benefit in healthy older adults:** in healthy adults aged 60 and 75 years, 1 week of beetroot juice did not change exercise capacity, hand-grip strength, timed up-and-go, chair rising or walking speed [48].

**Muscle strength in older women:** in postmenopausal women, daily 20 g of beetroot extract (∼548 mg/8.8 mmol nitrate) for eight weeks increased maximal knee-extensor torque compared with placebo [23].

**Heart failure:** in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction, one week of daily beetroot juice (6.1 mmol nitrate) improved submaximal endurance by 24%, while a single dose did not [46].

**Peripheral artery disease:** in a pilot trial in people with peripheral artery disease and claudication, beetroot juice (4.2 mmol of nitrate) before each session of a 12-week exercise program raised claudication onset time by 180.3±46.6 s, against 59.2±57.3 s with placebo; 6-minute walk distance rose by 53.3 m against 24.6 m [47]. The confidence interval of the effect size crossed zero [47].

Overall, a 2017 review of trials in older adults found better time to exhaustion in some and unclear effects on cognition [37].

#### In Elite Athletes

- In highly trained cyclists, 0.5 L of beetroot juice a day for 6 days had no effect on oxygen uptake kinetics or performance [39].
- In people with differing fitness, the fitter the person, the smaller the gain in 3-km time-trial performance from nitrate [40].

#### Post-Exercise Recovery

A 600 mg beetroot extract, taken 120 min before exercise, made no meaningful difference to the recovery of heart rate, blood pressure or heart rate variability in healthy men [25]. There is no good public evidence on recovery after harder exercise yet.

#### Combining with L-Citrulline

There is no good public evidence on combining beetroot with L-citrulline yet.

### Antioxidant and Anti-Inflammatory Effects

In a crossover pilot trial in 24 hypertensive subjects, raw beetroot juice for 2 weeks lowered hs-CRP and TNF-alpha and raised flow-mediated dilation more than cooked beetroot did [49]. Both forms lowered blood pressure, interleukin-6 and E-selectin, and raw juice raised total antioxidant capacity [49].

### Endothelial Function

A 2016 meta-analysis of randomized trials with 246 participants found that nitrate and beetroot improved vascular function (SMD 0.36; 95 % CI 0.16, 0.56) [50]. The effect was larger with higher doses and smaller in older people and in those at greater cardiometabolic risk [50].

In postmenopausal women (60-85 yr), 12 weeks of beetroot extract with 8.8 mmol nitrate a day improved femoral artery flow-mediated dilation; each group had only 8 women [51]. In the 4-week hypertension trial, endothelial function improved by ≈20% [1].

### Liver and Metabolic Health

Human evidence is limited. In people with type 2 diabetes, 2 weeks of beetroot juice did not improve insulin sensitivity [30]. In older adults, a 12-week course of beetroot extract lowered insulin levels, though overall changes in blood tests were not statistically significant [22]. A review found no metabolic benefit in trials in older adults [37]. There is no good public evidence on liver outcomes yet.

### Cognitive Function

In healthy older adults, 3 days of concentrated beetroot juice did not change cognitive function or brain metabolites [36]. A review of trials in older adults found the effects on cognition unclear [37].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### By Indication

**For blood pressure:** trials that lowered blood pressure in hypertension used 250 mL daily of beetroot juice for 4 weeks [1] or ∼400 mg nitrate a day for 60 d [33]. One-week trials in treated hypertension found no effect [35][34].

**For exercise performance:** trials used 300 to 600 mg nitrate [44]. At least 5 mmol per serving seems to be the minimal effective dose for most people [11]. Cyclists took a single dose 2.5 h before a time trial [28], and benefits showed more often after several days of loading [44].

**For peripheral artery disease:** one pilot trial gave 4.2 mmol of nitrate in concentrated beetroot juice before each exercise session [47].

**For heart failure:** one trial gave beetroot juice with 6.1 mmol nitrate daily for one week [46].

The acceptable daily intake for nitrate from all sources is 3.7 mg/kg body weight per day [3]. To convert trial doses, 1 mmol of nitrate is 62 mg [16].

### Practical Dosing Guidance

- **Check the nitrate:** the dose that matters is nitrate, not grams of beetroot, and most commercial juices do not state it [24].
- **Timing:** blood pressure fell most about 3 hours after a dose [15].
- **Loading:** exercise benefits showed more often after several days of nitrate [44].
- **Protect the pathway:** avoid antibacterial mouthwash around the time of a dose, because it blunts the conversion of nitrate to nitrite [26].
- **Store juice cold:** home-made juice keeps its nitrate far better in the fridge or freezer, or with lemon juice added [24].

### How to Read a Beetroot Supplement Label

Look for the nitrate content per serving. Most commercial beetroot juices do not state it [24], and in one study 64% of beetroot supplements did not meet all labelling rules [21]. In tests of 24 juice products, only a few reliably gave at least 5 mmol of nitrate per serving [11], and most powder and capsule supplements gave less nitrate a day than a serving of fresh beetroot [21]. Some powders also add nitrate salts such as betaine nitrate, so check the ingredient list and stay within the doses trials used. Choose a product that an independent lab has tested.

### Adjustments for Special Populations

**Older adults:** the effect on endothelial function appears smaller in older people [50], yet in one trial blood pressure fell in older adults and not in young ones [32]. The evidence is conflicting.

**Athletes:** the IOC lists nitrate among the few performance supplements with good evidence, and advises trying any supplement in training before using it in competition [41].

**Women:** one trial found exercise benefits in men but not in women [45].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

Trials report few adverse events. A 12-week extract trial in older adults reported no serious adverse events [22], the heart failure trial reported none linked to beetroot juice [46], and the 4-week hypertension trial found the juice well tolerated [1].

- **Red or pink urine (beeturia):** reported in 10-14% of people, and more common in iron deficiency [9].
- **Stomach upset:** some people report stomach upset or diarrhea, but no public trial data give a rate. Lower the dose or stop if it happens.
- **Headache:** nitrates in heart medicines and food additives are common headache triggers [52]. At simulated altitude, beetroot juice made headache worse [10].

### Hypotensive Effects

Beetroot nitrate can lower blood pressure: in one hypertension trial, 24-hour pressure fell by 7.7/5.2 mm Hg [1]. If your blood pressure is already low, or you take blood pressure medicines, ask your doctor first.

### Kidney Stones and Oxalate

Beets are among the few foods shown to raise urinary oxalate significantly [4]. Reported oxalate values for beets vary widely across food databases, from 36.9-794.12 mg/100 g [53]. Raw beets contain about 325 mg of potassium per 100 g, according to USDA FoodData Central. Damaged kidneys can let potassium build up to levels that disturb the heart rhythm, so people with chronic kidney disease may need to limit it [64]. Brad notes that beets are one of the highest food sources of oxalate and can lead to kidney stones in susceptible people ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)). If you have had calcium oxalate kidney stones, or have kidney disease, ask your doctor before taking concentrated beetroot products.

### Migraine

Nitrates, such as heart medicines and food additives, are common headache triggers [52]. People with migraine carried more nitrate-reducing mouth bacteria in one cohort, an association that does not show cause [52]. If you get migraines, it may be worth starting with a small dose and stopping if headaches get worse.

### Methemoglobinemia

Methemoglobin is a form of hemoglobin that cannot bind oxygen [2]. In a large hospital review, dapsone was the most common cause of acquired methemoglobinemia; other causes included benzocaine, nitrites and, rarely, food contaminated with nitrates [2]. Vegetable-induced methemoglobinemia has been reported [54], and dietary nitrate has long been linked to methemoglobinemia in infants [17]. Nitrate poisoning is still reported in babies fed home-made vegetable foods [63]. Do not give beetroot supplements or concentrated beetroot juice to infants or young children.

The acceptable daily intake (ADI) for nitrate is 3.7 mg/kg body weight per day [3]. The evidence behind that limit is conflicting: EFSA judged that methemoglobin made from nitrate converted in saliva would not be clinically significant, yet, despite the uncertainty, found insufficient evidence to withdraw the ADI [3]. The median nitrate intake in healthy people is 108 mg a day [55]. Hypothetical DASH-style diets supplied from 174 to 1222 mg of nitrate, depending on the vegetables chosen [17]. If you take dapsone or use benzocaine, or already eat a lot of nitrate, ask your doctor before taking concentrated beetroot products.

### N-Nitroso Compounds and Cancer Risk

Nitrate can lead to N-nitroso compounds (NOCs), which are carcinogenic in many animal species [7]. In 29 healthy recreationally active volunteers, beetroot juice raised urinary NOCs after one dose and further after a week of daily doses; vitamin C blunted the rise after one dose but not after a week [7]. In a later trial of 231 participants with mild hypertension, 300 mg nitrate daily for 5 weeks, from leafy greens or potassium nitrate, did not raise urinary N-nitrosamines [8]. That trial had no beetroot arm, so the evidence is conflicting. A systematic review urged caution, because drinking beetroot juice may easily push nitrate intake above the ADI [6]. Nitrate from vegetables may form fewer NOCs than nitrate added during food processing or found in contaminated water [56].

### Thyroid Function

In a cell study of the human iodide transporter, nitrate competed with iodide, but perchlorate was 240 times more potent [57]. There is no good public trial evidence on beetroot and thyroid function. If you have a thyroid condition, ask your doctor first.

### Diving and Breath-Hold Activities

Two US Air Force trainees had hypoxic events during breath-hold swims after taking beetroot powder [5]. The authors suggest that vasodilation from beetroot may counter the body's normal response to breath-hold diving and raise the risk of blackout [5]. If you free dive or train with breath-holds, it may be safer to skip beetroot supplements beforehand.

### Azetidine-2-Carboxylic Acid (Aze) and Nerve Damage

Garden beets contain azetidine-2-carboxylic acid (Aze), which can be built into proteins in place of proline [58]. In mice, 600 mg/kg of Aze damaged the cells that make myelin [59]. The authors propose that Aze may play a part in multiple sclerosis, a hypothesis not tested in people [59]. Whether the small amounts in beets matter for humans is not known.

### Insulin Effects

In older adults, 20 g daily of beetroot extract for 12 weeks lowered insulin levels [22]. If you have diabetes or take medicines that lower blood sugar, ask your doctor first.

### Heavy Metal Contamination

In a study of 37 beetroot-based supplements, 5 were contaminated with cadmium at 4-134% of the provisional tolerable monthly intake, while lead was not detected [60]. Choose a product that an independent lab has tested.

### Allergic Reactions

Allergy to beet is very rare; the few reports include asthma from inhaling the vapor of cooked beet [61]. If you get a rash or itching after beetroot, stop taking it.

## 6. Drug Interactions

Beetroot nitrate lowers blood pressure through nitric oxide [15][1], so its main interaction risk is with medicines that also lower blood pressure.

| Drug Class | Examples | Interaction | Clinical Implication |
|-----------|---------|------|----------|
| Blood pressure medicines | ACE inhibitors, ARBs, calcium channel blockers, diuretics | Possible added blood pressure lowering; trials in treated patients disagree [1][35] | Monitor blood pressure; do not stop or change a prescribed medicine without your doctor |
| PDE5 inhibitors | Sildenafil, tadalafil | No public studies with beetroot; tadalafil amplifies the effect of the body's own nitric oxide ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)) | Ask your doctor or pharmacist first |
| Blood thinners | Warfarin, aspirin | Dietary nitrate reduced platelet aggregation in laboratory tests; no clinical interaction studies [15] | Ask your doctor or pharmacist first |
| Medicines that can cause methemoglobinemia | Dapsone, benzocaine | The most frequent causes of acquired methemoglobinemia [2] | Ask your doctor before taking concentrated beetroot products |
| Diabetes medicines | Metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin | Beetroot extract lowered insulin levels in one trial [22] | Ask your doctor first |

### Additional Considerations

- Antibacterial mouthwash is not a drug, but it blunts the conversion of nitrate to nitrite and the rise in plasma nitrite [26][27].
- If you take several blood pressure medicines, ask your doctor before adding beetroot supplements.
- Beetroot does not replace prescribed blood pressure medicine. Never stop or change a medicine without your doctor.
- Beetroot supplements on top of a vegetable-rich diet may push total nitrate intake above the ADI [6][17].
- If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 7. Dietary Sources

Beetroot is one of several nitrate-rich vegetables. Approximately 80% of dietary nitrates are derived from vegetable consumption [17].

### Nitrate Content of Common Foods

Brad cites a worldwide meta-analysis that found approximately 4,825 mg of nitrate per kg in rocket (arugula), against about 3,000 mg/kg in beetroot ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)). In a trial in healthy adults, drinks made from rocket or spinach, each with 800 mg nitrate, lowered blood pressure like beetroot juice [18]. A serving of fresh beetroot gave more nitrate than most daily doses of beetroot supplements [21].

### Practical Notes on Dietary Nitrate

- **Cooking:** Brad's advice is to eat nitrate-rich vegetables raw or lightly cooked, because long boiling reduces nitrate (Brad's current guidance, 10 Aug 2026).
- **Vegetables lower blood pressure too:** a week of high-nitrate vegetables lowered mean resting systolic pressure (in mmHg) from 107 to 103 in healthy young women [62].
- **Typical intake:** median nitrate intake is about 108 mg a day [55].
- **Processed meat is not the same:** vegetable nitrate limits N-nitroso compound formation, unlike nitrate added during food processing [56].
- **Store juice cold:** home-made juice loses its nitrate within days at room temperature [24].

### Whole Beets vs. Supplements

A serving of fresh beetroot gave more nitrate than most daily doses of beetroot supplements in one analysis [21]. Brad's view is that whole foods are better than supplements: if you want the benefits of beets, eat beets ([Brad, 25 Feb 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-truth-about-nitric-oxide-supplements-nitralis)). His own choice is rocket, which is low in oxalate ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/this-2-food-beats-every-nitric-oxide-supplement)).
