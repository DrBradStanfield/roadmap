## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. NIH notes that a small number of studies suggest elderberry may relieve symptoms of flu, colds or other upper respiratory infections [3], and a 2020 rapid review of five clinical studies in 936 adults found it may shorten and ease cold and flu symptoms when taken within 48 hours of the first symptoms [28]. A 2004 trial in 60 adults gave 15 mL of elderberry syrup four times a day for 5 days and found symptoms eased on average 4 days earlier than with placebo [24], but a 2020 trial in 87 people aged 5 and over with confirmed flu found no evidence that elderberry shortened or eased the illness [15]. There is no good evidence that it helps COVID-19 [3]. Raw or unripe elderberries, leaves and stems contain cyanide-producing substances, which can cause nausea, vomiting and severe diarrhea, and cooking eliminates the toxin; NIH advises anyone who takes medicine to talk with their health care provider first, and little is known about safety in pregnancy or breastfeeding [3].

## Key safety points

- Raw or unripe elderberries, leaves and stems contain cyanide-producing substances, which can cause nausea, vomiting and severe diarrhea; large amounts may cause serious illness, cooking eliminates the toxin, and leaves or stems should not be crushed into juice [3][18]. If someone becomes very drowsy or hard to wake, faint, or weak, dizzy or numb after eating or drinking them, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department [18].
- If you take any medicine, talk with your health care provider before using elderberry; some herbs and medicines interact in harmful ways [3]. A woman taking elderberry with the cancer drug pazopanib developed grade 3 liver injury; the case report's authors proposed an interaction [46].
- Little is known about whether elderberry is safe in pregnancy or while breastfeeding, and no reliable evidence supports its use then [3][28].
- Case reports have linked elderberry products to acute pancreatitis and to autoimmune hepatitis [38][42].
- Some people with summer hay fever are allergic to elderberry, with eye and nose symptoms or shortness of breath [39]. For trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or fainting, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.
- In clinical studies, side effects were rare and none were serious [28].

## 1. Overview

Elderberry is the dark purple berry of the black (European) elder tree (*Sambucus nigra*), which grows in Europe, North America, Western Asia and North Africa [3]. Three subspecies now supply elderberry foods and supplements: the European *S. nigra* ssp. *nigra*, the American *S. nigra* ssp. *canadensis* and the blue elderberry *S. nigra* ssp. *cerulea* [56].

The berries carry anthocyanins and other phenolics, and berries picked at higher altitude held more anthocyanins [8]. The fruit also contains a lectin, *Sambucus nigra* agglutinin-III (SNA-III) [7].

Elderberry has been used in folk medicine to treat colds and flu [3].

NIH notes that only a small number of studies have tested elderberry for colds, flu and other upper respiratory infections, and that little research in people has looked at its other uses [3]. Small trials suggest it may shorten flu symptoms, but a 2020 placebo-controlled trial found no benefit [15][24]. In a trial among air travellers it did not significantly cut the number of colds [21], and a trial in postmenopausal women found no change in heart disease risk markers [16].

**Important safety note:** Raw or unripe elderberries and other parts of the elder tree, such as the leaves and stem, contain cyanide-producing substances, which can cause nausea, vomiting and severe diarrhea; cooking eliminates this toxin [3]. Large quantities may cause serious illness [3]. If someone becomes very drowsy or hard to wake, faint, or weak, dizzy or numb after eating or drinking them, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department [18].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Common Supplement Forms

Elderberry supplements come in several forms:

| Form | Description | Typical Standardization | Notes |
|------|-------------|------------------------|-------|
| Liquid syrup | Elderberry extract in a syrup, often sweetened | Varies | Form used in the Norwegian flu trial [24]; Sambucol was tested in an earlier trial [14]. |
| Extract capsules | Capsules of dried extract | Varies | A standardised, membrane-filtered extract was used in the air-travel cold trial [21]. |
| Lozenges | Extract in a lozenge | Varies | Included with extracts in a review of clinical studies [28]. |
| Gummies | Chewable elderberry preparations | Varies widely | Popular consumer format; limited clinical data. |
| Effervescent tablets | Dissolvable tablets, often with added vitamins or minerals | Varies | Limited clinical data. |
| Powder | Dried and powdered elderberry | Varies | Can be mixed into food or drinks. |

### Key Bioactive Compounds

Elder berries contain anthocyanins and other phenolics, whose levels varied with the altitude where the plants grew [8]. They also contain the lectin SNA-III, which binds sugars that carry galactose and N-acetylgalactosamine [7]. The berries hold less cyanogenic glycoside than the leaves or flowers [8].

### Sambucol: The Most-Studied Formulation

Sambucol is a standardised black elderberry extract [29]. It was the product given in a placebo-controlled trial during an influenza B outbreak in a kibbutz, and in lab studies of influenza viruses and immune cells [14][29].

### Standardization Challenges

Products are standardised in different ways, which makes direct comparison difficult. The plant itself also varies: in one study, anthocyanins and cyanogenic glycosides in elder berries changed with the altitude and location of the growing site [8].

### Bioavailability Considerations

In a 12-week trial, anthocyanins from an elderberry extract did not build up in the blood, though their metabolism after a dose increased [16].

### How to Take

In the Norwegian flu trial, adults took elderberry syrup four times a day for 5 days, when symptoms had lasted 48 hours or less [24].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Influenza (Flu) Treatment

Most elderberry trials have tested whether it shortens or eases flu. The results are mixed: earlier small trials found benefit, and a 2020 trial found none.

**Positive trial 1, influenza B outbreak in a kibbutz:** In a placebo-controlled, double-blind trial, symptoms including fever improved within 2 days in 93.3% of people taking Sambucol, while 91.7% of the placebo group improved within 6 days. Nearly 90% of the Sambucol group were fully cured within 2 to 3 days, against at least 6 days with placebo [14].

**Positive trial 2, Norway (n=60):** In a randomised, double-blind trial of 60 adults aged 18 to 54 years with flu-like symptoms for 48 hours or less, 15 mL of elderberry syrup four times a day for 5 days relieved symptoms on average 4 days earlier than placebo and reduced the use of rescue medicines [24]. The authors called for a larger study to confirm the result.

**Negative trial, United States (n=87):** In an investigator-initiated, double-blind trial of 87 emergency room patients aged 5 and over with confirmed flu and symptoms for less than 48 hours, elderberry extract did **not** shorten or ease the illness. Days to mild symptoms averaged 4.9 with placebo and 5.3 with elderberry, and days to complete resolution averaged 8.7 and 8.6. A post hoc analysis suggested that people taking elderberry alone, without oseltamivir (Tamiflu), did 2 days worse than those taking placebo alone. Patients over 12 years took 15 mL (5.7 g) four times a day and children aged 5 to 12 years took 15 mL twice a day, for 5 days [15].

**Meta-analysis evidence:** A 2019 meta-analysis that included 180 participants found that elderberry substantially reduced upper respiratory symptoms, with a large mean effect size [27]. A 2020 rapid review of five clinical studies in 936 adults found that elderberry taken within 48 hours of the first symptoms may make cold and flu symptoms shorter and milder, while its effect on cough was unclear [28]. A 2017 review found too few studies comparing elderberry with standard antiviral medicines to make detailed recommendations [43].

**Synthesis:** The positive flu trials were small, and the 2020 trial found no benefit. The evidence for elderberry treating flu symptoms is weak and inconsistent.

### Common Cold Treatment and Prevention

**Travel cold study, Australia (n=312):** In a randomised, double-blind trial of 312 economy-class passengers flying from Australia, a standardised elderberry extract did not significantly reduce the number of colds (12 with elderberry and 17 with placebo, p = 0.4). Among those who caught a cold, the placebo group had more cold days (117 vs 57, p = 0.02) and higher symptom scores (583 vs 247, p = 0.05) [21].

**Synthesis:** The one prevention trial found no significant drop in colds [21]. The same trial suggests elderberry may shorten colds and ease symptoms in people who catch one, and its authors called for more research.

### Immune System Modulation

**In vitro evidence:** In immune cells from 12 healthy donors, Sambucol products raised production of the inflammatory cytokines IL-1 beta, TNF-alpha, IL-6 and IL-8, by 2 to 45 fold for the extract, with the largest rise in TNF-alpha (44.9 fold) [29]. Sambucol also reduced hemagglutination and blocked replication of several human influenza A and B strains in cell culture [14], and elderberry flavonoids bound to H1N1 virus and blocked infection [30].

**Anti-inflammatory effects:** In a 2-year placebo-controlled trial, Inflaminat, a herbal mix of black elder berries, violet tricolor herb and calendula flowers, slowed thickening of the carotid artery wall [31]. Because it is a combination, the result cannot be credited to elderberry alone.

**Clinical relevance:** The immune effects seen in lab studies have not been shown to give clinical benefits beyond the small flu and cold trials above. The rise in cytokines has also raised theoretical safety concerns (see COVID-19 section below).

### COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)

Elderberry has been promoted for COVID-19, but there is no good evidence to support this use [3], and a 2020 review found no evidence for treating or preventing COVID-19 with it [28].

Some authors have raised concern that elderberry may overstimulate the immune system and raise the risk of a cytokine storm [55], the immune overreaction seen in some severe COVID-19 cases [33]. The concern grew from lab work showing that Sambucol raised inflammatory cytokine production [29]. A 2022 review found not yet enough evidence to support it, but called for more research in COVID-19 patients [55].

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and Federal Trade Commission have taken action against companies that marketed elderberry products with unsubstantiated claims of effectiveness for COVID-19 [3].

### Cardiovascular and Metabolic Effects

**Cardiovascular, negative trial:** In a randomised, placebo-controlled trial of 52 healthy women past menopause (mean age 58.2), 500 mg a day of anthocyanins as cyanidin glycosides from elderberry, taken as 2 capsules twice a day for 12 weeks, did **not** change markers of heart disease risk, including inflammatory markers, platelet reactivity, blood fats and glucose. Liver and kidney function stayed within clinically acceptable ranges [16].

**Metabolic, emerging evidence:** In a crossover trial in which 18 adults with overweight or obesity completed both arms [35], drinking elderberry juice twice a day for a week lowered blood glucose after a test meal, raised fat oxidation and shifted gut bacteria, with more Firmicutes, Actinobacteria and Bifidobacterium [34]. An earlier pilot of the same design, with 9 people analysed, found no difference in glucose or insulin response, and fat oxidation was higher after placebo [53]. Larger and longer trials are needed to confirm these findings [34].

**Thyroid effects:** Serum from the 18-person trial showed higher thyroxine (T4) after a week of elderberry juice than after placebo (49.3 vs 42.3 ng/mL), and higher thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) (0.104 vs 0.094 ng/mL). The authors linked this to a higher metabolic rate and called their work a preliminary analysis [35].

### Antioxidant Activity

Elderberry has high antioxidant activity in lab tests [8]. Lab antioxidant activity does not reliably predict benefit in people: the 12-week trial in postmenopausal women found no change in inflammatory markers [16].

### Antiviral Activity (Laboratory Studies)

Beyond the clinical trials described above, laboratory studies show that elderberry can block influenza viruses:

- Elderberry extract inhibited H1N1 infection in cell culture, and two of its flavonoids bound to the virus and blocked it from entering cells [30]
- Sambucol inhibited replication of several influenza A and B strains in cell culture [14]

These findings provide mechanistic plausibility for potential antiviral effects but have not been consistently demonstrated in human clinical trials.

## 4. Recommended Dosing

No official Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) or Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL) has been established for elderberry, as it is classified as a dietary supplement and food, not an essential nutrient.

### Dosing from Clinical Trials

| Indication | Dose | Duration | Form | Evidence Quality |
|------------|------|----------|------|:---:|
| Flu symptom relief (adults) | 15 mL elderberry syrup, 4 times daily | 5 days | Liquid syrup | Low (small trial) [24]; no benefit in a 2020 trial [15] |
| Flu symptom relief (children aged 5 to 12 years) | 15 mL elderberry extract, twice a day | 5 days | Liquid | No benefit found (single trial) [15] |
| Cardiovascular support | 500 mg anthocyanins from elderberry daily | 12 weeks | Capsules | No benefit demonstrated [16] |

### Practical Dosing Guidance

**For cold and flu symptom relief (most common use):** In the trials, adults took 15 mL of elderberry syrup four times a day for 5 days, when symptoms had lasted 48 hours or less [15][24].

**Important caveats:**
- The trials that found benefit started within 48 hours of symptom onset [24][28]
- A 2020 trial found no benefit, so efficacy is not established [15]
- Elderberry did not significantly reduce the number of colds in air travellers; it has been studied to shorten symptoms in people who are already ill [21]
- Do not use elderberry as a substitute for influenza vaccination or antiviral medications such as oseltamivir (Tamiflu)

### Children

Evidence in children is very limited. The 2020 trial gave children aged 5 to 12 years 15 mL twice a day and found no benefit [15]. Homemade preparations from raw or unripe berries carry the cyanide risk described in Section 5 [3].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Tolerability

In clinical studies, elderberry has generally been well tolerated: a 2020 review found adverse events were rare and none were serious [28], and a pilot juice trial found it well tolerated [53].

### Raw and Unprocessed Elderberry: Cyanide Risk

This is the most serious safety concern with elderberry. Raw or unripe elderberries and other parts of the elder tree, such as the leaves and stem, contain cyanide-producing substances; cooking eliminates this toxin [3]. These substances, cyanogenic glycosides, release hydrogen cyanide when enzymes break them down [54].

**Symptoms of raw elderberry poisoning:** Nausea, vomiting and severe diarrhea; large quantities may cause serious illness [3]. If someone becomes very drowsy or hard to wake, faint, or weak, dizzy or numb after eating raw or unripe elderberries, leaves or stems, or drinking juice made with them, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department [18].

**Case example:** In 1984 the CDC reported an outbreak in California after wild elderberries were crushed with their leaves and branches to make juice [18]. Within 15 minutes of drinking it, 11 of the 25 people at a gathering began to have nausea and vomiting [18]. Eight were flown to hospital with nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps and weakness, some also dizzy and numb, and one was stuporous and kept overnight; all recovered quickly [18]. The report advised against crushing leaves and stems into juice [18].

**Toxic plant parts:** Elder leaves and flowers hold more cyanogenic glycosides than the berries [8]. In American elderberry, the stems and green berries had the highest cyanide-producing potential [54].

**Processing effectiveness:** Cooking eliminates the toxin [3]. A lab analysis found no measurable cyanogenic glycosides in commercial American elderberry juice [54].

### Acute Pancreatitis (Case Report)

A case report, "An Unusual Culprit of Drug-Induced Pancreatitis", described acute pancreatitis in a middle-aged man linked to the black elderberry product Sambucol [38]. A single case cannot prove cause, but it suggests rare reactions are possible.

### Allergic Reactions

Elderberry can cause allergy. Of 3,668 patients tested at random, 0.6% were sensitised to elderberry, and some people with summer hay fever react to elder flowers or elderberry products with eye and nose symptoms (rhinoconjunctivitis) or shortness of breath [39]. The main allergen did not cross-react with birch, grass or mugwort pollen [39]. For trouble breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or fainting, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

### Thyroid Effects

A week of elderberry juice raised T4 and TSH in a small trial in adults with overweight or obesity [35]. Its meaning for people on thyroid medicine is unknown. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Autoimmune Disease Concerns

A case report described autoimmune hepatitis in a patient on long-term elderberry-containing supplements and proposed a probable link [42]. Because lab studies show elderberry can raise inflammatory cytokines [29], people with an autoimmune disease may want to talk to their doctor before using it.

### Pregnancy and Breastfeeding

Little is known about whether it is safe to use elderberry during pregnancy or while breastfeeding [3]. A 2020 review found no reliable evidence to support its use in pregnant or breastfeeding women [28]. Talk to your doctor before using it at these times.

### Children

One placebo-controlled trial that included children aged 5 and over found no benefit [15]. Homemade preparations from raw or unripe berries carry the cyanide risk described above [3]. Ask your child's doctor before using elderberry.

### Cytokine Storm Concern (Theoretical)

Lab work showing that Sambucol raises inflammatory cytokine production by immune cells [29] has raised a theoretical concern about elderberry in severe viral infections where a cytokine storm is a risk [55], such as severe COVID-19 [33]. A 2022 review found not yet enough evidence to support this concern, but called for more research in COVID-19 patients [55]. The question remains unresolved.

## 6. Drug Interactions

### CYP450 Enzyme Inhibition

Small lab studies suggest elderberry extracts weakly inhibit CYP3A4, a liver enzyme that breaks down many drugs [46]. In a lab study of six herbs used in pregnancy, the authors named fennel, horsetail and raspberry leaf, not black elderberry, as possible clinically relevant inhibitors of CYP1A2, CYP2D6 or CYP3A4 [45].

A middle-aged woman who had taken elderberry supplements for years developed intense nausea, loose stools and grade 3 liver injury, with a fivefold rise in liver enzymes, by the fourth week of the cancer drug pazopanib, which CYP3A4 breaks down. Her liver enzymes returned to normal after all her medicines were stopped, and pazopanib was later restarted without the problem coming back. The authors proposed an interaction between elderberry and pazopanib [46].

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Immunosuppressant Drugs

If you take medicines that suppress the immune system, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Thyroid Hormones

Elderberry juice raised T4 and TSH in one small study [35]; its meaning for people on thyroid medicine is unknown. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Chemotherapy Drugs

How elderberry interacts with cancer medicines is unknown, and the authors of the pazopanib case called for clinical studies in people with cancer [46]. Talk to your oncologist before using elderberry during cancer treatment.

### Ulcerative Colitis Medications

If you take medicines for ulcerative colitis or other prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### General Recommendation

If you take any type of medicine, talk with your health care provider before using elderberry; some herbs and medicines interact in harmful ways [3].

## 7. Dietary Sources

### Fresh Elderberries

Raw elderberries must be cooked before eating: cooking eliminates the cyanide-producing substances in raw or unripe berries [3].

### Nutritional Profile (per 100 g raw elderberries)

USDA FoodData Central lists the nutrient content of raw elderberries [51].

### Prepared Elderberry Foods

Elderberry is commonly consumed in the following prepared forms:

- **Elderberry syrup:** Cooked berries concentrated into a sweetened syrup.
- **Elderberry wine:** A traditional European fermented drink.
- **Jams, jellies, and preserves:** Made from cooked berries combined with sugar and pectin.
- **Elderberry juice:** Juice from cooked berries. Can be diluted with water.
- **Baked goods:** Elderberries baked into pies, cakes, muffins, and pancakes.
- **Elderflower products:** The creamy-white flowers of the elder tree are used to make cordials and teas (elderflower-based products are distinct from elderberry supplements).

### Preparation Safety

All elderberry preparations should follow these safety principles:

- **Always cook elderberries** before eating; cooking eliminates the toxin [3]
- **Remove stems** before processing: in American elderberry, the stems and green berries had the highest cyanide-producing potential [54]
- **Never consume** raw or unripe elderberries, leaves or stems [3]

### Foraging and Growing

The leaves, stems and unripe berries of wild elder contain cyanide-producing substances, so foragers should cook any berries they pick before eating them [3].
