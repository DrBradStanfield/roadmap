## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. Red yeast rice can contain monacolin K, which is structurally identical to the statin lovastatin, and products with considerable amounts of it may lower blood cholesterol [4]. In a trial in nearly 5,000 Chinese patients with a previous heart attack, the red yeast rice extract Xuezhikang lowered major coronary events from 10.4% to 5.7% over an average of 4.5 years [9], but in a 2023 trial that compared six supplements with low-dose rosuvastatin, red yeast rice did not lower LDL cholesterol significantly more than placebo [71]. Monacolin K content varies widely between products and is rarely on the label, and some products reach the range of prescription lovastatin doses [4][5][7]. Products with significant monacolin K can have the same muscle, kidney and liver side effects and drug interactions as statins, and some contain citrinin, a toxin that can damage the kidneys [4].

## Key safety points

- Do not take it with a prescription statin, because monacolin K is identical to lovastatin [4][7].
- Avoid it with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors such as itraconazole or clarithromycin, and with gemfibrozil, cyclosporine or grapefruit juice; other fibrates and niacin at 1 g a day or more need caution [72]. On warfarin, check with your doctor first [73].
- It is not recommended in pregnancy or breastfeeding [4][72].
- It can injure the liver; lovastatin is ruled out in active liver disease [4][53][72].
- Statin-type muscle damage is possible, including rhabdomyolysis, which can be life-threatening [7][48][72]. Stop it and see your doctor promptly about unexplained muscle pain or weakness [72]; if you also feel very unwell, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department [48].
- Some products contain citrinin, which can damage the kidneys [4]. Separately, tablets in Japan in 2024 were contaminated with a different toxin, puberulic acid, and were linked to serious kidney injury [62][74].
- It may cause diarrhea, nausea or stomach pain [4].

## 1. Overview

Red yeast rice is rice fermented by a fungus, usually *Monascus purpureus*, which is technically a mold rather than a yeast [4]. In China and its neighbors it is used as a food colorant and in wine making [6], and as a traditional medicine for blood circulation stasis, indigestion and diarrhea [3].

Depending on the strain and the fermentation conditions, the fungus can enrich the rice with monacolins, including monacolin K, which is structurally identical to the medicine lovastatin [4]. Like other statins, lovastatin slows the body's production of cholesterol [4]. More than 101 compounds have been isolated from red yeast rice, mainly monacolins, pigments, organic acids, sterols and flavonoids [3].

Red yeast rice contains 14 monacolins that inhibit cholesterol synthesis in the liver [8].

Dose is the key difference from prescription lovastatin. In 26 US brands, the monacolin K consumed per day at the labeled serving would range from 0.09 to 10.94 mg [5], and EFSA concluded that supplements could give monacolin K within the range of therapeutic lovastatin doses [7].

According to the FDA, red yeast rice products with enhanced or added lovastatin cannot be sold as dietary supplements in the United States, because lovastatin was approved as a drug before it was marketed as a food or supplement [4]. Labels usually do not state the monacolin K content, and some researchers have reported commercial lovastatin added illegally to red yeast rice [4][6].

The strongest outcome evidence comes from the China Coronary Secondary Prevention Study (CCSPS), which tested Xuezhikang, a partially purified red yeast rice extract [9].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Standard Red Yeast Rice

Most supplements are capsules of fermented rice powder, and their strength varies widely. In 2017, none of 28 US brands listed monacolin K on the label; two had none detectable, and in the other 26 it ranged more than 60-fold, from 0.09 to 5.48 mg per 1,200 mg of red yeast rice [5].

The content depends on the fungal strain and the fermentation conditions [4].

### Xuezhikang

Xuezhikang is a partially purified extract of red yeast rice made in China, and it was the product tested in CCSPS [9]. In Chinese trials it was given at 1.2 g a day, containing 10 mg of lovastatin [31].

### Ankascin 568-R Plus

Ankascin 568 plus is a different kind of product: an extract of *Monascus purpureus* NTU 568 that contains the pigments monascin and ankaflavin [12]. In a 12-week, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 40 adults with borderline high cholesterol, one 500 mg capsule a day lowered total cholesterol by 11.9% and LDL cholesterol by 19.0% from baseline after 4 weeks [12]. This is one small trial.

### Red Yeast Rice + CoQ10 Combinations

Some products combine red yeast rice with coenzyme Q10 (CoQ10). In a trial in 104 adults with metabolic syndrome, a once-daily tablet with 10 mg of monacolin K and 30 mg of CoQ10, added to diet for 2 months, lowered LDL cholesterol by 21.8% relative to diet alone [21]. The result belongs to the combination, not to either ingredient alone.

### Red Yeast Rice + Nattokinase Combinations

In a 6-month trial in 47 adults with high blood lipids, nattokinase combined with red yeast rice extract (two capsules twice daily, each with 300 mg of extract) lowered LDL cholesterol by 41% and total cholesterol by 25%, while nattokinase alone showed no effect until month six [14]. In a 90-day trial in 178 people with stable coronary artery disease, nattokinase plus red yeast rice (9.0 mg of monacolins a day) lowered triglycerides, total cholesterol and diastolic blood pressure, and raised HDL cholesterol, more than either one alone or placebo [15].

### Key Bioavailability Considerations

Monacolin K in its lactone form is identical to lovastatin [7].

Lovastatin is broken down by the enzyme CYP3A4, so drugs that block this enzyme can raise its blood levels (see Section 6) [72].

In a study comparing three red yeast rice products with lovastatin tablets, lovastatin dissolved faster from the red yeast rice products, and volunteers taking one of them (LipoCol Forte) absorbed more lovastatin and its active acid form than those taking lovastatin tablets [18].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Cholesterol and Lipid Lowering

Randomized trials and meta-analyses show LDL cholesterol falls with red yeast rice, though products and doses differ between trials [25].

**Foundational trial, Heber 1999.** In a 12-week double-blind trial in 83 US adults with high cholesterol on a low-fat diet, a proprietary red yeast rice supplement at 2.4 g a day lowered mean total cholesterol from 254 to 208 mg/dL by week 8, compared with placebo [19]. It also lowered LDL cholesterol and triglycerides; HDL cholesterol did not change significantly [19].

**Combined with lifestyle changes, Becker 2008.** In an open-label trial in 74 adults, 12 weeks of lifestyle changes, red yeast rice and fish oil lowered LDL cholesterol by 42.4%, similar to 39.6% with simvastatin 40 mg a day [20]. That result belongs to the whole regimen, not to red yeast rice alone.

**Combined with diet, Mazza 2018.** In the red yeast rice and CoQ10 trial above, blood glucose and blood pressure also fell more than with diet alone [21].

**Low dose, Minamizuka 2021.** In 18 Japanese adults with mild dyslipidemia on diet therapy, 200 mg a day of red yeast rice containing 2 mg of monacolin K for 8 weeks lowered LDL cholesterol more than diet alone (median change -0.96 vs -0.20 mmol/L) [23]. Total cholesterol, apolipoprotein B and blood pressure also fell. The trial was small and had no placebo group [23].

**Meta-analysis, 2024 (14 trials).** A meta-analysis of 14 double-blind trials lasting 4 to 24 weeks found that red yeast rice extract lowered total cholesterol by a mean of 37.43 mg/dL (95% CI -47.08 to -27.79) and LDL cholesterol by 35.82 mg/dL (95% CI -43.36 to -28.29), but did not change HDL cholesterol or triglycerides [25].

**Compared with a statin, 2023.** In a 28-day trial that 190 adults completed, rosuvastatin 5 mg a day lowered LDL cholesterol more than placebo and six supplements, and none of the supplements, red yeast rice included, lowered it significantly compared with placebo [71].

**When combined with other medications.** A woman with familial hypercholesterolemia who could not take any statin missed her LDL cholesterol target on a PCSK9 inhibitor and ezetimibe; adding a red yeast rice product with 3 mg of monacolin K gave very good LDL control [26].

**Adding nattokinase to red yeast rice** lowered total cholesterol and triglycerides more than red yeast rice alone in the 2024 trial of 178 people with stable coronary artery disease [15].

**Adding plant sterols to red yeast rice.** In 187 people taking red yeast rice 1,800 mg twice daily, adding phytosterol tablets (900 mg twice daily) gave no further fall in LDL cholesterol at 12, 24 or 52 weeks [27]. People who also joined a 12-week lifestyle program were 2.3 times more likely to reach an LDL cholesterol less than 100 mg/dL and lost more weight over the year (-2.3 vs -0.3 kg) [27].

### Cardiovascular Outcomes and Mortality

The most important evidence for red yeast rice comes from studies of heart attacks and deaths rather than cholesterol levels alone.

**China Coronary Secondary Prevention Study (CCSPS).** This multicenter trial randomly assigned nearly 5,000 Chinese patients with a previous heart attack and average LDL cholesterol to Xuezhikang or placebo for an average of 4.5 years [9]. Results:

- Major coronary events (nonfatal heart attack or death from coronary heart disease) fell from 10.4% to 5.7%, an absolute decrease of 4.7% and a relative decrease of 45% [9]
- Cardiovascular deaths fell by 30% and deaths from any cause by 33% [9]
- The need for coronary revascularization fell by a third [9]
- Total and LDL cholesterol and triglycerides fell, and HDL cholesterol rose [9]

**2022 meta-analysis.** A meta-analysis of randomized trials of red yeast rice preparations in metabolic syndrome found lower mortality (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.49 to 0.78) and fewer major adverse cardiovascular events (RR 0.54, 95% CI 0.43 to 0.66), with no increase in adverse reactions (RR 1.00, 95% CI 0.69 to 1.43) [11]. The authors noted heterogeneity between the studies [11].

**Limitation:** CCSPS was done in Chinese patients with one standardized extract, and supplements sold elsewhere differ widely in content [5][9].

### Tolerability in Statin-Intolerant Patients

A major interest in red yeast rice is its use by people who stopped statins because of muscle pain [30].

**Becker 2009.** In 62 people who had stopped statins because of muscle pain, red yeast rice 1,800 mg twice daily for 24 weeks, with a 12-week lifestyle program, lowered LDL cholesterol from baseline by 43 mg/dL at week 12 and by 35 mg/dL at week 24, against 11 mg/dL and 15 mg/dL with placebo [30]. Creatinine phosphokinase (CPK) levels and pain scores did not differ between the groups [30].

**Xue 2017.** In a 4-week pilot trial in 60 adults, red yeast rice 1,200 mg a day lowered lipids about as much as simvastatin 20 mg a day; fatigue scores rose with simvastatin but not with red yeast rice [31].

**Venero 2010.** In a chart review of 25 patients who had not tolerated other lipid-lowering drugs, red yeast rice lowered LDL cholesterol by 21% over an average of 74 days, and 92% tolerated it [32].

### Liver Cirrhosis Risk

In Taiwanese health insurance data on adults with newly diagnosed high cholesterol, liver cirrhosis was less common in 34,367 red yeast rice users than in 34,367 matched lovastatin users (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.57 to 0.63) [35]. Red yeast rice users also had less jaundice, ascites and hepatic coma after cirrhosis, and lower mortality (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.38 to 0.61) [35]. This was an observational study, and the authors did not claim cause and effect or that red yeast rice is better than lovastatin [35].

### Diabetes and Glucose Metabolism

In a US cohort, statin users had a higher risk of new-onset diabetes than nonusers (adjusted HR 2.20, 95% CI 1.35 to 3.58) [37].

**Large Taiwanese study.** Among 34,504 adults starting a prescription red yeast rice product (LipoCol Forte) and 34,504 matched adults starting lovastatin, diabetes occurred at 1.01 versus 2.59 per 100 person-years (adjusted HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.43 to 0.50), with a dose-response relationship [38]. This was an observational study.

**Metabolic syndrome benefits.** In the red yeast rice and CoQ10 trial, serum glucose fell by 3.4% relative to diet alone [21].

**2022 meta-analysis.** In metabolic syndrome trials, red yeast rice preparations lowered fasting plasma glucose (MD -0.46 mmol/L), HbA1c (MD -0.49) and insulin resistance measured by HOMA-IR (MD -0.93), with heterogeneity between studies [11].

### Anti-Inflammatory and Antioxidant Effects

**Human trial.** In a crossover trial in 25 adults with moderately high cholesterol, 4 weeks of a product combining 10 mg of monacolins with antioxidants changed hs-CRP by -2.33%, against 2.11% with placebo, and improved endothelial function [70]. The result belongs to the combination, and the trial was small and short [70].

**Preclinical data.** Laboratory studies report anti-atherosclerotic, anti-diabetic and anti-hypertensive activity for red yeast rice extracts and compounds, but further studies are needed [3].

### Blood Pressure

In the red yeast rice and CoQ10 trial, systolic blood pressure fell by 5.2 mmHg against 3.0 mmHg with diet alone [21], and the low-dose Minamizuka trial also found lower blood pressure [23]. A 2022 meta-analysis found a fall in mean arterial pressure (MD -3.79 mmHg) [11].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Evidence-Based Dose Ranges

The following table summarizes the doses used in key trials:

| Study | Daily dose | Length |
|-------|------|:---:|
| Heber 1999 [19] | Red yeast rice 2.4 g | 12 weeks |
| Xuezhikang in Chinese trials [31] | 1.2 g, containing 10 mg of lovastatin | Varied |
| Becker 2009 [30] | Red yeast rice 1,800 mg twice daily | 24 weeks |
| Becker 2013 [27] | Red yeast rice 1,800 mg twice daily | 52 weeks |
| Xue 2017 [31] | Red yeast rice 1,200 mg | 4 weeks |
| Minamizuka 2021 [23] | Red yeast rice 200 mg, containing 2 mg of monacolin K | 8 weeks |
| Mazza 2018 [21] | One tablet with 10 mg of monacolin K and 30 mg of CoQ10 | 2 months |
| Liu 2024 [15] | 9.0 mg of monacolins (with or without nattokinase) | 90 days |

**Typical effective dose range:** There is no standard dose, because products differ so much in monacolin K content [5]. Where trials stated the content, they gave 2 to 10 mg of monacolin K a day [21][23].

**Minimum effective dose:** 200 mg a day containing 2 mg of monacolin K lowered LDL cholesterol in one small trial of 18 people [23].

**Xuezhikang dose:** Xuezhikang, the extract tested in CCSPS, has the strongest outcome evidence [9]. In Chinese trials it was given at 1.2 g a day, containing 10 mg of lovastatin [31].

### Expert Panel Recommendations

The International Lipid Expert Panel published a position paper on red yeast rice in 2022 [43]. It stated that red yeast rice preparations have been shown to be safe and effective for lowering LDL cholesterol and cardiovascular events, but said that conventional drugs with the strongest evidence for event reduction should always be preferred [43].

**Who should not take it:** The lovastatin label rules lovastatin out with hypersensitivity to it, active liver disease, and pregnancy or breastfeeding [72]. People with severe kidney disease or a muscle disease, and children and teenagers, should check with their doctor before using red yeast rice.

### European Regulatory Limit

A 2018 scientific opinion from the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) judged monacolins from red yeast rice supplements a significant safety concern at 10 mg a day, noted severe reactions at intakes down to 3 mg a day, and found no intake it could call safe [7]. A 2019 meta-analysis of trials took a different view: it suggested that red yeast rice is generally tolerable and safe [4][64].

### How to Take Red Yeast Rice

- **Do not combine it with a prescription statin.** Monacolin K is the same compound as lovastatin [4][7].
- **Tell your doctor.** Red yeast rice can have the same side effects and drug interactions as statins [4].

### Product Selection

Labels rarely state the monacolin K content, so you cannot know how much a product holds [4]. Key considerations:

- Strength can be far from the label: of 37 supplements sold in the European Union, nine held less monacolin K than labeled and 24 held more [69]
- Citrinin was found in all 37 of those products, and four labeled "citrinin free" were contaminated [69]
- Strength also varies widely between brands sold in the United States [5]

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

Red yeast rice products may cause digestive problems such as diarrhea, nausea and stomach pain [4]. Of 187 adverse reactions reported to the Netherlands pharmacovigilance centre, 33 were gastrointestinal [45]. In the Italian surveillance system, 12 of 55 reactions were gastrointestinal and 9 were skin reactions [47].

### Muscle Problems (Myopathy and Rhabdomyolysis)

Like statins, red yeast rice products with significant monacolin K can damage muscle [4]. Lovastatin occasionally causes myopathy (muscle pain, tenderness or weakness with creatine kinase above ten times the upper limit of normal), which sometimes takes the form of rhabdomyolysis, with or without kidney failure, and rare deaths have occurred [72]. EFSA concluded that monacolin K from red yeast rice could cause severe musculoskeletal effects, including rhabdomyolysis [7].

Of 187 reactions reported in the Netherlands, 64 were musculoskeletal [45]. In Italy, 19 of 55 reactions were muscle pain or a rise in creatine phosphokinase and one was rhabdomyolysis, and 13 cases needed hospital care [47]. The US CAERS database held 27 reports of rhabdomyolysis or myopathy [46].

A 2019 meta-analysis of 53 randomized trials with 8,535 people found no increase in muscle problems with red yeast rice (OR 0.94, 95% CI 0.53 to 1.65) [64].

**Severe case report.** A patient developed life-threatening rhabdomyolysis, with dangerously high potassium and liver injury, two days after starting an over-the-counter red yeast rice supplement, needed intensive care, and recovered fully [48].

Stop red yeast rice and see your doctor promptly about unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness, especially with fever or feeling unwell [72]. If muscle pain comes with dark urine or you feel very unwell, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

A case of anaphylaxis, a severe allergic reaction, to red yeast rice has been reported [49]. For swelling of the face, lips or throat, or trouble breathing, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

### Liver Injury (Hepatotoxicity)

Red yeast rice products with significant monacolin K can cause liver damage, as statins can [4]. In early trials, persistent rises in liver enzymes to more than 3 times the upper limit of normal occurred in 1.9% of adults taking lovastatin for at least a year, and rare fatal and non-fatal liver failure has been reported with statins, including lovastatin [72].

**Documented case reports include:**

- Symptomatic hepatitis linked to herbal red yeast rice, reported in 2008 [51]
- A 63-year-old woman who developed severe hepatitis after six months on a product combining guggulsterol and red yeast rice extract; her liver tests returned to normal after she stopped it, and she had earlier developed liver toxicity on lovastatin [52]
- A 64-year-old woman who developed acute hepatitis 6 weeks after starting a red yeast rice supplement; her liver enzymes fell after she stopped it and received intravenous methylprednisolone [53]
- Ten liver injury reports among 55 reactions in the Italian surveillance system [47]

The lovastatin label rules lovastatin out in active liver disease and urges caution with heavy alcohol use or past liver disease, so check with your doctor first if either applies to you [72].

### Myasthenia Gravis

A 2018 case report described myasthenia gravis worsening after red yeast rice use [56]. Statins have also been reported to bring on myasthenia gravis; in one report, two patients developed it 4 weeks after starting atorvastatin [75]. If you have myasthenia gravis, check with your doctor first.

### Sleep Disturbances

In a sleep laboratory study in 12 healthy people, lovastatin 40 mg increased time awake after falling asleep with continued use, while pravastatin did not [58]. Whether red yeast rice does the same has not been studied.

### Diabetes Risk

Statin users in a US cohort had a higher risk of new-onset diabetes (adjusted HR 2.20) [37]. In Taiwan, people starting a prescription red yeast rice product had a lower diabetes rate than matched people starting lovastatin (adjusted HR 0.46) [38]. Both were observational studies.

### Citrinin Contamination

Some red yeast rice products contain citrinin, a toxin that can damage the kidneys [4]. Citrinin also has liver-toxic, embryocidal and fetotoxic properties [59].

- In 37 supplements sold in the European Union, citrinin was found in all of them; a single product met the limit of 100 μg/kg introduced after April 2020, and four labeled "citrinin free" were contaminated [69]
- In 15 supplements on the Polish market, no citrinin was detected [59]

### The 2024 Japan Kobayashi Pharmaceutical Incident

From late March 2024, serious food poisoning was reported in Japan among people taking red yeast rice tablets to lower cholesterol [62]. The tablets contained puberulic acid, made by a *Penicillium* mold that had contaminated the factory [62]. In a nationwide survey of 192 affected patients, 94.1% had reduced kidney function (eGFR under 60 ml/min/1.73m2), often with Fanconi syndrome, and 87% still had an eGFR under 60 ml/min/1.73m2 at the last observation [74]. Citrinin was not produced by the strains involved [74].

### Special Populations

**Pregnancy and breastfeeding:** There are no studies of red yeast rice in pregnancy or breastfeeding, and it is not recommended [4]. The lovastatin label rules lovastatin out in pregnancy and in nursing mothers [72].

**Kidney disease:** Many people who developed rhabdomyolysis on lovastatin had kidney problems, often from long-standing diabetes, and the label says they merit closer monitoring [72]. Some red yeast rice products also contain citrinin, which can damage the kidneys [4].

### Monitoring Recommendations

The lovastatin label recommends liver enzyme tests before starting and as clinically indicated, and says periodic creatine kinase checks may be considered, though they may not prevent myopathy [72]. Reviewers of US adverse event reports advised clinicians to monitor red yeast rice users for muscle and liver safety [46]. Report unexplained muscle pain, tenderness or weakness, or yellowing of the skin or eyes, to your doctor promptly [72].

## 6. Drug Interactions

Red yeast rice products can have the same drug interactions as statins [4]. The lovastatin label lists the interactions below [72].

### Prescription Statins

Do not combine red yeast rice with a prescription statin: monacolin K is the same compound as lovastatin, so the doses add together [4][7].

### CYP3A4 Inhibitors (Increase Toxicity Risk)

Lovastatin is broken down by CYP3A4. Drugs that block this enzyme can raise lovastatin levels and so make muscle damage more likely [72]:

| Substance | Notes |
|-----------|-------|
| Itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole, voriconazole | Contraindicated with lovastatin [72] |
| Erythromycin, clarithromycin, telithromycin | Contraindicated with lovastatin [72] |
| HIV protease inhibitors and cobicistat-containing products | Contraindicated with lovastatin [72] |
| Nefazodone | Contraindicated with lovastatin [72] |
| Cyclosporine | Avoid with lovastatin [72] |
| Danazol, diltiazem, dronedarone, verapamil | Lovastatin limited to 20 mg a day; ask your doctor [72] |
| Amiodarone | Lovastatin limited to 40 mg a day; ask your doctor [72] |
| Colchicine, ranolazine | Myopathy, including rhabdomyolysis, reported or more likely with lovastatin [72] |
| Grapefruit juice | Avoid [72] |

### CYP3A4 Inducers (Reduce Effectiveness)

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Fibrates

Avoid combining lovastatin with gemfibrozil; other fibrates and niacin at 1 g a day or more need caution, because they can cause muscle damage on their own [72].

### Anticoagulants

Lovastatin has raised the effect of warfarin: two patients developed bleeding, and the report advised close monitoring of prothrombin time when the two are combined [73]. If you take warfarin, check with your doctor before starting red yeast rice.

### Blood Pressure Medications

Red yeast rice lowered blood pressure slightly in some trials [11][21]. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Alcohol

The lovastatin label urges caution in people who drink substantial amounts of alcohol [72].

### CoQ10 Depletion

Statins lowered blood CoQ10 levels in a meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials [76]. Whether red yeast rice does the same is less clear.

### Other Hepatotoxic Substances

Because red yeast rice can injure the liver [52][53], check with your doctor before combining it with other products that can harm the liver.

## 7. Dietary Sources

In East Asia, red yeast rice is used both as a food and as a traditional medicine [3][6].

### Traditional Food Uses

In China and its neighboring countries, red yeast rice is used as a food colorant, for dyeing and in wine making [6].

### Traditional Chinese Medicine Applications

In traditional Chinese medicine, red yeast rice has long been used to treat blood circulation stasis, indigestion, diarrhea and limb weakness [3].

### Food vs. Supplement Distinction

Common red yeast rice is used in food products, while functional red yeast rice is made for medicinal use [6]. Traditional red yeast rice may contain only trace amounts of monacolin K [4].
