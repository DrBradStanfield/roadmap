## 1. Overview

The term CLA (conjugated linoleic acid) covers a group of positional and geometric isomers of linoleic acid with conjugated double bonds, found mainly in milk, meat and their products from ruminant animals [10][53]. Two isomers matter most: cis-9, trans-11 (c9,t11; also called rumenic acid), the main form in the diet, and trans-10, cis-12 (t10,c12) [6][53].

Vaccenic acid, a trans fat made in the rumen, is converted in tissues to rumenic acid by delta-9 desaturase. In a controlled feeding study in 30 healthy people, this conversion averaged 19%, with large differences between people [16].

Commercial CLA supplements contain both main isomers, and trials often used a 50:50 blend of c9,t11 and t10,c12 [17][39][53]. In one children's trial the product was 80% CLA [39]. The t10,c12 isomer drives the effects on fat cells, and it is also the isomer that raised insulin resistance in obese men [6][8].

Measured CLA intake from food was 212 mg a day in men and 151 mg a day in women in one US study [7]. That is far below the 3 to 3.4 g a day used in most trials [8][17].

The human evidence for CLA is modest. A 2024 meta-analysis found small falls in body mass and fat mass, but the high-quality trials showed no fat loss, and a 2012 review of long-term trials judged the clinical relevance uncertain [11][12]. CLA has also been linked to insulin resistance, lower HDL cholesterol and rare cases of acute hepatitis [8][38][45].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### CLA Isomers

The two main isomers act differently, and their effects on body composition and growth appear to run through separate mechanisms [6].

**cis-9, trans-11 CLA (c9,t11; Rumenic Acid):** This is the main dietary form of CLA [6]. Its level in dairy and beef depends on what the cows or steers are fed [6]. People also make it from dietary vaccenic acid [16]. In a 3-month trial in 25 abdominally obese men, 3 g a day of purified c9,t11 CLA lowered insulin sensitivity by 15% compared with placebo [52].

**trans-10, cis-12 CLA (t10,c12):** In fat cells this isomer reduces lipid uptake by inhibiting lipoprotein lipase and stearoyl-CoA desaturase [6]. It is also the isomer linked to insulin resistance, lower HDL cholesterol, higher oxidative stress markers and, in mice, severe fatty liver [8][13][47].

### Supplement Forms

In one children's trial the product was 80% CLA in a 50:50 isomer blend [39]. CLA has been given as free fatty acids and as triacylglycerol; in a 1-year trial both forms lowered body fat mass compared with placebo [26].

### Bioavailability

In a 1-year trial of 180 overweight adults, body fat mass was 8.7% lower with the triacylglycerol form and 6.9% lower with the free fatty acid form than with placebo [26].

Vaccenic acid in food adds to the body's supply of rumenic acid: in one feeding study, about 19% of dietary vaccenic acid was converted [16].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Body Composition and Fat Loss

CLA is marketed for fat loss. The effects in human trials are small and inconsistent.

**Large-scale meta-analyses:** A 2024 meta-analysis of 70 randomized trials (4,159 adults) found that CLA lowered body mass by 0.35 kg (95% CI -0.54 to -0.15), fat mass by 0.44 kg (95% CI -0.66 to -0.23) and body fat percentage by 0.77% (95% CI -1.09 to -0.45), and raised fat-free mass by 0.27 kg (95% CI 0.09 to 0.45) [12]. In the high-quality trials, CLA did not change fat mass or body fat percentage, and the authors judged the weight effect too small to matter clinically [12].

**Long-term supplementation:** A 2007 meta-analysis of 18 trials found that, at a median dose of 3.2 g a day, CLA reduced fat mass by 0.09 kg a week more than placebo; the effect grew for up to 6 months and then leveled off by 2 years (Whigham et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2007) [25]. A 2012 review of 7 trials lasting at least 6 months found 0.70 kg more weight loss (95% CI -1.09 to -0.32) and 1.33 kg more fat loss (95% CI -1.79 to -0.86) than placebo, and judged the clinical relevance uncertain (Onakpoya et al., *Eur J Nutr*, 2012) [11].

**Six-month RCT:** In 118 overweight and obese adults, 3.4 g a day of CLA for 6 months lowered body fat mass by 3.4% more than placebo, mostly in the legs, and was well tolerated (Gaullier et al., *Br J Nutr*, 2007) [44]. A 1-year trial in 180 overweight adults also found lower body fat mass with CLA than with placebo [26].

**Chinese RCT in overweight/obese individuals:** In 63 overweight and obese Chinese adults, 1.7 g of CLA twice daily in milk for 12 weeks lowered body weight, fat mass and waist-to-hip ratio from baseline (Chen et al., *Nutrition*, 2012) [27]. Total cholesterol, triglycerides, LDL cholesterol and fasting glucose rose and HDL cholesterol fell, but these changes were not significant [27].

**Dose:** In a 12-week dose-ranging trial in 60 overweight or obese adults, doses above 3.4 g a day gave no additional fat loss [56].

**Effect size in context:** Reviewers describe CLA's effect on fat as substantial in mice but modest in obese people, and too small to be sure of clinical value [11][12][15].

**Mechanisms:** Proposed mechanisms include reduced lipid uptake by fat cells through inhibition of lipoprotein lipase and stearoyl-CoA desaturase, more fat burning through carnitine palmitoyltransferase-1, and effects on PPARgamma and UCP-1 [6][33].

### Inflammation

CLA has mixed effects on inflammatory markers.

**Meta-analysis of inflammatory markers:** A 2023 meta-analysis of 42 trials with 1,109 participants found that CLA lowered interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha) but slightly raised C-reactive protein (CRP), so it may act in both directions [14].

**Isomer-specific effects:** In 60 men with the metabolic syndrome, t10,c12 CLA raised CRP by 110% and a urinary marker of lipid peroxidation by 578% compared with placebo [13].

### Cancer

CLA has shown anti-cancer effects in animals, but human evidence comes from observational studies.

**Animal and in vitro studies:** Both main isomers inhibited carcinogenesis in animal models [6]. A 2019 review of pre-clinical and human studies concluded that CLA may have efficacy against cancer, obesity and atherosclerosis [15].

**Colorectal cancer:** In 60,708 Swedish women followed for an average of 14.8 years, those eating 4 or more servings of high-fat dairy foods a day had a rate ratio for colorectal cancer of 0.59 (95% CI 0.44 to 0.79) compared with those eating less than 1 serving; for the highest versus lowest quarter of CLA intake the rate ratio was 0.71 (95% CI 0.55 to 0.91) (Larsson et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2005) [36]. This study measured CLA from food, not supplements [36].

**Breast cancer:** In Finnish postmenopausal women, the odds ratio for breast cancer in the highest versus lowest fifth of serum CLA was 0.4 (95% CI 0.2 to 0.9), and the authors suggest that a diet of CLA-rich foods, particularly cheese, may protect (Aro et al., *Nutr Cancer*, 2000) [37]. They add that the study could not separate the effect of CLA from other parts of the diet [37].

**Synthesis:** These human studies are observational and about CLA in food, so they cannot show that CLA supplements prevent cancer [36][37].

### Cardiovascular Health

CLA's effects on blood fats are mixed, and some are unfavorable.

**Lipid profile effects:** A 2022 meta-analysis of 56 trials found no significant effect on LDL cholesterol (WMD 0.49; 95% CI -0.75 to 2.74) and a small fall in HDL cholesterol (WMD -0.40; 95% CI -0.72 to -0.07) (Asbaghi et al., *Front Nutr*, 2022) [38]. The abstract's text calls this an improvement, so the direction is uncertain [38].

**Animal studies:** A 2019 review describes anti-atherosclerotic effects of CLA in pre-clinical studies [15].

**The Gaullier (2004) trial:** During the 1-year trial, LDL rose with the free fatty acid form, HDL fell with the triacylglycerol form, and lipoprotein(a) rose with both [26].

**The Chen (2012) study findings:** In the 12-week Chinese trial, total cholesterol, triglycerides and LDL rose and HDL fell, but these changes were not significant [27].

**The t10,c12 isomer and HDL:** In abdominally obese men, t10,c12 CLA lowered HDL cholesterol by 4% compared with placebo, and a CLA mixture lowered it by 2% [8]. In type 2 diabetes, by contrast, a 50:50 blend raised HDL by 8% [17].

### Insulin Sensitivity and Diabetes

CLA's effects on blood sugar control are the main safety concern, particularly for the t10,c12 isomer.

**Insulin resistance concerns:** In a 12-week trial in 60 abdominally obese men, 3.4 g a day of purified t10,c12 CLA raised insulin resistance by 19% and blood glucose by 4% compared with placebo (Risérus et al., *Diabetes Care*, 2002) [8]. Purified c9,t11 CLA at 3 g a day also lowered insulin sensitivity, by 15%, in obese men (Risérus et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2004) [52].

**Mixed-isomer supplement effects:** In the 2002 trial, the CLA mixture did not change glucose metabolism [8]. In 32 people with diet-controlled type 2 diabetes, however, 3 g a day of a 50:50 blend for 8 weeks raised fasting glucose by 6.3% and reduced insulin sensitivity [17].

**Findings in overweight children:** In a 7-month trial in 62 children aged 6 to 10, 3 g a day of 80% CLA lowered body fat compared with placebo (body fat fell 0.5% with CLA and rose 1.3% with placebo) but did not change glucose or insulin [39].

**Practical implication:** People with the metabolic syndrome, diabetes or prediabetes should talk to their doctor before using CLA [8][17].

### Immune Function

Reviews list immune modulation among CLA's reported effects, mostly from laboratory and animal work [10][46].

**Human data:** In 36 breastfeeding women, cheese enriched with c9,t11 CLA did not change immune measures such as T-helper cells and interleukin-2 [9].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Doses Used in Clinical Research

Most trials used about 3 to 3.4 g of CLA a day, often as a 50:50 blend of the two main isomers [8][17][25].

| Use studied | Dose (g/day) | Duration | Finding |
|-----------|:-:|:-:|------|
| Fat loss (meta-analysis) | 3.2 (median) | Up to 2 years | Modest fat loss [25] |
| Fat loss (RCT) | 3.4 | 6 months | Lower body fat mass [44] |
| Dose-ranging | 1.7 to 6.8 | 12 weeks | No added fat loss above 3.4 [56] |
| Type 2 diabetes | 3.0 | 8 weeks | Higher fasting glucose [17] |
| Overweight children | 3.0 | 7 months | Lower body fat, lower HDL [39] |

**Most commonly studied dose:** About 3.2 g a day was the median dose across trials in a 2007 meta-analysis [25].

**The Chen (2012) protocol:** 1.7 g of CLA twice daily in milk for 12 weeks lowered body weight and fat mass from baseline [27].

### Timing and Administration

Trials gave CLA in divided doses; in one, 1.7 g was taken twice daily in 200 mL of milk [27].

### Duration

Fat-loss trials have run from 12 weeks to 1 year or longer [25][26][27]. In a 2007 meta-analysis, the effect grew for up to 6 months and then leveled off by 2 years [25]. A 2012 review of trials lasting 6 months or more judged the clinical relevance uncertain [11].

### Dietary Intake vs. Supplementation

Measured CLA intake from food was 212 mg a day in men and 151 mg a day in women in one US study, far below trial doses of about 3 g [7][17]. Food also supplies mainly the c9,t11 isomer, not the t10,c12 isomer that drives the effects on fat cells [6].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety Profile

Trials of 6 months and 1 year found no difference in adverse events between CLA and placebo [26][44]. Reviews still list effects on liver function, milk fat, glucose metabolism and oxidative stress as safety concerns, and no firm conclusion on safety has been reached [10][46].

### Common Side Effects

The most common side effects are digestive:

- **Gastrointestinal upset:** Constipation, diarrhea and soft stools were the adverse events reported in long-term trials [11]

### Metabolic Concerns

**Insulin resistance:** In abdominally obese men, purified t10,c12 CLA raised insulin resistance by 19% and blood glucose by 4% compared with placebo [8]. In type 2 diabetes, a 50:50 blend raised fasting glucose and reduced insulin sensitivity [17].

**HDL cholesterol reduction:** In abdominally obese men, both t10,c12 CLA and a CLA mixture lowered HDL cholesterol compared with placebo [8]. In children, HDL fell more with CLA than with placebo [39].

**Pro-oxidant effects:** t10,c12 CLA raised a urinary marker of lipid peroxidation by 578% in obese men, and c9,t11 CLA raised it by 50% [13][52].

### Liver Effects

**Hepatitis case reports:** By 2015, CLA had been linked to three reported cases of acute hepatitis worldwide [45]. One woman developed fulminant liver failure and needed a liver transplant [55]. In another, symptoms were tiredness, jaundice and itching, and liver enzymes returned to normal after she stopped CLA [54]. If yellow skin or eyes or itching appear while taking CLA, stop it and see a doctor the same day; if you are very unwell, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

**Liver function:** Reviews of human trials list effects on liver function among the safety concerns for CLA [46].

**Animal data:** In a review of 64 experiments, t10,c12 CLA caused severe fatty liver in mice and a milder response in rats, hamsters and humans; where fatty liver occurred, body fat fell at the same time (Vyas et al., *J Nutr Metab*, 2012) [47].

### Breast Milk

In a crossover trial in 9 breastfeeding women, 1.5 g a day of a CLA supplement lowered milk fat compared with placebo, and the authors advise lactating women not to take CLA supplements [53]. Cheese enriched with c9,t11 CLA did not change milk fat [9].

### Special Populations

- **Pregnant women:** No trials in pregnancy were found; ask your doctor first.
- **Nursing mothers:** CLA lowered breast-milk fat in a small trial; the authors advise lactating women not to take it [53]
- **Children:** In overweight children, CLA lowered HDL more than placebo and slowed bone mineral gain [39]
- **Liver disease:** Given the hepatitis case reports, ask your doctor before using CLA [45]
- **Diabetes/prediabetes:** CLA may worsen insulin resistance; ask your doctor first [8][17]

## 6. Drug Interactions

### Antidiabetic Medications

Purified t10,c12 and c9,t11 CLA reduced insulin sensitivity in obese men, and in type 2 diabetes a 50:50 blend raised fasting glucose, which could work against glucose-lowering medicines [8][17][52]. People taking metformin, sulfonylureas, insulin or other glucose-lowering medicines should ask their doctor before using CLA.

### Lipid-Lowering Medications

In a 1-year trial, HDL cholesterol fell with one form of CLA and LDL cholesterol rose with the other, compared with levels at the start [26]. People taking statins or other lipid-lowering medicines should discuss CLA with their prescriber.

### General Recommendations

People taking prescription medicines, particularly for diabetes or heart disease, should talk to their doctor before starting CLA.

## 7. Dietary Sources

### Natural Food Sources

CLA is found mainly in milk, meat and their products from ruminant animals, and c9,t11 is the main dietary form [6][10].

### CLA Content in Common Foods

| Food Source | CLA Content (mg/g milk fatty acids) | Notes |
|-----------|:-:|------|
| Milk, cows on typical diets | 3.8 to 5.3 | Corn-based or alfalfa silage and grain diets [42] |
| Milk, cows on all pasture | 22.1 | No supplemental feed [42] |

### Factors Affecting CLA Content in Foods

**Animal diet:** Cows grazing pasture with no supplemental feed had 500% more CLA in milk fat than cows on typical dairy diets [42]. Grass-based diets also raise CLA in beef on a g/g fat basis [50].

**Fresh grass:** As the share of fresh grass in the cow's diet rises, the c9,t11 CLA content of milk fat rises in a straight line [51].

**Fat content matters:** CLA content is reported per gram of fat [42][50]. Food supplies mainly c9,t11, not the t10,c12 isomer that acts on fat cells [6].

### Dietary Intake Estimates

| Population | Estimated Daily CLA Intake |
|-----------|:-:|
| US women | 151 mg/day [7] |
| US men | 212 mg/day [7] |

Even these intakes are far below the 3 to 3.4 g a day used in trials [7][8][17].
