## Key safety points

- There is no good public evidence on PQQ drug interactions, and human trials have been small and short. Ask your doctor or pharmacist first if you take a blood thinner such as warfarin, a statin, cancer treatment, an immunosuppressant or any other prescription medicine.
- If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor first. EFSA's safety opinion covers healthy adults and excludes pregnant and lactating women [2].
- If you have kidney disease, ask your doctor first. In mice the kidneys cleared most absorbed PQQ, and high doses caused reversible kidney changes in female rats [3][4].
- If you have cancer or are having cancer treatment, ask your oncologist first. In lab studies PQQ killed some tumour cells and acted as a pro-oxidant, and its effect alongside treatment is unknown [5][6].
- Trials used about 20 mg a day, and EFSA judged a maximum proposed intake of 20 mg a day safe for healthy adults [7][8][2]. Long-term safety still needs study [9].
- Trial abstracts report no adverse events, but the trials were small [10][11]. Full trial reports may list mild effects the abstracts do not mention. If you notice new symptoms after starting PQQ, stop it and talk to your doctor.

## 1. Overview

Pyrroloquinoline quinone (PQQ) is a redox-active, aromatic tricyclic o-quinone [12][13]. It was first identified as a redox cofactor for bacterial dehydrogenases [12][14]. Mammals do not make PQQ, but trace amounts turn up in human and rat tissues because many foods contain it [12].

PQQ is not accepted as a vitamin or conditional vitamin [15]. The case for calling it one comes from animal work. Mice fed a diet with no PQQ grew poorly, failed to reproduce and developed friable skin [16], and the response resembles a vitamin deficiency that reverses when PQQ is added back [15]. Some researchers have declared it a vitamin, but the question remains controversial [17].

Most of the interest in PQQ comes from cell and animal studies. In cells, PQQ increases mitochondrial content [18][13]. In animals it has been linked to better growth and reproduction and to protection from heart and brain ischemia [19]. Human evidence is thin: a handful of small trials, most lasting 12 weeks, at about 20 mg a day [10][7][20][8]. Many had authors from companies that make PQQ ingredients: Mitsubishi Gas Chemical [7][8][21], Ryusendo [10] and CalerieLife [22]. ROHTO Pharmaceutical funded another [23].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### PQQ Disodium Salt

Supplements and trials use PQQ as its disodium salt [2][24]. FDA notice files describe it as a reddish-brown crystalline powder with the molecular formula C14H4N2Na2O8 and a molecular weight of 374.17 [24]. An industry review describes it as a red trihydrate crystal [25]. Makers report that it stays stable for up to two years at room temperature [24][1].

### Chemical Properties

PQQ is an aromatic tricyclic o-quinone that takes part in redox cycling [12][26]. That chemistry cuts both ways. In isolated rat liver mitochondria, PQQ protected against lipid peroxidation and damage to the respiratory chain, but in cell culture it generated hydrogen peroxide and killed cells [6]. Another lab study found that PQQ oxidizes proteins through redox cycling [26]. The authors of the first study conclude that PQQ can act as an antioxidant or a pro-oxidant, depending on its environment [6].

### Branded Ingredients

Two branded ingredients appear in most human trials. BioPQQ, from Mitsubishi Gas Chemical, was used in the cholesterol trial and an attention trial [8][7], and EFSA assessed it as a novel food [2]. A review by Mitsubishi authors states that it is now approved as a food in Japan and the EU [25]. mnemoPQQ, developed by Ryusendo, was used in a cognition trial and a muscle trial [27][10][11]. Other makers have filed FDA notices for PQQ disodium salt, including Nascent Health Sciences and Zhejiang Hisun Pharmaceutical [24][1].

### Fermentation vs. Chemical Synthesis

PQQ disodium salt is made in two ways. BioPQQ is produced by fermentation with the bacterium Hyphomicrobium denitrificans and has a minimum purity of 99.0% [2]. Hisun's ingredient is also fermented with H. denitrificans [1]. Nascent's ingredient is made by a multi-step chemical synthesis [24]. Purity specifications differ between makers: at least 98% in Nascent's notice and at least 85% in Hisun's [24][1]. There is no good public evidence that one production method works better in people. Choose a product that an independent lab has tested.

### Absorption and Pharmacokinetics

Information on how PQQ is absorbed, distributed and excreted is limited in both animals and humans, as EFSA noted [2]. The clearest data come from mice. In mice given labelled PQQ by mouth, 62% was absorbed (range 19 to 89%) in the lower intestine, and the kidneys excreted 81% of the absorbed dose within 24 hours [3]. At 24 hours only skin and kidney held significant amounts, and greater than 95% of the PQQ in blood sat in the blood cells rather than plasma [3].

In people, PQQ has been measured in plasma and urine after a dose [28][29], but these abstracts give no peak time or half-life. PQQ is water-soluble [8]. The trial abstracts do not say whether it was taken with food.

In mice, the gut bacteria made little if any PQQ; the diet was the more obvious source [30].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Mitochondrial Biogenesis

Most of the case for PQQ rests on its effect on mitochondria in lab studies. In mouse liver cells (Hepa1-6), 10 to 30 µM PQQ for 24 to 48 hours increased mitochondrial DNA, mitochondrial enzyme activity and cellular oxygen use [18]. It acted by phosphorylating CREB at serine 133 and raising PGC-1alpha, and it had no effect when either was knocked down [18]. PQQ also activated NRF-1 and NRF-2 and raised Tfam expression [18].

A 2017 study found the same effect at lower, physiologically relevant levels: 10 to 100 nM PQQ for 48 hours increased mitochondrial DNA content in mouse fibroblasts [13]. Here the route ran through SIRT1, an NAD+-dependent enzyme, and PQQ raised cellular NAD+ [13]. A 2021 review adds that PQQ deficiency early in life reduces mitochondrial content in animals [15]. In rats, energy expenditure was lower without PQQ and tracked liver mitochondrial content [31].

Human data are indirect. In men not trained in endurance exercise, 20 mg a day for six weeks of endurance training raised PGC-1alpha protein more than placebo, but aerobic performance did not improve [20].

### Memory and Cognition

Small trials, most run by ingredient makers, suggest PQQ may modestly improve some memory and attention scores in healthy middle-aged and older adults. The one trial in people with mild cognitive impairment tested a blend and found little.

In one trial, 64 healthy Japanese adults, at least 40 and younger than 80, who reported becoming more forgetful took mnemoPQQ (21.5 mg a day of PQQ disodium salt) or placebo for 12 weeks; 58 finished [10]. Compared with placebo, the PQQ group scored better on composite memory, verbal memory, reaction time, complex attention, cognitive flexibility, executive function and motor speed, and on a forgetfulness questionnaire and the MMSE-J [10]. The abstract reports no adverse events. The authors were mainly from Ryusendo, the maker of mnemoPQQ [10].

In a trial of 41 healthy older adults, 20 mg of BioPQQ a day or placebo was given for 12 weeks [7]. On the Stroop test, the PQQ group's interference ratio changed significantly less than placebo's. On a tablet test of visual-spatial function, a gain appeared only in a subgroup analysis of people with an initial score less than 70 [7]. One author worked at Mitsubishi Gas Chemical [7].

A Mitsubishi-run trial gave 20 mg a day for 12 weeks to adults aged 20 to 65 years and reports improved composite and verbal memory [21]. In those aged 20 to 40 years, cognitive flexibility, processing speed and execution speed improved after 8 weeks [21]. The abstract does not give the number of participants.

In 34 older adults with mild cognitive impairment (mean age 71.9; 28 women), a mixture of PQQ with dihydrogen-producing minerals (Alpha Hope, CalerieLife) or placebo was taken twice daily for six weeks [22]. The primary outcome, serum BDNF, rose in the PQQ group, but the difference from placebo was not significant (P = 0.14) [22]. The only cognitive benefit over placebo the abstract reports was in the orientation domain of the ADAS-Cog (P = 0.03) [22]. Because the product was a blend, this trial cannot show what PQQ does on its own. One author worked at CalerieLife [22].

A completed trial in healthy postmenopausal women tested a supplement that combines PQQ with GABA, caffeine and B vitamins, so it will not isolate PQQ either [32].

Lab and animal work underpins the interest. Rats fed a PQQ-supplemented diet learned better than controls [7], and trial reports from Ryusendo describe PQQ raising nerve growth factor and suppressing amyloid beta fibril formation in earlier research [10][27]. These findings have not been shown to translate into human benefit.

Taken together, three small trials in healthy adults, each with authors from an ingredient maker, report modest gains on some test scores. The trial in mild cognitive impairment, which tested a blend, found little. Each lasted 6 to 12 weeks [22][7].

### Muscle Strength, Exercise Endurance, and Body Composition

In a mnemoPQQ trial, 64 healthy Japanese adults took 21.5 mg a day or placebo for 12 weeks, and 62 finished (mean ages 54.2 and 54.5 in the two groups) [11]. The abstract reports significant gains in leg extension strength (the primary outcome) and grip strength in the PQQ group, along with better results on 10-meter shuttle walking, 6-minute walking and 10-meter walking tests (p < 0.01) [11]. The abstract reports no adverse events [11]. Full trial reports may list mild effects the abstracts do not mention.

In 23 men not trained in endurance exercise, on a supervised six-week endurance program, 20 mg a day of PQQ did not improve aerobic performance compared with placebo [20]. Peak oxygen uptake and test duration improved in both groups, and PQQ raised PGC-1alpha protein more than placebo [20]. The authors conclude that PQQ does not appear to have ergogenic effects on aerobic performance or body composition [20]. This is the only PQQ trial here that tested exercise training. In this six-week trial, PQQ neither added to nor blunted the training gains, though the trial was small and short.

A completed trial randomized 24 basketball players to 20 mg a day of PQQ or placebo for six weeks [33].

The evidence is too thin and inconsistent to recommend PQQ for strength, endurance or body composition.

### Cholesterol-Lowering

One trial suggests a possible modest effect on LDL cholesterol. In 29 healthy Japanese adults aged 40 to 57 with normal to moderately high triglycerides, 20 mg a day of BioPQQ for 12 weeks left triglycerides unchanged [8]. Mean LDL cholesterol fell from 136.1 to 127.0 mg/dL in the PQQ group, a marginally significant change [8]. In the 11 people whose baseline LDL was at least 140 mg/dL, LDL fell significantly compared with placebo [8]. The lead author worked at Mitsubishi Gas Chemical [8].

The finding has not been repeated, and a fall of this size is small next to standard cholesterol treatments. In a short 76-hour study in 10 healthy adults, PQQ did not change cholesterol, LDL, HDL or triglycerides [28]. In a rat model of type 2 diabetes, PQQ injected at 4.5 mg/kg for 3 days lowered plasma triglycerides during a 6-hour fast more than twofold compared with saline [31].

### Energy, Mood, and Sleep Quality

In an open-label trial, 17 adults took 20 mg of PQQ a day for 8 weeks [34]. All six Profile of Mood States measures improved, including vigor, fatigue and tension-anxiety, and so did several sleep scores [34]. Changes in global scores correlated with changes in the cortisol awakening response (R = -0.55) [34]. The trial had no placebo group, and placebo effects are large for mood, fatigue and sleep, so it cannot show that PQQ caused the change.

The idea that PQQ boosts energy rests on its mitochondrial effects in cells [18][13]. No placebo-controlled trial has shown that it reduces fatigue.

### Inflammation

In a crossover study, 10 adults (5 women, 5 men) drank PQQ in a fruit-flavored drink [28]. After a single 0.2 mg/kg dose, markers of antioxidant potential changed during the 48 hours of measurement [28]. After daily doses of 0.3 mg/kg for 76 hours, plasma C-reactive protein, IL-6 and urinary trimethylamine N-oxide fell, while standard clinical indices stayed normal [28]. The abstract describes no placebo group, and the study had only 10 people.

At the cellular level, PQQ acts as an antioxidant in mitochondria but as a pro-oxidant in cell culture [6][26]. These mixed lab effects give some biological plausibility, but the human evidence is one small, short, uncontrolled study.

### Metabolic and Insulin-Sensitizing Effects

The metabolic evidence comes from animals. In rats fed with or without PQQ, there were no striking differences in serum glucose, insulin or free fatty acids, but PQQ-deprived rats had higher plasma di- and triacylglycerides [31]. In mice exposed to a Western-style diet before and after birth, PQQ did not change weight gain but reduced body fat and liver lipids and raised metabolic flexibility [35]. A review from Mitsubishi-affiliated authors lists anti-diabetic effects among PQQ's reported benefits [12].

In the one short human study that checked routine blood tests, glucose did not change [28]. There is no good public evidence that PQQ improves insulin sensitivity, blood glucose or body weight in people.

### Liver Protection

In mice, PQQ given to the offspring of mothers fed a Western-style diet reduced liver lipids, oxidative stress and inflammatory gene expression, and the changes persisted after PQQ was stopped at weaning [35]. A follow-up study found that short-term PQQ before weaning reduced liver inflammation and collagen fibril formation at 20 weeks, even when PQQ was withdrawn at 3 weeks [36].

There is no good public evidence on PQQ for liver disease in people yet.

### Statin-Related Side Effects

There is no good public evidence on this yet.

### Neuroprotection and Stroke (Preclinical Only)

In adult rats, a single intravenous dose of PQQ at 10 mg/kg, given at the start of a 2-hour stroke model or 3 hours after it began, reduced infarct volume measured 72 hours later [37]. A 3 mg/kg dose given at 3 hours also worked, but 1 mg/kg did not, and behavior scores improved at 10 and 3 mg/kg [37]. An earlier study had reported smaller infarcts in 7-day-old rat pups [37].

Animal work also links PQQ to protection from heart and brain ischemia [19]. In rats, heart injury after ischemia and reperfusion was worse without PQQ [31]. In cells, PQQ protected against mitochondrial poisons including rotenone [18].

There is no good public evidence on PQQ for stroke, brain injury or heart attack in people.

### Potential Synergy with CoQ10 and NAD+ Precursors

A review by Mitsubishi authors states that taking PQQ with coenzyme Q10 further enhanced its cognitive effects [25]. The abstract does not name the trial, so the claim cannot be checked.

PQQ links to NAD+ in lab work: it raised cellular NAD+ in mouse fibroblasts [13], and a review describes it enhancing NAD+-dependent sirtuin activity [15]. There is no good public evidence on combining PQQ with NMN or NR.

### Animal Deficiency Evidence

Mice fed a chemically defined diet with no PQQ grew poorly, failed to reproduce and became osteolathyritic, with friable skin and weak collagen [16]. In a dose study, mice given at least 1000 ng PQQ per gram of diet had 8 pups per litter, compared with 4 to 5 in the groups given 300 ng/g or less [38]. The apparent requirement for optimal neonatal growth was at least 300 ng PQQ per gram of diet [38]. Deprived mice also made less interleukin 2 at the age when T cells multiply [38].

A later mouse study found lower conception, fertility and pup viability without PQQ, and slower growth in offspring [39]. In rats, PQQ deficiency lowered energy expenditure and raised plasma triglycerides [31]. These findings support a role for PQQ in animals, but PQQ is still not accepted as a vitamin [15].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Clinical Trial Doses

Most trials gave 20 mg of PQQ disodium salt a day; the two mnemoPQQ trials gave 21.5 mg a day [7][8][20][10][11]. An industry review calls 20 mg a day the baseline intake level [25].

| Hwang et al., 2020 (exercise) [20] | 20 mg | 6 weeks | Placebo-controlled, 23 men | No performance gain |
| Nakano et al., 2015 (cholesterol) [8] | 20 mg | 12 weeks | Placebo-controlled, 29 adults | Marginal LDL fall |
| Nakano et al., 2012 (mood and sleep) [34] | 20 mg | 8 weeks | Open label, 17 adults | Mood and sleep scores improved |
| Harris et al., 2013 (inflammation) [28] | 0.3 mg/kg | 76 hours | Crossover, 10 adults | Lower CRP and IL-6 |
| Tsuji et al., 2026 (ovarian reserve) [23] | 20 mg | 90 ± 10 days | Open label, 35 women analyzed | No overall change in AMH |

The 2026 trial in healthy women aged 25 to 42 years (35 analyzed) found no overall change in anti-Mullerian hormone after 20 mg a day; it had no control group and was funded by ROHTO Pharmaceutical [23]. In an exploratory subgroup of 13 older women with lower baseline AMH, AMH fell significantly (p = 0.033); the authors call the subgroup findings inconsistent and say they do not permit conclusions [23].

### How to Take

The trial abstracts do not say whether PQQ was taken with food or at a set time of day. PQQ is water-soluble [8]. There is no good public evidence on the best time to take it.

### GRAS Status and Regulatory Position

- United States, 2016 (Nascent): the FDA had no questions about Nascent's conclusion that PQQ disodium salt is GRAS in energy, sport and isotonic drinks, meal replacement beverages and water at a maximum level of 8 mg per serving [24]. The letter notes that PQQ disodium salt is also used as a dietary supplement at up to 20 mg a day [24].
- United States, 2016 (Hisun): the FDA had no questions about GRAS use in energy, sport and isotonic drinks at up to 5 mg per serving and in fortified water at up to 20 mg per serving [1].
- What GRAS means: in both letters the FDA states that it has not made its own determination of GRAS status [24][1].
- European Union, 2017: EFSA concluded that BioPQQ is safe in food supplements for healthy adults, except pregnant and lactating women, at a maximum proposed intake of 20 mg a day (0.29 mg/kg body weight per day for a 70-kg person) [2]. It based this on a margin of exposure of 344 against a rat no-observed-adverse-effect level of 100 mg/kg body weight per day [2].

### Practical Dosing Summary

- Trial dose: 20 mg a day, or 21.5 mg a day in the mnemoPQQ trials [20][10]
- Upper end of regulatory review: EFSA assessed a maximum proposed intake of 20 mg a day for healthy adults, excluding pregnant and lactating women [2]
- Vitamin status: PQQ is not accepted as a vitamin or conditional vitamin [15]
- Duration: the longest human study summarized for the FDA ran 24 weeks at 20 mg a day [1]
- Compared with food: 20 mg a day is at least 250 times the estimated background intake from food [2]
- Brad's view: he would not take PQQ (see Current view)

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Reported Adverse Effects

The published abstracts of the main trials report no adverse events. The cognition and muscle trials each state that none were observed [10][11], and the attention trial found no abnormal blood or urine findings [7]. Full trial reports may list mild effects the abstracts do not mention. In its FDA notice, one ingredient maker summarizes placebo-controlled human studies with no adverse effects at up to 60 mg of PQQ a day for four weeks, the highest dose tested [1]. These trials were small and short. If you notice new symptoms after starting PQQ, stop it and talk to your doctor.

### Severe Adverse Effects

No adverse effects were reported in the human studies summarized in the FDA notices [24][1]. A few small, short trials cannot rule out rare or delayed harms.

### Long-Term Safety

The longest human study summarized for the FDA ran 24 weeks at 20 mg a day [1]. A 2024 review calls for research on PQQ's long-term effectiveness and safety [9].

### Toxicology and Genotoxicity Data

In rats, the median lethal dose of BioPQQ was 1000 to 2000 mg/kg in males and 500 to 1000 mg/kg in females [4]. In a 14-day study, high doses raised relative kidney weight with tissue changes in female rats only, and a 28-day study in females found more protein and crystals in the urine; these findings reversed during recovery [4]. The no-observed-adverse-effect level in the 13-week study was 100 mg/kg per day, the highest dose tested [4].

For mnemoPQQ, a 90-day rat study found no systemic toxicity at the highest daily dose of 600 mg/kg body weight [27]. PQQ injected into the abdomen of rats at 11.5 mg/kg per injection, daily for 4 days, damaged the kidney tubules and raised serum creatinine [40].

In genotoxicity tests, BioPQQ was negative in the Ames test and in a mouse micronucleus test at up to 2,000 mg/kg, with weak positive results in one type of hamster cell test that the authors judged of little relevance [41]. mnemoPQQ was also non-mutagenic [27]. PQQ occurs naturally in human milk at nanomolar to micromolar concentrations [42].

### Special Populations

**Pregnancy and lactation:** EFSA's safety opinion excludes pregnant and lactating women [2]. An unpublished rat study summarized in an FDA notice found no effects on embryo survival or development [24]. Mouse studies have given PQQ during pregnancy [35], but that is animal research, not evidence of safety in people. Ask your doctor first.

**Children:** EFSA's assessment covers healthy adults only [2]. There is no good public evidence on PQQ in children.

**Kidney disease:** in mice the kidneys excreted 81% of absorbed PQQ within 24 hours [3]. High oral doses caused reversible kidney changes in female rats [4], and injected PQQ damaged rat kidney tubules [40]. No human study in kidney disease was found. Ask your doctor first if you have kidney disease.

**Cancer patients:** in lab studies PQQ triggered cell death in several tumour cell lines with little effect on two normal cell lines [5], and it can act as a pro-oxidant in culture [6]. Whether this helps or interferes with cancer treatment is unknown. Ask your oncologist first.

## 6. Drug Interactions

There is no good public evidence on PQQ drug interactions yet. Human trials have been small and short. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Theoretical Considerations

Some lab findings raise questions no human study has answered. PQQ can act as a pro-oxidant and kill tumour cells in culture [6][5], and in one short study it lowered C-reactive protein and IL-6 [28]. Whether any of this matters for people taking blood thinners such as warfarin, statins, chemotherapy or immunosuppressants is unknown. Ask your doctor or pharmacist first if you take any of these.

## 7. Dietary Sources

PQQ occurs in many foods at nanogram levels. A 1995 survey by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry found free PQQ in every sample, at 3.7 to 61 ng/g or ng/mL [43]. A 2007 survey with a more selective method found lower levels, 0.19 to 7.02 ng per g fresh weight [17]. The two surveys disagree, so food values are uncertain.

### Food Sources

The survey abstracts give ranges, not values for single foods [43][17]. Levels in human tissues and body fluids are 5 to 10 times lower than those in foods, which suggests that the PQQ in human tissues comes at least partly from the diet [43]. In one tissue survey, the human spleen had the highest level (5.9 ng/g), and PQQ was below detection limits in the brain and heart [44].

### Context for Dietary Intake

EFSA notes that 20 mg a day is at least 250 times higher than the estimated background intake from food [2]. Supplements are measured in milligrams; foods hold nanograms per gram. Human milk contains PQQ at nanomolar to micromolar concentrations [42].

### Sources of PQQ in Nature

PQQ is made by bacteria, where it works as a cofactor for enzymes such as methanol dehydrogenase and glucose dehydrogenase [3]. It is also a potent plant growth factor [19], and it may raise crop yields by making insoluble phosphate available to plants [14]. Mammals do not make it [12], and in mice the gut bacteria made little if any [30].
