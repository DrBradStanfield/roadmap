## Current view (reviewed 10 November 2026)

A 2026 meta-analysis of 35 trials and more than 2,500 people gives reasonable evidence that collagen peptides may improve skin hydration and elasticity, but it found no effect on wrinkles, and longer studies are likely needed to settle wrinkles ([Brad, 15 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-collagen-peptides-for-skin-story-is-changing)). Elasticity only clearly improved in trials lasting at least 12 weeks, and Brad's own correction of errors in the 2026 Grimes and Mahto dataset points the same way ([Brad, 15 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-collagen-peptides-for-skin-story-is-changing)). Trials used 0.5 to 10 g a day (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). In the three trials Brad could find that compare collagen peptides directly with another protein, the results lean in favour of collagen, although none is conclusive on its own, and for osteoarthritis pain collagen did not come out ahead where trials matched the protein ([Brad, 15 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-collagen-peptides-for-skin-story-is-changing)). Brad's guidance suggests collagen peptides for adults concerned about skin ageing, skin hydration or skin elasticity, never for a skin disease, rash, wound, ulcer or burn (Brad's current guidance, 2 Nov 2026). Brad sells a supplement that contains collagen, so he asks readers to weigh his view with that conflict of interest in mind ([Brad, 15 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-collagen-peptides-for-skin-story-is-changing)).

## Key safety points

- Collagen peptides lowered fasting blood glucose in a trial of people with type 2 diabetes who kept taking their usual medicines. If you take insulin or other diabetes medicines, check with your doctor and monitor your blood sugar [1].
- People with fish allergy may react to fish collagen, even after a negative test for parvalbumin, the main fish allergen, because standard fish allergy tests contain little collagen [2]. Eggshell membrane products come from eggs, so ask your doctor first if you have an egg allergy.
- Gelatin raised urinary oxalate in a feeding study. If you have had calcium oxalate kidney stones, check with your doctor before taking collagen, especially at higher doses [3].
- Side effects in osteoarthritis trials were mostly mild to moderate stomach and bowel complaints [4].

## 1. Overview

Collagen is the main structural protein in humans [5]. Hydroxyproline is one of the amino acids in collagen [3]. Skin collagen falls with age and is lower in women than in men at all ages [6], and aged skin makes less type I and type III collagen [7].

Supplements are made from cow, pig and fish skin or hide [8], chicken sternum cartilage [9] and chicken eggshell membrane [10]. Joint supplements such as UC-II use type II collagen from cartilage [9].

The best evidence is for skin. A 2026 meta-analysis of 35 trials found that collagen peptides may improve skin hydration and elasticity, but found no reliable effect on wrinkles [11]. A 2025 meta-analysis found no effect when it looked only at high-quality trials or at trials without company funding [12], and a 2026 re-analysis judged the earlier positive meta-analyses unreliable [13]. Most skin trials were paid for by collagen makers or had authors with ties to them [13]. Trials for joints, bone, muscle and wound healing are smaller and give mixed results; the newest joint trials found no benefit over placebo [14][15].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

Collagen supplements come in four main forms. They differ in dose and in what they were tested for.

### Hydrolyzed Collagen (Collagen Peptides)

Hydrolysed collagen, also called collagen peptides or collagen hydrolysate, is collagen broken into short peptides. Products differ in animal source and peptide size. In healthy active men, hydrolysed and non-hydrolysed collagen proteins did not differ significantly in how their amino acids reached the blood [16].

Branded peptides include Verisol, tested for skin [17], and Tendoforte, tested for Achilles tendon pain [18].

### Gelatin

Gelatin is a less broken-down form of collagen. Gelatin and gelatin hydrolysates were the test materials in several studies of absorption and metabolism [19][20][3].

### Undenatured Collagen

Undenatured collagen has not been broken down. The best-studied product, UC-II, is type II collagen from chicken sternum cartilage, taken at 40 mg a day [9]. It is thought to act on the immune system through oral tolerance rather than by supplying protein; a pilot study of a similar product reported less cartilage damage this way, but how it works in people is not settled [21]. In 2014 the lab test used to measure UC-II was changed, which changed the percentage of undenatured collagen reported; a letter from Lonza says the material itself did not change [22].

### Eggshell Membrane Hydrolysates

Eggshell membrane supplements are made from the thin membrane inside chicken eggshells. Branded forms include BiovaFlex, tested at 450 mg a day for knee osteoarthritis [10], and a hydrolysate tested at 450 mg a day for hair, skin and nails [23].

### Vegan Collagen Alternatives

There is no good public evidence on this yet.

### Source Comparison: Bovine vs. Porcine vs. Marine

In a crossover study in healthy volunteers, collagen hydrolysates from fish, pig and cow, of different molecular weights, gave comparable uptake of free hydroxyproline, and all gave relevant blood levels of collagen peptides [8]. So no animal source appears to be absorbed better than another. The blood levels needed to change skin or other tissues are not known [8]. Five of the study's authors worked for Rousselot [8].

### Key Bioavailability Principles

**Absorption:** After people take gelatin hydrolysates, short collagen peptides, mainly Pro-Hyp, appear in the blood [19]. In mice, labelled gelatin hydrolysate built up in cartilage, at more than twice the level seen in controls [20]. Whether these peptides act on skin or cartilage in people is not proven.

**Dipeptide content:** In a five-arm knee osteoarthritis trial with authors from Nitta Gelatin, 2.5 g of a dipeptide-rich "Type J" collagen peptide matched 10 g of conventional collagen peptides [26]. Peptide make-up may matter as well as total dose.

**Collagen vs. other proteins:** Collagen lacks tryptophan, so it is an incomplete protein [27]. A 20 g serve of collagen protein raised blood glycine more, and leucine less, than dairy protein [16]. In 45 young recreational athletes, 30 g of whey protein after resistance exercise raised muscle protein synthesis above placebo, while 30 g of collagen did not [28]. In older women, whey raised muscle protein synthesis more than collagen peptides did [29].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Skin Aging and Wrinkles

The pooled analyses disagree. The newest and largest, from 2026, found benefits for hydration and elasticity but not wrinkles [11].

**Earlier meta-analyses (2021 and 2023):** A 2021 analysis of 19 studies with 1,125 people found benefits for hydration, elasticity and wrinkles [30]. A 2023 analysis of 26 trials with 1,721 people found improved hydration and elasticity [31].

**Meta-analysis (2025):** Myung and colleagues pooled 23 trials with 1,474 people. All trials together showed better hydration, elasticity and wrinkles, but trials without company funding, and high-quality trials, showed no effect. The authors concluded there is no clinical evidence that collagen prevents or treats skin aging [12]. Brad argued that the result for the independent trials only just missed statistical significance, and that 7 of the 9 trials graded high quality found a benefit [32].

**Re-analysis (2026):** Grimes and Mahto re-checked the 2021 and 2023 meta-analyses and found inaccurate reporting of data, including standard error used where standard deviation belonged, and pooling of measures that are not comparable. Their dose meta-regression found no evidence of benefit, and they noted that most collagen studies are industry funded [13]. Brad checked their published dataset against the original papers and reported the collagen and comparison groups reversed in eight trials. After his correction, the elasticity effect rose from 0.37 to about 0.6 and became statistically significant, and hydration stayed at about 0.5 [32]. This is Brad's own analysis, reported in his video post.

**Meta-analysis (2026):** Batool and colleagues pooled 35 randomised trials with 2,534 people. Collagen peptides improved skin hydration (effect size 0.44) and elasticity (0.62, after setting aside two trials with extreme negative results), and reduced water loss through the skin. Hydration improved as early as week 4, while elasticity and barrier gains needed at least 12 weeks; the effect depended more on time than on dose. Apparent effects on wrinkles, roughness and dermal density did not hold up, and the authors suggest trials of 8 to 12 weeks may be too short to show fewer wrinkles. Independently run trials gave effects similar to sponsored ones. Doses ranged from 500 mg to 10 g a day [11].

**Other 2026 reviews:** An umbrella review of 16 systematic reviews (113 trials, 7,983 people) found collagen consistently linked with better skin, musculoskeletal and osteoarthritis outcomes [33]. A systematic review of 25 skin trials rated most of them at high risk of bias [34]. Brad notes that almost all pooled trials compared collagen with a placebo that had no protein [32].

**Collagen or just more protein?** Few trials compare collagen with the same amount of another protein. Brad counts three and says they lean in favour of collagen, though none is conclusive on its own [32]:

- In a Korean trial in 44 healthy adults, 3 g a day of collagen hydrolysate with 2 mg a day of astaxanthin for 12 weeks improved facial elasticity and water loss compared with placebo [35].
- In 31 men with burns, a collagen-based supplement healed wounds faster than an isocaloric placebo (see Tendon, Ligament, and Wound Healing) [36].
- In the New Zealand CHAMP trial, women aged at least 50 years took 15 g of collagen, milk protein matched for protein and energy, or maltodextrin for four months. Collagen may have increased skin thickness on the inner forearm, an area that sees little sun; the author called for more research [5].

**Individual trials:** Some small trials reported fewer wrinkles, but the 2026 pooled analysis found no reliable effect on wrinkles [11]. Many trials were run or paid for by makers.

- In 114 women aged 45 to 65 years, 2.5 g a day of Verisol for 8 weeks raised skin procollagen type I by 65% and elastin by 18% compared with placebo, in a subgroup that had skin samples taken [17].
- In 69 women aged 35 to 55, 2.5 g or 5 g a day for 8 weeks improved skin elasticity compared with placebo. Four weeks after stopping, elasticity stayed higher in the older women [37].
- In 66 women aged 35 to 55, 2.5 g a day of bovine collagen peptides for 8 weeks improved skin elasticity and hydration compared with placebo [38].
- In 36 postmenopausal women, 5 g a day of marine collagen hydrolysate for 4 weeks improved cheek elasticity compared with placebo, and the difference remained 4 weeks after stopping [39].
- In 46 women aged 45 to 59 years, hydrolysed fish cartilage for 90 days increased dermis echogenicity compared with placebo. One author worked for Abyss Ingredients [40].
- In 85 East Asian women aged 43 to 65 years who all used the same face cream, 5 g a day for 84 days improved dermis density and skin moisture compared with placebo. Elasticity improved within 28 days with collagen, and the placebo group caught up by day 84. Four of the five authors worked for Rousselot [41].
- In 113 women aged 39 to 59, a chicken sternal cartilage supplement (500 mg twice daily; hydrolysed type II collagen with hyaluronic acid and chondroitin sulfate) raised skin elasticity and skin collagen content by 12% compared with placebo, with no difference in skin-surface water [42]. An earlier open-label study of 1 g a day in 26 women found a rise in skin collagen at 6 weeks that was no longer significant at 12 weeks [43].
- In 32 healthy volunteers, 3 g a day of collagen peptides for 12 weeks improved hydration and elasticity compared with no supplement; adding 500 mg of vitamin C gave no extra benefit [44].
- In 39 hospital inpatients aged 65 or older, a nutrition supplement with 10 g of collagen peptides a day raised skin hydration scores from 43.7 to 51.7 by week 8. The study was open-label, with no placebo [45].

**Cellulite:** In 105 women aged 24 to 50 with moderate cellulite, 2.5 g a day for 6 months reduced cellulite and skin waviness on the thighs in women of normal weight; the effect was smaller in overweight women [46]. In 114 women aged 20 to 50, 1 g a day of low-molecular-weight fish collagen peptides for 24 weeks improved cellulite severity and skin elasticity at weeks 12 and 24 compared with placebo [47].

**"Ozempic face":** Reviews describe fast weight loss on semaglutide and other GLP-1 medicines leading to facial volume loss and loss of collagen and elastin [48][49]. We found no trial that tests collagen supplements for these changes.

### Hair and Nails

Evidence for hair and nails is limited.

**Hair:** In 88 healthy middle-aged adults, 450 mg a day of hydrolysed eggshell membrane for 12 weeks was linked with better hair thickness, less breakage and more growth, and with better crow's feet and skin tone; nails did not improve [23]. In 114 women with thinning hair, 1 g a day of fish collagen peptides for 24 weeks increased hair diameter compared with placebo [47].

**Nails:** In an open-label study, 25 people took 2.5 g a day of Verisol for 24 weeks. Nail growth rose 12% and broken nails fell 42% compared with before treatment, but with no placebo group the findings are uncertain [50].

### Joint Pain and Osteoarthritis

Joint trials give mixed results, and the newest trials found no benefit over placebo. A 2012 systematic review of eight studies found too little evidence to recommend collagen hydrolysate for osteoarthritis, and rated the evidence moderate to very low in quality [4]. A 2026 umbrella review found favourable osteoarthritis outcomes [33], but Brad notes that almost all of the pooled trials used a placebo with no protein [32]. In the CHAMP trial, which matched the protein, collagen did no better than milk protein for knee pain or function [5].

**Undenatured collagen (UC-II):**

- Newest trials: in 2026, a four-arm trial in 216 Thai adults with knee osteoarthritis found that 40 mg a day of UC-II for 24 weeks did not beat placebo; neither did glucosamine sulfate or diacerein [14]. In 2025, tablets combining UC-II with hydrolysed collagen did not beat placebo over 12 weeks in 68 people with knee osteoarthritis. The authors declared no competing interests and noted that most earlier trials were industry sponsored [15].
- Older positive trials: in 191 people with knee osteoarthritis, 40 mg a day of UC-II for 180 days reduced WOMAC scores more than placebo and more than glucosamine plus chondroitin. Two of the three authors worked for InterHealth Nutraceuticals [9]. In a 90-day trial with no placebo, UC-II reduced WOMAC scores by 33%, compared with 14% for glucosamine plus chondroitin [51]. In 55 healthy people with knee discomfort only during exercise, 40 mg a day for 120 days improved knee extension compared with placebo [52].
- In 64 healthy Japanese adults, 3.2 mg a day of another undenatured type II collagen (NEXT-II) for 12 weeks improved knee flexion compared with placebo [21].

**Hydrolysed collagen (collagen peptides) for joints:**

- In 200 people aged at least 50 years with joint pain, 1,200 mg a day for 6 months gave a clinically meaningful pain improvement in 51.6% of people, against 36.5% with placebo. At 3 months the difference was not significant (44.1% vs. 39.6%) [24].
- In 167 active adults with self-reported knee pain, 10 g a day for 12 weeks eased knee pain no more than placebo and left inflammation, cartilage and bone markers unchanged [25].
- In knee osteoarthritis, collagen peptides from pork skin or bovine bone for 13 weeks reduced WOMAC, pain and quality-of-life scores more than placebo. The listed affiliation is Nitta Gelatin India [53].
- In 100 people with knee osteoarthritis, 10 g a day for 90 days did better than 1.5 g of glucosamine sulfate on several measures, but the trial had no placebo [54].
- In 90 adults aged 40 to 65 with joint discomfort, a hydrolysed chicken type II collagen supplement for 8 weeks reduced WOMAC stiffness, physical activity and overall scores more than placebo; at week 4 the overall score fell 36.9% against 14.3%. Two authors were paid by the funder for analysis and writing [55].
- In a 90-day trial in 100 people with knee osteoarthritis, dipeptide-rich collagen peptides at 2.5, 5 or 10 g, and conventional collagen peptides at 10 g, all helped; authors included staff of Nitta Gelatin [26].

**Eggshell membrane for joints:**

- In 88 adults with knee osteoarthritis, 450 mg a day of BiovaFlex for 12 weeks gave no significant gain over placebo in WOMAC scores, walking distance or range of motion in the main analysis. A change-from-baseline analysis suggested benefit, greatest in those who walked worst at the start. The sponsor funded the writing [10].
- A 2024 meta-analysis of five trials found a small improvement in total WOMAC score (effect size -0.34) [56].
- In a trial comparing two doses with placebo in knee osteoarthritis, pain fell in all groups but most with the higher dose, which also increased leg strength [57].

### Bone Health

**Bone mineral density:** In 131 postmenopausal women with age-related bone loss, 5 g a day of specific collagen peptides for 12 months increased bone mineral density at the spine and the femoral neck compared with placebo [58].

**4-year follow-up:** In an open-label follow-up, 31 of these women took the peptides for 4 years in total. Their spine bone mineral density rose by a clinically relevant amount. With no control group, the evidence is weaker [59].

### Diabetes and Blood Sugar

In 100 people with type 2 diabetes and high blood pressure, all on their usual medicines, marine collagen peptides twice daily for 3 months lowered fasting blood glucose and HbA1c and raised insulin sensitivity compared with a control group [1]. In laboratory tests, the collagen tripeptide Gly-Pro-Hyp moderately blocks the enzyme DPP-4 [60], the target of DPP-4 inhibitor diabetes medicines.

### Muscle Mass and Strength

- In 53 men with sarcopenia (average age 72) doing 12 weeks of resistance training 3 times a week, 15 g a day of collagen peptides increased fat-free mass and muscle strength more than placebo [61]. A correction to this paper was published in 2025 [62].
- In 20 middle-aged men (average age 47) doing twice-weekly leg resistance training for 12 weeks, 30 g a day of hydrolysed collagen gave no extra gains in strength or muscle size over placebo. Patellar tendon size grew more with collagen (+6.8 vs. +1.2 mm2) [63].
- In 45 young adults, a single dose of collagen, or of matching free amino acids, did not raise muscle protein synthesis [64].

**Bottom line:** For building muscle, whey looks more effective than collagen [28][29]. Collagen may help older men with sarcopenia who also do resistance training, but this rests on one trial [61].

### Tendon, Ligament, and Wound Healing

Several small studies, some funded by makers, suggest collagen may aid healing.

**Pressure ulcers:**

- In 71 long-term care residents with pressure ulcers who completed a trial, a concentrated, fortified collagen protein hydrolysate supplement gave about twice the rate of ulcer healing seen with placebo by week 8 [65].
- In 112 people with pressure ulcers who completed a 16-week trial, 10 g a day of collagen hydrolysate in two doses was compared with placebo; the product richer in Pro-Hyp and Hyp-Gly peptides lowered healing scores and wound area. Nitta Gelatin funded the study, and two of its researchers were authors [66].

**Burns:** In a pilot trial of 31 men with burns over 20 to 30% of the body, a collagen-based supplement for 4 weeks sped wound healing compared with an isocaloric placebo (hazard ratio 3.7, 95% CI 1.434 to 9.519) [36].

**Laser skin recovery:** In a pilot study of 8 healthy volunteers having fractional laser treatment, the 4 who took 3 g a day of collagen peptide for 4 weeks had faster fading of redness than controls [67].

**Tendon and ligament injuries:**

- In 50 athletes with chronic ankle instability, 5 g a day of specific collagen peptides for 6 months improved self-rated ankle stability compared with placebo, and they had fewer ankle re-injuries during a 3-month follow-up. Ankle stiffness measured with a device did not differ [68].
- In a crossover pilot study of 20 patients with long-standing Achilles tendinopathy doing twice-daily calf-strengthening exercises, VISA-A scores rose 12.6 points in the first 3 months with Tendoforte against 5.3 points with placebo [18].

**Gum health:** In 39 adults who had plaque removed by a professional, a daily collagen peptide supplement until day 90 left fewer gum sites bleeding than placebo (3.0% vs. 9.4%) [69].

### Fatigue

In 66 healthy but easily fatigued adults, 10 g a day of collagen peptides for 8 weeks lowered fatigue scores on the Profile of Mood States compared with placebo (T-score 47.0 vs. 51.5) [70].

### Hyaluronic Acid (a Common Collagen Co-Ingredient)

Skin supplements often pair collagen with hyaluronic acid. In 40 healthy Asian adults aged 35 to 64 years, 120 mg a day of hyaluronic acid for 12 weeks improved wrinkles, skin water content, water loss and elasticity compared with placebo. Three of the authors worked for Kewpie Corporation [71].

### Practical Dosing Notes

**Skin:** Trials in the 2026 meta-analysis used 500 mg to 10 g a day. Hydration changed from week 4, and elasticity needed at least 12 weeks [11].

**Joints:** Collagen peptide trials used 1.2 to 10 g a day [24][25], and UC-II trials used 40 mg [9][14]. These work in different ways and should not be compared by weight. In one trial the gain over placebo showed at 6 months but not at 3 months [24].

**Older dose ranges:** A 2019 analysis described 2.5 to 15 g a day as the effective amounts in the literature [27]. Brad's older posts gave doses in this range.

## 5. Dietary Sources

Collagen is found in animal foods that contain skin, bone and connective tissue.

### Top Food Sources

There is no good public evidence on how much collagen common foods contain. In one study, healthy men drank 300 mL of bone broth so their blood amino acids could be measured [16], but bone broth has not been tested in the skin or joint trials described above.

### Nutrients That Support Collagen Synthesis

In one trial, adding 500 mg of vitamin C to 3 g of collagen peptides gave no further gain in skin hydration or elasticity [44].

### Collagen Boosters and Builders

There is no good public evidence on this yet.

## 6. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

Collagen is generally well tolerated. In osteoarthritis trials, the most reported side effects were mild to moderate stomach and bowel complaints [4]. A 2019 systematic review of skin trials found no reported adverse events [72], and a 2026 review found a low rate of adverse events [34]. In rheumatoid arthritis trials of tiny doses of type II collagen, one (20 to 2,500 micrograms a day) found no treatment-related adverse events [73], and another (0.1 mg a day) found fewer and milder side effects than methotrexate [74]. Stop taking collagen and talk to your doctor if you develop a rash or another new symptom.

### Liver Effects

There is no good public evidence on this yet. If you have liver disease, ask your doctor before starting.

### Allergic Reactions

- **Fish collagen allergy:** In 101 people with fish allergy, 21% were sensitised to fish collagen, and eight of them had no antibodies to parvalbumin, the main fish allergen, for some fish species [2]. Standard fish allergy tests contain little collagen, so people with fish allergy may react to fish collagen even after a negative test [2].
- **Egg allergy:** Eggshell membrane products come from eggs, so ask your doctor first if you have an egg allergy.

### Kidney Stone Risk

Hydroxyproline, an amino acid in collagen, may be turned into glyoxylate, which is thought to be the main source of oxalate [3]. On controlled diets, urinary oxalate was 43% higher with 30 g of gelatin than with 30 g of whey protein, and gelatin loads of 5 and 10 g changed urinary oxalate while loads of 1 and 2 g did not [3]. People who have had calcium oxalate kidney stones may want to check with their doctor before taking collagen, especially at higher doses.

### Blood Sugar Effects

Collagen peptides may reduce blood glucose, as marine collagen peptides did in a trial of people with type 2 diabetes on their usual medicines [1]. If you take blood sugar-lowering medicines (insulin, metformin, glyburide), check with your doctor and monitor your blood sugar when you start collagen.

### Tryptophan Depletion

Collagen has no tryptophan [27], an amino acid the body uses to make serotonin. In people whose depression had lifted on antidepressants, a tryptophan-free amino acid drink brought back depression in 14 of 21 [75]. Eating very large amounts of collagen in place of other protein could, in theory, lower tryptophan. This is unlikely at usual doses: a diet can take 36% of its protein as collagen peptides and still meet amino acid needs, and 2.5 to 15 g a day is below that level [27].

### BSE/Prion Concern

There is a theoretical risk that bovine collagen could carry the prion that causes bovine spongiform encephalopathy (BSE, or "mad cow disease"). The risk is thought to be very low. US food rules bar prohibited cattle materials from human food, define specified risk material as tissues such as brain and spinal cord from cattle 30 months and older, and exclude gelatin, hides and hide-derived products from the prohibited materials [76].

### Breast Cancer Concern

Concern has been raised that collagen could promote breast cancer recurrence. In a mouse model of breast cancer, a signal that recruits immune cells to residual tumours promoted recurrence, and those cells may add to collagen deposits in the tumour [77]. This is a finding about collagen made inside tumours, not a study of collagen supplements.

### Interference with Laboratory Tests

Gelatin loads of 5 and 10 g changed blood hydroxyproline [3], so collagen and gelatin may affect hydroxyproline blood or urine tests. If you are having one, ask your doctor whether to avoid collagen and gelatin beforehand.

## 7. Drug Interactions

### Blood Sugar-Lowering Medications

Collagen peptides lowered blood glucose in a trial of people with type 2 diabetes who kept taking their usual medicines [1], and a collagen tripeptide blocks DPP-4 in the laboratory [60]. Combined with insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas (glyburide, glipizide) or other diabetes medicines, this could in theory raise the risk of low blood sugar. Monitor your blood sugar if you add collagen to a diabetes medicine.

### Other Considerations

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Does collagen actually work for wrinkles?**
Not on current pooled evidence. A 2026 meta-analysis of 35 trials found no reliable effect on wrinkles, while finding benefits for hydration and elasticity, and suggested trials of 8 to 12 weeks may be too short to show it [11]. A 2025 meta-analysis found no effect in trials without company funding or in high-quality trials [12].

**Is collagen powder better than collagen pills?**
Neither form is better in itself. What matters is the dose: skin trials used 0.5 to 10 g a day [11], which may take several capsules but fits in one scoop of powder.

**Is marine (fish) collagen better than bovine collagen?**
A head-to-head study found comparable uptake from fish, pig and cow collagen hydrolysates [8]. There is no strong evidence that one source works better. People with fish allergy should be careful with fish collagen [2].

**Does bone broth provide enough collagen?**
Bone broth contains collagen, but the amount per serving varies with the bones and how they are cooked, and bone broth has not been tested in the skin or joint trials described above.

**Can I take collagen and whey protein together?**
Yes. Whey raises muscle protein synthesis more than collagen does [28][29], so collagen is not a substitute for whey or other complete proteins for building muscle.

**Does vitamin C improve collagen supplement effectiveness?**
In one trial, 500 mg of vitamin C taken with 3 g of collagen peptides gave no further gain in skin hydration or elasticity [44].

**How long do the benefits last after stopping collagen?**
Few studies have looked. In two trials, skin elasticity stayed higher 4 weeks after stopping [37][39]. For bone, a 4-year study looked only at continued use [59].

**Is collagen safe during pregnancy?**
There is no good public evidence on collagen in pregnancy. Ask your doctor or midwife before starting any supplement.
