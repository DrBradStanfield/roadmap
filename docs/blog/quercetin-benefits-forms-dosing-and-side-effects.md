## Current view (reviewed 25 October 2026)

Brad leaves quercetin and fisetin out of his recommendations until there is robust, published human data showing a benefit (Brad's current guidance, 31 Jul 2026). The Interventions Testing Program trialed fisetin and saw no healthspan, lifespan or senolytic activity, so in his opinion the preclinical work does not support quercetin or fisetin supplements (Brad's current guidance, 19 Feb 2026). The famous 2019 study of dasatinib plus quercetin, a senolytic combination, in diabetic kidney disease included just nine people and lasted three days, with no placebo group ([Brad, 1 Jul 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-everyone-took-never-worked)). In an Alzheimer's trial of the same combination, cognitive and neuroimaging endpoints did not significantly differ from baseline, and the first lung-fibrosis study, of fourteen patients with no comparison group, was designed only to check whether the drugs were tolerable ([Brad, 1 Jul 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-everyone-took-never-worked)). After years of headlines, there is still not a single trial showing these supplements deliver a real health benefit in humans, though he thinks a properly run human trial might one day surprise us ([Brad, 1 Jul 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-everyone-took-never-worked)).

## Key safety points

- Quercetin's metabolites strongly displaced warfarin from its carrier protein in lab tests, and the authors warn that high doses could interfere with warfarin therapy. If you take warfarin, check with your doctor first [89].
- In healthy volunteers it raised blood levels of diclofenac and pravastatin and lowered midazolam levels. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting [78][82][84].
- In rats it raised blood levels of amiodarone, a heart-rhythm drug where small changes matter. If you take amiodarone, ask your doctor first [83].
- Intravenous quercetin damaged the kidneys at high doses in a cancer trial, and animal studies suggest it may worsen existing kidney damage. With kidney disease, talk to your doctor first [64][68].
- Animal studies raise a concern that it may promote estrogen-dependent cancers, and lab and rat studies show it can disrupt the thyroid. Ask your doctor first if either applies to you [68][74].
- Doses up to 2,000 mg a day were tolerated for one week, but there are no adequate safety data for doses of 1,000 mg or more taken for more than 12 weeks [68][69].
- Side effects in human studies have been rare and mild [68].

## 1. Overview

Quercetin is one of the flavonoids, a group of natural plant compounds built on two benzene rings linked through a heterocyclic pyrone ring [77]. It is the most abundant flavonoid in fruit and vegetables [74].

Quercetin occurs in plants mainly as glycosides, bound to sugars; rutin (quercetin rutinoside) in tea is one example [20]. Before absorption, two enzymes in the lining of the small intestine, lactase-phlorizin hydrolase and cytosolic beta-glucosidase, strip off the sugar [5].

In a survey of US health professionals, onions, tea and apples were the richest sources of flavonols and flavones [8].

Mean intake of flavonols and flavones in that survey was about 20 to 22 mg per day, and quercetin made up 73% in women and 76% in men [8]. An older US estimate put average daily quercetin intake at 25 mg [77].

Cell and animal studies point to anticancer, anti-inflammatory and antiviral actions [9], and quercetin has been studied extensively as an antioxidant [11]. Reviews describe anti-allergic effects such as inhibition of histamine release and of IgE antibody formation [13], and quercetin blocked histamine release from cultured human mast cells [14].

In people, the trials are mostly short and small, and they hint at modest effects on blood pressure, chronic prostatitis, rheumatoid arthritis and seasonal allergy symptoms. Quercetin has also attracted attention as a senolytic, a compound meant to clear senescent (aged, dysfunctional) cells, in small early trials combined with the cancer drug dasatinib [15][17]. Brad's view above concerns that use against ageing. The evidence for other uses is limited by small samples, short durations and inconsistent results.

Plain quercetin is poorly absorbed: in rats, only 2.0% of an oral dose reached the blood [22]. In people with an ileostomy, 24 ± 9% of pure quercetin and 52 ± 15% of the quercetin glucosides in onions were absorbed from the small intestine [28]. This has driven development of formulations meant to improve absorption, including Quercetin Phytosome (lecithin-bound) and EMIQ (enzymatically modified isoquercitrin), discussed in the Forms and Bioavailability section below.

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Standard Forms

**Quercetin (free-form/aglycone):** The most basic supplement form. Fat helps: in a crossover study, 1095 mg of quercetin aglycone taken with a high-fat breakfast (15.4 g of fat) raised total blood quercetin (AUC) by 32% compared with a fat-free breakfast [88].

**Quercetin dihydrate:** Each quercetin molecule carries two bound water molecules, so a given weight of the dihydrate holds slightly less quercetin than the same weight of plain quercetin.

**Rutin (quercetin-3-O-rutinoside):** A quercetin glycoside found in tea [20]. In six healthy men, a red onion soup providing 47 mg of quercetin led to similar urinary quercetin as a quercetin dihydrate tablet providing 544 mg [19]. Blood quercetin peaks hours later after rutin than after free quercetin, and absorption from rutin varies widely between people [20].

### Enhanced Bioavailability Formulations

**Quercetin Phytosome:** A formulation that complexes quercetin with food-grade lecithin. Twelve healthy volunteers took it on an empty stomach in a crossover study, and it gave blood quercetin levels up to 20 times higher than usually obtained with plain quercetin [21]. The study did not test it alongside a fatty meal, the usual advice for plain quercetin.

**EMIQ (Enzymatically Modified Isoquercitrin):** Made by adding glucose chains to isoquercitrin, a quercetin glycoside derived from rutin [23]. In rats, 35% of an oral dose of EMIQ reached the blood, against 12% for isoquercitrin and 2.0% for plain quercetin [22]. In people, EMIQ was absorbed more efficiently than isoquercitrin or rutin [23].

### Pharmacokinetics

Once absorbed, quercetin circulates as conjugates. After an onion meal, the main forms in plasma were quercetin-3-glucuronide, 3'-methylquercetin-3-glucuronide and quercetin-3'-sulfate, and no glucosides were found [27]. After quercetin or rutin, plasma held glucuronides and sulfates of quercetin and some free quercetin, but no rutin [20].

Urine carried only 0.5% of the absorbed quercetin in an ileostomy study [28].

### How to Take Quercetin

Fat helps absorption, so take quercetin with food that includes some fat: a high-fat breakfast raised peak blood quercetin by 45% compared with a fat-free one [88].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Blood Pressure

Pooled trials suggest quercetin may lower blood pressure slightly. A meta-analysis of 7 trials (587 patients) found reductions in systolic blood pressure (WMD: -3.04 mm Hg, 95% CI: -5.75, -0.33) and diastolic blood pressure (WMD: -2.63 mm Hg, 95% CI: -3.26, -2.01), with the clearer effect in trials using 500 mg a day or more [31]. A later meta-analysis of 17 trials (896 participants) found similar falls in systolic (WMD, -3.09 mmHg; 95%CI, -4.59 to -1.59) and diastolic blood pressure [33].

### Blood Sugar and Insulin Resistance

Quercetin may lower blood sugar and insulin slightly at best, and effects vary with dose and trial length.

A placebo-controlled trial among 84 women with polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) gave 1 g of quercetin a day (two 500 mg capsules) for 12 weeks. HOMA-IR, an index of insulin resistance, was lower with quercetin (1.84) than with placebo (2.21) [34].

A meta-analysis of trials in metabolic syndrome and related disorders found no overall effect on fasting glucose, insulin resistance or HbA1c; fasting glucose fell only in trials lasting at least 8 weeks or using at least 500 mg a day [35].

The 17-trial meta-analysis found no significant change in glucose concentrations [33].

### Chronic Prostatitis

Small trials suggest quercetin may ease chronic prostatitis. Men with category III chronic pelvic pain syndrome were given quercetin 500 mg or placebo twice daily for 1 month in a blinded trial; 67% on quercetin improved their symptom score by at least 25%, against 20% on placebo; 13 men on placebo and 15 on quercetin completed it [37].

In an open-label follow-up without a placebo group, 17 men took a supplement combining quercetin with bromelain and papain, and 82% improved by at least 25% [37].

### Rheumatoid Arthritis

In a placebo-controlled trial, 50 women with rheumatoid arthritis had quercetin 500 mg a day or placebo for 8 weeks. Quercetin eased early morning stiffness and pain in the morning and after activity, and lowered disease activity (DAS-28) and disability scores compared with placebo [38]. Tender and swollen joint counts did not differ between the groups [38].

### Seasonal Allergy

In cultured human mast cells, quercetin blocked histamine and other inflammatory signals [14]. It also blocked the rise in cell calcium and the activation of NF-kappa B [14]. Quercetin was more effective than cromolyn (a prescription mast cell stabilizer) at blocking IL-8 and TNF release from human LAD2 mast cells stimulated by substance P [14].

In rats given an allergy-like nasal reaction with a chemical irritant, oral quercetin for 5 to 7 days reduced sneezing and nasal rubbing [39]. Human trials are few and small, and the benefit may be modest.

**Study 1, EMIQ for cedar pollen allergy:** In a placebo-controlled trial in 24 people with Japanese cedar pollen allergy, 100 mg of EMIQ a day for 8 weeks, starting 4 weeks before pollen release, lowered the combined eye symptom and medication score but did not improve nasal symptoms [24]. IgE levels did not fall significantly [24].

**Study 2, Quercetin Phytosome for pollen allergy:** In a placebo-controlled trial in 66 people with pollen allergy symptoms, 200 mg of quercetin a day as Quercetin Phytosome for 4 weeks improved several symptom scores, including eye itching, sneezing, nasal discharge and sleep disorder, compared with placebo [42].

### Viral Infections

In laboratory and animal studies, quercetin 3-beta-O-d-glucoside (Q3G, isoquercitrin) was active against Ebola and Zika viruses [44][45], and a related quercetin galactoside bound a key enzyme of the SARS coronavirus [46]. Against Ebola, Q3G appears to act on the early steps of viral entry into cells [44].

**Ebola (animal study):** Q3G protected mice from Ebola even when given as little as 30 minutes before infection [44].

**Upper respiratory tract infections in athletes:** In a trial of 40 cyclists, all trained men, 1000 mg of quercetin a day, taken for 3 weeks before, during and for 2 weeks after 3 days of cycling for 3 hours a day, cut upper respiratory tract infections in the 2 weeks after exercise compared with placebo (1 of 20 against 9 of 20) [48]. Quercetin did not change exercise-induced shifts in immune measures such as natural killer cell activity [48].

**Large URTI prevention trial:** A randomized, placebo-controlled trial in 1002 adults taking one of two quercetin doses, 500 and 1000 mg/day, for 12 weeks found no overall effect on URTI rates or symptoms [49]. In a subgroup of 325 adults aged 40 and older who rated themselves as fit, 1000 mg a day reduced total sick days by 31% and severity by 36% [49].

#### COVID-19 Studies

In a pilot trial, 120 healthcare workers were given Quercetin Phytosome 250 mg twice a day or placebo for 3 months. COVID-19 occurred in 1 of 60 on quercetin and 4 of 60 on placebo, and the authors reported a protection factor of 14% [50].

Two small open-label trials without a placebo tested it in outpatients with early COVID-19:

**Early COVID-19 (study 1):** Among 152 outpatients, adding 1000 mg of Quercetin Phytosome a day to usual treatment for 30 days was linked to less frequent and shorter hospitalization, less need for oxygen, fewer ICU admissions and fewer deaths [52].

**Early COVID-19 (study 2):** Among 42 outpatients, 16 of 21 people given Quercetin Phytosome with standard care tested negative after one week, against 2 of 21 on standard care alone [51]. One patient on standard care died on day 20 [51].

**Severe COVID-19:** In an open-label trial in 60 hospital patients with severe COVID-19 who were receiving remdesivir or favipiravir, adding 1000 mg of quercetin daily for 7 days was linked to partly earlier discharge, but differences in deaths, ICU admissions and ICU stay were not significant [53].

These trials were small, unblinded or without a placebo, so they cannot show whether quercetin helps with COVID-19.

### Memory and Cognition

In a 26-week trial in 46 healthy overweight older adults, capsules providing 200 mg of resveratrol and 320 mg of quercetin a day improved word retention compared with placebo [56]. A later 26-week trial of 200 mg of resveratrol a day in 60 older adults found no significant effect on its main memory test [57]. These results cannot be credited to quercetin.

### Senolytic Effects (Aging Research)

Quercetin, typically in combination with the cancer drug dasatinib, has attracted attention as a senolytic agent, meant to clear senescent cells that build up with age [15][17].

**Alzheimer's disease (Phase 1):** In an open-label phase 1 trial (NCT04063124) of dasatinib plus quercetin, five people with early Alzheimer's disease completed 12 weeks [15]. Dasatinib reached the spinal fluid but quercetin was not detected there, and cognitive and neuroimaging endpoints did not significantly differ from baseline [15]. The treatment was well tolerated [15].

**Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis:** A first-in-human, open-label pilot study gave 14 patients dasatinib (100 mg a day) plus quercetin (1250 mg a day) three days a week over three weeks [17]. It showed the approach was feasible; walking and other physical function tests improved, but lung function did not change [17].

**Diabetic kidney disease:** A 2019 study of dasatinib plus quercetin included just nine people and lasted three days, with no placebo group ([Brad, 1 Jul 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-everyone-took-never-worked)).

The senolytic research uses quercetin in combination with dasatinib, a prescription cancer drug, and these results cannot be extrapolated to quercetin supplementation alone. Brad's reading is that these were pilots that only checked safety and feasibility, and that no trial has yet shown a real health benefit in humans ([Brad, 1 Jul 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-everyone-took-never-worked)).

### Fatty Liver Disease (MASLD)

A placebo-controlled crossover trial in 41 adults with fatty liver disease (then called NAFLD) gave 500 mg of quercetin a day for 12 weeks; 36 completed it [58]. Liver fat fell from 11.5% to 9.6% with quercetin, against a 0.1% fall with placebo [58]. Body weight fell by 1.5 ± 2.6 kg with quercetin and 0.2 ± 1.8 kg with placebo, which may explain part of the effect [58]. Other secondary and safety outcomes did not differ, and no adverse events were linked to quercetin [58].

### Exercise Performance

Quercetin does not appear to improve exercise performance meaningfully. A meta-analysis of seven studies (288 subjects), with a mean daily intake of 960 ± 127 mg, found only trivial gains and concluded that it is unlikely to boost endurance exercise, whether people are trained or not [59].

### Testosterone

In women with PCOS, 1 g of quercetin a day for 12 weeks slightly lowered testosterone compared with placebo (0.71 ng/dl against 0.77 ng/dl) [34]. In rats, quercetin lessened the fall in testosterone caused by cadmium [61], but this does not show that it raises testosterone in healthy people.

### Sleep

In rats, quercetin injected into the abdomen increased non-REM sleep and decreased REM sleep [62].

### Cancer

In laboratory and animal studies, quercetin can slow cancer cell growth and promote cancer cell death [12]. Human evidence is very limited.

In a phase I trial, quercetin given by intravenous infusion to people with cancer caused kidney toxicity at higher doses [64]. Tumor markers fell in two patients, a woman with ovarian cancer and a patient with hepatoma, a liver cancer [64].

In a trial in 20 adults having high-dose chemotherapy for blood cancers, 250 mg capsules of quercetin taken twice a day for four weeks did not significantly reduce oral mucositis (a common chemotherapy side effect) compared with placebo, and mucositis was more severe in the quercetin group (mean severity 2.6 against 2), which the authors say may reflect poorer oral health in that group [65].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

There is no established Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) or Daily Value (DV) for quercetin, as it is not classified as an essential nutrient.

### Dosing by Indication (from clinical studies)

| Indication | Dose | Duration | Source |
|------------|:----:|:--------:|--------|
| Chronic prostatitis | 500 mg twice daily (1,000 mg/day) | 1 month | Shoskes 1999 [37] |
| Insulin resistance (PCOS) | 1 g/day (two 500 mg capsules) | 12 weeks | Rezvan 2017 [34] |
| Blood pressure | 500 mg/day or more | Varied | Serban 2016 [31] |
| Rheumatoid arthritis | 500 mg/day | 8 weeks | Javadi 2017 [38] |
| Fatty liver disease (MASLD) | 500 mg/day | 12 weeks | Li 2024 [58] |
| Seasonal allergy (EMIQ) | 100 mg EMIQ daily | 8 weeks, from 4 weeks before pollen season | Hirano 2009 [24] |
| Seasonal allergy (Phytosome) | 200 mg quercetin/day (as Phytosome) | 4 weeks | Yamada 2022 [42] |
| COVID-19 prevention (Phytosome) | Quercetin Phytosome 250 mg twice a day | 3 months | Rondanelli 2022 [50] |
| URI prevention in athletes | 1000 mg/day | 3 weeks before to 2 weeks after a training block | Nieman 2007 [48] |

### General Supplementation Guidance

- **Typical supplement dose:** Supplements, mostly plain quercetin, often supply daily doses of up to 1000 mg [68].
- **Clinical safety range:** Adverse effects in human studies have been rare and mild, but there are no adequate data on doses of 1000 mg or more taken for more than 12 weeks [68]. Up to 2000 mg a day was tolerated for one week [69].
- **How to take:** Alongside food with some fat, which helps absorption [88]. Several trials gave 500 mg twice daily [37][82].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

Adverse effects of supplemental quercetin have been rarely reported in human studies, and any such effects were mild [68].

### Dose-Escalation Safety Data

In a one-week trial in people with COPD, a chronic lung disease, 500, 1000 or 2000 mg of quercetin a day caused no drug-related severe adverse events; one patient reported reflux, which occurred in both the placebo and quercetin groups [69].

There are no adequate data to judge the safety of doses of 1000 mg or more taken for more than 12 weeks [68].

### Long-Term Animal Toxicity

In a 2-year feeding study, male rats given high doses of quercetin had more renal tubule adenomas, a type of kidney tumor; female rats showed no such effect [77]. Rats in the top dose group ate about 1,900 mg/kg of body weight a day, far above supplement doses [77].

### Kidney Concerns

In animals, quercetin may make existing kidney damage worse [68]. Given intravenously in a cancer trial, quercetin caused kidney toxicity: 3 of 14 patients at 945 mg/m2 had clinically significant renal toxicity, and it was dose-limiting at 1700 mg/m2 [64]. People with kidney disease should talk to their doctor before taking it.

### Blood Pressure

Because quercetin may lower blood pressure a little [31], take care if your blood pressure runs low or you take medicine to lower it.

### Thyroid Function

In thyroid cells, quercetin reduced iodide uptake and the activity of thyroid-specific genes, and it lowered radioiodine uptake in rats; the authors call it a thyroid disruptor and advise caution with supplemental use [74]. Flavonoids may also reduce the thyroid hormone available to tissues by inhibiting deiodinase enzymes, and in thyroid tumor cells they can decrease radioiodine uptake, which could weaken radioiodine therapy [75]. If you have thyroid disease or are due for radioiodine treatment, ask your doctor first.

### Estrogenic Activity

In animal studies, quercetin may help tumors grow, especially estrogen-dependent cancers [68]. Those with hormone-sensitive conditions should consult a healthcare provider before using quercetin.

### Iron Absorption

Quercetin binds iron. In rats, it reduced the release of iron from gut cells into the blood and lowered the iron exporter ferroportin [72]. The authors suggest that polyphenol-rich diets might limit the rate of iron absorption from the gut, which could help people at risk of iron overload [72]. The same effect could work against people who are low in iron, so take care if you are prone to anemia.

### Alcohol Interaction

Quercetin may slow the clearance of acetaldehyde, a by-product of alcohol [76]. In the laboratory, quercetin-3-glucuronide, a common circulating quercetin metabolite, inhibited ALDH2, the enzyme that breaks down acetaldehyde (IC50 of 9.6 µM) [76]. White wine and other alcoholic drinks carry far less quercetin than red wine, and the researchers suggest this could explain red wine headaches; the idea still needs testing in people [76].

### Pregnancy and Lactation

Safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding has not been established, so check with your doctor before using quercetin supplements.

## 6. Drug Interactions

Quercetin can change how much of some drugs reaches the blood [68]. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Warfarin

In laboratory tests, quercetin's main metabolites bound strongly to albumin, the blood protein that carries warfarin, and displaced warfarin from it; the authors warn that high quercetin doses could strongly interfere with warfarin therapy [89]. The same lab study found that quercetin and its metabolites inhibited CYP2C9, the enzyme that clears warfarin, weakly or not at all, and judged a CYP2C9 effect on warfarin very unlikely [89]. A human study points the other way for CYP2C9 in general: quercetin raised blood levels of diclofenac, apparently by inhibiting CYP2C9 [82]. If you take warfarin, check with your doctor before using quercetin.

### OATP1B1 Substrates (Increased Absorption)

Quercetin may raise blood levels of drugs carried into the liver by the transporter OATP1B1. Sixteen healthy men had quercetin 500 mg or placebo once a day for 14 days, then one 40 mg pravastatin tablet; quercetin increased pravastatin exposure (AUC) by 24% and prolonged its half-life by 14% [78].

Many statins are OATP1B1 substrates, and the transporter also handles repaglinide and fexofenadine [79].

### CYP2C9 Inhibition (Increased Drug Effects)

Quercetin inhibited the enzyme CYP2C9 in lab tests on human liver enzyme preparations [81]. In 12 healthy volunteers, a 10-day course of quercetin, 500 mg twice a day, raised blood levels of diclofenac (Voltaren, Flector), a CYP2C9 substrate, and the authors advise caution with drugs cleared by CYP2C9 [82].

### Amiodarone

In rats, 14 days of quercetin before a dose of the heart-rhythm drug amiodarone (Cordarone, Pacerone) raised peak amiodarone levels by 45.52% [83]. The authors attribute the effect to quercetin blocking drug transporters and CYP enzymes, and suggest amiodarone doses may need adjusting [83]. Amiodarone has a narrow therapeutic index, so small changes in its blood levels can matter [83]; anyone on it should ask their doctor before using quercetin.

### CYP3A Induction (Decreased Drug Effects)

Quercetin may speed up CYP3A, an enzyme that clears many drugs, which could make those drugs weaker. Eighteen healthy men had quercetin 500 mg or placebo once a day for 13 days; quercetin lowered blood levels of one 7.5 mg midazolam tablet given on day 14, and the effect was more prominent in men carrying the CYP3A5*1 gene variant [84]. In lab tests on human liver enzymes, by contrast, quercetin was a potent CYP3A4 inhibitor [81], so the direction of the effect in people is not settled.

### Chemotherapy Agents

If you are having cancer treatment, check with your oncologist before using quercetin supplements.

## 7. Dietary Sources

Quercetin in foods is mostly bound to sugars as glycosides [20]. In one study, the quercetin glucosides in onions were absorbed better than pure quercetin or rutin [28].

### Top Food Sources

| Food | Notes |
|------|------|
| Onions | Among the richest US sources of flavonols [8] |
| Tea | Among the richest US sources of flavonols [8] |
| Apples | Among the richest US sources of flavonols [8] |
| Red wine | Much higher quercetin than white wine [76] |
| Tomatoes | Higher quercetin when grown organically [87] |

### Practical Notes on Dietary Quercetin

- **Organic produce may contain more quercetin.** Ten-year mean quercetin levels in organically grown tomatoes were 79% higher than in conventionally grown ones, a rise that tracked increasing soil organic matter [87].
- **Food-source quercetin can be well absorbed.** The quercetin glucosides in onions were absorbed better than pure quercetin [28], and an onion soup with 47 mg of quercetin gave similar urinary quercetin to a 544 mg dihydrate tablet [19].
- **A food-first approach** built on onions, tea and apples raises dietary quercetin intake [8].
