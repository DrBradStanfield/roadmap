## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. A 2014 review found few well-controlled human studies of shilajit and called for more using standardised products [9]. Small placebo-controlled trials suggest that purified shilajit at 250 mg twice a day for 90 days may raise testosterone in men aged 45 to 55 years [12], and that 250 mg or 500 mg of an extract, taken daily for 48 weeks, may slow bone loss in postmenopausal women with osteopenia [8], but these are single trials and larger ones are needed [9]. Short trials reported few side effects, yet lead, arsenic and mercury above permissible limits have been found in some samples [33][34] and thallium in some supplements [27].

## Key safety points

- Shilajit lowered blood glucose in rats [43], so it could add to diabetes medicines. If you take them, ask your doctor first and watch for signs of low blood sugar.
- In one case report, shilajit was a possible cofactor in anaphylaxis [36]. If you develop hives with trouble breathing or feel faint, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
- Avoid shilajit in pregnancy. A pregnant woman who took mumijo developed persistent high blood pressure and low potassium [35].
- Some shilajit samples and supplements contain lead, arsenic, mercury or thallium [27][33][34]. Never take raw shilajit [17], and choose products that publish heavy-metal test results.
- Shilajit contains iron, aluminium and other metals [33]. Talk to your doctor first if you have iron overload or kidney disease.
- Short trials reported no change in liver or kidney tests [14][17] and no more side effects than placebo [37]. Long-term safety is unknown.

## 1. Overview

Shilajit is a pale-brown to blackish-brown exudate that seeps from rock layers in many mountain ranges, especially the Himalayas and the Hindu Kush [31]. It is also called mumie, moomiyo or mummiyo [9], and momiai or mineral pitch [37].

It has been used as a rejuvenator and adaptogen for thousands of years in the traditional medicine of several countries [31]. Ayurveda and Siddha medicine call it a rasayana, meaning a rejuvenator [6]. A 2011 review found it in 20 classical formulations and 24 proprietary drugs, yet concluded that its bioactivity lacks substantial evidence [6].

Shilajit is now sold for testosterone, fertility, energy, bone health and memory. Human trials are few and small, and some used a branded extract. A 2014 review called for more well-controlled human studies of standardised products [9].

### Chemical Composition

The full chemical makeup of shilajit is not known with certainty, and it differs from region to region [32]. Its main parts are:

- **Humic substances:** More than 80% of its weight, according to a 2025 review [32].
- **Minerals:** Almost 20% of its weight is calcium, potassium and magnesium [32]. Samples also hold many other elements, including iron, aluminium, lead, arsenic and mercury [33].
- **Other organic compounds:** Amino acids (mainly glycine), proteins, fatty acids, and phenolic compounds such as caffeic and gallic acid [32].
- **Fulvic acid and dibenzo-alpha-pyrones:** These and their derivatives appear to be the main active constituents [9]. Fulvic acid content varies widely between products (see Forms below).

### Geological Formation

Shilajit is thought to form over centuries as microorganisms break down certain plants [22], leaving a mix of humic substances and plant and microbial metabolites in the rock [31]. Its origin is still debated: some researchers think it forms from plant residues or animal droppings, others that it is a mineral that seeps slowly from rock [32].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

Shilajit is sold as purified resin and as more concentrated extracts. The main difference is how much fulvic acid each holds.

### Comparison of Forms

| Form | Fulvic Acid Content | Processing | Doses in Trials | Key Characteristics |
|------|:-:|:-:|:-:|------|
| **Purified Resin** | 6.61% in one trial [17] | Soaked in water, filtered, boiled down and dried [17] | 2 g a day [17]; 500 mg a day [28] | Far less fulvic acid per gram than extracts. |
| **Standardized Extract** | 56.75% fulvic acids in one standardised product [41] | Processed and standardised to set levels of fulvic acids and dibenzo-alpha-pyrones [41] | See the dosing table below | Used in most trials, including the branded extract PrimaVie [15]. |

### Key Bioavailability Considerations

**Resin vs. extract potency.** The resin in one trial held 6.61% fulvic acid [17], while one standardised extract held 56.75% fulvic acids [41]. A gram of resin therefore delivers much less fulvic acid than a gram of extract. Most trials used extracts, so their results may not apply to resins.

**Standardization variability.** Some trials used a branded extract, PrimaVie [15][30]. Results from one standardised extract may not apply to other products.

**How to take shilajit.** In the sperm study, capsules were taken twice daily after major meals [14].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

Human trials of shilajit are few, small and short; the longest lasted 48 weeks [8]. Some used a branded extract, and at least one was funded by Natreon Inc. [15]. A 2014 review called for more well-controlled human studies [9].

### Testosterone and Male Fertility

Two small trials suggest shilajit may raise testosterone, and one uncontrolled study reported better sperm measures. Larger independent trials are needed.

**Testosterone in healthy older men.** In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, men aged 45 to 55 years took purified shilajit at 250 mg twice a day for 90 days [12]. Total testosterone, free testosterone and DHEAS rose significantly compared with placebo (P < 0.05), while LH and FSH stayed steady [12].

**Testosterone and fertility in oligospermic men.** In a study with no placebo group, 35 men with low sperm counts took 100 mg of processed shilajit twice daily for 90 days [14]. In the 28 who finished, total sperm count rose 61.4%, motility rose 12.4% to 17.4%, and serum testosterone rose 23.5% compared with baseline [14]. Without a control group, these changes could reflect chance, regression to the mean or a placebo effect.

**Systematic review.** A 2024 review of 52 studies of testosterone boosters found that most failed to raise total testosterone. It rated purified shilajit extract (PrimaVie) as possibly effective for men with late-onset hypogonadism [30].

**Synthesis.** The evidence rests on one small placebo-controlled trial and one uncontrolled study [12][14]. A 2024 review rated the extract only "possibly effective" [30]. Larger, independently funded trials are needed before shilajit can be recommended for low testosterone.

### Energy, Physical Performance, and Fatigue

**Fatigued muscular strength in recreationally active men.** In a trial funded by Natreon Inc., 63 recreationally active men took 250 mg or 500 mg of PrimaVie shilajit a day, or placebo, for 8 weeks [15]. In the stronger half of the men, the 500 mg group lost less strength after a fatiguing leg exercise (8.9% decline, against 17.0% on 250 mg and 16.0% on placebo) and had lower resting serum hydroxyproline [15]. The 250 mg dose did no better than placebo [15].

**Open-label pilot study.** In a 28-day study with no placebo group, 25 healthy men aged 21 to 55 years took 500 mg a day of a shilajit resin (TruBlk) [28]. Within the group, leg press strength rose 12.94%, lean mass rose 1.5% and fatigue scores fell 32.40% [28]. Several authors list affiliations with a botanicals company. Without a control group, training, expectation and chance could explain changes of this size.

**Mechanism.** In rats, shilajit reversed signs of chronic fatigue and protected mitochondrial function [41]. A 2014 review cited animal and human data for better physical performance and less fatigue, with more ATP production [9].

**Synthesis.** One sponsored trial found less strength loss at 500 mg a day, in a subgroup [15]. The rest of the evidence is uncontrolled or from animals.

### Cholesterol and Lipid Profiles

In a double-blind trial in India, 20 medical students aged 16 to 30 years took 2 g a day of purified shilajit (6.61% fulvic acid) for 45 days, and 10 took sugar capsules [17]. In the shilajit group, values changed as follows [17]:

- Triglycerides: 116.9 mg/dL before, 92.7 mg/dL after
- Total cholesterol: 145.3 mg/dL before, 125.1 mg/dL after
- LDL cholesterol: 81.8 mg/dL before, 63.30 mg/dL after
- VLDL cholesterol: 23.30 mg/dL before, 18.50 mg/dL after
- HDL cholesterol: 41.2 mg/dL before, 43.6 mg/dL after

The paper tested each group before and after treatment; it reported no test between the shilajit and placebo groups [17]. The volunteers were young and healthy.

A later trial in overweight adults found no change in the lipid profile after 8 weeks of 250 mg twice a day [19]. In rats fed a high-fat diet, shilajit lowered triglycerides, total cholesterol and LDL [43]. The human evidence is too weak to recommend shilajit for cholesterol.

### Blood Sugar and Diabetes

**No effect in human trials.** In the lipid trial, blood sugar stayed the same after 2 g a day of purified resin for 45 days [17]. In overweight adults, 250 mg twice a day for 8 weeks did not change blood glucose [19].

**Animal data.** In rats with fatty liver from a high-fat diet, shilajit lowered serum glucose [43].

**Synthesis.** Human trials in people without diabetes found no change in blood sugar [17][19]. The glucose-lowering effect seen in rats has not been tested in people with diabetes, so shilajit cannot be recommended for blood sugar control.

### Collagen Production and Bone Health

**Collagen gene expression.** In a study with no placebo group, overweight or obese adults took 250 mg of a standardised extract twice daily for 8 weeks [19]. Muscle biopsies showed higher expression of a cluster of 17 extracellular matrix probe sets, including collagen, elastin and fibronectin [19]. One author lists an affiliation with Natreon India [19]. In healthy women, 250 mg twice a day for 14 weeks improved skin blood flow compared with baseline or placebo and switched on genes linked to blood-vessel growth [29].

**Collagen synthesis biomarker.** In 35 recreationally trained men, 500 or 1,000 mg a day for 8 weeks raised serum pro-c1α1, a marker of type 1 collagen synthesis, while placebo did not [20]. Whether this improves tendons, bone or skin was not tested.

**Bone mineral density in postmenopausal women with osteopenia.** In a randomised, double-blind trial, 60 postmenopausal women aged 45 to 65 years with osteopenia took placebo or 250 mg or 500 mg of a standardised aqueous shilajit extract daily for 48 weeks [8]:

- Bone density at the lumbar spine and femoral neck fell steadily on placebo.
- Both shilajit doses slowed this loss in a dose-dependent way, with significantly better percentage changes from baseline at 24 and 48 weeks than placebo (p < 0.001).
- Markers of bone turnover CTX-1, BALP and RANKL fell, and OPG rose, in both shilajit groups.
- Oxidative stress markers improved (lower MDA, higher GSH), and hsCRP, a marker of inflammation, fell.

This is the longest shilajit trial described here, but it was small and used one extract, so the results may not apply to other products [8].

**Fracture healing.** In a double-blind trial in Iran, 160 adults with new tibial fractures took two 500 mg capsules of momiai (shilajit) or placebo for 28 days [37]. Bone union took a mean of 129 days, against 153 days on placebo (p < 0.049), and side effects did not differ between groups [37].

### Cognitive Function and Alzheimer's Disease

**Tau protein aggregation (in vitro).** In lab studies, fulvic acid from shilajit blocked tau self-aggregation, a feature of Alzheimer's disease [22]. Fractions of Andean shilajit from Chile also inhibited tau self-aggregation and supported neuron growth in rat and mouse cell cultures [21].

**Human data.** A 2012 review described a clinical trial of shilajit combined with B vitamins in patients with mild Alzheimer's disease [38]. Any effect of a combination cannot be credited to shilajit alone.

**Synthesis.** The evidence is laboratory work plus one combination trial [21][22][38]. Shilajit cannot be recommended for memory or Alzheimer's disease.

### Antioxidant and Anti-inflammatory Effects

A 2011 review noted that the antioxidant activity of shilajit in living animals had been tested at an irrelevant dose and without a positive control [6]. In people, antioxidant and inflammation markers improved only as secondary outcomes: lower MDA, higher GSH and lower hsCRP in the bone trial [8], lower semen MDA in the sperm study [14], and higher SOD, vitamin C and vitamin E in the lipid trial [17]. These are small, secondary findings, not evidence of a clinical benefit.

### Immune Modulation

Fulvic acid fractions from shilajit showed complement-fixing activity in lab tests [40]. A 2011 review judged the immune-modulating activity of shilajit unproven [6].

### Anti-aging and Mitochondrial Function

In a rat model of chronic fatigue, shilajit protected mitochondrial function [41]. A 2014 review linked its effects to more ATP production [9]. Claims about ageing in people rest on this animal and laboratory work.

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Important Caveat

No dose of shilajit is established for any condition. The doses below come from small published trials and are not proven treatment doses.

### Doses Used in Clinical Studies

| Indication | Dose Used | Form | Duration | Study Design | Reference |
|-----------|:-:|:-:|:-:|:-:|------|
| Testosterone (healthy men 45-55) | 250 mg twice daily | Purified shilajit | 90 days | RCT, placebo-controlled | Pandit 2016 [12] |
| Sperm (men with low sperm count) | 100 mg twice daily | Processed shilajit | 90 days | No control group; 28 of 35 finished | Biswas 2010 [14] |
| Fatigue and strength | 250 mg or 500 mg/day | PrimaVie shilajit | 8 weeks | RCT, n=63 | Keller 2019 [15] |
| Collagen marker | 500 mg/day or 1,000 mg/day | Shilajit | 8 weeks | RCT, n=35 | Neltner 2024 [20] |
| Blood lipids | 2 g/day | Purified resin (6.61% fulvic acid) | 45 days | Double-blind, 20 shilajit and 10 placebo | Sharma 2003 [17] |
| Bone density (postmenopausal) | 250 mg or 500 mg/day | Standardised aqueous extract | 48 weeks | RCT, placebo-controlled, n=60 | Pingali 2022 [8] |
| Fracture healing | Two 500 mg capsules | Momiai (shilajit) | 28 days | RCT, placebo-controlled, n=160 | Sadeghi 2020 [37] |
| Physical performance (pilot) | 500 mg/day | Shilajit resin (TruBlk) | 28 days | Open-label, n=25 | Yadav 2026 [28] |

### General Dosing Guidance

- **Extracts:** Most trials used extracts. In the one trial that compared doses for strength, 500 mg a day worked and 250 mg a day did not [15].
- **Purified resin:** The one controlled resin trial used 2 g a day [17].
- **Duration:** The longest trial lasted 48 weeks [8]. How long shilajit takes to work, if it works, is unknown.

### Practical Notes

- No maximum safe dose has been set. Trials have used up to 2 g a day of resin for 45 days [17] and up to 1,000 mg a day of extract for 8 weeks [20].
- Choose products that state their fulvic acid content and publish heavy-metal test results [27][34].
- Without the fulvic acid content, you cannot match the doses used in trials.

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Tolerability

Short trials have reported few problems. In the sperm study, liver and kidney tests were unchanged after 90 days [14]. In the lipid trial, 2 g a day for 45 days left blood pressure, pulse, blood counts, and liver and kidney tests unchanged [17]. No adverse effects were reported in a 14-week skin study [29], no serious ones in a 28-day pilot [28], and side effects in the fracture trial did not differ from placebo [37]. In rats given up to 5,000 mg/kg for 91 days, only the highest dose caused negligible changes in the liver and intestine [39].

However, several important caveats apply:

- The trials were small and short, too small to detect uncommon harms.
- Long-term safety is unknown. The longest trial lasted 48 weeks [8].

### Potential Side Effects

Reported or potential side effects include:

- **Allergic reactions:** A woman developed hives after a meal that included shilajit, then anaphylaxis with breathing difficulty, low blood pressure and loss of consciousness after exertion; the authors judged shilajit a possible cofactor [36]. If you develop hives with trouble breathing or feel faint, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
- **High blood pressure and low potassium:** A pregnant woman who took mumijo for 6 months developed persistent high blood pressure and low potassium, a liquorice-like syndrome. She was treated with potassium and by stopping mumijo [35].
- **Stomach upset or headache:** Some people may get these.

### Heavy Metal Contamination

Heavy metals are the main quality concern with shilajit. A 2024 review found that shilajit can hold around 65 metals, including toxic metals such as lead, arsenic, mercury and cadmium. Most reported levels were under WHO and FDA limits for herbal drugs, but a few studies found levels above them [34].

- Indian and Pakistani shilajit tested in a 2022 study contained lead, arsenic, mercury and aluminium above permissible limits [33].
- A 2025 study found thallium at up to 0.5 µg/g in shilajit supplements, or up to 0.095 µg per pill, and more thallium in some supplements than in raw shilajit [27].
- Raw shilajit carries heavy metals, microbial debris and other impurities that purification is meant to remove [17]. Never take raw shilajit.
- Look for products with third-party test results for lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium and thallium [27][34].

### Blood Sugar Concerns

Human trials in people without diabetes found no change in blood sugar [17][19], but shilajit lowered blood glucose in rats [43]. Use it with caution if you have low blood sugar or take blood sugar-lowering medicines, as it could add to their effect and raise the risk of hypoglycemia.

### Populations That Should Avoid Shilajit

- **Children:** No safety data exist.
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding women:** Should avoid shilajit. A case of high blood pressure and low potassium in pregnancy has been linked to mumijo [35], and safety in breastfeeding is unknown.
- **People with hemochromatosis or iron overload conditions:** Shilajit contains iron [33], which could worsen iron overload.
- **People with kidney disease:** Shilajit contains aluminium and other metals [33], which could be a problem for people whose kidneys clear them less well.

## 6. Drug Interactions

### Documented or Plausible Interactions

| Drug Class | Examples | Interaction Risk | Clinical Implication |
|-----------|---------|:-:|----------|
| **Diabetes medications** | Insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors | Possible | Shilajit lowered blood glucose in rats [43] and could add to the effect of these drugs, causing low blood sugar. Monitor blood sugar closely if combining. |
| **Anticoagulants/blood thinners** | Warfarin, heparin | Unknown | No studies. If you take a blood thinner, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting. |
| **Immunosuppressants** | Cyclosporine, tacrolimus | Theoretical | Fulvic acid from shilajit showed complement-fixing activity in lab tests [40], though a review judged its immune effects unproven [6]. Interference with these drugs is a theoretical risk. |

### Drug Absorption Considerations

In lab work, fulvic acid extracted from shilajit formed a complex with the diuretic furosemide [42]. Such binding could alter how medicines are absorbed. While this interaction has not been studied for shilajit taken by mouth, it is prudent to separate shilajit from medications by at least 2 hours as a precaution.

### General Recommendation

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting. This matters most for diabetes medicines [43].

## 7. Dietary Sources

Shilajit is not a food. It is an exudate from rock layers [31], sold as a supplement.

### Natural Occurrence

Shilajit seeps from rock layers in many mountain ranges, especially the Himalayas and the Hindu Kush [31]. Andean shilajit comes from the Andes of Chile [21]. A 2024 review counted almost 20 regions of the world where it is found [34]. Its composition varies with its origin [32].

### Quality Indicators for Consumers

When evaluating shilajit products:
- **Fulvic acid content disclosure:** Content differs widely, from 6.61% in one purified resin [17] to 56.75% fulvic acids in one standardised extract [41]. A label that states it lets you compare products.
- **Third-party heavy metal testing:** Look for certificates of analysis for lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium and thallium [27][34].

## 9. Summary and Bottom Line

Shilajit is a mineral-rich exudate with a long history in Ayurveda. It contains fulvic acid and dibenzo-alpha-pyrones, but the human evidence for most claims is preliminary.

**What has some evidence (but needs more research):**
- Higher testosterone in men aged 45 to 55 (one small RCT, 250 mg twice a day for 90 days) [12][30]
- Slower bone loss in postmenopausal women with osteopenia (one RCT, 250 mg or 500 mg a day for 48 weeks) [8]
- Less strength loss after fatiguing exercise (one sponsored RCT, 500 mg a day for 8 weeks, in a subgroup) [15]
- Faster healing of tibial fractures (one RCT) [37]

**What lacks convincing human evidence:**
- Cholesterol reduction (one small trial with only before-and-after tests; another found no change) [17][19]
- Blood sugar control (no change in human trials; rat data only) [17][19][43]
- Cognitive function or Alzheimer's prevention (laboratory data and one combination trial) [21][38]
- Anti-aging effects (animal data) [41]
- Immune enhancement (laboratory data) [6][40]

**Safety:** Short trials reported few side effects [14][17][37]. Some samples and supplements carry heavy metals [27][33][34], and case reports link shilajit to anaphylaxis [36] and to high blood pressure with low potassium in pregnancy [35].
