## Current view (reviewed 10 November 2026)

Brad would not take NR or NMN: after years of human trials he does not see a benefit, and in a big trial people with Parkinson's disease did worse on NR than on placebo ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). In that trial, NOPARK, 410 people with early Parkinson's disease took 500 mg of NR twice a day or a placebo for a year; the NR group scored 2.7 points worse on the standard Parkinson's rating scale, a small difference but in the wrong direction, while serious side effects were less common on NR, 8% versus 14%, though that gap could easily be down to chance ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). NR and NMN raise NAD in the blood, but pooled trials show no meaningful benefit for muscle, NMN did not help blood sugar or cholesterol, and in a long COVID trial blood NAD went up about three-fold with no difference from placebo in thinking, fatigue, sleep or mood ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). He also doubts the idea that we all run low on NAD as we age: blood NAD stays remarkably stable as we get older, and older adults who exercised at least three times a week had muscle NAD levels comparable to younger adults ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). He calls NR and NMN megadosing vitamin B3 and does not think that is completely risk-free: high-dose niacin, another form of vitamin B3, did not reduce heart attacks or strokes in people already on a statin, and people with the highest levels of 4PY, a B3 breakdown product that NR and NMN raise, had about twice the risk of a heart attack, stroke or death, though this is an association ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). If you have Parkinson's disease and take NR, raise it with your neurologist; the approach he points to instead is exercise ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)).

## Key safety points

- Nicotinamide, which NAD boosters can supply, raised carbamazepine levels and slowed the breakdown of primidone in a small study; check with your doctor first if you take an anticonvulsant [1].
- Intravenous NADH raised levodopa levels in a small Parkinson's study, and in a year-long trial in early Parkinson's, people on NR did worse than on placebo. If you have Parkinson's, talk to your neurologist before taking or continuing an NAD booster [2][3].
- Animal studies raise a theoretical concern that raising NAD+ may help some cancers grow or spread; if you have cancer or are on cancer treatment, talk to your oncologist before taking an NAD booster [4].
- In old mice, NMN raised markers of kidney inflammation (early mouse evidence, not peer reviewed). If you are older or have kidney disease, ask your doctor first; the authors urge kidney-safety checks in older people [5].
- The European safety opinion on NMN judged up to 300 mg a day safe for adults but excluded pregnant and breastfeeding women [6].
- The US upper limit for supplemental niacin, which covers nicotinic acid and nicotinamide, is 35 mg a day for adults. Very high doses of nicotinamide can harm the liver; a review called adult doses above 3 g a day potentially toxic and discouraged unsupervised use [7][8].
- NR trials have reported nausea, flushing, cramps, headache and bruising, though in several NR and NMN trials no more often than on placebo; long-term safety is not known [9][10][44][56].

## 1. Overview

NAD, short for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, is a coenzyme found in all living cells [11]. It carries electrons from one chemical reaction to the next, which is how cells turn food into usable energy, and it exists in an oxidized form (NAD+) and a reduced form (NADH) [11]. NAD+ also acts as a signalling molecule that helps control hundreds of key processes, from energy metabolism to cell survival (Rajman et al., *Cell Metab*, 2018) [12].

The body makes NAD+ from the amino acid tryptophan and from forms of vitamin B3: nicotinic acid (niacin), nicotinamide (niacinamide) and nicotinamide riboside (NR) [13]. Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) is an intermediate on the way to NAD+ (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [14]. NR and NMN are sold as supplements but are not marketed or labelled as sources of niacin [7].

Interest in NAD-boosting supplements grew from research suggesting that NAD+ falls with age. A 2012 study found that NAD+ decreased in human skin tissue with advancing age (Massudi et al., *PLoS One*, 2012) [15], and a 2013 study in mice showed that raising NAD+ levels could reverse aspects of mitochondrial dysfunction (Gomes et al., *Cell*, 2013) [16]. These findings raised the hope that boosting NAD+ could slow aging.

Newer human studies question how general that decline is. A 2021 review found that claims of an overall fall in NAD+ with age rest on very limited evidence, often from a single tissue or cell type, and that this is particularly true in humans (Peluso et al., *Nutrients*, 2021) [17]. A 2026 study that measured whole-blood NAD+ in seven independent groups of people found that levels stayed stable with age and with lifestyle changes, though they rose with NR, which suggests blood NAD+ may not be a reliable marker of aging (Trętowicz et al., *Nat Metab*, 2026) [18]. A second 2026 study, of 1,068 adults, found that whole-blood NAD did not decline with age in healthy people (it stayed stable in men and rose in women) but was lower in people with cardiovascular disease or high blood pressure; the authors say this does not show that NAD supplements treat either condition (Romenskaja-Avgul et al., *Nat Metab*, 2026) [19]. Muscle may differ: in a 2022 study, muscle NAD+ was lower in older adults, lowest in those who were physically impaired, and closer to young levels in exercise-trained older adults (Janssens et al., *Nat Aging*, 2022) [20].

The three main categories of NAD-boosting supplements are:

- **NAD+/NADH**: the coenzyme itself, taken by mouth; the trials below used a stabilized oral form of NADH (ENADA) [21][22].
- **Nicotinamide riboside (NR)**: a precursor to NAD+ that is converted to NMN by nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1/NRK2), then to NAD+ by NMNATs [11][23]. It is sold as NR chloride (Niagen) [9] and, combined with pterostilbene, in Elysium Health's Basis [24].
- **Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)**: like NR, a precursor to NAD+ [14]. Trials have used the beta form, β-NMN [25][6].

This article reviews the human clinical evidence, forms, dosing, safety, and practical considerations for each of these NAD-boosting compounds.

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### NAD+ and NADH

The oral NADH trials below used 10 to 20 mg a day, mostly of a stabilized form (ENADA) [21][26][22].

### Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

NR is available in several commercial forms:

- **Nicotinamide riboside chloride (Niagen)**: a crystal form of NR chloride, tested in an 8-week trial in overweight adults [9].
- **NR with pterostilbene (Basis)**: Elysium Health's product combines NR with pterostilbene, a polyphenol found in blueberries [24].

In mice, isotope tracing showed that the main route from oral NR to the body's NAD is conversion into nicotinic acid by gut bacteria (Chellappa et al., *Cell Metab*, 2022) [27]. Once inside cells, NR is phosphorylated by nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1 and NRK2) to form NMN, which is then adenylated by NMNATs to form NAD+ [11][23]. A pharmacokinetic study showed that single doses of 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR raised blood NAD+ metabolites in a dose-dependent way (Trammell et al., *Nat Commun*, 2016) [28]. In an 8-week placebo-controlled trial in overweight adults, 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR (Niagen) a day raised whole-blood NAD+ by 22%, 51% and 142% within 2 weeks, and the rises held for the rest of the study (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [9].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

NMN, like NR, is an NAD+ precursor, and in mice oral NMN is quickly used to make NAD+ in tissues (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [14]. A 2025 mouse study found that only a small part of oral NMN or NR was absorbed directly from the small intestine; most was converted by gut bacteria to nicotinic acid, and even NMN or NR injected into a vein was broken down, passed into bile and converted in the gut (Yaku et al., *Sci Adv*, 2025) [29]. In mice, a gene called Slc12a8 encodes a specific NMN transporter in the small intestine; it carries NMN but not NR (Grozio et al., *Nat Metab*, 2019) [30].

### NR vs. NMN: Head-to-Head

In a randomised, open-label study of 65 healthy adults, 14 days of NR or NMN raised circulating NAD+ by a similar amount, while nicotinamide did not; in the lab, human gut bacteria turned NR and NMN into nicotinic acid (Christen et al., *Nat Metab*, 2026) [31]. A small study gave 1,200 mg a day of NR or NMN to 6 healthy people and 6 people with Parkinson's disease: blood NAD rose slowly and levelled off after about two weeks, brain NAD rose after four weeks, and responses varied a lot from person to person (Berven et al., *iScience*, 2026) [32]. Because gut bacteria convert both to nicotinic acid first, Brad considers the NR-versus-NMN debate beside the point ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

Taking NMN under the tongue does not appear to help: in 14 healthy men, sublingual NMN raised the B3 breakdown products 2PY and 4PY faster than swallowed NMN, but blood NAD+ did not differ between the two routes (Wakabayashi et al., *Sci Rep*, 2026) [33].

### Quality Concerns

Choose a product that an independent lab has tested. When researchers tested NMN and urolithin A supplements bought online or in pharmacies, the amounts ranged from 28.6% above to 100% below the label (Sandalova et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [34].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Parkinson's Disease: Nicotinamide Riboside

**NOPARK (n=410, negative):** In a phase 3 trial at 11 centres in Norway, 410 people with Parkinson's disease diagnosed within the previous 2 years were randomised to 500 mg of NR twice daily or placebo for 52 weeks. On the MDS-UPDRS rating scale, where higher scores mean more severe disease, NR scored 2.72 points worse than placebo (95% CI 0.47 to 4.98; P = .02), below the 5.2 points the authors give as a clinically important worsening. Non-motor symptoms also worsened more on NR (4.22 points; P = .009). Serious adverse events were less common on NR, 17 of 205 (8.3%) against 29 of 205 (14.1%) on placebo. The authors conclude that NR did not improve clinical outcomes and was associated with worse outcomes than placebo (Brakedal et al., *JAMA*, 2026) [3].

**Earlier small trials:** In a trial in newly diagnosed, untreated people, 1,000 mg of NR or placebo for 30 days showed that NR was well tolerated, raised brain NAD by a variable amount and lowered inflammatory markers; people whose brain NAD rose showed mild clinical improvement (Brakedal et al., *Cell Metab*, 2022) [41]. In a four-week safety trial of 1,500 mg twice daily in 20 people, NR raised blood NAD+ up to 5-fold; the authors linked a gain in rating-scale scores partly to a shorter time since the last levodopa dose (Berven et al., *Nat Commun*, 2023) [42].

**Possible explanation, unproven:** In a mouse model of Parkinson's disease, NR first partly eased symptoms but later lowered dopamine and the expression of related genes in the brain (Turconi et al., *Heliyon*, 2024) [43].

Several of these trials had links to NR patents: in NOPARK, two authors reported NR patents for Parkinson's disease licensed to Niagen Bioscience [3]. Brad notes that the team published the negative result anyway ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)).

### Parkinson's Disease: NADH

NADH has been proposed as a way to raise the body's own production of levodopa, the building block of dopamine, which is the brain chemical that falls in Parkinson's disease [2].

**Placebo-controlled trial (negative):** In a double-blind study, five people with Parkinson's disease received 25 mg of NADH intravenously once a day for 4 days, then 25 mg by intramuscular injection after 2 and 4 weeks, while four people received saline on the same schedule. Both groups tended to improve after the infusions, but the changes were not significant, and nothing changed in the following weeks (Dizdar et al., *Acta Neurol Scand*, 1994) [40].

**Open-label study (n=885):** In an open-label study, 885 people with Parkinson's disease took NADH, about half by intravenous infusion and half by mouth. About 80% were reported to benefit, and the oral form improved disability about as much as the infused form. There was no placebo group (Birkmayer et al., *Acta Neurol Scand Suppl*, 1993) [39].

**Open-label study with levodopa (n=15):** In an open study, 15 people with Parkinson's disease received intravenous infusions of 10 mg NADH, each lasting 30 minutes, for 7 days on top of their usual treatment. Their UPDRS scores improved, and NADH raised the amount of levodopa reaching the blood (Kuhn et al., *J Neural Transm (Vienna)*, 1996) [2].

### Exercise Performance: NMN

**Meta-analysis (NMN and NR, adults over 60):** A 2025 meta-analysis of randomised trials found that NMN did not improve skeletal muscle index, handgrip strength, gait speed or the chair stand test; it concluded that current evidence does not support NMN or NR for preserving muscle mass and function in older adults (Prokopidis et al., *J Cachexia Sarcopenia Muscle*, 2025) [65].

**Systematic review of 10 RCTs (NMN, 437 participants):** A 2024 review of trials using 150 to 1,200 mg NMN a day for 4 to 12 weeks found non-significant improvements in physical performance. No serious adverse effects were seen, and the reported side effects were judged unrelated to NMN (Wen et al., *Cureus*, 2024) [66].

**Recreational runners (n=48):** In a 6-week trial, 48 recreational runners training 5 to 6 times a week took placebo or NMN at 300, 600 or 1,200 mg a day. Oxygen uptake and power at the first and second ventilatory thresholds rose more in the medium-dose and high-dose groups than with placebo, but maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max), O2-pulse and peak power did not change. The authors suggest the gain likely comes from better oxygen use in muscle (Liao et al., *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*, 2021) [64].

### Muscle Strength and Physical Function in Older Adults: NMN

**High-dose NMN in older adults:** In a 24-week trial in adults aged 65 years or more, 1,250 mg of NMN daily raised blood NAD+ and related metabolites and was generally well tolerated, though the authors say careful safety monitoring is warranted. It did not improve hearing (the main outcome) or physical performance, and small changes in other measures did not survive correction for multiple testing (Igarashi et al., *npj Aging*, 2026) [68].

**Older men with diabetes (n=14):** In men aged 65 or over (average age 81.1) with diabetes and reduced grip strength or walking speed, 250 mg NMN daily for 24 weeks was safe but did NOT improve grip strength or walking speed compared with placebo (Akasaka et al., *Geriatr Gerontol Int*, 2023) [69].

**Healthy older men:** In a 6- or 12-week trial, older men took 250 mg of NMN a day or placebo. NMN was well tolerated and raised blood NAD+. Gait speed and left-hand grip strength showed nominally significant gains that the authors say need checking in larger studies, and body composition did not change (Igarashi et al., *NPJ Aging*, 2022) [67].

### Energy and Physical Performance: Nicotinamide Riboside

**Physical performance review:** A 2020 review found that NR is considered safe in older people, but that its effect on physical performance on its own is unclear (Custodero et al., *Exp Gerontol*, 2020) [45].

**Animal study:** In aged mice, NR restored muscle stem cell function (Zhang et al., *Science*, 2016) [49].

### Blood Sugar and Insulin Resistance: Nicotinamide Riboside

**Obese men with insulin resistance (n=40):** In a 12-week trial in 40 obese, insulin-resistant men aged 40 to 70, 1,000 mg of NR twice daily did not improve insulin sensitivity or glucose metabolism and did not change body composition. There were no serious adverse events, and safety blood tests stayed normal (Dollerup et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2018) [55].

### Insulin Sensitivity: NMN

**Pooled trials:** A 2024 meta-analysis of 8 randomised trials (250 to 2,000 mg a day for 14 days to 12 weeks) found no significant benefit of NMN on fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c, insulin resistance or blood lipids (Chen et al., *Curr Diab Rep*, 2024) [63].

**Postmenopausal women with prediabetes (n=25):** In a 10-week trial in overweight or obese postmenopausal women with prediabetes, NMN raised muscle insulin sensitivity and insulin signalling, which did not change on placebo (Yoshino et al., *Science*, 2021) [61]. One critic noted that the placebo group started with more liver fat (14.8 ± 2.0% vs 6.3 ± 1.2%), and since NMN may act on liver fat, the trial was not effectively randomised (Brenner, *Science*, 2021) [62].

### Cholesterol: Nicotinamide Riboside

In an 8-week human trial, NR alone did not raise LDL cholesterol (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [9]. In diabetic mice, NR lowered cholesterol in the liver (Lee et al., *J Med Food*, 2015) [54].

### Alzheimer's Disease: Nicotinamide Riboside

**Memory-predominant mild cognitive impairment (n=42):** In a 12-week pilot trial, 42 adults with amnestic mild cognitive impairment completed NR or placebo. NR doubled blood NAD+ but did not improve cognition, total brain blood flow or blood pressure, and there were no serious adverse effects (Martens et al., *Alzheimers Dement*, 2026) [53].

**Self-reported cognitive decline (n=37):** In a crossover trial in adults over 55 with subjective cognitive decline or mild cognitive impairment, 37 of whom completed it, cognition did not change after 8 weeks of 1 g of NR a day. Blood levels of phosphorylated tau 217, a marker of Alzheimer's disease, fell 7% after NR and rose 18% with placebo (p = 0.02), but two other blood markers (GFAP and NfL) did not differ. Adverse events did not differ between NR and placebo (Wu et al., *Alzheimers Dement (N Y)*, 2025) [52].

**Mild cognitive impairment (n=20):** In a 10-week pilot trial in 20 older adults with mild cognitive impairment, NR was increased step by step to a final dose of 1 g a day. Blood NAD+ rose 2.6-fold and adverse events did not differ from placebo, but cognition did not change. Walking speed improved in the placebo group, possibly a practice effect, but not in the NR group (Orr et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [51].

**Biomarker analysis (n=22):** In 22 healthy older adults, 500 mg of NR twice a day for 6 weeks raised NAD+ in blood vesicles thought to come from neurons and lowered some markers linked to neurodegeneration, such as amyloid-beta 42. These are blood markers, not clinical outcomes (Vreones et al., *Aging Cell*, 2023) [50].

**Animal study:** In a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease, NR slowed cognitive decline and cut amyloid-beta production (Gong et al., *Neurobiol Aging*, 2013) [48].

### Alzheimer's Disease and Cognitive Function: NADH

**Alzheimer's disease (n=26, controlled):** In a 6-month randomised, double-blind trial, 26 people with probable Alzheimer's disease took 10 mg a day of stabilized oral NADH or placebo. The NADH group showed no sign of cognitive decline and scored higher than placebo on the Mattis Dementia Rating Scale, but attention, memory and clinician-rated dementia severity did not differ (Demarin et al., *Drugs Exp Clin Res*, 2004) [22].

**Dementia (n=19, uncontrolled):** In a 3-month open-label study, 25 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's, vascular or fronto-temporal dementia took 10 mg of NADH a day alongside their usual medicines. The 19 who finished showed no cognitive benefit on standard tests (Rainer et al., *J Neural Transm (Vienna)*, 2000) [38].

### Long COVID: Nicotinamide Riboside

**Brain fog and fatigue:** In a 24-week trial, 58 adults with long COVID were randomised to NR (2,000 mg a day) or to placebo first. NR raised NAD+ 2.6- to 3.1-fold after 5 to 10 weeks, but cognition, fatigue, sleep, anxiety and depression did not improve compared with placebo (Wu et al., *eClinicalMedicine*, 2025) [56]. Side effects judged possibly related to NR included nausea, flushing, rash, vertigo, bruising, muscle and leg cramps, and worse headaches [56]. Dropout was high in the group given NR from the start: 51.4% by 20 weeks, against 14.3% in the group given placebo first [56]. Niagen Bioscience was one of the funders [56].

### Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS): NADH

NADH helps trigger energy production through ATP, the cell's energy carrier, which is why it has been tested for chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) [21].

**NADH + CoQ10 combination (n=207, negative):** In a larger trial in 207 people with ME/CFS, 8 weeks of 20 mg NADH plus 200 mg CoQ10 a day lowered cognitive fatigue and the overall FIS-40 fatigue score from baseline in the treated group. The abstract reports these changes only within the treated group over time, not as a difference from placebo, and the authors call for further studies to confirm any benefit (Castro-Marrero et al., *Nutrients*, 2021) [36].

**NADH + CoQ10 combination (n=73, positive):** In an 8-week randomised, double-blind trial in 73 people with CFS, 20 mg of NADH plus 200 mg of CoQ10 a day lowered the total fatigue impact score more than placebo did. The authors called for larger trials to confirm it (Castro-Marrero et al., *Antioxid Redox Signal*, 2015) [26].

**Crossover trial (n=26):** In a randomised, double-blind crossover trial, 26 people with CFS took either 10 mg of NADH a day, in its stabilized form (ENADA), or placebo, for 4 weeks each, with a 4-week washout between. On NADH, 8 of 26 (31%) responded favourably, against 2 of 26 (8%) on placebo, and no severe adverse effects were linked to NADH. The authors called it a pilot study and asked for further trials (Forsyth et al., *Ann Allergy Asthma Immunol*, 1999) [21].

**Controlled trial with active comparator (n=31):** In a trial of 31 people with CFS randomised to NADH or to nutritional supplements plus psychological therapy for 24 months, the 12 who took NADH had a large, significant drop in symptom scores in the first 3 months of treatment. After that, scores were similar in both groups, and the authors say the result should be further assessed in a larger patient sample (Santaella et al., *P R Health Sci J*, 2004) [35].

**Expert opinion:** A European expert consensus lists NADH among supplements that may be tried for fatigue or cognitive problems in ME/CFS (Nacul et al., *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2021) [37].

### Peripheral Artery Disease: Nicotinamide Riboside

**Walking distance (n=90):** In a 6-month randomised trial in 90 people with lower extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD), NR improved 6-minute walk distance compared with placebo (+7.0 vs -10.6 metres; between-group difference +17.6 metres, 90% CI +1.8 to +∞). Adding resveratrol did not add benefit, and the authors call for a larger trial to confirm the result (McDermott et al., *Nat Commun*, 2024) [46].

**Cognitive function in PAD (n=8):** Over 4 weeks, 8 older adults with PAD took NR in an open-label pilot. The authors report preliminary results of better blood-flow responses in the brain and better scores across several cognitive domains, but with no control group they call for controlled trials in larger groups to confirm it (Szarvas et al., *J Pharmacol Exp Ther*, 2025) [47].

### Sleep: NMN

**Older Japanese adults (n=108):** In a 12-week trial, 108 older adults took 250 mg of NMN or placebo once a day, in the morning or the afternoon. The authors report that afternoon NMN improved lower-limb function (a sit-to-stand test) and reduced drowsiness, with the largest effects of any group, but drowsiness also improved with afternoon placebo and sit-to-stand times improved in all groups (Kim et al., *Nutrients*, 2022) [70].

**Older adults (n=60):** Another trial gave 250 mg NMN daily for 12 weeks. It found no difference from placebo in its main outcome, a stepping test. Among secondary outcomes, the NMN group had lower Global PSQI and "Daytime dysfunction" scores on the Pittsburgh Sleep Questionnaire, a shorter 4-metre walking time and higher blood NAD+ than placebo, with no adverse effects related to NMN (Morifuji et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [58].

### Raising NAD+ Levels: Nicotinamide Riboside

Several trials show that NR raises blood NAD+ [28][9][44], but trials have not shown a benefit you would notice ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

**NR + pterostilbene (n=120):** In a two-month trial in 120 healthy adults aged 60 to 80 years, Basis at the recommended dose or double the dose raised whole-blood NAD+ by about 40% and 90% after 4 weeks, and the rise was sustained for the rest of the trial. No serious adverse events were reported. The maker, Elysium Health, funded the trial and employed most of the authors (Dellinger et al., *NPJ Aging Mech Dis*, 2017) [24].

**Eight-week dose-response (overweight adults):** In overweight but otherwise healthy adults, 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR (Niagen) a day for 8 weeks raised whole-blood NAD+ by 22%, 51% and 142% within 2 weeks, and the rises held to the end of the study. Nobody reported flushing, adverse events did not differ from placebo, and LDL cholesterol did not rise (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [9]. ChromaDex funded the study and employed two of the authors [9].

**Older adults (crossover):** In a 2 × 6-week crossover trial in healthy middle-aged and older adults, NR was well tolerated and raised NAD+ metabolism; the authors suggest future trials look at blood pressure and arterial stiffness (Martens et al., *Nat Commun*, 2018) [44].

**Single-dose study:** Single doses of 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR produced dose-dependent rises in blood NAD+ metabolites in the first clinical trial of NR pharmacokinetics. NR's supplier sponsored the study (Trammell et al., *Nat Commun*, 2016) [28].

### Raising NAD+ Levels: NMN

Like NR, NMN raises blood NAD+ in trials (Yi et al., *Geroscience*, 2023; Morifuji et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [57][58]. Trials have used 250 mg to 2,000 mg a day [10].

**Multicenter dose trial (n=80):** Healthy middle-aged adults took placebo or 300 mg, 600 mg or 900 mg NMN once daily for 60 days. Blood NAD rose in all NMN groups compared with placebo, and was highest at 600 mg and 900 mg. Six-minute walking distance improved more in all NMN groups than with placebo, and blood biological age (an algorithm-based estimate) rose in the placebo group but stayed unchanged with NMN. NMN did not change insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) compared with placebo, and no safety issues were found up to 900 mg daily (Yi et al., *Geroscience*, 2023) [57].

**Healthy middle-aged adults (n=36):** Taking 125 mg NMN twice daily (250 mg/day) for 12 weeks raised serum nicotinamide compared with placebo. Arterial stiffness tended to fall, but the difference from placebo was not significant. NMN was well tolerated with no adverse events (Katayoshi et al., *Sci Rep*, 2023) [59].

**Blood pressure:** A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 trials with 349 participants found a small fall in diastolic blood pressure on NMN, of 2.15 mmHg; systolic pressure fell only in people aged 60 and over. The authors call the evidence preliminary (Zhang et al., *Nutrients*, 2026) [60].

### Claims About Slowing Aging: NMN

The central claim driving NMN's popularity is that it counters age-related decline by restoring NAD+ levels. A 2016 mouse study found that NMN improved energy metabolism, physical activity, and insulin sensitivity (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [14] ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

However, the translation to humans has been disappointing:

- Human trials consistently show higher blood NAD+, but no benefit you would notice in muscle, metabolism, or the brain ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).
- All the human NMN studies Brad reviewed in 2024 were short term, so long-term effects are unknown ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).
- The Interventions Testing Program, which tests compounds in mice at three independent sites, found that NR (a similar NAD+ precursor) did NOT extend lifespan [71] ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-scam-new-evidence)).
- A 2024 preprint (not yet peer reviewed) reported that lifelong NMN increased median lifespan by 8.5% in female mice but not in male mice (Kane et al., *bioRxiv*, 2024) [72].

Brad's reading of the evidence as of 2026: NAD+ levels can be increased, but we do not have strong evidence that NAD+ precursors help combat aging in humans ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

### Preclinical Evidence: NMN (Animal Studies)

While human evidence remains limited, preclinical research in animal models has been more promising, which is partly why NMN has attracted so much interest. Key preclinical findings include:

**Aging and metabolism in mice:** Long-term oral NMN supplementation (up to 300 mg/kg/day for 12 months) in wild-type aging mice suppressed weight gain, improved energy metabolism, enhanced insulin sensitivity, eye function, and physical activity, while mitigating age-associated physiological deterioration without toxicity (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [14].

**Vascular function in mice:** In old mice, NMN supplementation restored endothelium-dependent dilation of the carotid artery, reduced aortic stiffness and oxidative stress, and restored vascular SIRT1 activity (de Picciotto et al., *Aging Cell*, 2016) [73].

**Important caveat:** The more rigorous the studies became, especially in humans, the less support emerged for these early findings ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

## 4. Recommended Dosing

The doses below are those used in studies, not a recommendation. Brad would not take NR or NMN ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). For comparison, the US upper limit for niacin from supplements is 35 mg a day for adults; it covers nicotinic acid and nicotinamide, not people taking niacin under medical supervision [7].

### NADH

Oral NADH trials have used 10 to 20 mg a day, mostly of stabilized NADH [21][26][22]:
- **Chronic fatigue syndrome:** 10-20 mg daily, alone or combined with 200 mg CoQ10 [21][26][36]
- **Cognitive support (Alzheimer's/dementia):** 10 mg daily [38][22]
- **Parkinson's disease:** 10 mg by infusion, or 25 mg by infusion and injection [2][40]

### Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

Clinical studies have used between 100 mg and 3,000 mg a day [9][42]:
- **Higher-dose protocols:** 500 mg twice a day [50][3] to 1,000 mg twice daily [55]
- **Longest large trial:** 500 mg twice daily for 52 weeks in early Parkinson's disease, where people on NR did worse than on placebo [3]
- **Highest trial dose:** 1,500 mg twice daily for four weeks, in a safety trial in Parkinson's disease, where NR was well tolerated with no moderate or severe adverse events [42]
- **Onset of effect:** NAD+ increases are detectable within 2 weeks and maintained with continued use [9]

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

Clinical studies have used 250 mg to 2,000 mg daily [25][10]:
- **Lower-dose trials:** 250 mg daily [67][70]
- **Exercise performance:** 300, 600 or 1,200 mg a day [64]
- **Higher-dose trials:** 1,250 mg once daily for up to 4 weeks, in a safety trial [25], and for 24 weeks in older adults [68]; a 2026 meta-analysis pooled trials of 250 to 2,000 mg a day [10]
- **European safety opinion:** an EFSA expert panel judged β-NMN safe at up to 300 mg a day for adults, excluding pregnant and breastfeeding women [6]

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### NADH

The NADH trials reported few side effects:
- No severe adverse effects were linked to 10 mg of NADH a day in a 4-week crossover trial in CFS [21]
- No side effects were reported in an open-label study of 17 people with Alzheimer-type dementia treated for 8 to 12 weeks [74]

Intravenous NAD+ is a different matter. During a 6-hour infusion, NAD+ was rapidly and completely removed from the blood for at least the first 2 hours (Grant et al., *Front Aging Neurosci*, 2019) [75]. In a small preprint trial of NR and NAD+ infusions, NAD+ infusions caused more infusion symptoms than NR infusions and raised white blood cell and neutrophil counts, suggesting an inflammatory response (Hawkins et al., *medRxiv*, 2024) [76]. A 2026 review of clinic records found moderate to severe stomach and bowel symptoms, a faster heart rate and chest pressure during NAD+ infusions (Reyna et al., *Front Aging*, 2026) [77]. Brad advises against NAD+ IV drips ([Brad, 20 Nov 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/popular-longevity-treatment-makes-you-sick)).

### Nicotinamide Riboside

NR has been well tolerated in trials, including at 1,000 mg twice daily [55], and side effects were no more common than on placebo in several trials [9][51][52]. Side effects reported in trials include:
- **Flushing:** nobody reported flushing in the Niagen dose trial described above [9]. In one single-dose study, flushing was reported by two people at 300 mg and feeling hot by two at 1,000 mg, with no link to dose [28]. In a 6-week trial, two of the three people who reported flushing were taking placebo, and the authors suggest NR may be less likely than niacin to cause flushing [44].
- **Gastrointestinal:** nausea, indigestion, abdominal discomfort and diarrhea [24][44][56]
- **Musculoskeletal:** leg cramps, muscle cramps [44][56]
- **Other:** worse headaches, fatigue, increased bruising, rash, vertigo [24][44][56]
- **Highest tested dose (1,500 mg twice daily):** a four-week trial in Parkinson's disease found NR well tolerated, with no moderate or severe adverse events and no significant difference in mild adverse events, though serum homocysteine rose slightly at first (Berven et al., *Nat Commun*, 2023) [42]
- **Year-long trial:** in early Parkinson's disease, serious adverse events were less common on NR than on placebo, but symptoms worsened more on NR [3]
- **LDL cholesterol:** in the Basis trial (NR with pterostilbene), overweight people had rises in LDL cholesterol on the recommended dose and on double the dose, with larger rises in total and LDL cholesterol on double the dose, though the groups differed at baseline [24]. NR alone (Niagen) did not raise LDL cholesterol in an 8-week trial [9].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

NMN has been well tolerated in short-term human trials:
- **No harmful side effects** were seen in the 18 short-term human NMN studies Brad reviewed in 2024, but long-term safety is unknown ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements))
- **European safety opinion:** an EFSA expert panel judged β-NMN safe for adults at up to 300 mg a day, excluding pregnant and breastfeeding women, and the human study it reviewed raised no safety concerns (EFSA NDA Panel, *EFSA J*, 2026) [6]
- **Higher-dose trial:** In 31 healthy adults aged 20 to 65, 1,250 mg NMN once daily for up to 4 weeks caused no changes beyond normal variation in blood, urine and body-composition tests, and no severe adverse events (Fukamizu et al., *Sci Rep*, 2022) [25]
- **Pooled trials:** A 2026 meta-analysis whose safety analyses drew on 10 randomised trials (250 to 2,000 mg/day, 14 days to 24 weeks), found that NMN did not increase overall, serious or system-specific adverse events, and did not significantly raise the liver enzymes ALT or AST. The authors note that larger and longer trials are still needed to confirm long-term safety (Yang et al., *Nutrients*, 2026) [10]
- **Animal toxicology:** In a 91-day rat study, the highest dose with no observed adverse effect was 1,000 mg/kg/day in females and 500 mg/kg/day in males; 2,000 mg/kg/day reduced body weight and food intake (Turner et al., *Front Pharmacol*, 2021) [78]. A separate 90-day rat study put this level at no less than 800 mg/kg body weight and found NMN was not mutagenic (Yu et al., *Curr Res Toxicol*, 2024) [79]

### Vitamin B3 Upper Intake Level

All NAD boosters are sources of vitamin B3. In the United States, the upper limit for vitamin B3 from supplements is 35 mg a day for adults. It is based on flushing from nicotinic acid (niacin), but it applies to nicotinamide too, though not to people taking B3 under medical supervision [7]. The NIH fact sheet does not say whether the limit covers NR or NMN, which are not marketed or labelled as sources of niacin [7]. The European Union's upper level for nicotinamide is 900 mg a day, and up to 300 mg a day of β-NMN, the most EFSA judged safe, corresponds to 109.7 mg a day of nicotinamide (EFSA NDA Panel, *EFSA J*, 2026) [6]. For NR chloride, EFSA judged up to 300 mg a day safe for healthy adults and up to 230 mg a day safe in pregnancy and breastfeeding, and in 2021 it noted that intakes of nicotinamide or its precursors well above the body's needs might cause harm [80][81].

Brad's concern with megadosing B3 rests mostly on trials of nicotinic acid, a form of B3 that NR and NMN do not contain, though gut bacteria turn them into it [27][29]. In people with vascular disease on a statin, 2 g of extended-release nicotinic acid daily, combined with laropiprant, did not reduce major vascular events and increased serious problems with diabetes control, infections and bleeding (HPS2-THRIVE, *N Engl J Med*, 2014) [82], and another trial was stopped early for lack of benefit (AIM-HIGH, *N Engl J Med*, 2011) [83]. A review of three trials found all-cause mortality 10% higher with 1 to 3 g a day of extended-release nicotinic acid plus a statin than with a statin alone [7]. Separately, high blood levels of 2PY and 4PY, breakdown products of excess B3, were linked to a higher 3-year risk of major cardiovascular events, and 4PY inflamed blood vessels in mice (Ferrell et al., *Nat Med*, 2024) [84]. NMN raised 2PY and 4PY in a small study [85]. These links are associations; no NR or NMN trial has measured heart attacks or strokes ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)).

High intakes of nicotinamide may cause:
- Liver injury: reversible liver damage at very high doses in animals and people, and occasional minor liver enzyme changes at lower therapeutic doses [8]
- Raised anticonvulsant levels: nicotinamide raised carbamazepine levels and slowed the breakdown of primidone in a small study [1]
- Falls in platelet counts: in a trial in people on hemodialysis, nicotinamide at 500 mg a day, then 1,000 mg a day, lowered platelet counts (Shahbazian et al., *Nefrologia*, 2011) [86]
- Impaired glucose control: minor insulin resistance has been reported [8]

A 2000 review advised treating nicotinamide doses above 3 g a day in adults as potentially toxic, and discouraged unsupervised use (Knip et al., *Diabetologia*, 2000) [8].

On the liver specifically: high doses of niacin (nicotinic acid) can cause mild to moderate rises in liver enzymes, and high doses or certain formulations have been linked to acute liver injury that can be severe or even fatal (LiverTox, *NIDDK*) [87]. Liver damage from high-dose niacin can cause fatigue, nausea and loss of appetite; if these or unexplained belly pain start while you take high doses of B3, stop and see a doctor [7]. Short-term NMN trials have not shown a rise in liver enzymes [10], but long-term data are lacking.

### Cancer Concerns

Animal and laboratory research raises a theoretical concern that raising NAD+ may help some cancers grow or spread [4][88].

**Practical recommendation:** Brad would not take NR or NMN himself ([Brad, 29 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nr-in-parkinsons-what-the-nopark-trial-found)). The concern rests on animal findings; if you have cancer or are on cancer treatment, talk to your oncologist before taking an NAD booster [4].

**Mouse study on breast cancer:** In a mouse model of triple-negative breast cancer, NR supplementation significantly increased how often cancer developed and how often it spread to the brain (Maric et al., *Biosens Bioelectron*, 2023) [4].

**Proposed alternative approach:** In glioblastoma, a brain cancer, blocking the enzyme that makes NAD+ lowered NAD+ and reduced the self-renewal of cancer stem cells, which suggests that lowering NAD+, not raising it, may help treat some cancers (Gujar et al., *PNAS*, 2016) [88].

**Other forms of B3:** A 2000 review found no evidence that nicotinamide causes cancer in people [8]. In a 12-month trial in 386 people at high risk of skin cancer, 500 mg of nicotinamide twice daily lowered the rate of new non-melanoma skin cancers by 23% (95% CI 4 to 38) compared with placebo (Chen et al., *N Engl J Med*, 2015) [91].

**Contrary rodent data:** Other rodent studies point the other way: NR given with chemotherapy did not weaken, and may have strengthened, the drugs' effect on tumours (Hamity et al., *Pain*, 2020; Acklin et al., *Neurooncol Adv*, 2022) [89][90].

### Kidney Concerns: NMN

A mouse study raises a concern that NMN may worsen kidney inflammation in old age [5]:

**Mouse study:** In aged (26-month-old) male mice, 8 weeks of NMN raised kidney markers of inflammation (IL-1beta and Ccl2) and tubule injury (Kim-1), and raised blood levels of uremic toxins. Young mice did not show this susceptibility (Saleh et al., *bioRxiv*, 2024, preprint) [5].

This is early animal evidence that has not been peer reviewed. The authors call for kidney-safety checks in older people [5]; if you are older or have kidney disease, ask your doctor first.

## 6. Drug Interactions

### General Precautions

- **Anticonvulsant drugs:** Nicotinamide (a metabolite of all NAD precursors) slowed the breakdown of primidone and raised carbamazepine levels in a small study of three patients, so check with your doctor if you take an anticonvulsant (Bourgeois et al., *Neurology*, 1982) [1].
- **Glucose-lowering medications:** High doses of nicotinamide may cause minor insulin resistance, which could matter if you take diabetes medicines [8].
- **Cancer treatment:** Given the theoretical concern about NAD+ promoting cancer cell growth, individuals on cancer treatment should consult their oncologist before taking NAD-boosting supplements [4].
- **Levodopa (Parkinson's):** In one small study, intravenous NADH raised levodopa levels in the blood; coordination with a neurologist is advisable [2]. In a year-long trial in early Parkinson's disease, people on NR did worse than on placebo [3].

### Consultation Recommended

Talk with your doctor before starting an NAD booster, especially if you:
- Have cancer or are being treated for it
- Have Parkinson's disease [3]
- Have kidney disease, or are older and taking NMN [5]
- Take anticonvulsant or glucose-lowering drugs
- Are pregnant or breastfeeding [6]

## 7. Dietary Sources

### NAD+ Precursors from Food

The body makes NAD+ from dietary tryptophan and vitamin B3, and its baseline needs can be met from tryptophan or from less than 20 mg of daily niacin [13]. The US recommended intake for adults is 16 mg niacin equivalents (NE) a day for men and 14 mg NE for women; 1 NE is 1 mg of niacin or 60 mg of tryptophan [7].

Animal foods such as poultry, beef and fish give more niacin per serving than plant foods such as nuts, legumes and grains. Examples from the NIH fact sheet [7]:

| Food | Serving | Niacin |
|------|---------|:-:|
| Beef liver | 3 oz | 14.9 mg |
| Chicken breast | 3 oz | 10.3 mg |
| Turkey breast | 3 oz | 10.0 mg |
| Sockeye salmon | 3 oz | 8.6 mg |
| Brown rice | 1 cup | 5.2 mg |
| Peanuts | 1 oz | 4.2 mg |

### NMN in Foods

Reliable figures for NMN in foods are not available.

### Nicotinamide Riboside in Foods

NR is found naturally in cow's milk, which typically contains about 12 micromol of NAD+ precursor vitamins per litre, 40% of it as NR (Trammell et al., *J Nutr*, 2016) [92].
