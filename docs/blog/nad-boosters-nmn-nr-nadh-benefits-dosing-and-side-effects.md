## Current view (reviewed 20 October 2026)

NMN and NR reliably raise blood NAD+, but after twenty years and dozens of clinical trials there is still no sign that this turns into anything you would notice in muscle, metabolism or the brain ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). A 2025 meta-analysis of 10 randomised trials in adults over 60 found no improvement in skeletal muscle index, handgrip strength, gait speed or chair stand tests, and in a long COVID trial NR tripled blood NAD+ without improving a single symptom ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Gut bacteria break both NMN and NR down to nicotinic acid, a cheap form of vitamin B3, before the body rebuilds NAD+ ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Until there is better long-term research, he does not recommend NMN or any form of vitamin B3 above about 50 mg a day ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)). He advises against NAD+ IV drips: in the first trials the infused NAD+ was rapidly removed from the blood, and most participants had nausea, headache, diarrhea and muscle tightness, with signs of inflammation ([Brad, 20 Nov 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/popular-longevity-treatment-makes-you-sick)). The approach he recommends instead is regular exercise, since exercise-trained older adults had NAD+ levels similar to young people ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

## 1. Overview

Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) is a coenzyme essential for energy production in mitochondria throughout the body. It exists in two interconvertible forms: NAD+ (the oxidized form, carrying a positive charge) and NADH (the reduced form, with a hydrogen atom attached, making it electrically neutral) [1][2]. Every living cell depends on NAD+ to convert nutrients into usable energy via the electron transport chain. NAD+ also acts as a signalling molecule that helps control hundreds of key processes, from energy metabolism to cell survival (Rajman et al., *Cell Metab*, 2018) [2].

The body synthesizes NAD+ from dietary precursors — primarily niacin (vitamin B3) and its derivatives, including nicotinamide (niacinamide), nicotinamide riboside (NR), and nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) [1][3]. Some NAD+ is stored in red blood cells, while excess is broken down in the liver and excreted in urine [1].

Interest in NAD-boosting supplements was sparked by preliminary laboratory research suggesting that NAD+ levels decline with age. A 2012 study found that NAD+ decreased in human skin tissue with advancing age (Massudi et al., *PLoS One*, 2012) [5], and a 2013 study in mice showed that raising NAD+ levels could reverse aspects of mitochondrial dysfunction (Gomes et al., *Cell*, 2013) [6]. These findings raised the hope that boosting NAD+ could provide anti-aging benefits. However, a later study among more than 300 adults found no difference in blood NAD+ between younger (18 to 29 years) and older (>60 years) adults, with levels stable across older age groups (63 to 87 years), which suggests blood NAD+ may not be a reliable marker of aging (Tretowicz, *Nat Metab*, 2026) [1].

If you already consume adequate vitamin B3 (14-16 mg daily for adults) from your diet — which is not difficult, as B3 is present in many foods — and/or supplements, it is unclear whether any of the NAD-boosting supplements discussed in this article provide meaningful additional benefit [1].

The three main categories of NAD-boosting supplements are:

- **NAD+/NADH** — Direct supplementation with the coenzyme itself, usually in the reduced NADH form. NADH is 99.85% NAD by weight [1].
- **Nicotinamide riboside (NR)** — A precursor to NAD+ that is converted to NMN by nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1/NRK2), then to NAD+ by NMNATs [4][7]. Sold as Niagen (nicotinamide riboside chloride), nicotinamide riboside hydrogen malate, and crystalline nicotinamide riboside (as in Elysium Health's Basis product) [1].
- **Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)** — Like NR, a precursor to NAD+ [1]. Typically marketed as NMN or beta-NMN (identical compounds) [1].

This article reviews the human clinical evidence, forms, dosing, safety, and practical considerations for each of these NAD-boosting compounds.

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### NAD+ and NADH

NADH, not NAD+, is the form evaluated in clinical studies, although both are sold [1]. Stabilized NADH (such as the branded product ENADA) has been used at doses of 5-20 mg in clinical trials [1]. Oral NADH is absorbed in the small intestine, though bioavailability data in humans is limited. The primary advantage of NADH supplementation is that it provides the coenzyme directly without requiring conversion. However, because NADH is chemically unstable and sensitive to light, moisture, and stomach acid, stabilized or enteric-coated formulations are necessary for oral delivery [1][9].

### Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

NR is available in several commercial forms:

- **Nicotinamide riboside chloride (Niagen)** — The most extensively studied form in clinical trials. Manufactured by ChromaDex (now Niagen Bioscience). Found in the product Tru Niagen [1].
- **Nicotinamide riboside hydrogen malate** — An alternative salt form [1].
- **Crystalline nicotinamide riboside** — Used in Elysium Health's Basis product, combined with pterostilbene. ChromaDex has claimed that the nicotinamide riboside in Basis is not identical to Niagen, though both raise NAD+ levels [1][10].

Isotope-tracing and human gut-sample studies reviewed by Brad in 2026 found that oral NR is largely broken down by gut bacteria to nicotinic acid, which the body then rebuilds into NAD+ ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Once inside cells, NR is phosphorylated by nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1 and NRK2) to form NMN, which is then adenylated by NMNATs to form NAD+ [4][7]. A pharmacokinetic study among 11 healthy adults showed that single doses of 100 mg, 300 mg, or 1,000 mg of Niagen increased NAD+ levels in a dose-dependent fashion (Trammell et al., *Nat Commun*, 2016) [11]. In a longer trial, daily doses of 100 mg, 300 mg, and 1,000 mg of Niagen for 8 weeks increased whole blood NAD+ levels by 22%, 51%, and 142%, respectively, within two weeks, and these increases were maintained throughout the study (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [12]. Most commercially available NR supplements provide approximately 250 mg per daily dose [1].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

Like NR, NMN is a precursor to NAD+ and can raise NAD+ levels in some cells (Shade, *Integr Med (Encinitas)*, 2020) [1][8]. Brad's 2026 review found that NMN is also broken down by gut bacteria to nicotinic acid before NAD+ is rebuilt, even when NMN or NR is injected into a vein: the body passes it into bile and back to the gut ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). In mice, a gene called Slc12a8 encodes a specific NMN transporter in the small intestine; it carries NMN but not NR (Grozio et al., *Nat Metab*, 2019) [50].

### NR vs. NMN: Head-to-Head

In a randomised study of 65 participants published in *Nature Metabolism*, NR and NMN each roughly doubled circulating NAD+ after 14 days, with no significant difference between them ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). A smaller crossover study gave 6 healthy adults 1,200 mg a day of each and found about a 2.3-fold higher NAD+ increase with NR, but responses were not very consistent from one measurement to the next ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Because gut bacteria convert both to nicotinic acid first, Brad considers the NR-versus-NMN debate beside the point ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

### Quality Concerns

Many reports of poor quality come from Niagen Bioscience (formerly ChromaDex), which sells the NR ingredient Niagen and competes with other NAD+ boosters [1]. Its 2021 tests of 22 popular NMN supplements found that only 3 contained their claimed amount or slightly more, another 5 contained 88-99%, and 14 contained less than 1% of claimed NMN [1]. Its tests between March 2021 and February 2026 of 70 NR supplements found that only 26 contained at least 90% of the listed amount, 23 contained 1% to 89%, and 21 contained less than 1% or none detectable [1]. This variability is a reason to choose products that have passed independent testing.

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS) — NADH

Chronic fatigue syndrome has been associated with increased activity of enzymes that deplete ATP, the primary energy currency of cells. Since NADH is directly involved in ATP generation, there has been interest in using it to increase energy and reduce fatigue in CFS patients [1].

**Crossover trial (n=26):** A study among 26 people with CFS who received 10 mg of stabilized NADH (ENADA) or placebo daily for 4 weeks showed that 31% of NADH recipients experienced a clinically meaningful improvement in symptoms (defined as improvement of at least 10%), compared to only 8% on placebo. However, no formal between-group statistical comparison was performed. One participant reported feeling overly stimulated while taking NADH (Forsyth et al., *Ann Allergy Asthma Immunol*, 1999) [13].

**Controlled trial with active comparator (n=20):** A study of 20 CFS patients — 12 receiving 5 mg NADH daily (increased to 10 mg if symptoms did not improve) and 8 receiving nutritional supplements plus psychological therapy for up to 24 months — showed that both groups improved during the first 3 months compared to baseline, but there was no significant between-group difference, and neither group improved beyond 3 months (Santaella et al., *P R Health Sci*, 2004) [14].

**NADH + CoQ10 combination (n=73, positive):** A study among 73 CFS patients found that taking 20 mg NADH with 200 mg CoQ10 daily for 8 weeks modestly improved overall Fatigue Impact Scale (FIS-40) scores by approximately 7.5 points (out of 160) compared to baseline, and this improvement was statistically significant compared to placebo (Castro-Marrero et al., *Antioxid Redox Signal*, 2015) [15].

**NADH + CoQ10 combination (n=207, negative):** A larger study among 207 CFS patients found the same combination of 20 mg NADH and 200 mg CoQ10 daily for 8 weeks did NOT improve physical functioning, psychosocial symptoms, overall FIS-40 scores, or sleep quality compared to placebo. Although the NADH/CoQ10 group showed modest improvement in perception of cognitive fatigue (approximately 1.1 points out of 40) compared to baseline, this was not significant versus placebo (Castro-Marrero et al., *Nutrients*, 2021) [16].

**Expert opinion:** Based on the limited evidence, the European Network on Myalgic Encephalomyelitis/Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (EUROMENE) has stated that NADH may be tried by people with CFS to relieve fatigue or cognitive dysfunction (Nacul et al., *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2021) [17].

### Alzheimer's Disease and Cognitive Function — NADH

**Dementia (n=19, uncontrolled):** An uncontrolled study among 19 people with mild to moderate dementia (Alzheimer's, vascular, and fronto-temporal types) found that 10 mg of NADH (ENADA) daily for 3 months did not improve measures of cognition compared to baseline (Rainer et al., *J Neural Transm (Vienna)*, 2000) [18].

**Alzheimer's disease (n=24, controlled):** A study among 24 people with probable Alzheimer's disease showed that those who took 10 mg of NADH (ENADA) daily for 6 months experienced no cognitive decline and had higher scores on the Mattis Dementia Rating Scale compared to placebo. However, there was no between-group difference in attention, memory, or clinician-rated dementia severity (Demarin et al., *Drugs Exp Clin Res*, 2004) [19].

### Parkinson's Disease — NADH

NADH is a coenzyme involved in the synthesis of L-DOPA, a precursor of dopamine — the neurotransmitter deficient in Parkinson's disease [1].

**Open-label study (n=885):** An open-label study among 885 Parkinson's patients already receiving conventional medication found that 5 mg enteric-coated NADH by mouth or 12.5 mg NADH by 30-minute infusions every other day for 2 weeks improved disability scores on the Birkmayer-Neumayr scale by similar amounts (19.8% vs 20.6%, respectively) (Birkmayer et al., *Acta Neurol Scand Suppl*, 1993) [20].

**Open-label study with levodopa (n not specified):** An open-label study showed that 10 mg NADH by 30-minute infusion for 7 days along with 100 mg levodopa improved UPDRS scores by 9.3 points (out of 199) and slightly increased blood levels of levodopa compared to baseline (Kuhn et al., *J Neural Transmission*, 1996) [21].

**Placebo-controlled trial (negative):** A placebo-controlled study showed that infusions of 25 mg NADH for 4 days, then intramuscular injections of 25 mg NADH on days 20 and 35, did NOT improve UPDRS scores compared to placebo. Both groups improved compared to baseline (7.6 and 5.3 points, respectively) (Dizdar et al., *Acta Neurol Scand*, 1994) [22].

### Depression — NADH

**Open-label study (n=188):** One open-label study among 188 people with depression found that 5 mg NADH by mouth or 12.5 mg NADH by injection daily for up to approximately 10 months improved symptoms in 93% of participants, with 32% showing marked improvement (improvement greater than half the original disability). However, the lack of a control group prevents conclusions about causality (Birkmayer et al., *New Trends Clin Neuropharmacol*, 1991) [1].

### Raising NAD+ Levels — Nicotinamide Riboside

Multiple studies confirm that NR reliably raises blood NAD+ levels, though translating this biochemical change into clinical benefit has proven difficult [1][11][12].

**Dose-response in healthy adults (n=11):** Single doses of Niagen at 100 mg, 300 mg, or 1,000 mg increased NAD+ levels without serious adverse events. Two people reported flushing at 300 mg and two others reported "feeling hot" at 1,000 mg (Trammell et al., *Nat Commun*, 2016) [11].

**Eight-week dose-response (n=overweight adults):** Daily doses of 100 mg, 300 mg, and 1,000 mg of Niagen for 8 weeks increased whole blood NAD+ by 22%, 51%, and 142%, respectively, within two weeks. These increases were maintained for the study duration. There were no reports of flushing and no significant differences in adverse events between NR and placebo groups. There was no elevation of LDL cholesterol (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [12].

**Older adults (n=24):** Healthy adults aged 55-79 taking 500 mg Niagen twice daily for six weeks had a 60% increase in average blood NAD+ levels compared to placebo. Flushing was reported in one NR participant and two placebo participants. Nausea, leg cramps, and increased bruising were each reported in one NR participant (Martens et al., *Nat Commun*, 2018) [24].

**NR + pterostilbene (n=120):** A study of Basis (Elysium Health) — 250 mg NR plus 50 mg pterostilbene per capsule — among 120 older adults found that one capsule or two capsules daily for two months increased blood NAD+ by 40% and 55%, respectively. There was a slight but significant increase in LDL ("bad") cholesterol with two capsules and among overweight people taking one capsule. There were no significant changes in total cholesterol, triglycerides, or blood pressure, except a slight decrease in diastolic blood pressure with two capsules daily. Six of 80 people taking Basis reported nausea, mild fatigue, or moderate abdominal discomfort (Dellinger et al., *NPJ Aging Mech Dis*, 2017) [10].

### Energy and Physical Performance — Nicotinamide Riboside

Due to NAD's role in cellular energy production, it was hoped that NR would improve functional energy levels. However, this has not been demonstrated in clinical studies [1].

The maker of Niagen (ChromaDex, now Niagen Bioscience) previously claimed that Niagen increased energy but removed that claim in 2021 following a challenge by the National Advertising Division (NAD) of the Better Business Bureau. The claim was apparently based only on laboratory research showing increased cellular energy, not functional improvements [1]. In March 2026, the NAD recommended that Niagen Bioscience discontinue claims that Tru Niagen is "clinically proven to significantly increase NAD+ levels, and help maintain them with daily use," along with broader health benefit claims. Niagen Bioscience agreed to discontinue certain claims but appealed others; in May 2026 the National Advertising Review Board affirmed the decision, and the company said it would comply [1].

**Physical performance review:** A review of clinical studies evaluating NR for physical performance in older adults found small, short-term studies with mixed results (Custodero et al., *Exp Gerontol*, 2020) [25].

### Peripheral Artery Disease — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Walking distance (n=90):** A study among 90 adults (average age 71) with lower extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD) found that 500 mg NR (from ChromaDex) twice daily for 6 months increased 6-minute walking distance by an average of 7 meters, while walking distance decreased by 10.6 meters in the placebo group. This between-group difference was statistically significant — although largely driven by the decline in the placebo group rather than improvement with NR. NR did NOT improve maximum treadmill walking time, degree of difficulty when walking, or overall physical activity compared to placebo (McDermott et al., *Nat Commun*, 2024) [26].

**Cognitive function in PAD (n=8):** A small study among 8 older adults (average age 67) with PAD found that 1,000 mg NR daily for 4 weeks did NOT significantly improve executive function, attention, language, processing speed, or episodic memory compared to baseline (Szarvas et al., *J Pharmacol Exp Ther*, 2025), according to ConsumerLab's reading [1]. The study's authors report cognitive enhancement across multiple domains after supplementation in this open-label pilot, and call for controlled trials in larger groups to verify it [27].

### Alzheimer's Disease — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Animal studies:** NR has been shown in mice to reduce amyloid plaque components and improve cognitive function (Gong et al., *Neurobiol Aging*, 2013) [28], and to improve stem cell function (Zhang et al., *Science*, 2016) [29].

**Biomarker analysis (n=22):** Analysis of blood from 22 healthy middle-aged and older adults who took 500 mg Niagen twice daily for 6 weeks suggested NR raised NAD+ levels in the brain and reduced biomarkers associated with Alzheimer's. However, this was based on measurements of peripheral blood compounds believed to originate from the brain — it does not prove NR can treat or prevent Alzheimer's disease (Vreones et al., *Aging Cell*, 2023) [30].

**Mild cognitive impairment (n=20):** A study among 20 older adults (average age 76) with mild cognitive impairment found that NR did NOT significantly improve short-term memory, executive function, attention, concentration, working memory, language, or orientation compared to placebo. Those in the placebo group actually showed greater improvements in physical function, lower extremity strength, and gait speed. NR was given as a titrated dose: 250 mg for week 1, 500 mg for week 2, 750 mg for week 3, then 1,000 mg daily. One person experienced severe nausea, which resolved when the dose was temporarily reduced (Orr et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [31].

**Self-reported cognitive decline (n=37):** A study among 37 adults (average age 67) with self-reported cognitive decline or mild cognitive impairment found that 1 g of NR (TruNiagen by ChromaDex) daily for 8 weeks did NOT improve overall cognitive function or scores for immediate memory, language, attention, delayed memory, or visuospatial index compared to placebo. Although the NR group showed a small reduction in phosphorylated tau 217 (a blood biomarker for Alzheimer's) while the placebo group showed an 18% increase (a statistically significant difference), there were no significant between-group differences in other Alzheimer's biomarkers, including neurofilament light chain and glial fibrillary acidic protein. One person taking NR experienced worsening of an underlying gait disorder (Wu et al., *Alzheimers Dement (N Y)*, 2025) [32].

**Memory-predominant mild cognitive impairment (n=42):** A study among 42 adults (average age 72) found that 500 mg NR twice daily for 12 weeks nearly doubled blood NAD+ compared to placebo but did NOT improve cognitive function, memory, blood flow in the brain, or cardiovascular function (Martens, *Alzheimers Dement*, 2026) [1].

### Cholesterol — Nicotinamide Riboside

NR has been promoted for lowering cholesterol levels in the liver, although this has only been shown in laboratory and animal studies (Lee et al., *J Med Food*, 2015) [33] and not in clinical trials [1].

### Blood Sugar and Insulin Resistance — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Overweight men with insulin resistance (n=40):** A study in Denmark among 40 overweight men with insulin resistance (HOMA-IR >= 2.5) found that 1,000 mg NR (Niagen) twice daily for three months did NOT decrease fasting blood sugar levels or HbA1c, or improve insulin sensitivity compared to placebo. There were no improvements in body composition (body fat, lean mass, or total body mass). Side effects included itching, excessive sweating, bloating, and changes in stool (Dollerup et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2018) [34].

### Long COVID — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Brain fog and fatigue:** In a 24-week study of adults with long COVID (particularly "brain fog" and at least two other symptoms), 1,000 mg NR twice daily raised NAD+ levels, but there were NO significant improvements in cognitive function, fatigue severity, sleep quality, anxiety symptoms, or depression compared to placebo (Wu et al., *eClinicalMedicine*, 2025) [35]. According to ConsumerLab, the study had 32 adults (average age 47) and NAD+ rose about 2- to 3-fold [1]. Side effects possibly related to NR included muscle and leg cramps, nausea, bruising, worsening of headaches, flushing, rash, and vertigo [1]. This study was funded by Niagen Bioscience [1]. The paper itself reports 58 participants and a 2.6- to 3.1-fold rise in NAD+ after 5 to 10 weeks of NR [35]. Brad's account of the same trial agrees: 58 participants and a 2.6- to 3.1-fold rise in NAD+ over 20 weeks ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

Note: The FDA sent a warning letter to ChromaDex in November 2020 for promoting Niagen as preventing or treating COVID-19, including claims that low NAD+ levels can worsen COVID-19 [1].

### Raising NAD+ Levels — NMN

Like NR, NMN raises NAD+ levels in the blood [1]. Daily doses in studies have ranged from 250 mg to 1,200 mg, with the larger amount typically divided over two doses [1].

**Postmenopausal women with prediabetes (n=25):** Taking 250 mg NMN daily for 10 weeks increased NAD+ content in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (immune cells) by approximately 43% compared to placebo. However, skeletal muscle NAD+ content was not increased (Yoshino et al., *Science*, 2021) [36].

**Multicenter dose trial (n=80):** Healthy middle-aged adults took placebo or 300 mg, 600 mg or 900 mg NMN once daily for 60 days. Blood NAD rose in all NMN groups compared with placebo, and was highest at 600 mg and 900 mg. Six-minute walking distance improved more in all NMN groups than with placebo, and blood biological age (an algorithm-based estimate) rose in the placebo group but stayed unchanged with NMN. NMN did not change insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) compared with placebo, and no safety issues were found up to 900 mg daily (Yi et al., *Geroscience*, 2023) [51].

**Healthy middle-aged adults (n=36):** Taking 125 mg NMN twice daily (250 mg/day) for 12 weeks raised serum nicotinamide compared with placebo. Arterial stiffness tended to fall, but the difference from placebo was not significant. NMN was well tolerated with no adverse events (Katayoshi et al., *Sci Rep*, 2023) [52].

### Insulin Sensitivity — NMN

**Postmenopausal women with prediabetes (n=25):** Taking 250 mg NMN daily for 10 weeks increased muscle insulin sensitivity by 25% compared to baseline, with upregulated insulin signaling (pAKT, pmTOR) and muscle remodeling genes. There was no improvement in muscle insulin sensitivity in the placebo group, and neither group showed improvement in liver insulin sensitivity (Yoshino et al., *Science*, 2021) [36]. However, this study has been criticized because at baseline, the placebo group had 2.35 times more liver fat than the treatment group, and liver fat is known to reduce muscle insulin sensitivity. It is therefore unclear if the difference was due to NMN or baseline differences in liver fat (Brenner, *Science*, 2021) [37].

### Exercise Performance — NMN

**Recreational runners (n=48):** A study among 48 recreational runners (average age 36) taking 300 mg or 600 mg NMN twice daily for 6 weeks while training 5-6 times weekly found dose-dependent improvements in the time until breathing begins to increase during exercise (VT1) and power output at the anaerobic threshold (VT2) compared to placebo. However, neither dose increased maximum oxygen consumption or improved the ratio of oxygen consumption to heart rate. A lower dose (150 mg twice daily) did not improve any outcome. No adverse effects were reported. The researchers speculated that NMN may improve skeletal muscle oxygen utilization but noted it does not appear to provide a cardiac benefit (Liao et al., *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*, 2021) [38].

**Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (NMN and NR, adults over 60):** A 2025 meta-analysis found no significant improvement in skeletal muscle index, handgrip strength, gait speed, or chair stand test. The authors concluded: "Despite positive pre-clinical findings, our results highlight a negligible effect of both NMN and NR" ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

**Systematic review of 10 RCTs (NMN, 437 participants):** A 2024 review of trials using 150 to 1,200 mg NMN a day for 4 to 12 weeks found non-significant improvements in physical performance. No serious adverse effects were seen, and the reported side effects were judged unrelated to NMN (Wen et al., *Cureus*, 2024) [53].

### Muscle Strength and Physical Function in Older Adults — NMN

**Healthy older men (n=20):** A study among 20 healthy older men (average age 72) found that 250 mg NMN daily for 12 weeks increased blood NAD+ levels but did NOT produce statistically significant increases in skeletal muscle mass, walking speed, leg strength, or grip strength, nor reductions in abdominal or liver fat mass, nor improvements in cognitive function compared to placebo. Note: The abstract of this study has been described as misleading in its conclusion that NMN can be "efficient for preventing aging-related muscle dysfunctions" (Igarashi et al., *NPJ Aging*, 2022) [39].

**Older men with diabetes (n=14):** In men aged 65 or over (average age 81.1) with diabetes and reduced grip strength or walking speed, 250 mg NMN daily for 24 weeks was safe but did NOT improve grip strength or walking speed compared with placebo (Akasaka et al., *Geriatr Gerontol Int*, 2023) [54].

### Sleep — NMN

**Older Japanese adults (n=108):** A study among 108 older adults (average age 72) found that 250 mg NMN daily for 12 weeks did NOT improve sleep quality or duration compared to placebo, regardless of whether it was taken in the morning or evening. Those who took NMN in the afternoon had a modest decrease in daytime drowsiness compared to placebo (but morning dosing did not have this effect). Afternoon dosing also led to slightly greater improvement on one test of physical performance (repeated sit-to-stand) compared to morning NMN or placebo, although all three groups improved modestly on this test (Kim et al., *Nutrients*, 2022) [40].

**Older adults (n=60):** Another trial gave 250 mg NMN daily for 12 weeks. It found no difference from placebo in its main outcome, a stepping test. Among secondary outcomes, the NMN group had lower Global PSQI and "Daytime dysfunction" scores on the Pittsburgh Sleep Questionnaire, a shorter 4-metre walking time and higher blood NAD+ than placebo, with no adverse effects related to NMN (Morifuji et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [55].

### Anti-Aging Claims — NMN

The central claim driving NMN's popularity is that it counters age-related decline by restoring NAD+ levels. Laboratory research suggested that NAD+ in the skin, brain, and blood declines by about 10% to 20% with age, but no research appears to show that a decline of this size is clinically meaningful [1]. A 2016 mouse study found that NMN improved energy metabolism, physical activity, and insulin sensitivity (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [41] ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

However, the translation to humans has been disappointing:

- Human trials consistently show higher blood NAD+, but no benefit you would notice in muscle, metabolism, or the brain ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).
- All the human NMN studies Brad reviewed in 2024 were short term, so long-term effects are unknown ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).
- The Interventions Testing Program, which tests compounds in mice at three independent sites, found that NR (a similar NAD+ precursor) did NOT extend lifespan [42] ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-scam-new-evidence)).
- A 2024 preprint (not yet peer reviewed) reported that lifelong NMN increased median lifespan by 8.5% in female mice but not in male mice (Kane et al., *bioRxiv*, 2024) [56].

Brad's reading of the evidence as of 2026: NAD+ levels can be increased, but we do not have strong evidence that NAD+ precursors help combat aging in humans ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

### Preclinical Evidence — NMN (Animal Studies)

While human evidence remains limited, preclinical research in animal models has been more promising, which is partly why NMN has attracted so much interest. Key preclinical findings include:

**Aging and metabolism in mice:** Long-term oral NMN supplementation (up to 300 mg/kg/day for 12 months) in wild-type aging mice suppressed weight gain, improved energy metabolism, enhanced insulin sensitivity, eye function, and physical activity, while mitigating age-associated physiological deterioration without toxicity (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [41].

**Vascular function in mice:** In old mice, NMN supplementation restored endothelium-dependent dilation of the carotid artery, reduced aortic stiffness and oxidative stress, and restored vascular SIRT1 activity (de Picciotto et al., *Aging Cell*, 2016) [57].

**Important caveat:** The more rigorous the studies became, especially in humans, the less support emerged for these early findings ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

## 4. Recommended Dosing

The doses below are those used in studies, not a recommendation. Until there is better long-term research, Brad does not recommend NMN or any form of vitamin B3 above about 50 mg a day ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).

### NADH

Clinical studies have used 5-20 mg daily of stabilized NADH [1]:
- **Chronic fatigue syndrome:** 10-20 mg daily, alone or combined with 200 mg CoQ10 [13][15][16]
- **Cognitive support (Alzheimer's/dementia):** 10 mg daily [18][19]
- **Parkinson's disease:** 5 mg orally (enteric-coated) or 12.5-25 mg by infusion/injection [20][21][22]

NADH supplements should be taken on an empty stomach for optimal absorption, as food can interfere with the enteric coating [1].

### Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

Clinical studies have used doses ranging from 100 mg to 3,000 mg daily, often divided into two doses [1][11][12][24]:
- **Higher-dose protocols:** 500-1,000 mg twice daily (used in PAD, long COVID, and cognitive trials) [26][31][35]
- **Highest trial dose:** 1,500 mg twice daily. ConsumerLab reports an increased risk of abdominal pain at this dose [1]; the trial itself reported NR as well tolerated, with no significant difference in mild adverse events [43]
- **Onset of effect:** NAD+ increases are detectable within 2 weeks and maintained with continued use [12]

Most commercial NR products provide approximately 250 mg per daily dose [1].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

Clinical studies have used 250 mg to 2,000 mg daily [1][58][59]:
- **Lower-dose trials:** 250 mg daily [36][39]
- **Exercise performance:** 600-1,200 mg daily, divided into two doses [38]
- **Higher-dose trials:** 1,250 mg once daily for up to 4 weeks, in a safety trial [58]; a 2026 meta-analysis pooled trials of 250 to 2,000 mg a day [59]
- **European upper safe level:** an expert panel judged 300 mg a day safe for most adults, excluding those who are pregnant or breastfeeding [1]

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### NADH

NADH is generally well tolerated at doses of 5-20 mg daily. Reported side effects are rare and mild [1]:
- Feeling overly stimulated (reported in one CFS trial participant) [13]
- No serious adverse events have been reported in clinical trials

### Nicotinamide Riboside

NR is generally well tolerated at doses up to 1,000 mg twice daily. Commonly reported side effects include [1][12][24][34]:
- **Flushing:** Reported at 300 mg (2 participants) and 1,000 mg (2 participants feeling "hot") in one study [11]. Generally less common than with niacin.
- **Gastrointestinal:** Nausea, bloating, changes in stool, abdominal discomfort [10][34]
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps, muscle cramps [24][35]
- **Other:** Headache, itching, excessive sweating, increased bruising, rash, vertigo [24][34][35]
- **At high doses (1,500 mg twice daily):** ConsumerLab reports an increased risk of abdominal pain [1]. The trial itself (Berven et al., *Nat Commun*, 2023) reported NR as well tolerated, with no moderate or severe adverse events and no significant difference in mild adverse events [43]
- **LDL cholesterol:** A combination of NR and pterostilbene (Basis) caused a slight but significant increase in LDL cholesterol with two capsules daily and among overweight people taking one capsule [10]. Importantly, NR alone (Niagen) at comparable doses did not elevate LDL cholesterol [12].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

NMN has been well tolerated in short-term human trials:
- **No harmful side effects** were seen in the 18 short-term human NMN studies Brad reviewed in 2024, but long-term safety is unknown ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements))
- **European expert panel:** No side effects of NMN were reported in clinical studies using up to 900 mg/day for 2 months, or 250 mg/day for 6 months. The panel concluded that NMN is safe for most adults, excluding those who are pregnant or breastfeeding, at a maximum of 300 mg per day (EFSA Panel, *EFSA J*, 2026) [1]
- **Higher-dose trial:** In 31 healthy adults aged 20 to 65, 1,250 mg NMN once daily for up to 4 weeks caused no changes beyond normal variation in blood, urine and body-composition tests, and no severe adverse events (Fukamizu et al., *Sci Rep*, 2022) [58]
- **Pooled trials:** A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 randomised trials (250 to 2,000 mg/day, 14 days to 24 weeks) found that NMN did not increase overall, serious or system-specific adverse events, and did not significantly raise the liver enzymes ALT or AST. The authors note that larger and longer trials are still needed to confirm long-term safety (Yang et al., *Nutrients*, 2026) [59]
- **Animal toxicology:** In a 91-day rat study, the highest dose with no observed adverse effect was 1,000 mg/kg/day in females and 500 mg/kg/day in males; 2,000 mg/kg/day reduced body weight and food intake (Turner et al., *Front Pharmacol*, 2021) [60]. A separate 90-day rat study put this level at no less than 800 mg/kg body weight and found NMN was not mutagenic (Yu et al., *Curr Res Toxicol*, 2024) [61]

### Vitamin B3 Upper Intake Level

All NAD boosters are sources of vitamin B3, which has an established Tolerable Upper Intake Level of 35 mg per day for adults in the United States. Nearly all NAD-boosting products exceed this limit [1]. However, this limit is based on skin flushing, which is primarily associated with nicotinic acid (niacin), not nicotinamide or its derivatives [1]. European agencies have set separate Tolerable Upper Intake Levels for nicotinamide (niacinamide) specifically: 500 mg in the UK and 900 mg in the European Union [1]. Brad's concern with long-term high doses: niacin megadoses of 1 to 3 grams a day increased all-cause mortality by 10%, and a 2024 study found that the B3 metabolites 2PY and 4PY appear to worsen blood vessel inflammation and increase the risk of cardiovascular death ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).

Toxicities at high intakes of nicotinamide include [1]:
- Liver injury
- Damage to the macula (part of the retina)
- Gout
- Declines in platelet counts
- Impaired glucose control
- Increased blood levels of anticonvulsant drugs

On the liver specifically: high doses of niacin (nicotinic acid) can cause mild to moderate rises in liver enzymes, and high doses or certain formulations have been linked to acute liver injury that can be severe or even fatal (LiverTox, *NIDDK*) [62]. Short-term NMN trials have not shown a rise in liver enzymes [59], but long-term data are lacking.

### Cancer Concerns

There is a theoretical concern, based on animal research, that compounds that raise NAD+ levels — including NR, NADH, and possibly NMN — may increase the risk of cancer development, promote the growth of existing cancers, and increase the risk of metastasis [1][44][45].

**Mouse study on breast cancer:** A laboratory study in mice found that 70% of those consuming a nicotinamide riboside-enriched diet for 2 weeks prior to being implanted with triple-negative breast cancer (a highly aggressive type with high NR uptake) developed detectable tumors, compared to 55% of control mice. Metastases to the brain were also more common among mice given NR (82% vs. 25%) (Maric et al., *Biosens Bioelectron*, 2023) [44]. It is unknown whether NR affects less aggressive cancers — some animal research suggests it may not (Hamity et al., *Pain*, 2020; Acklin et al., *Neurooncol Adv*, 2022) [46][47].

**Proposed alternative approach:** One group of researchers has suggested that reducing NAD+ levels — rather than raising them — may be a promising cancer treatment approach (Gujar et al., *PNAS*, 2016) [48].

**Anecdotal PSA elevation:** One consumer reported that after one year taking Elysium Basis (which provides NR), PSA levels rose sharply from 1.8 to 4.9, returning to 1.9 after stopping the supplement for one month. Elysium Health acknowledged that "a handful of people" had reached out about PSA changes but stated they have no data showing a correlation [1].

**No link with other forms of B3:** There does not appear to be any evidence linking other forms of vitamin B3 (niacin and niacinamide) with increased cancer risk. Higher intake of niacin has been associated with lower risk of cancer-related death, and niacinamide supplementation was shown in one study to reduce the risk of some types of skin cancer [1].

**Practical recommendation:** While no evidence currently demonstrates that NR, NMN, or NADH causes cancer in humans, it would be prudent to avoid NAD-boosting supplements if you have been diagnosed with cancer [1].

### Kidney Concerns — NMN

There is some concern that NMN supplementation may cause or worsen kidney inflammation associated with aging, based on an animal study [1][49]:

**Mouse study:** Aged mice (equivalent to approximately 79-year-old humans) given water containing NMN for 8 weeks showed increased levels of an inflammatory protein (IL-1beta) in their kidneys, with the greatest increase in mice given NMN alone. Mice given NMN also experienced injury to part of the kidney and buildup of toxic NMN breakdown products in blood and organs due to less efficient kidney filtering (Saleh et al., *bioRxiv*, 2024 — preprint) [49].

Although this evidence is preliminary, it may be prudent for people with impaired kidney function, or at risk for kidney injury, to avoid NMN until more is known [1].

## 6. Drug Interactions

### General Precautions

- **Anticonvulsant drugs:** High doses of nicotinamide (a metabolite of all NAD precursors) may increase blood levels of anticonvulsant drugs [1].
- **Glucose-lowering medications:** High doses of nicotinamide may impair glucose control, which could interact with diabetes medications [1].
- **Immunosuppressants and chemotherapy:** Given the theoretical concern about NAD+ promoting cancer cell growth, individuals on cancer treatment should consult their oncologist before taking NAD-boosting supplements [1][44].
- **Levodopa (Parkinson's):** NADH has been studied alongside levodopa and appeared to modestly increase levodopa blood levels in one study; coordination with a neurologist is advisable [21].

### Consultation Recommended

Talk with your doctor before starting an NAD booster, especially if you [1]:
- Have been diagnosed with cancer
- Have impaired kidney function or are at risk of kidney injury
- Take anticonvulsant or glucose-lowering drugs
- Are pregnant or breastfeeding

## 7. Dietary Sources

### NAD+ Precursors from Food

The primary dietary source of NAD+ is niacin (vitamin B3) and its derivatives. Most people obtain adequate B3 from food alone, as it is widely distributed in the diet [1]. The Recommended Dietary Allowance for niacin is 14 mg/day for women and 16 mg/day for men [1].

**Top dietary sources of niacin (vitamin B3):**

| Food | Serving | Niacin (mg NE) |
|------|---------|:-:|
| Chicken breast | 3 oz (85g) | 11.4 |
| Tuna (light, canned) | 3 oz (85g) | 8.6 |
| Turkey breast | 3 oz (85g) | 10.0 |
| Salmon | 3 oz (85g) | 8.6 |
| Beef (lean) | 3 oz (85g) | 6.2 |
| Peanuts | 1 oz (28g) | 4.4 |

| Fortified cereals | 1 serving | 20.0+ |

NE = niacin equivalents (includes tryptophan conversion, where 60 mg tryptophan = 1 mg niacin).

### NMN in Foods

Reliable figures for NMN in foods are not available from the sources this review uses.

### Nicotinamide Riboside in Foods

NR is found naturally in cow's milk (approximately 3.9 micromol/L) and trace amounts in other foods, but like NMN, dietary amounts are too low to achieve the doses used in clinical trials [7].
