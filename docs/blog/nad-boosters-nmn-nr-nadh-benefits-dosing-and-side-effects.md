## Current view (reviewed 20 October 2026)

NMN and NR reliably raise blood NAD+, but after twenty years and dozens of clinical trials there is still no sign that this turns into anything you would notice in muscle, metabolism or the brain ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). A 2025 meta-analysis of 10 randomised trials in adults over 60 found no improvement in skeletal muscle index, handgrip strength, gait speed or chair stand tests, and in a long COVID trial NR tripled blood NAD+ without improving a single symptom ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Gut bacteria break both NMN and NR down to nicotinic acid, a cheap form of vitamin B3, before the body rebuilds NAD+ ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Until there is better long-term research, he does not recommend NMN or any form of vitamin B3 above about 50 mg a day ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)). He advises against NAD+ IV drips: in the first trials the infused NAD+ was rapidly removed from the blood, and most participants had nausea, headache, diarrhea and muscle tightness, with signs of inflammation ([Brad, 20 Nov 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/popular-longevity-treatment-makes-you-sick)). The approach he recommends instead is regular exercise, since exercise-trained older adults had NAD+ levels similar to young people ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

## Key safety points

- Nicotinamide, which all NAD boosters can supply, raised carbamazepine levels and slowed the breakdown of primidone in a small study; if you take an anticonvulsant, talk to your doctor before starting [68].
- Intravenous NADH raised levodopa levels in a small Parkinson's study, so check with your neurologist if you take levodopa [21].
- Animal studies raise a theoretical concern that raising NAD+ may help some cancers grow or spread; if you have cancer or are on cancer treatment, talk to your oncologist before taking an NAD booster [44].
- In old mice, NMN raised markers of kidney inflammation (early mouse evidence, not peer reviewed). If you are older or have kidney disease, ask your doctor first; the authors call for kidney-safety checks in older people [49].
- The European safety opinion on NMN judged up to 300 mg a day safe for adults but excluded pregnant and breastfeeding women [65].
- Very high doses of nicotinamide can harm the liver; a review advised treating adult doses above 3 g a day as potentially toxic and discouraged unsupervised use [67].
- NR trials report mostly mild side effects such as nausea, flushing, cramps, headache and bruising, and the long-term safety of NMN is not yet known [24][35][59].

## 1. Overview

NAD, short for nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, is a coenzyme found in all living cells [4]. It carries electrons from one chemical reaction to the next, which is how cells turn food into usable energy, and it exists in an oxidized form (NAD+) and a reduced form (NADH) [4]. NAD+ also acts as a signalling molecule that helps control hundreds of key processes, from energy metabolism to cell survival (Rajman et al., *Cell Metab*, 2018) [2].

The body makes NAD+ from the amino acid tryptophan and from forms of vitamin B3: nicotinic acid (niacin), nicotinamide (niacinamide) and nicotinamide riboside (NR) [3]. Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) is an intermediate on the way to NAD+ (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [41].

Interest in NAD-boosting supplements grew from research suggesting that NAD+ falls with age. A 2012 study found that NAD+ decreased in human skin tissue with advancing age (Massudi et al., *PLoS One*, 2012) [5], and a 2013 study in mice showed that raising NAD+ levels could reverse aspects of mitochondrial dysfunction (Gomes et al., *Cell*, 2013) [6]. These findings raised the hope that boosting NAD+ could provide anti-aging benefits. However, a 2026 study that measured whole-blood NAD+ in seven independent groups of people found that levels stayed stable with age and with lifestyle changes, though they rose with NR, which suggests blood NAD+ may not be a reliable marker of aging (Trętowicz et al., *Nat Metab*, 2026) [63].

The three main categories of NAD-boosting supplements are:

- **NAD+/NADH**: the coenzyme itself, taken by mouth; the trials below used a stabilized oral form of NADH (ENADA) [13][19].
- **Nicotinamide riboside (NR)**: a precursor to NAD+ that is converted to NMN by nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1/NRK2), then to NAD+ by NMNATs [4][7]. It is sold as NR chloride (Niagen) [12] and, combined with pterostilbene, in Elysium Health's Basis [10].
- **Nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)**: like NR, a precursor to NAD+ [41]. Trials have used the beta form, β-NMN [58][65].

This article reviews the human clinical evidence, forms, dosing, safety, and practical considerations for each of these NAD-boosting compounds.

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### NAD+ and NADH

The oral NADH trials below used 10 to 20 mg a day, mostly of a stabilized form (ENADA) [13][15][19].

### Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

NR is available in several commercial forms:

- **Nicotinamide riboside chloride (Niagen)**: a crystal form of NR chloride, tested in an 8-week trial in overweight adults [12].
- **NR with pterostilbene (Basis)**: Elysium Health's product combines NR with pterostilbene, a polyphenol found in blueberries [10].

Isotope-tracing and human gut-sample studies reviewed by Brad in 2026 found that oral NR is largely broken down by gut bacteria to nicotinic acid, which the body then rebuilds into NAD+ ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Once inside cells, NR is phosphorylated by nicotinamide riboside kinases (NRK1 and NRK2) to form NMN, which is then adenylated by NMNATs to form NAD+ [4][7]. A pharmacokinetic study in 12 healthy adults showed that single doses of 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR raised blood NAD+ metabolites in a dose-dependent way (Trammell et al., *Nat Commun*, 2016) [11]. In an 8-week placebo-controlled trial in overweight adults, 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR (Niagen) a day raised whole-blood NAD+ by 22%, 51% and 142% within 2 weeks, and the rises held for the rest of the study (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [12].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

NMN, like NR, is an NAD+ precursor, and in mice oral NMN is quickly used to make NAD+ in tissues (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [41]. Brad's 2026 review found that NMN is also broken down by gut bacteria to nicotinic acid before NAD+ is rebuilt, even when NMN or NR is injected into a vein: the body passes it into bile and back to the gut ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). In mice, a gene called Slc12a8 encodes a specific NMN transporter in the small intestine; it carries NMN but not NR (Grozio et al., *Nat Metab*, 2019) [50].

### NR vs. NMN: Head-to-Head

In a randomised study of 65 participants published in *Nature Metabolism*, NR and NMN each roughly doubled circulating NAD+ after 14 days, with no significant difference between them ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). A smaller crossover study gave 6 healthy adults 1,200 mg a day of each and found about a 2.3-fold higher NAD+ increase with NR, but responses were not very consistent from one measurement to the next ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)). Because gut bacteria convert both to nicotinic acid first, Brad considers the NR-versus-NMN debate beside the point ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

Choose a product that has passed independent testing; consumerlab.com and Labdoor publish product test results.

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Chronic Fatigue Syndrome (CFS) — NADH

NADH helps trigger energy production through ATP, the cell's energy carrier, which is why it has been tested for chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS) [13].

**Crossover trial (n=26):** In a randomised, double-blind crossover trial, 26 people with CFS took either 10 mg of NADH a day, in its stabilized form (ENADA), or placebo, for 4 weeks each, with a 4-week washout between. On NADH, 8 of 26 (31%) responded favourably, against 2 of 26 (8%) on placebo, and no severe adverse effects were linked to NADH. The authors called it a pilot study and asked for further trials (Forsyth et al., *Ann Allergy Asthma Immunol*, 1999) [13].

**Controlled trial with active comparator (n=31):** In a trial of 31 people with CFS randomised to NADH or to nutritional supplements plus psychological therapy for 24 months, the 12 who took NADH had a large, significant drop in symptom scores in the first 3 months of treatment. After that, scores were similar in both groups, and the authors say the result should be further assessed in a larger patient sample (Santaella et al., *P R Health Sci J*, 2004) [14].

**NADH + CoQ10 combination (n=73, positive):** In an 8-week randomised, double-blind trial in 73 people with CFS, 20 mg of NADH plus 200 mg of CoQ10 a day lowered the total fatigue impact score more than placebo did. The authors called for larger trials to confirm it (Castro-Marrero et al., *Antioxid Redox Signal*, 2015) [15].

**NADH + CoQ10 combination (n=207, negative):** In a larger trial in 207 people with ME/CFS, 8 weeks of 20 mg NADH plus 200 mg CoQ10 a day lowered cognitive fatigue and the overall FIS-40 fatigue score from baseline in the treated group. But fatigue scores moved in parallel in the placebo group, and the authors note that the lack of differences between groups may reflect a placebo effect (Castro-Marrero et al., *Nutrients*, 2021) [16].

**Expert opinion:** A European expert consensus lists NADH among supplements that may be tried for fatigue or cognitive problems in ME/CFS (Nacul et al., *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2021) [17].

### Alzheimer's Disease and Cognitive Function — NADH

**Dementia (n=19, uncontrolled):** In a 3-month open-label study, 25 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's, vascular or fronto-temporal dementia took 10 mg of NADH a day alongside their usual medicines. The 19 who finished showed no cognitive benefit on standard tests (Rainer et al., *J Neural Transm (Vienna)*, 2000) [18].

**Alzheimer's disease (n=26, controlled):** In a 6-month randomised, double-blind trial, 26 people with probable Alzheimer's disease took 10 mg a day of stabilized oral NADH or placebo. The NADH group showed no sign of cognitive decline and scored higher than placebo on the Mattis Dementia Rating Scale, but attention, memory and clinician-rated dementia severity did not differ (Demarin et al., *Drugs Exp Clin Res*, 2004) [19].

### Parkinson's Disease — NADH

NADH has been proposed as a way to raise the body's own production of levodopa, the building block of dopamine, which is the brain chemical that falls in Parkinson's disease [21].

**Open-label study (n=885):** In an open-label study, 885 people with Parkinson's disease took NADH, about half by intravenous infusion and half by mouth. About 80% were reported to benefit, and the oral form improved disability about as much as the infused form. There was no placebo group (Birkmayer et al., *Acta Neurol Scand Suppl*, 1993) [20].

**Open-label study with levodopa (n=15):** In an open study, 15 people with Parkinson's disease received intravenous infusions of 10 mg NADH, each lasting 30 minutes, for 7 days on top of their usual treatment. Their UPDRS scores improved, and NADH raised the amount of levodopa reaching the blood (Kuhn et al., *J Neural Transm (Vienna)*, 1996) [21].

**Placebo-controlled trial (negative):** In a double-blind study, five people with Parkinson's disease received 25 mg of NADH intravenously once a day for 4 days, then 25 mg by intramuscular injection after 2 and 4 weeks, while four people received saline on the same schedule. Both groups tended to improve after the infusions, but the changes were not significant, and nothing changed in the following weeks (Dizdar et al., *Acta Neurol Scand*, 1994) [22].

### Raising NAD+ Levels — Nicotinamide Riboside

Several trials show that NR raises blood NAD+ [11][12][24], but trials have not shown a benefit you would notice ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

**Dose-response in healthy adults (n=12):** Single doses of 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR raised blood NAD+ metabolites with no serious adverse events. Flushing was reported by two people at 300 mg, and feeling hot by two at 1,000 mg, and no event was linked to dose (Trammell et al., *Nat Commun*, 2016) [11].

**Eight-week dose-response (overweight adults):** In overweight but otherwise healthy adults, 100 mg, 300 mg or 1,000 mg of NR (Niagen) a day for 8 weeks raised whole-blood NAD+ by 22%, 51% and 142% within 2 weeks, and the rises held to the end of the study. Nobody reported flushing, adverse events did not differ from placebo, and LDL cholesterol did not rise (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [12].

**Older adults (n=24):** In a 6-week crossover trial in healthy adults aged 55 to 79, 500 mg of NR twice a day was well tolerated and raised NAD+ in blood immune cells (PBMCs) by about 60% compared with placebo. On NR, one person each reported nausea, flushing, leg cramps and increased bruising; two of the three people who reported flushing were taking placebo (Martens et al., *Nat Commun*, 2018) [24].

**NR + pterostilbene (n=120):** In a two-month trial in 120 healthy adults aged 60 to 80 years, Basis (Elysium Health) at one capsule a day (250 mg NR plus 50 mg pterostilbene) or two capsules raised whole-blood NAD+ by about 40% and 90% after 4 weeks. The rise held at 60 days on one capsule and fell back to about 55% above baseline on two. Diastolic blood pressure decreased significantly at day 60 on one capsule. Possibly related side effects were nausea in one person on one capsule, and fatigue, headache, indigestion, abdominal discomfort and diarrhea in five people on two capsules (Dellinger et al., *NPJ Aging Mech Dis*, 2017) [10].

### Energy and Physical Performance — Nicotinamide Riboside

Due to NAD's role in cellular energy production, it was hoped that NR would improve functional energy levels.

**Physical performance review:** A 2020 review found that NR is considered safe in older people, but that its effect on physical performance on its own is unclear (Custodero et al., *Exp Gerontol*, 2020) [25].

### Peripheral Artery Disease — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Walking distance (n=90):** In a 6-month randomised trial in 90 people with lower extremity peripheral artery disease (PAD), NR improved 6-minute walk distance compared with placebo (+7.0 vs -10.6 metres; between-group difference +17.6 metres, 90% CI +1.8 to +∞). Adding resveratrol did not add benefit, and the authors call for a larger trial to confirm the result (McDermott et al., *Nat Commun*, 2024) [26].

**Cognitive function in PAD (n=8):** Over 4 weeks, 8 older adults with PAD took NR in an open-label pilot. The authors report preliminary results of better blood-flow responses in the brain and better scores across several cognitive domains, but with no control group they call for controlled trials in larger groups to confirm it (Szarvas et al., *J Pharmacol Exp Ther*, 2025) [27].

### Alzheimer's Disease — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Animal studies:** In a mouse model of Alzheimer's disease, NR slowed cognitive decline and cut amyloid-beta production (Gong et al., *Neurobiol Aging*, 2013) [28]. In aged mice it also restored muscle stem cell function (Zhang et al., *Science*, 2016) [29].

**Biomarker analysis (n=22):** In 22 healthy older adults, 500 mg of NR twice a day for 6 weeks raised NAD+ in blood vesicles thought to come from neurons and lowered some markers linked to neurodegeneration, such as amyloid-beta 42. These are blood markers, not clinical outcomes (Vreones et al., *Aging Cell*, 2023) [30].

**Mild cognitive impairment (n=20):** In a 10-week pilot trial in 20 older adults with mild cognitive impairment, NR was increased step by step to a final dose of 1 g a day. Blood NAD+ rose 2.6-fold and adverse events did not differ from placebo, but cognition did not change. Walking speed improved in the placebo group, possibly a practice effect, but not in the NR group (Orr et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [31].

**Self-reported cognitive decline (n=37):** In a crossover trial in adults over 55 with subjective cognitive decline or mild cognitive impairment, 37 of whom completed it, cognition did not change after 8 weeks of 1 g of NR a day. Blood levels of phosphorylated tau 217, a marker of Alzheimer's disease, fell 7% after NR and rose 18% with placebo (p = 0.02), but two other blood markers (GFAP and NfL) did not differ. Adverse events did not differ between NR and placebo; one person had a flare of a long-standing gait disorder during NR (Wu et al., *Alzheimers Dement (N Y)*, 2025) [32].

**Memory-predominant mild cognitive impairment (n=42):** In a 12-week pilot trial, 42 adults with amnestic mild cognitive impairment completed NR or placebo. NR doubled blood NAD+ but did not improve cognition, total brain blood flow or blood pressure, and there were no serious adverse effects (Martens et al., *Alzheimers Dement*, 2026) [64].

### Cholesterol — Nicotinamide Riboside

In diabetic mice, NR lowered cholesterol in the liver (Lee et al., *J Med Food*, 2015) [33]. In an 8-week human trial, NR alone did not raise LDL cholesterol (Conze et al., *Sci Rep*, 2019) [12].

### Blood Sugar and Insulin Resistance — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Obese men with insulin resistance (n=40):** In a 12-week trial in 40 obese, insulin-resistant men aged 40 to 70, 1,000 mg of NR twice daily did not improve insulin sensitivity or glucose metabolism and did not change body composition. There were no serious adverse events, and safety blood tests stayed normal (Dollerup et al., *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2018) [34].

### Long COVID — Nicotinamide Riboside

**Brain fog and fatigue:** In a 24-week trial, 58 adults with long COVID were randomised to NR (2,000 mg a day) or to placebo first. NR raised NAD+ 2.6- to 3.1-fold after 5 to 10 weeks, but cognition, fatigue, sleep, anxiety and depression did not improve compared with placebo (Wu et al., *eClinicalMedicine*, 2025) [35]. Side effects judged possibly related to NR included nausea, flushing, rash, vertigo, bruising, muscle and leg cramps, and worse headaches [35]. Niagen Bioscience was one of the funders [35]. Brad's account of the same trial agrees: 58 participants and a 2.6- to 3.1-fold rise in NAD+ over 20 weeks ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

### Raising NAD+ Levels — NMN

Like NR, NMN raises blood NAD+ in trials (Yi et al., *Geroscience*, 2023; Morifuji et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [51][55]. Trials have used 250 mg to 2,000 mg a day [59].

**Multicenter dose trial (n=80):** Healthy middle-aged adults took placebo or 300 mg, 600 mg or 900 mg NMN once daily for 60 days. Blood NAD rose in all NMN groups compared with placebo, and was highest at 600 mg and 900 mg. Six-minute walking distance improved more in all NMN groups than with placebo, and blood biological age (an algorithm-based estimate) rose in the placebo group but stayed unchanged with NMN. NMN did not change insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) compared with placebo, and no safety issues were found up to 900 mg daily (Yi et al., *Geroscience*, 2023) [51].

**Healthy middle-aged adults (n=36):** Taking 125 mg NMN twice daily (250 mg/day) for 12 weeks raised serum nicotinamide compared with placebo. Arterial stiffness tended to fall, but the difference from placebo was not significant. NMN was well tolerated with no adverse events (Katayoshi et al., *Sci Rep*, 2023) [52].

### Insulin Sensitivity — NMN

**Postmenopausal women with prediabetes (n=25):** In a 10-week trial in overweight or obese postmenopausal women with prediabetes, NMN raised muscle insulin sensitivity and insulin signalling, which did not change on placebo (Yoshino et al., *Science*, 2021) [36]. One critic noted that the placebo group started with more liver fat (14.8 ± 2.0% vs 6.3 ± 1.2%), and since NMN may act on liver fat, the trial was not effectively randomised (Brenner, *Science*, 2021) [37].

### Exercise Performance — NMN

**Recreational runners (n=48):** In a 6-week trial, 48 recreational runners training 5 to 6 times a week took placebo or NMN at 300, 600 or 1,200 mg a day. Oxygen uptake and power at the first and second ventilatory thresholds rose more in the medium-dose and high-dose groups than with placebo, but maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max), O2-pulse and peak power did not change. The authors suggest the gain likely comes from better oxygen use in muscle (Liao et al., *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*, 2021) [38].

**Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs (NMN and NR, adults over 60):** A 2025 meta-analysis found no significant improvement in skeletal muscle index, handgrip strength, gait speed, or chair stand test. The authors concluded: "Despite positive pre-clinical findings, our results highlight a negligible effect of both NMN and NR" ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

**Systematic review of 10 RCTs (NMN, 437 participants):** A 2024 review of trials using 150 to 1,200 mg NMN a day for 4 to 12 weeks found non-significant improvements in physical performance. No serious adverse effects were seen, and the reported side effects were judged unrelated to NMN (Wen et al., *Cureus*, 2024) [53].

### Muscle Strength and Physical Function in Older Adults — NMN

**Healthy older men (n=42):** In 42 healthy men aged 65 years or more, randomised to 250 mg of NMN a day or placebo for 12 weeks (20 completed the full 12 weeks), NMN was well tolerated and raised blood NAD+. Gait speed and left-hand grip strength showed nominally significant gains that the authors say need checking in larger studies, and body composition did not change (Igarashi et al., *NPJ Aging*, 2022) [39].

**Older men with diabetes (n=14):** In men aged 65 or over (average age 81.1) with diabetes and reduced grip strength or walking speed, 250 mg NMN daily for 24 weeks was safe but did NOT improve grip strength or walking speed compared with placebo (Akasaka et al., *Geriatr Gerontol Int*, 2023) [54].

### Sleep — NMN

**Older Japanese adults (n=108):** In a 12-week trial, 108 older adults took 250 mg of NMN or placebo once a day, in the morning or the afternoon. The authors report that afternoon NMN improved lower-limb function (a sit-to-stand test) and reduced drowsiness, with the largest effects of any group, but drowsiness also improved with afternoon placebo and sit-to-stand times improved in all groups (Kim et al., *Nutrients*, 2022) [40].

**Older adults (n=60):** Another trial gave 250 mg NMN daily for 12 weeks. It found no difference from placebo in its main outcome, a stepping test. Among secondary outcomes, the NMN group had lower Global PSQI and "Daytime dysfunction" scores on the Pittsburgh Sleep Questionnaire, a shorter 4-metre walking time and higher blood NAD+ than placebo, with no adverse effects related to NMN (Morifuji et al., *Geroscience*, 2024) [55].

### Anti-Aging Claims — NMN

The central claim driving NMN's popularity is that it counters age-related decline by restoring NAD+ levels. A 2016 mouse study found that NMN improved energy metabolism, physical activity, and insulin sensitivity (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [41] ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).

However, the translation to humans has been disappointing:

- Human trials consistently show higher blood NAD+, but no benefit you would notice in muscle, metabolism, or the brain ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/new-study-says-i-was-wrong-about-nmn-and-nr)).
- All the human NMN studies Brad reviewed in 2024 were short term, so long-term effects are unknown ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).
- The Interventions Testing Program, which tests compounds in mice at three independent sites, found that NR (a similar NAD+ precursor) did NOT extend lifespan [42] ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-scam-new-evidence)).
- A 2024 preprint (not yet peer reviewed) reported that lifelong NMN increased median lifespan by 8.5% in female mice but not in male mice (Kane et al., *bioRxiv*, 2024) [56].

Brad's reading of the evidence as of 2026: NAD+ levels can be increased, but we do not have strong evidence that NAD+ precursors help combat aging in humans ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

### Preclinical Evidence — NMN (Animal Studies)

While human evidence remains limited, preclinical research in animal models has been more promising, which is partly why NMN has attracted so much interest. Key preclinical findings include:

**Aging and metabolism in mice:** Long-term oral NMN supplementation (up to 300 mg/kg/day for 12 months) in wild-type aging mice suppressed weight gain, improved energy metabolism, enhanced insulin sensitivity, eye function, and physical activity, while mitigating age-associated physiological deterioration without toxicity (Mills et al., *Cell Metab*, 2016) [41].

**Vascular function in mice:** In old mice, NMN supplementation restored endothelium-dependent dilation of the carotid artery, reduced aortic stiffness and oxidative stress, and restored vascular SIRT1 activity (de Picciotto et al., *Aging Cell*, 2016) [57].

**Important caveat:** The more rigorous the studies became, especially in humans, the less support emerged for these early findings ([Brad, 11 Jan 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nmn-nad-supplements-for-anti-aging-just-changed)).

## 4. Recommended Dosing

The doses below are those used in studies, not a recommendation. Until there is better long-term research, Brad does not recommend NMN or any form of vitamin B3 above about 50 mg a day ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).

### NADH

Oral NADH trials have used 10 to 20 mg a day, mostly of stabilized NADH [13][15][19]:
- **Chronic fatigue syndrome:** 10-20 mg daily, alone or combined with 200 mg CoQ10 [13][15][16]
- **Cognitive support (Alzheimer's/dementia):** 10 mg daily [18][19]
- **Parkinson's disease:** 10 mg by infusion, or 25 mg by infusion and injection [21][22]

### Nicotinamide Riboside (NR)

Clinical studies have used between 100 mg and 3,000 mg a day [12][43]:
- **Higher-dose protocols:** 500 mg twice a day [24][30] to 1,000 mg twice daily [34]
- **Highest trial dose:** 1,500 mg twice daily for four weeks, in a safety trial in Parkinson's disease, where NR was well tolerated with no moderate or severe adverse events [43]
- **Onset of effect:** NAD+ increases are detectable within 2 weeks and maintained with continued use [12]

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

Clinical studies have used 250 mg to 2,000 mg daily [58][59]:
- **Lower-dose trials:** 250 mg daily [39][40]
- **Exercise performance:** 300, 600 or 1,200 mg a day [38]
- **Higher-dose trials:** 1,250 mg once daily for up to 4 weeks, in a safety trial [58]; a 2026 meta-analysis pooled trials of 250 to 2,000 mg a day [59]
- **European safety opinion:** an EFSA expert panel judged β-NMN safe at up to 300 mg a day for adults, excluding pregnant and breastfeeding women [65]

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### NADH

The NADH trials reported few side effects:
- No severe adverse effects were linked to 10 mg of NADH a day in a 4-week crossover trial in CFS [13]
- No side effects were reported in an open-label study of 17 people with Alzheimer-type dementia treated for 8 to 12 weeks [9]

### Nicotinamide Riboside

NR has been well tolerated in trials at 500 mg twice a day [24] and 1,000 mg twice daily [34]. Side effects reported in trials include:
- **Flushing:** in one single-dose study, flushing was reported by two people at 300 mg and feeling hot by two at 1,000 mg, with no link to dose [11]. In a 6-week trial, two of the three people who reported flushing were taking placebo, and the authors suggest NR may be less likely than niacin to cause flushing [24].
- **Gastrointestinal:** nausea, indigestion, abdominal discomfort and diarrhea [10][24][35]
- **Musculoskeletal:** Leg cramps, muscle cramps [24][35]
- **Other:** worse headaches, fatigue, increased bruising, rash, vertigo [10][24][35]
- **Highest tested dose (1,500 mg twice daily):** a four-week trial in Parkinson's disease found NR well tolerated, with no moderate or severe adverse events and no significant difference in mild adverse events, though serum homocysteine rose slightly at first (Berven et al., *Nat Commun*, 2023) [43]
- **LDL cholesterol:** in the Basis trial (NR with pterostilbene), overweight people had rises in LDL cholesterol on one and two capsules, with larger rises in total and LDL cholesterol on two capsules, though the groups differed at baseline [10]. NR alone (Niagen) did not raise LDL cholesterol in an 8-week trial [12].

### Nicotinamide Mononucleotide (NMN)

NMN has been well tolerated in short-term human trials:
- **No harmful side effects** were seen in the 18 short-term human NMN studies Brad reviewed in 2024, but long-term safety is unknown ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements))
- **European safety opinion:** an EFSA expert panel judged β-NMN safe for adults at up to 300 mg a day, excluding pregnant and breastfeeding women, and the human study it reviewed raised no safety concerns (EFSA NDA Panel, *EFSA J*, 2026) [65]
- **Higher-dose trial:** In 31 healthy adults aged 20 to 65, 1,250 mg NMN once daily for up to 4 weeks caused no changes beyond normal variation in blood, urine and body-composition tests, and no severe adverse events (Fukamizu et al., *Sci Rep*, 2022) [58]
- **Pooled trials:** A 2026 meta-analysis of 10 randomised trials (250 to 2,000 mg/day, 14 days to 24 weeks) found that NMN did not increase overall, serious or system-specific adverse events, and did not significantly raise the liver enzymes ALT or AST. The authors note that larger and longer trials are still needed to confirm long-term safety (Yang et al., *Nutrients*, 2026) [59]
- **Animal toxicology:** In a 91-day rat study, the highest dose with no observed adverse effect was 1,000 mg/kg/day in females and 500 mg/kg/day in males; 2,000 mg/kg/day reduced body weight and food intake (Turner et al., *Front Pharmacol*, 2021) [60]. A separate 90-day rat study put this level at no less than 800 mg/kg body weight and found NMN was not mutagenic (Yu et al., *Curr Res Toxicol*, 2024) [61]

### Vitamin B3 Upper Intake Level

All NAD boosters are sources of vitamin B3. In the United States, the upper limit for vitamin B3 from supplements is 35 mg a day for adults. It is based on flushing from nicotinic acid (niacin), but it applies to nicotinamide too, though not to people taking B3 under medical supervision [70]. The European Union's upper level for nicotinamide is 900 mg a day, and up to 300 mg a day of β-NMN, the most EFSA judged safe, corresponds to 109.7 mg a day of nicotinamide (EFSA NDA Panel, *EFSA J*, 2026) [65]. Brad's concern with long-term high doses: niacin megadoses of 1 to 3 grams a day increased all-cause mortality by 10%, and a 2024 study found that the B3 metabolites 2PY and 4PY appear to worsen blood vessel inflammation and increase the risk of cardiovascular death ([Brad, 7 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/human-studies-nmn-supplements)).

High intakes of nicotinamide may cause:
- Liver injury: reversible liver damage at very high doses in animals and people, and occasional minor liver enzyme changes at lower therapeutic doses [67]
- Falls in platelet counts: in a trial in people on hemodialysis, nicotinamide at 500 mg a day, then 1,000 mg a day, lowered platelet counts (Shahbazian et al., *Nefrologia*, 2011) [69]
- Impaired glucose control: minor insulin resistance has been reported [67]
- Raised anticonvulsant levels: nicotinamide raised carbamazepine levels and slowed the breakdown of primidone in a small study [68]

A 2000 review advised treating nicotinamide doses above 3 g a day in adults as potentially toxic, and discouraged unsupervised use (Knip et al., *Diabetologia*, 2000) [67].

On the liver specifically: high doses of niacin (nicotinic acid) can cause mild to moderate rises in liver enzymes, and high doses or certain formulations have been linked to acute liver injury that can be severe or even fatal (LiverTox, *NIDDK*) [62]. Liver damage from high-dose niacin can cause fatigue, nausea and loss of appetite; if these or unexplained belly pain start while you take high doses of B3, stop and see a doctor [70]. Short-term NMN trials have not shown a rise in liver enzymes [59], but long-term data are lacking.

### Cancer Concerns

Animal and laboratory research raises a theoretical concern that raising NAD+ may help some cancers grow or spread [44][45][48].

**Mouse study on breast cancer:** In a mouse model of triple-negative breast cancer, NR supplementation significantly increased how often cancer developed and how often it spread to the brain (Maric et al., *Biosens Bioelectron*, 2023) [44]. Other rodent studies point the other way: NR given with chemotherapy did not weaken, and may have strengthened, the drugs' effect on tumours (Hamity et al., *Pain*, 2020; Acklin et al., *Neurooncol Adv*, 2022) [46][47].

**Proposed alternative approach:** In glioblastoma, a brain cancer, blocking the enzyme that makes NAD+ lowered NAD+ and reduced the self-renewal of cancer stem cells, which suggests that lowering NAD+, not raising it, may help treat some cancers (Gujar et al., *PNAS*, 2016) [48].

**Other forms of B3:** A 2000 review found no evidence that nicotinamide causes cancer in people [67]. In a 12-month trial in 386 people at high risk of skin cancer, 500 mg of nicotinamide twice daily lowered the rate of new non-melanoma skin cancers by 23% (95% CI 4 to 38) compared with placebo (Chen et al., *N Engl J Med*, 2015) [66].

**Practical recommendation:** Because of these animal findings, if you have cancer, talk to your oncologist before taking an NAD booster [44].

### Kidney Concerns — NMN

A mouse study raises a concern that NMN may worsen kidney inflammation in old age [49]:

**Mouse study:** In aged (26-month-old) male mice, 8 weeks of NMN raised kidney markers of inflammation (IL-1beta and Ccl2) and tubule injury (Kim-1), and raised blood levels of uremic toxins. Young mice did not show this susceptibility (Saleh et al., *bioRxiv*, 2024, preprint) [49].

This is early animal evidence that has not been peer reviewed. The authors call for kidney-safety checks in older people [49]; if you are older or have kidney disease, ask your doctor first.

## 6. Drug Interactions

### General Precautions

- **Anticonvulsant drugs:** Nicotinamide (a metabolite of all NAD precursors) slowed the breakdown of primidone and raised carbamazepine levels in a small study of three patients, so check with your doctor if you take an anticonvulsant (Bourgeois et al., *Neurology*, 1982) [68].
- **Glucose-lowering medications:** High doses of nicotinamide may cause minor insulin resistance, which could matter if you take diabetes medicines [67].
- **Immunosuppressants and chemotherapy:** Given the theoretical concern about NAD+ promoting cancer cell growth, individuals on cancer treatment should consult their oncologist before taking NAD-boosting supplements [44].
- **Levodopa (Parkinson's):** In one small study, intravenous NADH raised levodopa levels in the blood; coordination with a neurologist is advisable [21].

### Consultation Recommended

Talk with your doctor before starting an NAD booster, especially if you:
- Have cancer or are being treated for it
- Have kidney disease, or are older and taking NMN [49]
- Take anticonvulsant or glucose-lowering drugs
- Are pregnant or breastfeeding [65]

## 7. Dietary Sources

### NAD+ Precursors from Food

The body makes NAD+ from dietary tryptophan and vitamin B3, and its baseline needs can be met from tryptophan or from less than 20 mg of daily niacin [3].

### NMN in Foods

Reliable figures for NMN in foods are not available.

### Nicotinamide Riboside in Foods

NR is found naturally in cow's milk (approximately 3.9 micromol/L) and trace amounts in other foods, but like NMN, dietary amounts are too low to achieve the doses used in clinical trials [7].
