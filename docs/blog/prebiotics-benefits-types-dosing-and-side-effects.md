## Current evidence position

Brad has not made a video on this topic; this is a summary of the evidence, not his view. Prebiotics are substrates that the body's microbes use selectively to give a health benefit, and trials of short-chain FOS, galactooligosaccharides (GOS) and oligofructose-enriched inulin raised Bifidobacterium in stool [2][8][14][30]. For constipation, 12 g a day of chicory inulin modestly raised stool frequency in small trials, and a 2026 meta-analysis of 30 trials in IBS found that fiber supplements, psyllium in particular, improved response (risk ratio 1.21, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.41) [11][24][48]. Single trials reported benefits for mood, migraine and knee osteoarthritis pain, while a 6-month trial found no significant effect on blood lipids in healthy adults and a 24-week trial found no change in liver fat in fatty liver disease [12][13][27][28][35]. Gas and bloating are the main side effects, and in mice inulin-enriched diets led to liver cancer, mainly in animals born with a vessel that bypasses the liver; how often such shunts occur in people has yet to be studied [26][44][45][46].

## Key safety points

- Psyllium (ispaghula) husk has been reported to interact with lithium [49]. If you take lithium or other prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.
- If you have IBS, fructans such as inulin and FOS may bring on symptoms. In a trial in people with IBS and fructose malabsorption, 77% given fructans said their symptoms were not adequately controlled, against 14% given glucose, and symptoms rose with the dose [47].
- In mice, inulin-enriched diets caused liver cancer, mainly in animals born with a portosystemic shunt [44][45]. If you have liver disease, talk to your doctor before taking concentrated fermentable fiber.
- Most people taking in excess of 15 g a day of inulin or oligofructose get gut symptoms [26].
- Gas, bloating and flatulence are the common side effects, and they are usually mild [20][46]. People who weigh more or already eat more fiber tolerate inulin better [40].

## 1. Overview

A 2017 expert panel of the International Scientific Association for Probiotics and Prebiotics defined a prebiotic as a substrate that host microorganisms use selectively, conferring a health benefit [2]. All known prebiotics are carbohydrates, mostly oligosaccharides, that resist digestion in the small intestine and are fermented in the colon by its bacteria; inulin and oligofructose meet every part of the definition, including stimulating Bifidobacterium [3].

Gut bacteria turn fiber into short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs). These act on G-protein-coupled receptors, block histone deacetylases and serve as fuel, so they may affect many body processes [4]. One SCFA, butyrate, helps regulate fluid transport across the gut lining, damps mucosal inflammation and reinforces the epithelial barrier, and a growing number of studies point to a role in preventing colorectal cancer [5].

Natural intake is low. A US survey of more than 15,000 people estimated that American diets provided on average 2.6 g of inulin and 2.5 g of oligofructose a day, with wheat supplying about 70% and onions about 25% [6].

In a 2004 dose-response trial in healthy volunteers, 10 g a day of short-chain FOS, soybean oligosaccharides, GOS or type III resistant starch raised fecal bifidobacteria within 7 days, while long-chain inulin did not [8]. In its second phase, doses of 2.5, 5.0, 7.5 or 10 g a day did not differ significantly, and people with low starting counts responded most [8].

In the US, inulin, FOS and other oligosaccharides count as fiber on food labels because of their effects in the body, even though the standard total dietary fiber test does not measure them [3].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

Prebiotic fibers differ in chemistry, fermentation and the evidence behind them.

### Inulin and FOS

Inulin is a mix of fructan chains of different lengths, and oligofructose (FOS) is the short-chain form. Both occur naturally in foods such as wheat and onions [6]. In a 2004 trial, 10 g a day of short-chain FOS raised fecal bifidobacteria within 7 days, but long-chain inulin did not [8]. Oligofructose-enriched inulin, a blend of the two, is the product used in trials in children with overweight and in adults with fatty liver disease [28][30].

Inulin and FOS dissolve in liquids without forming a gel.

### Galactooligosaccharides (GOS)

GOS is made from lactose. One form, Bimuno-GOS (B-GOS), has been used in several trials: 5.5 g a day raised bifidobacteria in older adults [14]. GOS dissolves without gelling.

### Partially Hydrolyzed Guar Gum (PHGG)

PHGG is guar gum that has been partly broken down, so it does not thicken liquids the way guar gum does. A 2019 review concluded that guar fiber helps normalize both constipation and diarrhea and eases IBS symptoms, and it suggested 5 to 10 g a day [17].

### Acacia Fiber

Acacia fiber (gum arabic) is a soluble fiber with prebiotic effects [3]. In a 4-week trial in 180 adults with IBS-C, 10 g a day raised stool frequency compared with placebo [18].

### Glucomannan

Glucomannan is a soluble fiber from konjac root that forms a thick gel with water. In an 8-week trial in elite male taekwondo athletes with functional constipation, konjac glucomannan improved constipation symptoms and bowel movement frequency compared with placebo [19].

### Soluble Corn Fiber and Wheat Dextrin

Soluble corn fiber comes from corn starch. In a crossover trial in healthy men, 21 g a day in a snack bar raised Bifidobacterium and increased flatulence compared with no added fiber, though discomfort scores stayed low [20]. Wheat dextrin is made from wheat starch and also has prebiotic effects [3].

### Psyllium Husk

Psyllium husk comes from the seeds of Plantago ovata and forms a gel in water; it also has prebiotic effects [3]. The 2023 AGA-ACG guideline on chronic idiopathic constipation made a conditional recommendation for fiber [22]. A 2026 meta-analysis of 30 trials in 1,904 people with IBS found that fiber supplements improved response (risk ratio 1.21, 95% CI 1.03 to 1.41), with a benefit for psyllium specifically (risk ratio 1.53, 95% CI 1.06 to 2.19) [48].

### Key Principles for Form Selection

**For general gut health and bifidogenic effects:** Short-chain FOS, GOS and oligofructose-enriched inulin have the most trial evidence for raising Bifidobacterium [8][14][30].

**For constipation:** Fiber has a conditional guideline recommendation for chronic idiopathic constipation [22]. Chicory inulin at 12 g a day and konjac glucomannan modestly helped in small trials [19][24].

**For IBS:** Psyllium showed the clearest benefit in a 2026 meta-analysis, and β-galacto-oligosaccharides improved overall symptoms [48]. Fructans can trigger IBS symptoms [47].

**For mood and anxiety:** Oligofructose at 8 g a day improved mood scores in a 5-week trial, and a high-prebiotic diet reduced mood disturbance in an 8-week trial [13][25]. B-GOS lowered the waking cortisol response in a 3-week trial [16].

**For immune support in older adults:** B-GOS at 5.5 g a day for 10 weeks raised natural killer cell activity and IL-10 in adults aged 65 to 80 [14].

**For minimal GI side effects:** People who weighed more or ate more fiber tolerated 18 g a day of inulin better in a 2024 trial [40].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Cholesterol

Results for inulin and oligofructose are mixed. A 2002 review of nine human studies found that four showed modest drops in total cholesterol and LDL, three showed falls in triglycerides and three found no effect; doses were limited because most people get gut symptoms in excess of 15 g a day [26].

In an 8-week trial in women with type 2 diabetes, 10 g a day of inulin lowered total cholesterol by 12.90% and LDL cholesterol by 35.30% [9]. A longer trial did not confirm a benefit: in 17 healthy adults, 10 g a day of an inulin and oligofructose mix for 6 months had no significant effect on blood lipids [27].

In the BE GONE trial in 55 adults with obesity and a history of colorectal neoplasia, adding a daily cup of beans for 8 weeks increased gut microbial diversity and Bifidobacterium but did not change circulating lipoproteins [29].

**Synthesis:** Short-term studies of inulin and oligofructose give mixed lipid results, and a 6-month trial in healthy adults found no significant effect [9][26][27][29].

### Weight Loss and Appetite

Trials of inulin for weight and appetite have mixed results.

**Positive evidence in prediabetic adults:** In adults with prediabetes, both the inulin and cellulose groups lost about 5% of body weight during 9 weeks of dietitian support, but during the next 9 weeks the inulin group lost more (-2.3 ± 0.5% versus -0.6 ± 0.4%) [10]. At 18 weeks the inulin group had less fat in the liver and soleus muscle and ate less at a test meal; insulin did not change [10].

**Modest effects in overweight children:** In children aged 7 to 12 years with overweight or obesity (22 on the prebiotic, 20 on placebo), 8 g a day of oligofructose-enriched inulin for 16 weeks reduced body weight z-score, percent body fat and trunk fat compared with placebo [30]. In the same trial, children on the prebiotic felt fuller, and 11- and 12-year-olds ate less at a breakfast buffet; younger children did not [31].

**No effect on appetite or satiety hormones:** In a crossover trial in healthy adults, a test meal with 20 g of FOS or 20 g of PHGG did not change gut hormones, hunger, satiety or desire to eat compared with maltodextrin [32].

**Fatty liver disease:** In adults with MASLD, 16 g a day of oligofructose-enriched inulin for 24 weeks did not change liver fat, though trunk fat fell more and hunger ratings were lower than with placebo (see Fatty Liver section below) [28].

**Synthesis:** Inulin may help weight loss in some groups, such as adults with prediabetes and children with overweight, but a single 20 g dose did not change appetite in healthy adults [10][28][30][31][32].

### Blood Sugar Control

Evidence that inulin may improve blood sugar comes from small trials.

**Type 2 diabetes:** In 49 women with type 2 diabetes, 10 g a day of inulin for 2 months lowered fasting glucose by 8.47% and HbA1c by 10.43% compared with maltodextrin; fasting insulin and insulin resistance did not change significantly [33].

**Insulin resistance in overweight adults:** In a crossover trial in adults with overweight or obesity, 20 g a day of inulin or of an inulin-propionate ester for 42 days improved insulin resistance compared with cellulose [34]. Only the propionate ester lowered the inflammatory marker interleukin-8 [34].

**Synthesis:** Small trials suggest 10 to 20 g a day of inulin may improve some blood sugar measures in women with type 2 diabetes and insulin resistance in adults with overweight [33][34].

### Fatty Liver Disease (MASLD)

A double-blind trial randomized adults with metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD, formerly called NAFLD) to 16 g a day of oligofructose-enriched inulin (22 people) or a maltodextrin placebo (20 people) for 24 weeks, alongside weight-loss counseling from a dietitian [28].

Liver fat did not change more with the prebiotic than with placebo. The prebiotic group lost more trunk fat, reported less hunger and desire to eat, and had more Bifidobacterium in stool [28].

So in this trial, inulin added nothing to weight-loss counseling for liver fat.

### Constipation and Digestive Regularity

Fiber has been tested for several kinds of constipation. The 2023 AGA-ACG guideline on chronic idiopathic constipation made conditional recommendations for fiber, lactulose, senna, magnesium oxide and lubiprostone, and strong recommendations for polyethylene glycol, sodium picosulfate and three prescription drugs [22].

**Inulin for chronic constipation:** In a crossover trial in healthy adults with constipation, Orafti inulin taken as 3 doses of 4 g a day for 4 weeks raised stool frequency to a median of 4.0 stools a week, against 3.0 on placebo, and softened stools [11].

**Inulin for functional constipation:** In a crossover trial in 39 adults who had functional constipation, 12 g a day of chicory inulin improved stool frequency, abdominal symptoms and quality of life more than placebo [24]. Half of those who took inulin first improved most, so the authors also analyzed the first period alone, and it still favored inulin [24].

**PHGG for constipation and diarrhea:** A 2019 review of clinical studies concluded that guar fiber normalizes both constipation and diarrhea and eases IBS symptoms at 5 to 10 g a day [17].

**Glucomannan for functional constipation:** In elite male taekwondo athletes with functional constipation, 8 weeks of konjac glucomannan improved constipation symptoms, quality of life, bowel movement frequency and bowel function scores compared with placebo [19].

**Acacia fiber for IBS-C:** In a 4-week trial in 180 adults with IBS-C, 10 g a day of acacia fiber raised stool frequency compared with placebo but did not change stool consistency, stool mass or quality of life [18].

**Soluble corn fiber:** In healthy men, 21 g a day of soluble corn fiber in a snack bar for 21 days raised Bifidobacterium and stool weight and increased flatulence compared with no added fiber [20].

**Synthesis:** Fiber has a conditional guideline recommendation for chronic constipation, and psyllium had the clearest benefit in IBS trials [22][48]. Inulin at 12 g a day, acacia fiber and glucomannan modestly improved stool frequency or symptoms in small trials [11][18][19][24]. Higher doses of fermentable fiber bring more gas [20][26].

### Mood, Anxiety, and Depression

Prebiotics may affect mood through the gut-brain axis, and a few trials have tested this.

**FOS and depression/anxiety:** In a 5-week trial in 92 healthy working adults with mild to moderate anxiety and depression, oligofructose 8 g a day, alone or with 2 g a day of 2'-fucosyllactose, raised Bifidobacterium, Bacteroides, Roseburia and Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, and improved several mood measures more than maltodextrin or 2'-fucosyllactose alone [13].

**GOS and cortisol/attention bias:** In healthy volunteers, B-GOS daily for 3 weeks lowered the waking cortisol response in saliva and reduced attention to negative versus positive information compared with placebo; FOS had no effect [16].

**Prebiotic-rich foods and mood:** In the 8-week Gut Feelings trial in 119 adults with moderate psychological distress who ate little prebiotic food, a high-prebiotic diet reduced total mood disturbance compared with placebo (Cohen's d -0.60, 95% CI -1.18 to -0.03), and anxiety, stress and sleep also improved [25]. Adding a probiotic supplement to the diet gave no benefit [25].

**Synthesis:** Oligofructose 8 g a day and a high-prebiotic diet improved mood measures in single trials, and B-GOS lowered the cortisol response, but the trials were small and short [13][16][25].

### Migraine

In a 12-week trial in 80 women aged 20 to 50 years with migraine, 10 g a day of inulin reduced attack frequency (-2.09 versus -0.37), severity (-1.95 versus -0.84) and duration (-6.95 versus -2.05) more than placebo, and improved depression, anxiety and stress scores; quality of life did not differ [12].

The authors called for further studies to confirm these findings [12].

### Osteoarthritis

In a 6-week trial in 117 adults with knee osteoarthritis (mean age 67.5), 20 g a day of inulin reduced pain compared with placebo (mean difference -1.11, 95% CI -2.18 to -0.04) and improved grip strength and pain sensitivity [35]. Inulin also raised blood butyrate and GLP-1 [35].

The trial was not powered to test whether inulin and physiotherapy-supported exercise worked better together [35].

### Immune Function

**GOS in older adults:** In a crossover trial in adults aged 65 to 80, B-GOS 5.5 g a day for 10 weeks raised bifidobacteria and bacteroides, increased IL-10, IL-8, natural killer cell activity and C-reactive protein, and lowered IL-1β [14].

**GOS in elite athletes:** In elite rugby union players, 2.8 g a day of GOS for 168 days shortened upper respiratory symptoms by 2 days and lowered the severity and incidence of gut symptoms compared with placebo [15].

**Prebiotic-rich foods and immunity:** In the BE GONE trial, a daily cup of beans raised Faecalibacterium, Eubacterium and Bifidobacterium and shifted markers of inflammation; the changes in blood metabolites regressed after people returned to their usual diet [29].

**Synthesis:** GOS changed immune markers in older adults and shortened upper respiratory symptoms in athletes, and beans shifted inflammation markers while people ate them [14][15][29].

### Sleep

In rats, a diet with GOS and polydextrose improved sleep during and after sleep disruption (Bowers, *Front Neurosci* 2022) [36]. Human trials are mixed.

In a crossover trial in healthy adults, 237 mL a day of lactose-free milk with 5 g a day each of FOS and GOS for 4 weeks raised Bifidobacterium but did not change sleep quality, mental health or markers of stress and inflammation (Mysonhimer, *J Nutr* 2023) [37].

In healthy older adults, 5 g a day of PHGG for 12 weeks improved sleepiness on rising compared with placebo (Abe, *Nutrients* 2024) [38].

A high-prebiotic diet improved sleep in the Gut Feelings trial [25].

**Synthesis:** Evidence for sleep is thin and mixed: one PHGG trial and one diet trial found some improvement, and a FOS plus GOS trial found none [25][37][38].

### Cognitive Function

In a 12-week trial in healthy older adults, 5 g a day of PHGG improved visual memory scores compared with placebo [38].

**Synthesis:** Evidence for cognition rests on a single PHGG trial that found better visual memory [38].

### Skin Health

In a winter trial in 70 healthy adults (mean age 45.5), 5 g of PHGG reduced water loss through the skin and improved skin elasticity compared with placebo after 6 weeks, and the authors reported restored skin hydration, with no adverse events [39].

**Synthesis:** Evidence for skin rests on a single PHGG trial [39].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### General Guidance

In a 2004 dose-response trial, doses of 2.5, 5.0, 7.5 or 10 g a day of bifidogenic fibers raised bifidobacteria with no significant difference between doses [8]. Trials of specific outcomes have used 8 to 20 g a day of inulin, and most people get gut symptoms in excess of 15 g a day [26][30][35].

### Dosing by Indication

| General gut microbiome support | Short-chain FOS or GOS | 2.5-10 g/day | 7 days | No significant difference between doses [8] |
| Blood sugar/insulin resistance | Inulin | 10-20 g/day | 42 days to 2 months | Small trials [33][34] |
| Constipation (chronic) | Inulin | 12 g/day | 4 weeks | 3 doses of 4 g in one trial [11][24] |
| IBS (diarrhea or constipation) | PHGG | 5-10 g/day | Varies | Normalizes in both directions [17] |
| IBS-C | Acacia fiber | 10 g/day | 4 weeks | Raised stool frequency [18] |
| Mood/depression/anxiety | FOS | 8 g/day | 5 weeks | Single trial [13] |
| Migraine prevention | Inulin | 10 g/day | 12 weeks | Single trial [12] |
| Immune support (elderly) | GOS (Bimuno) | 5.5 g/day | 10 weeks | Crossover trial [14] |
| Immune support (athletes) | GOS | 2.8 g/day | 168 days | Shorter respiratory symptoms [15] |
| Osteoarthritis pain | Inulin | 20 g/day | 6 weeks | Single trial [35] |
| Constipation (guideline) | Fiber | Varies | Ongoing | Conditional recommendation [22] |

### How to Start

Gut side effects are more common at higher doses and in people who eat less fiber [26][40]. A cautious approach:

1. **Start low:** Begin with a small dose
2. **Increase gradually:** Raise the dose slowly over a few weeks
3. **Take with food or water:** Mix prebiotic powder into a beverage, shake, or food
4. **Split doses:** Spread larger doses through the day
5. **Stay hydrated:** Drink enough fluid, especially with gel-forming fibers

### Labeling Considerations

Fiber labels can differ from the amount of prebiotic ingredient, because inulin, FOS and other oligosaccharides count as fiber in the US on their physiological effects rather than by the standard fiber test [3]. Look for the grams of the prebiotic ingredient itself.

## 5. Dietary Sources

Food can supply prebiotic fiber, though American diets average only 2.6 g of inulin and 2.5 g of oligofructose a day [6].

### Food Sources of Inulin and FOS

Wheat and onions supply most of the inulin and oligofructose in American diets, about 70% and 25% [6]. Bananas, whole grain wheat and whole grain corn also have prebiotic effects [3].

### Additional Prebiotic-Rich Foods

**Cereals and grains:** Oats and barley are the main sources of cereal beta-glucans, a fiber that can lower blood glucose after meals and improve blood cholesterol (Henrion, *Nutrients* 2019) [41]. Whole grains of cereals and pseudo-cereals hold fibers such as arabinoxylan, beta-glucan and fructan (Prasadi, *Nutrients* 2020) [42].

**Legumes:** Lentils, common beans and chickpeas bought in US markets held 12 to 15 g of prebiotic carbohydrates per 100 g, including sugar alcohols, raffinose family oligosaccharides, fructooligosaccharides and resistant starch (Siva, *Front Nutr* 2019) [43].

**Unripe (green) banana:** Bananas have prebiotic effects [3].

### Practical Notes on Dietary Prebiotics

- **Wheat (about 70%) and onions (about 25%) supply most of the prebiotic fiber Americans eat** [6].
- **A food-first approach is possible.** Garlic, onions, leeks, asparagus, whole grains, legumes and bananas all supply prebiotic fiber, but average intake is far below the 8 to 20 g a day used in many trials [6][30][35].

## 6. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

The main side effects of prebiotic fiber are **gas, bloating and flatulence**, as colon bacteria ferment the fiber [26][46]. How much depends on:

- **Dose:** Most people taking in excess of 15 g a day of inulin or oligofructose get gut symptoms [26]
- **Baseline fiber intake:** People who habitually eat more fiber tolerate inulin better [40]
- **Body weight:** People who weigh more tolerate inulin better (Letourneau, *Int J Food Sci Nutr* 2024) [40]

In clinical trials:
- 8 g/day of short-chain FOS for 4 weeks: excess flatus and bloating were more frequent, but very mild [46]
- 18 g/day of inulin: tolerance varied with body weight and habitual fiber intake [40]
- 21 g/day of soluble corn fiber: more flatulence than no added fiber, with low discomfort scores [20]
- 5 g of PHGG in a skin trial: no adverse events reported [39]

### IBS and Low-FODMAP Diets

Fructans are among the poorly absorbed short-chain carbohydrates (FODMAPs) that can trigger IBS symptoms. In a rechallenge trial in 25 people with IBS and fructose malabsorption, 77% given fructans said their symptoms were not adequately controlled, against 14% given glucose, and symptoms rose with the dose [47]. Fiber can still help some people with IBS: β-galacto-oligosaccharides improved overall symptoms in a 2026 meta-analysis, and guar fiber eased IBS symptoms in studies reviewed in 2019 [17][48].

### Liver Cancer Concern (Animal Data)

In mice, adding the soluble fiber inulin to a defined diet caused liver cancer (hepatocellular carcinoma) that depended on gut bacteria; it did not occur in germ-free or antibiotic-treated mice (Singh, *Cell* 2018) [44]. A 2022 follow-up found that cancer-prone mice had a congenital portosystemic shunt, a vessel that lets blood bypass the liver, which raised bile acids in the blood; about 10% of mice from various sources had one (Yeoh, *Gastroenterology* 2022) [45].

The 2018 authors advised great caution about enriching foods with fermentable fiber [44]. The 2022 authors wrote that how often silent shunts occur in people awaits investigation, and that measuring blood bile acids may help assess liver cancer risk [45].

### Populations Requiring Caution

- **IBS patients:** Fructans can bring on symptoms [47]
- **Low-FODMAP diet followers:** Inulin and FOS are fructans, which this diet restricts [47]
- **People with chronic liver disease or early-stage liver cancer:** Concern comes from mouse studies of fermentable fiber and bile acids [44][45]
- **Low-weight individuals:** Tolerate inulin less well [40]
- **People on low-fiber diets:** Tolerate inulin less well [40]

## 7. Drug Interactions

Little research has looked at drug interactions with prebiotics.

### Potential Interactions

**Gel-forming fibers (glucomannan, psyllium):** A 1990 case report described an interaction between lithium and ispaghula (psyllium) husk [49]. Gel-forming fibers may slow the absorption of medicines taken at the same time, so taking medicines a few hours apart from them may help.

**Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas):** Inulin lowered fasting glucose in small trials [33]. If you take medicines that lower blood sugar, ask your doctor whether you need closer glucose checks.

### General Recommendation

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.
