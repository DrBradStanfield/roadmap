## Current evidence position

An integrative review found 23 clinical trials of acai or the related juçara berry up to April 2020; they suggest acai may improve antioxidant defence and ease metabolic stress and inflammation, but the trials differed widely [8]. In placebo-controlled trials, an acai drink in people with metabolic syndrome and acai pulp added to a low-calorie diet in overweight adults each lowered one or two markers of oxidative stress or inflammation, but blood fats and blood sugar did not change [13][14]. A cancer trial in 21 men with recurrent prostate cancer did not meet its main goal [19], and the brain and kidney findings come from animal studies [21][34]. ## Key safety points

- Raw acai pulp or juice from the Brazilian Amazon has caused outbreaks of Chagas disease, which can be fatal [39][40][49]. Freezing for a day did not kill the parasite [46], so travellers should avoid fresh acai pulp or juice there [48]. If fever or a racing heart follows, see a doctor; for chest pain or trouble breathing, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
- Some products sold as acai hold little or none, or carry unlisted ingredients [9]. One was linked to muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) [50]: for severe muscle pain or weakness with dark urine, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
- In rats, acai lowered blood levels of atorvastatin and raised those of alogliptin and empagliflozin [44], and acai extracts inhibited the drug enzyme CYP2C9 in lab tests [45]. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.
- If you are having cancer treatment, tell your oncology team before taking acai [45].
- Acai lowered fasting glucose and insulin in a small uncontrolled study [15]. If you take diabetes medicines, ask your doctor first.
- Short human studies found acai well tolerated [19][52], but they were small.

## 1. Overview

Acai is the small, dark purple fruit of *Euterpe oleracea*, a palm of the Arecaceae family [3]. The palm grows along the Amazon River, its tributaries and estuaries [4], and has been cultivated in tropical Central and South America for thousands of years [3]. Demand for the fruit outside that region has grown quickly [3].

In a freeze-dried pulp and skin powder, monounsaturated fats made up 60.2% of total fatty acids, saturated fats 28.7% and polyunsaturated fats 11.1%; oleic acid (53.9%) and palmitic acid (26.7%) were the two main fatty acids [4]. The same powder held 19 amino acids, plant sterols and minerals [4].

The main plant compounds are anthocyanins and proanthocyanidins, along with other flavonoids [4]. Two anthocyanins, cyanidin 3-glucoside and cyanidin 3-rutinoside, dominate [4]. Schauss and colleagues measured total anthocyanins at 3.1919 mg/g dry weight and total proanthocyanidins at 12.89 mg/g dry weight [4].

Lab and animal studies report antioxidant, anti-inflammatory and many organ-protective effects, and clinical trials suggest acai may protect against metabolic stress [3]. Human trials vary widely, and few report what the acai product contained [8].

This article focuses on human data where they exist.

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Available Forms

Acai is sold in several forms that differ in how much of the fruit they deliver:

| Form | Notes |
|------|------|
| Frozen pulp | Pulp is the form most trials used, at 200 g a day [13][15][16]. |
| Freeze-dried pulp and skin powder | The form Schauss and colleagues analysed in detail [4][5]. |
| Clarified juice | Gave lower blood anthocyanin levels than pulp in a head-to-head study [10]. |
| Capsules and liquids | Over half of 20 products tested held little or no acai, or enough water to dilute it heavily [9]. |
| Seed extract | Tested in a mouse kidney study [37]. |

### Anthocyanin Composition

Cyanidin 3-glucoside and cyanidin 3-rutinoside are the main anthocyanins in acai, and three others occur in smaller amounts [4]. Analysing individual anthocyanins helped show whether a supplement contained acai fruit at all [9].

### Antioxidant Capacity (ORAC)

In lab tests, freeze-dried acai powder had the highest activity of any food reported to date against the peroxyl radical (the ORAC-FL assay) [5]. Its superoxide scavenging capacity was 1614 units/g, the highest of any fruit or vegetable tested at the time [5]. In freshly isolated human white blood cells, acai antioxidants entered the cells and reduced the formation of reactive oxygen species [5].

These are test-tube results. The trials below test what happens in people.

### Pharmacokinetics in Humans

In a four-way crossover trial, 12 healthy volunteers consumed acai pulp, clarified acai juice or control foods at 7 mL/kg of body weight after an overnight fast [10]. Key findings:

- **Peak plasma anthocyanin concentration (Cmax):** 2321 ng/L for pulp and 1138 ng/L for juice
- **Time to peak (tmax):** 2.2 hours for pulp and 2.0 hours for juice
- **Area under the curve (AUClast):** 8568 ng h/L for pulp and 3314 ng h/L for juice
- **Plasma antioxidant capacity:** rose significantly after pulp and after the applesauce control, with individual increases of up to 3-fold for pulp and 2.3-fold for juice
- Antioxidant capacity in urine, reactive oxygen species and plasma uric acid did not change

Larger volumes gave lower relative absorption [10].

### Key Principles for Form Selection

**For anthocyanin delivery:** Pulp gave higher blood anthocyanin levels than clarified juice [10].

**For supplement capsules:** Be cautious. Over half of 20 acai supplements tested held little or no acai fruit or were heavily diluted with water, and two contained unlisted ingredients [9].

**For matching the research:** Most trials used pulp or juice drinks rather than capsules [12][13][14][15][16].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Antioxidant and Oxidative Stress

Human trials report some antioxidant effects, but results differ between markers [8][11].

**Healthy volunteers, single dose (n=12):** In the Mertens-Talcott et al. (2008) crossover trial, plasma antioxidant capacity rose significantly after acai pulp, as did the applesauce control [10]. Urinary antioxidant capacity, reactive oxygen species and uric acid did not change [10].

**Healthy adults, crossover trial (n=30):** In a randomised crossover study, 30 healthy adults drank 200 mL/day of acai or juçara juice for four weeks. Compared with baseline, acai juice increased total antioxidant capacity (66.7%), catalase (275.1%) and glutathione peroxidase (15.3%) and lowered the oxidative stress index (55.7%); the two juices did not differ from each other (de Liz et al., 2020) [12].

**Overweight adults with high blood lipids, RCT (n=69):** In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 200 g of acai pulp or placebo was added to a low-calorie diet for 60 days. Plasma 8-isoprostane, a marker of fat oxidation, fell more with acai than with placebo (Aranha et al., Clinical Nutrition, 2020) [13].

**Metabolic syndrome, RCT (n=37):** In a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 37 people with metabolic syndrome drank 325 mL of an acai beverage (1139 mg/L gallic acid equivalents of total polyphenolics) or placebo twice a day for 12 weeks. Plasma interferon gamma and urinary 8-isoprostane fell compared with placebo. The authors called this a weak indication of benefit from a small pilot study (Kim et al., Food & Function, 2018) [14].

**Systematic review of berry RCTs:** A 2023 review of 28 randomised trials of berries found that of about 56 oxidative stress markers measured, 32% showed significant benefit and 68% showed no significant difference (Stote et al., Antioxidants, 2023) [11].

**Synthesis:** Plasma antioxidant capacity rose after a single dose of acai pulp, as it did after applesauce, and acai lowered 8-isoprostane in two placebo-controlled trials. Many other markers did not change, and the clinical meaning of these blood tests is uncertain [8][11].

### Cardiovascular Health and Lipid Profile

**Overweight adults, uncontrolled pilot (n=10):** In the Udani et al. (2011) open-label pilot, 10 overweight adults (BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² and ≤ 30 kg/m²) ate 100 g of acai pulp twice daily for one month. Compared with baseline, fasting glucose and insulin fell (both p < 0.02), total cholesterol fell (p = 0.03), and LDL cholesterol and the total-to-HDL cholesterol ratio fell with borderline significance (both p = 0.051) [15]. Blood pressure and hs-CRP did not change [15]. With no control group, these changes could reflect chance or other effects.

**Healthy adults, crossover trial (n=30):** In the de Liz et al. (2020) trial, 200 mL/day of acai juice for four weeks raised HDL cholesterol by 7.7% compared with baseline [12]. The comparison juice, juçara, raised it by 11.4%, and the two did not differ [12].

**Overweight adults with high blood lipids, RCT (n=69):** In the Aranha et al. (2020) trial, blood lipids and glucose did not change with acai or placebo [13].

**Metabolic syndrome, RCT (n=37):** In the Kim et al. (2018) trial, markers of lipid and glucose metabolism did not change [14].

**Women, prospective study (n=40):** Forty healthy women aged 24 ± 3 years ate 200 g of acai pulp a day for 4 weeks. Total, LDL and HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, glucose and insulin did not change, but apolipoprotein A-I and the transfer of cholesteryl esters to HDL rose (Pala et al., Clinical Nutrition, 2018) [16].

**Synthesis:** The evidence on blood fats is weak and inconsistent. The two placebo-controlled trials found no change in lipids or glucose [13][14]. HDL cholesterol rose 7.7% from baseline in one crossover trial [12] but did not change in the study of 40 women [16]. The uncontrolled pilot cannot show cause and effect [15].

### Cancer

**Lab study, leukaemia cells:** Acai polyphenol fractions at 0.17 to 10.7 microM reduced the growth of HL-60 leukaemia cells by 56 to 86%, likely through caspase-3 activation (programmed cell death) [17].

**Animal studies:** A systematic review found 6 studies of acai in rodent models of cancer, including 2 of colon cancer, with acai given by mouth or as 2.5% to 5% of the diet. Acai reduced tumour incidence, cell growth, number and size in every model, and no genotoxic effects were seen (Alessandra-Perini et al., PLoS ONE, 2018) [18].

**COX enzyme inhibition:** In lab assays, freeze-dried acai acted as a potential inhibitor of the COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes [5].

**Phase II clinical trial, prostate cancer (n=21):** In a Simon 2-stage phase II trial, 21 men with biochemically recurrent prostate cancer (a rising PSA of at least 0.2 ng/mL) took an acai juice product twice daily until their PSA progressed (Kessler et al., Integrative Cancer Therapies, 2018) [19]. Key findings:
- 1 of 21 patients had a PSA response
- PSA doubling time lengthened in 71% of patients (95% confidence interval 48% to 89%)
- In a small number of responders, this lasted a long time
- The trial did not meet its primary endpoint of 50% PSA response

**Synthesis:** Acai slows cancer cells in the lab and tumours in rodents [17][18]. The one human trial cited here did not meet its primary endpoint [19]. Human evidence does not support acai for preventing or treating cancer.

### Cognitive Function and Neuroprotection

A 2018 review describes acai's protective effects on the brain in animals [51].

**Review of the evidence:** A 2023 scoping review found that acai and related *Euterpe* fruits protect nerve cells in experimental models through effects on inflammation, oxidative stress and the blood-brain barrier [43].

**Animal studies, vascular dementia:** In a mouse model of vascular dementia, acai reduced behaviour changes and the death of nerve cells in the CA1 and CA3 regions of the hippocampus, probably through the Nrf-2 and Beclin-1 pathways (Impellizzeri et al., Cells, 2022) [21].

**Synthesis:** Animal evidence suggests acai may protect the brain [21][51]. No trial cited here tested memory or brain health in people.

### Exercise Performance and Muscle Recovery

**Elite athletes, RCT (n=14):** Fourteen athletes ran to exhaustion at 90% of maximal oxygen uptake with and without an acai functional beverage that contained 27.6 mg of anthocyanins per dose. The beverage increased time to exhaustion (mean difference 69 s, 95% confidence interval -296 s to 159 s, p = 0.045), lowered perceived exertion and reduced exercise-induced metabolic stress (Carvalho-Peixoto et al., Applied Physiology, Nutrition, and Metabolism, 2015) [22]. The reported confidence interval does not fit the p-value.

**Junior hurdlers, pilot study (n=7):** Seven junior hurdlers (17.5 ± 1.2 years) drank 100 mL a day of an acai-based juice blend for six weeks during a training camp. Plasma antioxidant capacity rose, exercise-induced muscle damage fell and the lipid profile improved, but 300-m sprint times did not change (Sadowska-Krępa et al., Biology of Sport, 2015) [23].

**Young men, jump protocol (n=12):** Twelve men took 40 g/day of dehydrated acai (*Euterpe precatoria*, a related species) or placebo for 7 days around a jumping protocol that damages muscle. Acai raised antioxidant capacity (TEAC) by 11% at 24 hours and gave faster recovery of knee-flexor strength at 24 and 72 hours; fatigue did not differ (Dos Reis et al., Research in Sports Medicine, 2024) [24]. The abstract does not report soreness or muscle thickness.

**Synthesis:** Three small trials suggest acai may reduce exercise stress markers and help some measures of recovery [22][23][24]. Sprint performance did not improve [23], and the endurance result's reported confidence interval does not fit its p-value [22].

### Weight Loss and Obesity

Acai is widely sold for weight loss, but no trial cited here shows that it causes weight loss.

**Women, prospective study (n=40):** 200 g of acai pulp a day for 4 weeks did not change body measurements [16].

**Overweight adults with high blood lipids, RCT (n=69):** Adding acai pulp to a low-calorie diet for 60 days lowered an oxidative stress marker, but blood lipids and glucose did not change [13].

**Drosophila (fruit fly) model:** Acai added at 2% of the food lengthened the lives of female flies on a high-fat diet. It raised the activity of a heat-shock-related gene (l(2)efl) and two detoxification genes (GstD1 and MtnA) and lowered Pepck, a gene that drives glucose production (Sun et al., Experimental Gerontology, 2010) [27]. Fly data do not show weight loss in people.

**Mislabelled weight-loss products:** A 22-year-old man developed rhabdomyolysis about 2 weeks after starting a weight-loss supplement sold as acai; analysis found no acai in it [50].

**Regulator action:** In April 2011 the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) asked courts to halt 10 operations that used fake news websites to sell acai weight-loss products. It charged that the sites falsely claimed acai supplements "will cause rapid and substantial weight loss", and that the reporters pictured, typically said to have lost 25 pounds in four weeks, were fictional [54]. In January 2012 the acai marketer Central Coast Nutraceuticals agreed to pay $1.5 million to settle FTC charges that it falsely claimed its acai pill would cause rapid and substantial weight loss and made unauthorised charges to bank accounts after "free" trials; an $80 million judgment was to be suspended once the FTC received about $1.5 million in assets [55].

**Synthesis:** The human data on body size come from one 4-week study in 40 women, which found no change [16].

### Skin Health

The skin evidence comes from cell and animal wound models [28].

**Lab and animal studies, wound healing:** An acai berry water extract was not toxic to skin cells, increased the migration of fibroblasts, raised fibronectin and lowered MMP-1 gene activity. It also sped wound healing in animal models (Kang et al., Toxicological Research, 2017) [28].

**Synthesis:** Lab and animal data suggest acai extracts may help wounds heal [28]. No human trial cited here tested acai on skin.

### Gut Health and Prebiotic Effects

**Polyphenols after digestion:** In a simulated digestion study, 49.8% of acai pulp's polyphenols remained available. In a gut model seeded with human stool bacteria, digested pulp lowered *Bacteroides-Prevotella* and *Clostridium histolyticum* counts and raised short-chain fatty acids. The authors said human studies are needed (Alqurashi et al., Food Chemistry, 2017) [29].

**Synbiotic juice:** An acai juice fermented with *Bifidobacterium breve* and fibres kept its bacteria alive in refrigerated storage. In a lab model of the human colon, it raised bacterial diversity and short-chain fatty acids (Reges et al., Foods, 2024) [31].

**Synthesis:** Lab models suggest acai may act as a prebiotic, but no human trial cited here has measured gut bacteria or digestive symptoms [29][31].

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects

**COX enzyme inhibition:** Freeze-dried acai was a potential inhibitor of COX-1 and COX-2 in lab assays [5].

**Metabolic syndrome, RCT (n=37):** In the Kim et al. (2018) trial, 12 weeks of the acai beverage lowered plasma interferon gamma compared with placebo [14].

**Overweight adults, RCT (n=69):** In the Aranha et al. (2020) trial, IL-6 fell in the acai group and interferon gamma fell in both groups, but the groups did not differ [13].

**Synthesis:** Acai blocks COX enzymes in the lab and lowered some inflammatory markers in small trials [5][13][14]. hs-CRP did not change in the pilot study [15].

### Aging and Longevity

**Drosophila lifespan:** Acai at 2% of the food lengthened the lives of female flies on a high-fat diet and of flies under oxidative stress from low sod1 (Sun et al., Experimental Gerontology, 2010) [27].

**sod1-knockdown flies:** A supplement containing freeze-dried acai pulp lengthened life in flies with low sod1, even when started in late young adulthood, and reduced a marker of fat oxidation (Laslo et al., Age, 2013) [32].

**Red blood cell aging:** In a lab model of aging in human red blood cells, acai extract eased the loss of cell flexibility and partly reversed protein changes (Spinelli et al., Antioxidants, 2023) [33].

**Synthesis:** Acai's effects on aging have been studied only in flies and in cells [27][32][33].

### Kidney Protection

The kidney evidence comes from animal studies [34][36][37].

**Acute kidney failure, rats:** Acai berry extract at 100 and 200 mg/kg/day improved kidney function tests and renal oxidative stress markers in rats with glycerol-induced acute kidney failure [34].

**Kidney ischaemia-reperfusion, rats:** Acai extract given for 15 consecutive days before the injury lowered blood urea nitrogen, creatinine and markers of oxidative stress and inflammation, in a dose-dependent way [36].

**Kidney fibrosis, mice:** A polyphenol-rich acai seed extract reduced kidney damage and scarring in mice with kidney failure (Monteiro et al., Scientific Reports, 2022) [37].

**Synthesis:** Animal studies suggest acai may protect the kidney, but these are animal results only [34][36][37].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### No Established Recommendations

The doses below come from trials. They are the doses studied, not proven ones.

### Doses Used in Clinical Trials

The following doses have been used in published human clinical trials:

| Study | Form | Daily Dose | Duration | Outcome |
|-------|------|:-:|:-:|------|
| Mertens-Talcott et al. 2008 [10] | Pulp and clarified juice | 7 mL/kg body weight (single dose) | Single dose | Plasma antioxidant capacity rose after pulp |
| Udani et al. 2011 [15] | Pulp | 100 g twice daily | 1 month | Lower glucose, insulin and total cholesterol (uncontrolled) |
| Carvalho-Peixoto et al. 2015 [22] | Functional beverage (27.6 mg anthocyanins per dose) | Not reported | Not reported | Longer time to exhaustion in athletes |
| Sadowska-Krępa et al. 2015 [23] | Juice blend | 100 mL once a day | 6 weeks | Higher antioxidant capacity; no change in sprint time |
| Kim et al. 2018 [14] | Beverage (1139 mg/L polyphenolics) | 650 mL (325 mL twice daily) | 12 weeks | Lower interferon gamma and urinary 8-isoprostane in metabolic syndrome |
| Pala et al. 2018 [16] | Pulp | 200 g daily | 4 weeks | Higher apo A-I; no change in cholesterol |
| Aranha et al. 2020 [13] | Pulp with a low-calorie diet | 200 g daily | 60 days | Lower plasma 8-isoprostane in overweight adults |
| de Liz et al. 2020 [12] | Juice | 200 mL daily | 4 weeks | HDL up from baseline; no difference from juçara juice |
| Dos Reis et al. 2024 [24] | Dehydrated acai (*E. precatoria*) | 40 g daily | 7 days | Faster knee-flexor strength recovery |
| Kessler et al. 2018 [19] | Juice product | Twice daily | Until PSA progression | PSA doubling time lengthened in 71%; primary endpoint not met |

### General Guidance

Based on the doses used in clinical trials:

- **Pulp:** 200 g a day, taken once or as 100 g twice a day [13][15][16]
- **Juice:** 200 mL a day [12] to 325 mL twice a day [14]
- **Freeze-dried powder:** No trial-tested dose
- **Capsules:** None of the trials above used capsules, and their acai content varies widely [9]

### Standardization Problem

Acai content varies widely between supplements. In a study of 20 acai products, over half held little or no acai fruit or enough water to dilute it heavily, and two contained unlisted ingredients that changed their chemistry [9]. Testing several markers together, including individual anthocyanins, helped show which products held real acai [9].

### Nutritional Profile (Freeze-Dried Pulp)

Schauss and colleagues published a full nutrient analysis of a freeze-dried pulp and skin powder [4]. The abstract reports its fatty acid profile (above), 19 amino acids making up 7.59% of its weight, and sterols at 0.048% by weight [4].

### Comparison with Other Antioxidant-Rich Berries

The same powder had the highest ORAC-FL value of any food reported to date [5]. These lab scores measure activity in a test tube, not in the body.

## 6. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety

A critical review reported no undesirable effects of acai [3]. In trials, people took 200 g of pulp a day for 60 days [13] or 325 mL of an acai beverage twice a day for 12 weeks [14]. In an MRI study, acai pulp was given to 5 healthy volunteers and 35 patients, and no side effect was recorded [52], and the prostate cancer trial reported acai as well tolerated [19].

### Reported Side Effects

Side effects in trials appear to be few [19][52]. Possible effects include:

- **Gastrointestinal:** Diarrhoea may occur, especially with large amounts of pulp
- **Lower insulin levels:** Acai lowered fasting glucose and insulin in an uncontrolled pilot study, which may matter for people on diabetes medicines [15]

### Allergic Reactions

Acai comes from a palm (the Arecaceae family) [3]. Avoid acai if it has caused you an allergic reaction. If your lips, tongue or throat swell, or you have trouble breathing, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

### Chagas Disease Risk (Raw Juice in Endemic Areas)

Raw acai pulp or juice from the Brazilian Amazon can carry *Trypanosoma cruzi*, the parasite that causes Chagas disease [39][40]:

- In 2006, Pará state reported 178 cases of acute Chagas disease; 11 occurred in Barcarena, where studies linked them to eating acai [39]
- In another outbreak in the Brazilian Amazon, 10 patients fell ill, and the parasite was found in both their blood and the suspected acai juice [40]
- A 2026 review counted 114 outbreaks with 1,293 cases and 77 deaths; acai was the food most often implicated in Brazil (32 of 64 outbreaks) [49]
- In acai pulp, the parasite survived 24 h at room temperature, 144 h at 4°C and 26 h at -20°C; the authors concluded that cooling and freezing are not suitable ways to prevent infection [46]
- Heating acai pulp above 43°C for 20 minutes stopped the parasite causing disease in mice [47]

Travel health advisers counsel travellers to South America to avoid risky drinks and foods, including acai pulp [48]. In the review, most patients had fever, and many had heart problems [49]. If you develop a fever, facial swelling or a racing heart in the weeks after drinking fresh acai in the Amazon, see a doctor and mention the drink. For chest pain or trouble breathing, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

### Adulterated Products

Some acai products do not contain what the label says [9][50]:

- Over half of 20 acai supplements tested held little or no acai or were heavily diluted, and two contained unlisted ingredients [9]
- A weight-loss supplement sold as acai contained no acai; a young man who took it developed rhabdomyolysis, and a link was rated possible [50]
- Any dietary supplement may contain unknown contaminants [50], and a review noted adulteration in a commercial acai product [51]

For severe muscle pain or weakness with dark urine, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

### MRI Interference

In the gut, acai pulp brightened T1-weighted MRI images and darkened T2-weighted images. It contains iron, manganese and copper, and the authors linked the change to these paramagnetic minerals (Córdova-Fraga et al., Magnetic Resonance Imaging, 2004) [41]. Radiologists have used acai pulp, and acai juice in children, as an oral contrast agent for imaging the bile and pancreatic ducts [52][53]. Recent acai could therefore change the look of a gut MRI. If you have an abdominal MRI booked, tell the radiology team about any acai you have had.

### Special Populations

**Pregnancy and lactation:** There are no human studies of acai supplements in pregnancy or breastfeeding, so avoid concentrated supplements.

**Diabetes and blood sugar medications:** Acai lowered fasting glucose and insulin in an uncontrolled pilot study [15], though placebo-controlled trials found no change in blood sugar [13][14]. In rats, acai raised blood levels of the diabetes drugs alogliptin and empagliflozin [44]. If you take diabetes medicines, ask your doctor first and watch your blood sugar closely if you take large amounts of acai.

**Surgery:** Acai may affect blood sugar [15] and inhibited COX enzymes in lab tests [5]. Ask your surgeon whether to stop acai supplements before planned surgery.

## 7. Drug Interactions

The interaction evidence comes only from rats and lab enzyme tests [44][45].

### Known and Theoretical Interactions

| Drug/Drug Class | Interaction Type | Evidence Level | Clinical Notes |
|----------------|:-:|:-:|------|
| Antiplatelets (aspirin, clopidogrel) | Theoretical additive effect | Low (lab COX inhibition only) | Acai inhibited COX-1 and COX-2 in lab assays [5], the enzymes aspirin blocks. Check with your doctor before using concentrated acai. |
| Diabetes medications (insulin, metformin, sulfonylureas) | Possible additive effect on blood sugar | Low (one uncontrolled pilot) | Acai lowered fasting glucose and insulin in overweight adults [15]. Monitor blood sugar. |
| Atorvastatin | Lower blood levels | Low (rats only) | In rats, acai lowered atorvastatin's Cmax from 41.78 to 34.65 ng/ml [44]. Clinical relevance unknown. |
| Alogliptin (DPP-4 inhibitor) | Higher blood levels | Low (rats only) | In rats, acai raised alogliptin's Cmax from 43.43 to 68.71 ng/ml [44]. |
| Empagliflozin (SGLT2 inhibitor) | Higher blood levels | Low (rats only) | In rats, acai raised empagliflozin's Cmax from 173.99 to 250.1 ng/ml [44]. |
| Drugs broken down by CYP2C9 (such as warfarin) | Possible higher drug levels | Very low (lab only) | Some acai extracts inhibited CYP2C9 in lab tests; the authors flagged chemotherapy drugs [45]. Warfarin is cleared partly by CYP2C9, so its levels could rise in theory. If you are having cancer treatment, tell your oncology team before taking acai [45]. |

### Important Caveats

- The rat study's authors called for further studies to confirm the clinical significance of these changes [44]
- The rat doses may not match what people take
- Some products sold as acai contain unlisted ingredients with their own effects [9][50]
- If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is acai a "superfood"?**
Acai had the highest antioxidant score of any food in one set of lab tests [5] and holds mostly monounsaturated fat and many polyphenols [4]. "Superfood" has no scientific definition, and human trials show mixed effects on blood markers [8][11].

**Does acai help with weight loss?**
No human trial cited here shows that acai causes weight loss [13][16].

**How much acai should I take?**
Trials used 200 g of pulp a day, or juice drinks from 200 mL a day to 325 mL twice a day [12][13][14][15][16]. Supplement content varies widely between products [9].

**Is acai juice as good as acai pulp?**
Pulp gave higher blood anthocyanin levels: a peak of 2321 ng/L for pulp against 1138 ng/L for clarified juice [10].

**Can acai prevent cancer?**
Human evidence does not support it. Acai slows cancer cells in the lab and tumours in rodents [17][18], but the one human trial cited here (prostate cancer, phase II, n=21) did not meet its primary endpoint [19].

**Are acai supplements reliable?**
Many are not. Over half of 20 acai supplements tested held little acai or were heavily diluted, and two contained unlisted ingredients [9].

**Can I eat acai if I'm on blood thinners?**
Acai inhibited COX-1 and COX-2 in lab assays, the same enzymes aspirin blocks [5]. Some acai extracts also inhibited CYP2C9, an enzyme that helps clear warfarin, in lab tests [45]. No human interaction study exists, so check with your doctor before using concentrated acai supplements.

**Is acai safe during pregnancy?**
There are no human studies of acai supplements in pregnancy or breastfeeding, so avoid concentrated supplements.
