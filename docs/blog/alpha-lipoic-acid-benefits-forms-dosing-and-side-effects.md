## Current view (reviewed 23 October 2026)

Brad thinks alpha-lipoic acid offers real benefits for people with specific conditions such as diabetes, but for most healthy people the risks of taking it may outweigh the benefits ([Brad, 14 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/alpha-lipoic-acid-antioxidant)). His key study is a 2006 trial in 181 people with diabetic nerve damage: after five weeks, 600 mg a day reduced stabbing pain, burning and numbness, while 1,200 mg and 1,800 mg added no benefit but raised side effects such as nausea and dizziness ([Brad, 14 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/alpha-lipoic-acid-antioxidant)). He sees 600 mg a day as the optimal dose for diabetic neuropathy, and if you do take it he advises a product with the R-isomer rather than a mix of both isomers ([Brad, 14 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/alpha-lipoic-acid-antioxidant)). Its effect on blood sugar is small, so he does not see it as a primary treatment for blood sugar control ([Brad, 14 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/alpha-lipoic-acid-antioxidant)). He suggests people who exercise regularly think twice, because antioxidants may block the signals that make exercise work, and he notes that a combination of antioxidants including alpha-lipoic acid sped up cognitive decline in people with Alzheimer's disease ([Brad, 14 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/alpha-lipoic-acid-antioxidant)). It may raise the risk of low blood sugar with diabetes medicines and has rarely been linked to liver toxicity and insulin autoimmune syndrome, so talk to your doctor before starting, especially if you have a health condition or take medication ([Brad, 14 Sep 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/alpha-lipoic-acid-antioxidant)).

## Key safety points

- It may lower blood sugar. If you take insulin or other diabetes medicines, talk to your doctor first [29][47].
- Rarely, it triggers insulin autoimmune syndrome: low blood sugar episodes began 30 to 120 days into 600 mg a day and eased after stopping [37][42]. If someone is unconscious, having a seizure or cannot swallow safely, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Do not give food or drink by mouth.
- An older woman who took 6 g by mistake needed a ventilator [35]. A teenager who took at least 6 g died [50]. After an overdose, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.
- In one reported case, it caused liver inflammation that cleared on stopping and returned on restarting [51]. If you get itching or nausea, stop it and see your doctor.
- A 24-month trial of 1,200 mg a day found more protein in the urine [19]. One woman on 600 mg a day developed a kidney disease that cleared after stopping [43]. If a urine test shows protein, stop it and see your doctor.
- If you take thyroid medicine, talk to your doctor first: it reduced conversion of T4 to T3 in an experimental study [39].
- If you are having chemotherapy, talk to your oncologist first. It did not prevent nerve damage from platinum drugs in a trial [22].
- If you are pregnant, talk to your doctor first. One non-randomised study found no adverse effects [45].

## 1. Overview

Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA; other names are lipoic acid and thioctic acid) acts as a coenzyme of two mitochondrial enzymes, pyruvate dehydrogenase and alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase [40]. The body makes small amounts, and it is both water- and fat-soluble, so it can work throughout the cell [47].

As an antioxidant it scavenges free radicals, binds metals and restores glutathione levels inside cells [2]. Brad notes that it also helps recycle vitamins C and E once they have neutralised free radicals [47]. Lab and animal work suggests it acts beyond a simple antioxidant: it improves glucose handling, raises eNOS activity and switches on the Nrf2 detoxification pathway [2].

There are two mirror-image forms. The R-isomer is the natural form found in food and made by the body, while the S-isomer is synthetic [47]. Both the racemic mixture (R plus S) and R-lipoic acid are sold as supplements, and the racemic form has been the one used clinically [3].

Its best-defined use is preventing diabetic nerve damage (polyneuropathy) [2]. In Germany it has been an approved treatment for diabetic polyneuropathy for decades [40]. It has also been tested for blood sugar control, weight loss, fatty liver, Alzheimer's disease, multiple sclerosis and several pain conditions, with mixed results.

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Racemic (Mixed) Alpha-Lipoic Acid vs. R-Alpha-Lipoic Acid

Supplements contain either the racemic mixture or R-lipoic acid, and both are widely available [3]. Cell and animal studies suggest the natural R form works better than the racemic mixture, but how R-lipoic acid behaves in the human body has not been fully worked out [3]. Brad says the R-isomer is responsible for most of the antioxidant and metabolic benefits, and he advises choosing it over a mixture [47].

Human trials have used both forms. The 6-month blood sugar trial used the racemic (DL) form [8], and the 24-week weight trial used R-alpha-lipoic acid [12].

### Sodium R-Alpha-Lipoate (Na-RALA)

Pure R-lipoic acid tends to polymerise, so it is unstable, dissolves poorly in water and is poorly absorbed [3]. Converting it to its sodium salt (sodium R-lipoate) and dissolving it in water first was tested in 12 healthy adults given 600 mg [3]. The salt was less prone to polymerise, dissolved completely in water, and gave higher peak blood levels and total exposure than R-lipoic acid or the racemic form [3].

### Controlled-Release Formulations

In 12 healthy people given one 600 mg dose, a controlled-release form reached its peak blood level at 1.25 hours, about 2.5-fold later than a quick-release form [4]. In 21 people with type 2 diabetes who took 900 mg a day and then 1,200 mg a day of the controlled-release form for 12 weeks in total, no severe side effects were noted, and in 15 of them fructosamine fell from 313 to 283 micromol/L [4].

| Controlled-release ALA | Varies | Later peak level than quick-release [4] | Well tolerated for 12 weeks in type 2 diabetes [4]. |

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy is nerve damage that causes pain, burning, numbness and tingling in the feet and legs. Alpha-lipoic acid has been studied more for this than for anything else.

**SYDNEY 2 trial:** 181 people with diabetic nerve damage took alpha-lipoic acid once a day at 600 mg, 1,200 mg or 1,800 mg, or placebo, for 5 weeks [41]. Symptom scores fell by 51%, 48% and 52% with the three doses, against 32% with placebo [41]. Side effects (nausea, vomiting and vertigo) rose with dose, and the authors concluded that 600 mg once daily gives the best balance of benefit and risk [41]. This is the trial Brad relies on [47].

**Meta-analysis:** A 2012 meta-analysis found that 600 mg a day given intravenously for 3 weeks gives a clinically relevant drop in nerve pain [6]. Oral doses above 600 mg a day for 3 to 5 weeks also improved symptom scores, but whether that improvement is clinically relevant is unclear [6].

**Higher oral dose:** In a trial of 236 people with diabetic polyneuropathy, 1,800 mg a day for 12 weeks improved symptoms in 73.27% of people, against 18.27% with placebo [30].

**Comparison with pregabalin:** The PAIN-CARE crossover trial gave 55 people with neuropathic pain alpha-lipoic acid, pregabalin (Lyrica) and both together, each for 6 weeks [7]. From a baseline pain score of 5.32 out of 10, pain fell to 3.96 with alpha-lipoic acid, 3.25 with pregabalin and 3.16 with the combination [7]. Pregabalin worked better than alpha-lipoic acid, adding alpha-lipoic acid to pregabalin gave no extra benefit, and results were similar in diabetic and other neuropathies [7].

### Blood Sugar Control and Insulin Sensitivity

Alpha-lipoic acid may lower blood sugar slightly in type 2 diabetes. Brad sees the effect as small, and he does not regard it as a primary treatment for blood sugar [47].

**Six-month trial:** 38 people with type 2 diabetes took placebo or racemic alpha-lipoic acid at 300, 600, 900 or 1,200 mg a day, for half a year [8]. Fasting blood sugar and HbA1c trended down as the dose rose [8].

**Retrospective data:** During 4 years of observation in 322 people taking 400 to 1,200 mg a day, fasting blood sugar fell only at 800 and 1,200 mg a day [29].

**Mechanism:** In lab and animal work, alpha-lipoic acid improves how cells handle glucose [2].

### Weight Loss

In fat cells grown in the lab, alpha-lipoic acid increased the breakdown of stored fat (lipolysis) [9]. Human trials show small benefits.

**While dieting:** 97 overweight or obese women on a diet with 30% less energy than they used were given placebo, EPA (1.3 g a day), alpha-lipoic acid (0.3 g a day) or both, for 10 weeks [11]. Of these, 77 finished the study. Weight loss was greater in the groups given alpha-lipoic acid [11].

**Without diet changes:** 81 overweight adults with raised triglycerides took R-alpha-lipoic acid (600 mg a day) or placebo for 24 weeks and were told not to change their diet or activity [12]. Triglycerides did not fall, but BMI fell more with R-alpha-lipoic acid (by 0.8 more than placebo) [12]. Women and people with a BMI of 35 or more showed greater weight loss (5.0% and 4.8%) [12].

**Summary:** Brad notes that the weight loss is minimal and unlikely to make a big difference for most people, and that diet and exercise work far better [47].

### Liver Disease (MASLD/NAFLD)

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) is the newer name for non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD). Fat builds up in the liver, often alongside obesity and insulin resistance.

**Primary study:** 50 obese people with NAFLD took two 600 mg capsules of alpha-lipoic acid daily (1,200 mg) or placebo for 12 weeks, alongside 400 mg of vitamin E; 45 finished [13][14]. Liver enzymes, body composition and fat in the liver did not differ between groups [13][14]. With alpha-lipoic acid, adiponectin rose and IL-6 fell [13], and insulin and leptin fell [14].

**Summary:** Alpha-lipoic acid did not improve liver fat or liver enzymes in this trial, though it may improve adiponectin and insulin levels [13][14].

### Alzheimer's Disease and Cognitive Function

**Combination with omega-3 fatty acids:** A 12-month pilot trial randomised 39 adults with Alzheimer's to placebo, omega-3 fatty acids, or omega-3 fatty acids plus alpha-lipoic acid; 34 finished [16]. The combination slowed decline on the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) and in daily activities compared with placebo, and omega-3 fatty acids alone slowed decline in daily activities [16]. The trial had no alpha-lipoic acid-only group.

**Combination with vitamins E and C:** In a 16-week trial in 78 people with mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease, vitamin E plus vitamin C plus alpha-lipoic acid (900 mg a day) led to faster decline on the MMSE, which the authors flagged as a potential safety concern [44]. Brad cites this finding as a reason for caution [47].

**Limitations:** Both trials were small and tested combinations, so neither shows the effect of alpha-lipoic acid alone.

### Multiple Sclerosis

**Animal model evidence:** In mice with an MS-like disease, alpha-lipoic acid suppressed and treated the disease [17].

**Pilot trial:** In a 2-year pilot trial of daily oral 1,200 mg lipoic acid in people with secondary progressive MS (27 on lipoic acid, 24 on placebo), brain shrinkage slowed compared with placebo [18].

**Larger negative trial:** A 24-month trial in people with progressive MS (54 on lipoic acid, 61 on placebo) found that 1,200 mg a day did not slow the decline in walking speed and had no clinical effects different from placebo [19]. People taking alpha-lipoic acid dropped out more often (37% against 17%) and had more protein in the urine (proteinuria) [19].

**Summary:** In progressive MS the larger trial found no clinical benefit, and it caused proteinuria [19].

### Pain Conditions (Non-Diabetic)

**Pain with no known cause:** 210 adults with normal blood sugar and nerve, joint or muscle pain of unknown cause took placebo or alpha-lipoic acid at 400 mg a day or 800 mg a day for 2 months [20]. Pain scores fell with alpha-lipoic acid but not with placebo, results did not differ by type of pain, and no one taking it had low blood sugar or side effects [20].

**Back pain from a pinched nerve root:** An observational study followed 203 people through six weeks of rehabilitation. Half also took alpha-lipoic acid (600 mg) plus gamma-linolenic acid (GLA, 360 mg) each day, and that group improved significantly in pain and quality of life; the other half had rehabilitation alone [21]. This was a combination with no placebo group, so it cannot show the effect of alpha-lipoic acid alone.

### Chemotherapy-Induced Neuropathy

**Negative trial:** In people having cisplatin or oxaliplatin chemotherapy, alpha-lipoic acid at 600 mg three times a day for 24 weeks did not prevent nerve damage compared with placebo [22]. Only 29% of 243 people finished the study [22].

**Professional recommendation:** ASCO's 2020 guideline update found that no agents are recommended for preventing chemotherapy-induced nerve damage [23].

### Hearing Loss

**Age-related hearing loss:** In a 6-month trial in 120 older people with hearing loss (presbyacusis), alpha-lipoic acid plus vitamin C did not change hearing, nor did the other treatments tested [24]. In old rats, 6 weeks of alpha-lipoic acid slowed the progression of age-related hearing loss [25].

**Noise-induced hearing loss:** In a small preliminary study of young adults in three groups of 10, one 600 mg dose an hour before noise gave no protection, while 600 mg taken for 10 days beforehand was linked to protection at one frequency (6 kHz) [26].

### Burning Mouth Syndrome

Burning mouth syndrome causes an unexplained burning feeling in the mouth. In an 8-week placebo-controlled trial in 66 people, alpha-lipoic acid (400 mg), with or without vitamins, worked no better than placebo [27].

### Skin Aging

**Topical application:** In a 12-week trial in 33 women, each woman used a cream with 5% alpha-lipoic acid on one half of her face and the same cream without it on the other [28]. Skin roughness fell by 50.8% on the alpha-lipoic acid side and by 40.7% on the other side [28]. Oral supplements were not tested.

### Other Potential Uses

In cell and animal studies, alpha-lipoic acid improves glucose handling and dampens inflammatory signalling, and as of 2009 trials were testing it for vascular disease, high blood pressure and inflammation [2].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Dosing by Indication

The doses below are the ones the cited trials used.

| Indication | Dose Used | Duration in Studies | Evidence Level |
|------------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Diabetic peripheral neuropathy | 600 mg once daily (higher doses added side effects) | 5 weeks | Symptoms improved [41]; clinical relevance of oral doses unclear [6] |
| Blood sugar control | 300 to 1,200 mg a day (racemic) | 6 months | Fasting blood sugar and HbA1c trended down [8] |
| Weight loss | 300 mg a day while dieting; 600 mg a day of R-ALA | 10 to 24 weeks | Small extra weight or BMI loss [11][12] |
| Pain with no known cause | 400 mg a day or 800 mg a day | 2 months | Pain scores fell [20] |

### Timing and Administration

Trials have given alpha-lipoic acid once a day, as in SYDNEY 2 [41], or as 600 mg thrice daily [30]. In a tolerability study in people at least 65 years old, each dose was taken once daily with a meal [31].

### Important Note on "General Antioxidant" Dosing

The trials that found benefits used doses of hundreds of milligrams a day [8][11][41]. Brad thinks that for most healthy people, the risks of this supplement may outweigh the benefits [47].

### Storage

Pure R-lipoic acid is unstable because it tends to polymerise [3]. Store supplements as the label directs and out of reach of children.

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

**Gastrointestinal effects:** Nausea, vomiting and vertigo became more common as the dose rose in SYDNEY 2 [41]. In a trial of 1,800 mg a day, side effects occurred in 25.4% of people against 11.8% on placebo, mainly a burning feeling from the throat to the stomach (12.7%) [30]. In people at least 65 years old, the 600 mg dose was well tolerated, but some could not tolerate 800 mg or 1,200 mg because of flushing or upper stomach side effects [31]. People taking medicines to protect the stomach had no upper stomach side effects [31]. Heartburn-type symptoms may be more likely at higher doses [30][31][47].

**Other side effects:** During 4 years of observation, people taking 400 to 1,200 mg a day reported nausea, vomiting, dizziness, skin rash, low blood sugar and low blood pressure, with no difference between doses [29]. Brad lists nausea, skin rash and acid reflux as mild reactions [47].

**Skin reactions:** Case reports describe allergic contact dermatitis from creams and other skin products that contain alpha-lipoic acid [32][33][34].

### Serious Adverse Effects

**Overdose toxicity:** An elderly woman who took 6 g by mistake was confused 3 hours later, with nausea, vomiting and sweating, and then developed seizure-like activity [35]. She needed intubation and a ventilator, and she went home on day 3 [35]. A 14-year-old girl who took at least ten 600 mg tablets developed multiorgan failure, including seizures, and died within 24 hours [50]. There is no antidote, so treatment is supportive [35]. If someone has taken a large amount, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department.

**Liver injury:** A 63-year-old man with diabetes developed cholestatic hepatitis 45 days after starting 600 mg a day [51]. His liver tests settled after he stopped, became abnormal again two months after he restarted it, and returned to normal 2 months after he stopped again [51].

**Insulin autoimmune syndrome (IAS / Hirata disease):** Alpha-lipoic acid can trigger IAS, in which the body makes antibodies to its own insulin and blood sugar drops spontaneously [37][42]. In six European patients taking 600 mg a day, low blood sugar episodes began 30 to 120 days after starting, eased after they stopped, and were treated with glucose and prednisone [42]. In EFSA's review of 49 case reports, IAS resolved within weeks to months of stopping [37]. Risk is linked to gene variants [37]: HLA-DRB1*04:06 in East Asian people and HLA-DRB1*04:03 in white people, in whom lipoic acid is the common trigger [42][48]. If someone is unconscious, having a seizure, or cannot swallow safely, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Do not give food or drink by mouth.

**Pregnancy:** A retrospective study of 610 pregnant women who were treated daily with 600 mg for at least 7 weeks reported no adverse effects in mothers or newborns, compared with regional birth registry data [45]. It was not a randomised trial. If you are pregnant, talk to your doctor before taking it.

### Nutrient Interactions

**Thiamin (vitamin B1):** People who may be low in thiamine should talk to their doctor before taking it.

**Biotin (vitamin B7):** In rats given daily injections for 28 days, alpha-lipoic acid lowered the activity of two biotin-dependent liver enzymes by 28% to 36%, and giving biotin with it kept them normal [38].

### Thyroid Effects

In an experimental study, alpha-lipoic acid given with thyroxine (T4) for 9 days cut the T4-driven rise in T3 production by 56% [39].

### Kidney Concerns at High Doses

In the 24-month MS trial of 1,200 mg a day, people taking it had more proteinuria, and laboratory monitoring was increased to every 3 months because of it [19]. The earlier pilot trial had one case of unexpected kidney failure and one of glomerulonephritis among people taking it [18]. A woman who took 600 mg a day for 3 months developed membranous nephropathy, which resolved after she stopped [43].

## 6. Drug Interactions

### Anti-Diabetic Medications

As it may lower blood sugar, Brad warns it may raise the risk of low blood sugar in people taking diabetes medicines [47]. Low blood sugar has been reported in people taking it [29]. Single doses of 600 mg did not interact with glibenclamide or acarbose in healthy volunteers [49]. If you take insulin or other diabetes medicines, talk to your doctor before starting. If someone with diabetes is unconscious, having a seizure or cannot swallow safely, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK). Do not give food or drink by mouth.

### Chemotherapy Agents

Alpha-lipoic acid did not prevent nerve damage from platinum chemotherapy [22], and ASCO recommends no agent for preventing it [23]. If you are having chemotherapy, talk to your oncologist before taking it.

### Thyroid Medications

Alpha-lipoic acid reduced the conversion of T4 to T3 when given with T4 in an experimental study [39]. If you take thyroid medicine such as levothyroxine (T4), talk to your doctor before starting.

## 7. Dietary Sources

The body makes a little alpha-lipoic acid itself, and spinach, broccoli and meats also contain it [47].

### Food Sources of Alpha-Lipoic Acid

Brad names spinach, broccoli and meats as food sources [47].

### Practical Notes

- Brad prefers getting nutrients from whole foods such as spinach, broccoli and meats over relying on supplements [47].
