- About 30 to 50% of people allergic to latex react to some plant foods, avocado among them [48]. Reactions to eating avocado have included anaphylaxis, sometimes in people without latex allergy [47][49]. ## 1. Overview

Avocado oil is made from the flesh of the avocado fruit (*Persea americana* Mill.) rather than from a seed or kernel. It is rich in fat-soluble bioactive compounds, but their amounts depend on the fruit and the extraction method [1][63].

Interest in the oil has grown, but a 2019 review found little known about avocado oil and the health effects of eating it [64].

One tablespoon (approximately 14 g) provides approximately 124 kilocalories and 14 g of total fat, with roughly 10 g as monounsaturated oleic acid [5][6]. It contains no carbohydrates or protein. About 71% of its fatty acids are monounsaturated and 13% polyunsaturated, and 16% of its fatty acids are saturated [9].

A 2013 review proposed that this fat profile helps promote healthy blood lipids and helps the body take up fat-soluble vitamins and phytochemicals from other foods [9].

No large outcome trial has tested avocado oil, and its effects on heart disease have been shown only in laboratory and animal studies [63]. Olive oil, by contrast, was tested in PREDIMED, where a Mediterranean diet with extra-virgin olive oil led to fewer major cardiovascular events than a reduced-fat diet (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.91) [69]. Most human studies on avocado were of the whole fruit, and the fruit also supplies fiber, potassium and magnesium that the oil lacks [9].

Quality is a concern. Avocado oil is a high-value oil and, like olive oil, can be adulterated; high-oleic sunflower and safflower oils are close enough in makeup to serve as adulterants [65].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Grades of Avocado Oil

Avocado oil is sold as unrefined (virgin or extra virgin) and refined oil. Its quality depends on the quality and ripeness of the fruit and on the extraction method, including temperature, solvents and storage [64]. Little is published on how the different types are produced and sold [63].

### Extraction Methods

Oil is extracted by cold pressing (sometimes with enzymes), by centrifugation with water, with solvents, with ultrasound, or with supercritical fluid [63]. The method changes both the yield and the chemical makeup of the oil [1].

### Fatty Acid Profile

About 71% of avocado oil's fatty acids are monounsaturated and 13% polyunsaturated, and 16% of its fatty acids are saturated [9], but composition varies widely between cultivars such as Hass, Reed, Ettinger and Fuerte [70].

In one oil sample analysed by gas chromatography, oleic acid made up 47.20% of the fatty acids, palmitic acid 23.66% and linoleic acid 13.46% [40]. Avocado oil had a higher omega-6 to omega-3 ratio than olive oil (14.05 before heating) [62].

### Bioactive Compounds

Avocado oil is rich in fat-soluble bioactive compounds, but the amounts vary with the fruit and the processing [63]. In one comparison, avocado oil held more total phytosterols than olive oil (339.64 vs 228.27 mg/100 g) but less vitamin E (24.5 vs 35.52 mg/100 g) [62]. Data on carotenoids and chlorophylls in the oil are limited [63].

### Comparison to Olive Oil

| Property | Avocado Oil | Olive Oil |
|----------|:---:|:---:|
| Total phytosterols (mg/100 g, before heating) | 339.64 | 228.27 |
| Vitamin E (mg/100 g) | 24.5 | 35.52 |
| Vitamin E gone during heating at 180°C | At 4 hours | At 5 hours |
| Large outcome trials | None (laboratory and animal studies only) | PREDIMED (Mediterranean diet with extra-virgin olive oil) |

Sources: [62][63][69].

When both oils were heated at 180°C, the stability of avocado oil was similar to that of olive oil [62].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Heart Health and Cholesterol

The heart case for avocado oil rests on its monounsaturated fat and on trials of whole avocado. The oil's own effects on heart disease have been shown only in laboratory and animal studies, not in people [63].

**Evidence from whole avocado studies:** Since direct studies on avocado oil and cardiovascular outcomes are lacking, the best available evidence comes from trials using whole avocado fruit.

A 2016 meta-analysis of ten small randomized trials (229 people) found that with avocado, total cholesterol fell 18.80 mg/dL, LDL cholesterol 16.50 mg/dL and triglycerides 27.20 mg/dL. The authors advised substituting avocado for other fats rather than adding it to the diet (Peou, *J Clin Lipidol*, 2016) [27].

A 2018 meta-analysis of 18 studies (481 people) found that avocado raised HDL cholesterol by 2.84 mg/dL but did not change total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, triglycerides or body weight. No study reported heart attacks, strokes or other clinical events (Mahmassani, *Am J Clin Nutr*, 2018) [60].

The Habitual Diet and Avocado Trial (HAT) randomized 1,008 adults with a large waist to one large avocado a day or their usual diet. After 6 months, visceral fat, liver fat, hsCRP and the components of the metabolic syndrome did not differ between groups. Total and LDL cholesterol fell modestly in the avocado group, a nominally significant result; body weight, BMI and insulin were similar (Lichtenstein, *J Am Heart Assoc*, 2022) [28].

A HAT ancillary study of 134 people found that eating one avocado a day for 6 months left flow-mediated dilation (how well arteries widen) and arterial stiffness no better than the usual diet (Davis, *J Am Heart Assoc*, 2024) [29].

In a 12-week trial of 105 adults with overweight or obesity, a daily meal with one Hass avocado did not improve insulin sensitivity. Women in the avocado group lost more visceral fat than controls; men showed no difference (Khan, *J Nutr*, 2021) [30].

**Synthesis:** Whole avocado may modestly improve blood lipids when it replaces other fats, but the meta-analyses disagree, and recent trials found no change in body fat, insulin resistance or vascular function [27][28][29][30][60]. Whether avocado oil alone, without the fruit's fiber and minerals, does the same has not been tested in people [63].

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects

Avocado oil's effects on diabetes and heart disease have been shown only in laboratory and animal studies [63]. In trials of whole avocado, the inflammation markers hsCRP, interleukin 6 and tumor necrosis factor alpha did not change [28][60].

### Osteoarthritis and Joint Health (Avocado/Soybean Unsaponifiables)

Avocado-soybean unsaponifiables (ASU) are an extract of avocado and soybean oils used for hip and knee osteoarthritis [32]. They are not the same as eating avocado oil.

**Positive evidence (knee osteoarthritis):** A 2019 meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials found that ASU cut knee osteoarthritis pain scores by 17.36 mm on a visual analogue scale and 2.33 points on the Lequesne index, but did not help hip osteoarthritis. Side effects were similar to placebo (Simental-Mendía, *Int J Rheum Dis*, 2019) [32].

**Positive evidence (knee osteoarthritis, single trial):** In a 3-month placebo-controlled trial of people with mild to moderate knee osteoarthritis, the ASU product Arthrocen at 300 mg/day (61 people) improved pain, function and quality-of-life scores compared with placebo (58 people). Blood levels of the inflammatory markers IL-17 and TNF-alpha fell (Goudarzi, *Medicina (Kaunas)*, 2022) [33].

**Mixed evidence (hip osteoarthritis, long-term):** In the 3-year ERADIAS trial of 399 people with hip osteoarthritis, ASU 300 mg/day did not slow average joint space loss (-0.638 mm vs -0.672 mm) and did not improve symptoms. Fewer people on ASU were progressors, with joint space loss of at least 0.5 mm (40% vs 50%) (Maheu, *Ann Rheum Dis*, 2014) [34].

**Mixed evidence (hip osteoarthritis, structural):** In a 2-year trial of 163 people with hip osteoarthritis, ASU did not slow joint space loss overall. In a post hoc analysis of people with joint space at or below the median, loss was smaller with ASU (−0.43 mm with ASU, −0.86 mm with placebo) (Lequesne, *Arthritis Rheum*, 2002) [35].

**Synthesis:** ASU may modestly ease knee osteoarthritis pain, but hip trials found no clear symptom benefit and only hints of slower joint damage [32][34][35]. ASUs are specialized extracts, not the same as consuming avocado oil. Regular avocado oil consumption cannot be expected to provide the same effects as purified ASU supplements.

### Skin Health (Topical Use)

Avocado oil has been investigated for topical skin applications, though the evidence remains preliminary and based on small studies.

**Psoriasis:** In a 12-week trial in 13 people with plaque psoriasis, each person used a vitamin B12 cream containing avocado oil on one side of the body and calcipotriol (a vitamin D3 analog) on the other. Calcipotriol worked faster at first, but after 12 weeks the two did not differ, and the cream was rated better tolerated (Stücker, *Dermatology*, 2001) [39]. It is unclear whether the benefits were driven by the vitamin B-12, the avocado oil, or the combination.

**Wound healing:** In rats, avocado oil alone or as a 50% semisolid formulation sped wound closure and raised collagen density and tensile strength compared with petroleum jelly (de Oliveira, *Evid Based Complement Alternat Med*, 2013) [40]. No human study has confirmed this.

**Cosmetic applications:** The oil is of interest to the cosmetic and pharmaceutical industries [63].

**Synthesis:** While avocado oil has plausible moisturizing properties when applied topically, the clinical evidence is limited to small studies with short follow-up periods.

### Carotenoid Absorption Enhancement

Avocado oil may help the body absorb fat-soluble nutrients from other foods eaten at the same meal.

In a small crossover study (11 people), adding 24 g of avocado oil to a salad raised absorption of alpha-carotene, beta-carotene and lutein, much as 150 g of avocado did. With the avocado, absorption was 7.2, 15.3 and 5.1 times that from the avocado-free salad (Unlu, *J Nutr*, 2005) [61]. In two further studies, eating avocado with a high-beta-carotene tomato sauce or with carrots raised beta-carotene absorption 2.4-fold and 6.6-fold and increased its conversion to vitamin A (Kopec, *J Nutr*, 2014) [26].

The effect is attributed mainly to the fat in avocado [61].

This supports the practical recommendation of using avocado oil in salad dressings or drizzled over vegetables to help absorb carotenoids from the meal.

### Body Composition and Weight

Clinical trials using whole avocado do not support claims that avocado oil promotes weight loss or improves body composition.

In the HAT trial (1,008 adults), visceral fat, body weight and BMI after 6 months of one avocado a day did not differ from the usual-diet group (Lichtenstein, *J Am Heart Assoc*, 2022) [28].

The Khan trial (105 adults, 12 weeks) found more visceral fat loss with avocado in women only, and no change in insulin sensitivity (Khan, *J Nutr*, 2021) [30].

As a calorie-dense fat source (approximately 124 calories per tablespoon), avocado oil should be used as a replacement for other fats rather than added on top of existing dietary fat intake to avoid contributing to caloric excess [5][6].

### Anticancer Potential (Preclinical Only)

Compounds extracted from avocado fruit stop growth and trigger cell death in precancerous and cancer cell lines [66]. These were fruit extracts tested in the laboratory, and avocado oil's effects on cancer have been shown only in laboratory and animal studies, not in people [63].

### Thermal Stability and Cooking Safety

When avocado and olive oils were heated at 180°C, their stability was similar [62]. Vitamin E fell faster in avocado oil, disappearing at 4 hours versus 5 hours for olive oil, while oxidation products (TBARs) were higher in olive oil after 3 hours [62]. A 2019 review concluded that avocado oil keeps a very good nutritional value at low and high temperatures [64].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Dietary Intake Recommendations

There is no established Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) or specific intake guideline for avocado oil. Trials of whole avocado suggest it works best as a swap for other fats rather than an addition [27].

### Practical Dosing Guidelines

**As a culinary oil (general use):** No trial has tested a dose of avocado oil. Each tablespoon provides approximately 124 calories and 14 g of fat [5][6].

**For nutrient absorption enhancement:** The salad study used 24 g of avocado oil with carotenoid-rich vegetables [61].

**As a cooking oil replacement:** Use avocado oil to replace other fats rather than adding it to your current intake [27].

**Avocado and soy unsaponifiables (ASU) for joint health:** Trials used 300 mg/day for 3 months to 3 years [33][34]. Note that ASU supplements are distinct from dietary avocado oil.

**Topical use for skin:** Studies have tested a vitamin B12 cream containing avocado oil for psoriasis and, in rats, pure oil or a 50% semisolid formulation on wounds [39][40].

### Important Considerations

Avocado oil is calorie-dense. Two tablespoons provide approximately 248 calories. When using avocado oil, it should displace an equivalent amount of other dietary fat rather than being consumed in addition to existing fat intake.

There is no evidence that consuming avocado oil in amounts beyond normal culinary use provides additional health benefits.

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Safety

No upper intake limit has been established specifically for avocado oil. As with any fat, use it within your total calorie needs.

### Allergic Reactions

**Latex-fruit syndrome:** Latex allergy can come with avocado allergy, because some of their proteins cross-react. About 30 to 50% of people allergic to latex react to some plant foods, avocado among them (Wagner, *Biochem Soc Trans*, 2002) [48]. In a series of 17 people with avocado allergy, reactions included anaphylaxis in seven and hives or swelling in six, and 10 were also sensitized to latex (Blanco, *Allergy*, 1994) [47]. If you have trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat after eating avocado, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

**Non-latex avocado allergy:** Anaphylaxis to avocado without latex sensitization is rare but has been reported, for example in a 15-year-old boy within half an hour of eating food containing avocado (Abrams, *Allergy Asthma Clin Immunol*, 2011) [49].

**Topical allergy:** A 1987 case report described contact allergy to avocado oil in a sunscreen (de Groot, *Contact Dermatitis*, 1987) [50].

**Refined vs. unrefined and allergenicity:** If you have an avocado allergy, ask your doctor or an allergist before using either refined or unrefined avocado oil.

### Persin Content

Persin, found in all parts of the avocado plant, causes avocado poisoning in some animals, including parrots, ostriches, sheep, goats, dogs and rabbits, and has been linked to heart damage in horses [67][68]. These reports are in animals. Keep avocado away from pet birds and rabbits [67].

### Caloric Density

As a pure fat, avocado oil provides 124 calories per tablespoon [5][6]. Eating it on top of your usual fats adds those calories.

### Genotoxicity Concerns (In Vitro)

In a laboratory study, methanol extracts of avocado fruit and leaves caused chromosome damage in cultured human lymphocytes, rising with concentration; the leaf extract caused more damage than the fruit extract (Kulkarni, *J Environ Sci Health C Environ Carcinog Ecotoxicol Rev*, 2010) [53]. The study tested extracts on cells, not avocado oil eaten by people, so its relevance to diet is unknown.

### Quality and Adulteration Risks

Product quality may matter as much as the oil itself. Avocado oil is a high-value oil and can be adulterated; high-oleic sunflower and safflower oils are close enough in makeup to serve as adulterants [65]. Laboratory tests such as NMR spectroscopy can detect adulteration and oxidation products [65].

## 6. Drug Interactions

### Warfarin (Coumadin)

A 1991 report in *The Lancet* described warfarin antagonism by avocado: avocado appeared to weaken warfarin (Coumadin), a blood thinner (Blickstein, *The Lancet*, 1991) [54].

The report concerned the fruit, and avocado oil itself has not been studied. If you take warfarin, keep your avocado and avocado oil intake steady and tell your doctor before making large changes, since your INR (International Normalized Ratio) may need checking.

### Vitamin K Content

Avocado fruit contains vitamin K1, about 14 micrograms in half an avocado [9]. The amount in the oil is not well documented.

### Fat-Soluble Medication Absorption

As with any dietary fat, taking medicines with a meal containing avocado oil may change how some of them are absorbed. Follow the food instructions for each medicine.

### No Other Known Drug Interactions

Beyond warfarin, no specific drug interactions with avocado oil are well documented. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 7. Dietary Sources

### Choosing and Using Avocado Oil

**How to select quality avocado oil:**

- Quality depends on the quality and ripeness of the fruit, the extraction method, and storage [64].
- All oil goes rancid in time, so buy a bottle you will finish while it is fresh.

**Cooking with avocado oil:**

- Avocado oil keeps a very good nutritional value at low and high temperatures [64], and its stability at 180°C was similar to that of olive oil [62].

**Storing avocado oil:**

- Storage conditions affect quality [64]. Keep the bottle closed and away from heat and light.

### Avocado Oil in Global Production and Environmental Context

Hass is the most common commercial avocado cultivar in the world [9]. Little is published on how much of each type of avocado oil is produced and sold [63].

### Regulatory Standards

A 2019 review found no international regulations for avocado oil quality; some authors applied the olive oil parameters of the Codex Alimentarius or the International Olive Oil Council [64]. Laboratory methods such as NMR spectroscopy can profile fatty acids and detect sterols, oxidation products and adulteration [65].
