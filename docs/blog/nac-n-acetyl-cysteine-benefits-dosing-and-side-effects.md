Brad's plan is to take 1 g of GlyNAC, a combination of glycine and N-acetylcysteine (NAC), from the age of 45 (Brad's current guidance, 19 Feb 2026). He chose that age because glutathione, a powerful antioxidant, declines rapidly from 45 (Brad's current guidance, 31 Jul 2026). A placebo-controlled human study found that GlyNAC corrected glutathione deficiency in older adults and improved hand grip strength (Brad's current guidance, 31 Jul 2026). That study was small, with 36 participants (24 older adults and 12 younger adults), and the younger group gained no benefit ([Brad, 3 Feb 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-most-important-antioxidant-study-i-ve-ever-read)). Collagen supplements already contain a lot of glycine and trimethylglycine (TMG) adds more, so people already taking them are likely to have enough glycine, which leaves NAC as the only missing part of the pair (Brad's current guidance, 31 Jul 2026). ## Key safety points

- Ask your doctor before combining NAC with nitroglycerin. NAC strengthened nitroglycerin's effect on blood pressure, and in an angina trial the pair caused side effects, mainly intolerable headache, in 35% of patients [41][73].
- If you take a blood thinner or have a bleeding disorder, check with your doctor first. High-dose intravenous NAC had anticoagulant and platelet-inhibiting effects during surgery [66].
- If you have asthma, ask your doctor first. Asthma is a risk factor for reactions to intravenous NAC [75], which can include wheeze and bronchospasm [74].
- A suspected paracetamol (acetaminophen) overdose needs emergency care: call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department. Hospital NAC protects the liver best when started within 8 hours, so do not wait or treat it yourself with supplements [77][78].
- In mice, NAC sped up lung cancer growth, and the researchers urged caution for smokers and people with COPD [61][62].
- In oral trials, side effects were generally no more common on NAC than on placebo [20][80].

## 1. Overview

N-acetylcysteine (NAC), also called acetylcysteine, is a modified version of cysteine, an amino acid. Cysteine itself comes from protein foods [3]. The body uses NAC as a source of cysteine to make glutathione (GSH), and NAC also breaks disulfide bonds in proteins, which is how it thins mucus [4]. Glutathione is built from three amino acids: cysteine, glycine and glutamate. In a small study, older adults had lower levels of glycine, cysteine and glutathione in their red blood cells than younger adults [10].

The body does not need NAC from the diet, so it has no recommended intake. It has been used in medicine for decades, as a mucolytic and for paracetamol poisoning, and has been tested in many other conditions, including heavy metal toxicity and psychiatric disorders [4].

NAC is thought to work in three ways: by supplying cysteine for glutathione, by reacting directly with some free radicals, and by reducing disulfide bonds [4]. It also acts on glutamate signalling, which is the rationale for its psychiatric trials [8].

NAC was a prescription drug in the United States before it was sold as a supplement, which led the FDA to question its supplement status. The FDA's 2022 guidance sets out a policy under which NAC supplements can stay on sale [6].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Standard NAC (Free Form)

The trials described here tested plain N-acetylcysteine taken by mouth; the influenza trial, for example, used 600 mg tablets twice daily [23].

### Oral Bioavailability

Oral NAC is poorly absorbed into the bloodstream, which some researchers think may explain why large trials of NAC for oxidative-stress diseases were disappointing [9]. Even so, oral NAC raised blood glutathione in a trial of women with breast pain [47].

### NACET (N-Acetyl Cysteine Ethyl Ester)

NACET is an ethyl ester of NAC designed to cross cell membranes more easily. In rats, oral NACET raised glutathione in most tissues examined, including the brain, while oral NAC did not [9]. Whether NACET has an advantage in people is uncertain.

### GlyNAC (Glycine + NAC)

GlyNAC combines NAC with glycine, the two glutathione building blocks that are low in older adults [10]. The idea is that supplying both may restore glutathione better than NAC alone. The human GlyNAC trials (see Aging and Longevity) were small and came from one research group [7][72], and they did not compare GlyNAC with NAC alone.

### Delivery Forms

NAC is sold as capsules, tablets, effervescent tablets and powder. Oral doses in the trials described here ranged from 200 mg to 6 g a day [51][79].

### Storage and Stability

NAC contains a sulfur (thiol) group [4], so a faint sulfur smell is common. Store it sealed, somewhere cool and dry.

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Acetaminophen Overdose and Liver Protection

NAC is the standard antidote for paracetamol (acetaminophen) poisoning. It is a hospital treatment. Of 100 people treated with intravenous NAC for severe paracetamol poisoning, the 40 treated within eight hours had virtually complete protection against liver damage [78]. In a multicentre study of 2540 patients, an oral regimen of 140 mg per kilogram followed by 70 mg per kilogram every four hours for 17 more doses was protective when started within eight hours, and less effective with longer delays [77].

A suspected paracetamol overdose needs emergency care: call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) or go to the emergency department. Do not wait for symptoms, and do not try to treat an overdose yourself with NAC supplements.

**Important caveat:** NAC is often sold as a general "liver support" supplement because of its role in poisoning. Its use as an antidote in hospital does not show that everyday NAC supplements protect a healthy liver.

### Chronic Bronchitis and COPD

NAC thins mucus by breaking disulfide bonds [4]. A 2015 meta-analysis of 13 studies with 4155 patients found fewer exacerbations of chronic bronchitis or COPD with NAC (relative risk 0.75, 95% CI 0.66-0.84) [20]. The authors suggested at least 1200 mg per day for people with documented airway obstruction and 600 mg per day for those without [20].

An earlier meta-analysis of placebo-controlled trials, most using 600 mg a day, usually for 6 months, found a 23% decrease in the number of acute exacerbations compared with placebo [21]. In an open trial of 169 patients with moderate to severe COPD, adding 600 mg of NAC once a day to standard treatment for 6 months reduced exacerbations by 41% [22]. In 161 adults with bronchiectasis, 600 mg twice daily for 12 months lowered exacerbations (1.31 vs. 1.98 exacerbations per patient-year) [64].

NAC was well tolerated in the COPD meta-analysis, and the risk of adverse reactions did not rise with dose [20].

### Influenza and Immune Function

In a double-blind trial of 262 mostly older adults, NAC 600 mg twice daily for 6 months over winter did not prevent infection with the influenza virus. It did reduce influenza-like episodes and their severity: only 25% of virus-infected people on NAC developed symptoms, versus 79% on placebo [23].

In 36 healthy postmenopausal women, 600 mg of NAC a day for 2 to 4 months improved several immune cell functions, bringing them closer to those of younger women [13]. There was no placebo group, and infections were not measured.

These studies do not show that NAC prevents everyday illness in healthy people.

### COVID-19

In a retrospective study of 82 people in hospital with COVID-19 pneumonia, those also given oral NAC 600 mg twice daily for 14 days were less likely to need mechanical ventilation and less likely to die than those given standard care [26]. The authors called for properly designed prospective trials to confirm this, because a retrospective study cannot rule out other explanations [26].

### Mental Health: Schizophrenia

NAC has been tested as an add-on treatment in schizophrenia because it affects glutathione and glutamate [8].

In a 24-week double-blind trial of 140 people with chronic schizophrenia, NAC 1 g twice daily added to their usual medicine improved overall and negative symptom scores and akathisia (restlessness) compared with placebo, but not positive symptoms [28]. In a smaller 8-week trial, NAC up to 2 g a day added to risperidone improved negative symptoms [29].

A later trial in 84 people with treatment-resistant schizophrenia taking clozapine found that NAC 2 g daily did not improve negative symptoms, cognition or quality of life over a year [81]. So the benefit, if any, may depend on who is treated.

### Mental Health: Compulsive Behaviors

NAC is thought to restore glutamate levels in the nucleus accumbens, a brain area involved in compulsive behaviour [31][32].

**Skin picking (excoriation disorder):** In a 12-week trial of 66 adults, NAC 1,200 to 3,000 mg a day reduced skin-picking scores more than placebo (from 18.9 to 11.5 with NAC, from 17.9 to 14.1 with placebo). At the end, 47% of those on NAC were much or very much improved, compared with 19% on placebo [31].

**Trichotillomania (compulsive hair-pulling):** In a 12-week trial of 50 adults, NAC 1,200 to 2,400 mg a day reduced hair-pulling. Fifty-six percent were much or very much improved on NAC, versus 16% on placebo [32].

**Pathological gambling:** In a small pilot study, 27 people took NAC for 8 weeks, at an average effective dose of 1476.9 mg a day. Responders who continued on NAC in a 6-week double-blind phase kept their response more often than those switched to placebo [33].

### Mental Health: Bipolar Depression and Other Mood Disorders

A 2013 review found positive trials of NAC in depression and bipolar disorder, but noted that many need replication and are methodologically preliminary [8].

### Mental Health: OCD and Autism Spectrum Disorder

A 2015 systematic review found four clinical trials and five case reports of NAC in OCD and related disorders. Studies commonly ran 12 weeks at 2,400-3,000 mg/day, and NAC showed some activity in reducing symptoms, with few side effects [82]. Larger trials are needed.

In a 12-week pilot trial in 33 children with autism, NAC started at 900 mg a day and rose to 900 mg three times daily. It improved irritability scores compared with placebo [35].

### Substance Use Disorders

NAC has been tested for addiction because of its effects on glutamate [8]. A 2017 meta-analysis of seven trials (245 people) found NAC reduced craving more than placebo (Hedges' g 0.94; 95% CI 0.55-1.33), though the trials were few and varied in method [37].

Results for actual drug use are mixed. In adolescents with cannabis dependence, 8 weeks of NAC at 1200 mg twice daily more than doubled the odds of negative urine tests (odds ratio 2.4, 95% CI 1.1-5.2) [83]. A larger trial in adults with the same dose found no difference from placebo (odds ratio 1.00, 95% confidence interval 0.63-1.59) [84]. NAC is not an established treatment for addiction.

### Cardiovascular Disease

**Cardiovascular events in renal failure:** In 134 people on haemodialysis, NAC 600 mg twice daily reduced a composite of heart attack, stroke and other cardiovascular events (28% vs. 47%; relative risk 0.60, 95% CI 0.38 to 0.95) over a median of 14.5 months. Total mortality did not change [39].

**Homocysteine reduction:** High homocysteine is linked to cardiovascular disease. In a small crossover trial in 11 people, a high dose of NAC lowered plasma homocysteine by 45% but did not change lipoprotein(a) [12].

**Angina and myocardial infarction:** NAC strengthens the effects of nitroglycerin. Given intravenously before intravenous nitroglycerin, it lowered the nitroglycerin dose needed to drop blood pressure [41]. In a 4-month trial of 200 people with unstable angina, outcome events occurred in 13% of those given nitroglycerin patches plus oral NAC, versus 39% on placebo; but 35% on the combination had side effects, mainly intolerable headache [73]. In people having emergency angioplasty for a heart attack, high-dose intravenous NAC with low-dose nitroglycerin reduced infarct size [40].

**Kidney protection during angiography:** In a large trial of 5177 high-risk patients having angiography, 5 days of oral NAC gave no benefit over placebo for death, dialysis or lasting loss of kidney function (odds ratio, 1.02; 95% CI, 0.78 to 1.33) [42].

### Fertility (PCOS)

In 150 women with PCOS (polycystic ovary syndrome) whose ovulation did not respond to clomiphene, adding NAC 1.2 g/d raised the ovulation rate (49.3% vs. 1.3%) and pregnancy rate (21.3% vs. 0%) compared with placebo [43]. A meta-analysis of 8 studies (910 women) found higher ovulation and pregnancy rates with NAC than placebo, but lower than with metformin [56].

### Heavy Metal Detoxification

NAC has been used in heavy metal toxicity [4]. In 171 lead-exposed workers, daily NAC doses of 200, 400 or 800 mg for 12 weeks lowered blood lead compared with baseline levels [79]. A fourth group took no NAC, and there was no placebo group.

### Breast Pain (Cyclical Fibrocystic)

In a double-blind trial of 64 women aged 18 to 40 years with fibrocystic breast pain, 12 weeks of oral NAC reduced cyclical breast pain compared with placebo. It also lowered high-sensitivity C-reactive protein, a marker of inflammation, and raised plasma glutathione [47]. Larger studies are needed.

### Sjogren's Syndrome

In a 4-week double-blind crossover trial in 26 adults with Sjogren's syndrome, oral NAC improved eye soreness and irritation, bad breath and daytime thirst, but not the Schirmer test or tear break-up time [48].

### Ulcerative Colitis

NAC is not an established treatment for ulcerative colitis. If you have it, keep to the treatment your specialist advises.

### Parkinson's Disease

In 42 adults with Parkinson's disease, weekly intravenous NAC (50 mg/kg) plus oral NAC 500 mg twice daily for 3 months improved symptoms and increased dopamine transporter binding compared with standard care alone [50]. No placebo was used.

### Progressive Myoclonus Epilepsy

Four siblings with a rare inherited epilepsy first took vitamin E, selenium, riboflavin and zinc for 6 months, which improved awareness and speech. Adding 4 to 6 grams a day of NAC, with magnesium, markedly reduced their myoclonus (involuntary jerking) for up to 30 months [51]. A report of four people cannot show cause and effect.

### Hearing Loss (Noise-Induced)

Results are mixed.

**Positive:** In a crossover trial of 53 male workers exposed to noise, NAC 1200 mg/day for 14 days reduced temporary hearing loss after a shift. The effect was significant only in men lacking two genes for glutathione S-transferase (GSTM1 and GSTT1) [52].

**Negative:** In 566 military recruits during weapons training, NAC did not reduce the main measure of hearing threshold shifts compared with placebo, though one secondary measure favoured NAC [80].

### Acne

A meta-analysis of 8 studies in 910 women with PCOS found no difference in acne between NAC and placebo [56].

### Aging and Longevity

Glutathione falls with age [10]. In genetically mixed mice, NAC extended life span in males only, and this may have been due to the mice eating less [57].

**GlyNAC (Glycine + NAC) for aging:** Because older adults are low in both glycine and cysteine [10], researchers tested the two together.

In an open-label pilot trial, eight older adults took GlyNAC for 24 weeks. It corrected red blood cell glutathione deficiency, oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, and improved inflammation, insulin resistance, strength, gait speed and cognition. The benefits declined after 12 weeks off GlyNAC [7]. The pilot trial dosed by body weight: glycine 1.33 mmol/kg a day and cysteine 0.81 mmol/kg a day as NAC, roughly 100 mg of glycine and 130 mg of NAC per kilogram, or several grams of each a day for an adult [7], far above the dose in Brad's plan.

In a later placebo-controlled trial, 24 older adults were randomly assigned to GlyNAC or an alanine placebo for 16 weeks, and 12 younger adults took GlyNAC for 2 weeks [72]. Compared with the younger adults, the older adults had glutathione deficiency, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation and weaker physical function. GlyNAC, but not placebo, improved or corrected these defects, and it was well tolerated [72].

**Limitations:** The human GlyNAC studies were small and came from one research group [7][10][72]. Whether GlyNAC extends human life is unknown, and these trials did not test whether it beats NAC alone.

### Nasal Congestion and Sinusitis

Some sellers promote NAC for nasal congestion because it thins mucus. In 18 healthy volunteers, a nasal spray combining NAC with the decongestant tuaminoheptane reduced nasal resistance, as the decongestant xylometazoline alone did [85]. This does not show that NAC itself, or oral NAC, relieves congestion.

In cystic fibrosis, a Cochrane review found no evidence to recommend inhaled or oral thiol medicines such as NAC [60].

### Lung Cancer Concern (Preclinical)

In aging mice with lung oxidative stress, long-term NAC protected against emphysema but led to lung adenocarcinoma in 50% of mice lacking the protective gene JunD and 10% of aged control mice [61]. The authors wrote that this "warrants the utmost caution when considering the therapeutic use of antioxidants" [61].

In mouse models of lung cancer, NAC and vitamin E sped up tumour growth and shortened survival. The researchers suggested antioxidants may speed early tumours in high-risk groups such as smokers and people with COPD who take NAC for mucus [62]. These are mouse findings.

## 4. Recommended Dosing

The body does not need NAC from the diet, so no daily intake is recommended.

### Dosing by Indication (From Clinical Trials)

| Condition | Dose (per day) | Duration | Notes |
|-----------|:-:|:-:|------|
| Chronic bronchitis / COPD | 600 mg a day, or at least 1200 mg a day with airway obstruction | Usually 6 months | Fewer exacerbations [20][21][22] |
| Influenza-like illness | 600 mg twice daily | 6 months | Did not prevent infection; fewer symptoms [23] |
| Schizophrenia (add-on) | 1 g twice daily | 24 weeks | Added to usual medicine [28] |
| Skin picking | 1,200 to 3,000 mg | 12 weeks | [31] |
| Hair pulling | 1,200 to 2,400 mg | 12 weeks | [32] |
| Autism irritability (children) | 900 mg a day, rising to 900 mg three times daily | 12 weeks | Pilot trial [35] |
| PCOS with clomiphene | 1.2 g/d | With clomiphene | Add-on to fertility medicine [43] |
| Cardiovascular events on haemodialysis | 600 mg twice daily | Median 14.5 months | [39] |
| Lead exposure | 200, 400 or 800 mg | 12 weeks | No placebo group [79] |
| Noise-induced hearing loss | 1200 mg/day | 14 days | Mixed results [52][80] |

### How to Take NAC

Trials often split the daily dose in two, for example 600 mg twice daily [23][39].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### General Tolerability

Oral NAC was generally well tolerated in trials. In a meta-analysis of COPD trials, the risk of adverse reactions did not differ by dose [20]. In 566 military recruits, adverse events were about as common on NAC as on placebo (27.4% and 26.7%) [80], and two schizophrenia trials found no difference in side effects between NAC and placebo [29][81].

### Common Side Effects

In the oral trials above, side effects were no more common on NAC than on placebo [20][80]. Nausea and vomiting are the most common reactions to high-dose intravenous NAC given for poisoning [74].

### Body Odor

NAC contains sulfur [4], which gives the product its smell; whether NAC changes body odour is unclear.

### Sulfur Smell

NAC is a thiol (sulfur-containing) compound [4], so products often smell faintly of sulfur.

### Intravenous NAC Reactions

Intravenous NAC for paracetamol poisoning commonly causes reactions. In 169 patients, 10.1% had severe reactions; features included nausea (70.4%), vomiting (60.4%), flushing and, in 7.1%, wheeze and bronchospasm. Reactions were linked to histamine release [74]. A man with brittle asthma died after intravenous NAC, and the authors noted that asthma is a risk factor for reactions [75].

### Blood Clotting

High doses of NAC may affect blood clotting. In a randomised trial in 20 people having aortic surgery, high-dose intravenous NAC had anticoagulant and platelet-inhibiting effects, and the authors advised care when giving NAC to people with increased bleeding risk [66]. If you take a blood thinner or have a bleeding disorder, check with your doctor before taking NAC.

### Asthma

Asthma is a risk factor for reactions to intravenous NAC [75], which can include wheeze and bronchospasm [74]. If you have asthma, ask your doctor before taking NAC.

### Blood Test Interference

In the laboratory, high concentrations of NAC lowered results on some blood tests, including triglycerides, cholesterol, creatinine and uric acid. The concentrations needed were usually higher than those expected during intravenous or oral NAC treatment [76].

### Pregnancy and Lactation

If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your doctor before taking NAC.

### Lung Cancer Concern (Preclinical)

As described in the Evidence section, NAC sped up lung cancer in mice, and the researchers urged caution for smokers and people with COPD [61][62]. If you smoke or have COPD, discuss NAC with your doctor.

### Sulfonamide Allergy Cross-Reactivity

NAC is a thiol, a sulfur-containing compound [4], not a sulfonamide ("sulfa") drug. If you have a sulfa drug allergy, ask your doctor or pharmacist before taking it.

### Contaminated Products

Supplements can contain more or less than the label says. Independent testers, such as consumerlab.com and Labdoor, publish product test results.

## 6. Drug Interactions

| Drug / Class | Interaction | Clinical Recommendation |
|-------------|-------------|------------------------|
| Nitroglycerin | NAC strengthens nitroglycerin's effect on blood pressure; the combination caused frequent intolerable headache | Do not combine without your doctor's advice [41][73] |
| Blood thinners (anticoagulants) | High-dose NAC has anticoagulant and platelet-inhibiting effects | Check with your doctor first [66] |

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

## 7. Dietary Sources

NAC itself is not found in food. Cysteine, which NAC supplies, comes from protein foods [3].

### Cysteine-Rich Foods

USDA FoodData Central lists the cysteine content of individual foods [3].

### Practical Notes

- A normal protein intake supplies cysteine for glutathione in most people.
- Older adults have lower red blood cell glycine, cysteine and glutathione than younger adults, which is the rationale for GlyNAC [10].
