## Current view (reviewed 26 October 2026)

Brad lists coconut oil with fatty meats, full-fat dairy and palm oil as foods high in saturated fat, which research has shown can raise LDL cholesterol ([Brad, 4 Feb 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/how-to-prevent-a-heart-attack-for-5-45)). He cites research in which reducing saturated fats from sources like fatty meats, butter and coconut oil, and replacing them with unsaturated fats from sources like extra-virgin olive oil and avocados, resulted in a 17% reduction in the risk of heart disease ([Brad, 14 Oct 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/diet-strongly-suspected-best)). He says saturated fat clearly boosts LDL levels, and a high intake of saturated and trans fats makes low LDL hard to maintain ([Brad, 4 Feb 2025](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/how-to-prevent-a-heart-attack-for-5-45)). His plan uses extra-virgin olive oil as monounsaturated fat replacing butter or saturated fat, with a modest direct effect on LDL cholesterol (Brad's current guidance, 18 Mar 2026). Overall, he says the evidence suggests that we might want a diet that is high in protein, includes healthy unsaturated fats and is low in saturated fats ([Brad, 14 Oct 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/diet-strongly-suspected-best)).

## Key safety points

- In an 8-week trial in 150 people taking atorvastatin, LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol and lipoprotein(a) fell less with 1000 mg a day of added virgin coconut oil than on atorvastatin alone, though HDL cholesterol rose more [66]. The authors judged the add-on beneficial. If you take a statin, ask your doctor and have your cholesterol checked.
- MCTs have been considered contraindicated in cirrhosis, ketosis and acidosis [6]. If you have liver disease, ask your doctor first.
- Never use homemade coconut milk as infant formula. A 5-month-old fed only homemade coconut milk formula developed fatty liver, which resolved when it was stopped [72].
- Coconut allergy is rare, but anaphylaxis to coconut has been reported [68][71]. For trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).
- Coconut oil raises LDL cholesterol compared with nontropical vegetable oils [7]. Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fats lowers LDL cholesterol [9].
- Coconut fat did not help long-term blood sugar control and appeared to raise insulin resistance [29]. If you have diabetes, ask your doctor first.
- MCT oil can cause diarrhea, vomiting, bloating and cramps, more so at high doses [35][39][61]. Virgin coconut oil caused mild diarrhea in some people [60].

## 1. Overview

MCTs (medium chain triglycerides) are saturated fats built from fatty acids with medium-length carbon chains. The main ones in food are caprylic acid (C8), capric acid (C10) and lauric acid (C12). Coconut oil is high in saturated fat [7], and 45 to 50% of its fatty acids are lauric acid [57].

Medium-chain fatty acids are easily absorbed and metabolised by the liver, which can turn them into ketones, an alternative fuel for the brain [24]. These properties led to the use of MCTs in fat malabsorption, epilepsy and obesity [6].

Coconut oil's saturated fat is the main concern. Replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fats lowers LDL cholesterol, and trials that replaced it with polyunsaturated vegetable oil reduced cardiovascular disease by about 30% [9]. Coconut oil raises LDL cholesterol compared with nontropical vegetable oils [7].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

Each trial below used one specific form, and results from one form do not carry over to another.

### Virgin and Extra-Virgin Coconut Oil

Virgin coconut oil is the form used in most of the skin, cholesterol and Alzheimer's trials below [11][25][41][60][66].

### Refined Coconut Oil

No trial below names refined coconut oil as its test oil.

### MCT Oil

MCT oil is a concentrated source of medium-chain triglycerides. MCTs, given as oil, drinks or margarine, were the test product in the brain-energy, weight and sports trials below [14][15][19][35]. Because it is more ketogenic than long-chain fats, it allows more carbohydrate and protein in a ketogenic diet for epilepsy [61].

### Comparison Table

| Form | What it contains | Used in trials of |
|------|------|------|
| Virgin coconut oil | High in saturated fat; lauric acid makes up 45 to 50% of coconut oil's fatty acids [7][57] | Skin, cholesterol, Alzheimer's disease [11][25][41][60] |
| MCT oil or ketogenic MCT drink | Medium-chain triglycerides, more ketogenic than long-chain fats [61] | Brain energy, body fat, sports [15][19][35] |

### Bioavailability Considerations

A single drink of emulsified MCTs raised blood beta-hydroxybutyrate 90 minutes later [23], and a ketogenic MCT drink taken for 6 months raised brain ketone metabolism by 230% [19].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Weight Loss and Body Composition

MCTs may cause less fat gain than long-chain fats. In a 12-week double-blind trial in 73 people eating 2100 to 2400 kcal a day, those given 5 g a day of MCT in margarine lost more body fat than those given long-chain fat (3.8 vs 2.4 kg), and more visceral fat [15]. The authors suggested that higher thermogenesis after meals may explain it [15].

Coconut oil itself shows no such effect. A 2017 review found a lack of consistent evidence that coconut oil aids satiety or weight loss [3]. In 29 men with obesity on a balanced diet, a daily tablespoon (12 mL) of extra virgin coconut oil for 45 days changed body measurements no more than soybean oil did; HDL cholesterol rose (3.67 vs -3.79 mg/dL) [16]. In 29 people at raised cardiometabolic risk, mainly the amount of fat in a meal, not whether it was coconut or canola oil, affected hunger and satiety, and canola oil lowered the hunger hormone ghrelin more than coconut oil [17]. Across trials, coconut oil did not change body fat compared with nontropical vegetable oils [7].

**Synthesis.** Purified MCTs may give a small advantage over long-chain fats in a controlled diet [15]. Coconut oil has not helped weight loss [3][7][16].

### Cognitive Function and Alzheimer's Disease

The brain's use of glucose falls in Alzheimer's disease, while its uptake of ketones stays normal in mild cognitive impairment (MCI) [19][22]. MCTs raise blood ketones, so they have been tested as an alternative brain fuel [22][23].

**Mild cognitive impairment.** In a blinded trial, 52 people with MCI took 30 g a day of a ketogenic MCT drink or placebo for 6 months [19]. Brain ketone metabolism rose by 230% on the MCT drink, and episodic memory, language, executive function and processing speed improved compared with baseline [19]. In the full 6-month trial (15 g twice a day; 39 on the drink, 44 on placebo), free and cued recall, verbal fluency, naming and Trail-Making errors improved compared with placebo [20].

**White matter energy supply.** In 17 people on the MCT drink and 16 on placebo, the drink raised ketone uptake 2.5- to 3.2-fold in white matter, and better processing speed tracked with that uptake [21]. Whether this slows progression to Alzheimer's disease is unknown [20].

**Earlier Alzheimer's studies.** In 152 people with mild or moderate Alzheimer's disease, the ketogenic compound AC-1202 raised blood beta-hydroxybutyrate and gave a 1.9 point difference in ADAS-Cog score at day 45 compared with placebo; effects were clearest in people without the APOE4 gene [22]. In a single-dose study in 20 adults with Alzheimer's disease or MCI, an MCT drink helped ADAS-Cog performance only in people without APOE4 [23]. A 2015 review proposed that coconut may help prevent or treat Alzheimer's disease through ketones and other compounds [24].

**Recent Alzheimer's trials.** In a 6-month crossover trial with a 6-month open-label extension in people with probable Alzheimer's disease, the MCT dose target was 42 g a day; people managed an average of 25.2 g, and 80% stayed stable or improved [14]. The trial was small and partly open-label [14]. In a trial in Sri Lanka, 120 adults over 65 with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease took virgin coconut oil (30 mL a day) or canola oil for 24 weeks; cognition did not differ between groups, though MMSE scores improved in APOE4 carriers on coconut oil compared with non-carriers [25]. Of those enrolled, 30% dropped out [25].

**Synthesis.** An MCT drink improved some cognitive tests in MCI after 6 months [19][20]. Virgin coconut oil gave no cognitive benefit in Alzheimer's disease [25]. The evidence is too thin to recommend either oil to treat or prevent dementia.

### Blood Sugar and Insulin Control

In diabetic rats, a coconut oil diet lowered total cholesterol and showed a trend toward better glucose tolerance [27], and in mice an MCT diet caused less weight and fat gain than a long-chain fat diet [28]. Human trials tell a different story.

A meta-analysis of short-term meal studies and 11 long-term trials found that meals with coconut fat raised blood glucose slightly and lowered insulin after the meal [29]. Long-term coconut fat intake increased insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) and did not improve fasting glucose or insulin [29]. The authors concluded that the claim that coconut fat improves blood sugar control is disproved [29].

If you have diabetes, ask your doctor first.

### Blood Pressure

In hypertensive rats, virgin coconut oil, with or without exercise, lowered blood pressure [30], and lauric acid given into a vein lowered blood pressure [31]. These are animal studies. In a placebo-controlled trial in 45 people with stage 1 hypertension, extra virgin coconut oil, alone or with aerobic exercise training, had no antihypertensive effect [73].

### Sports Performance

Results are mixed: MCT alone slowed time trials [33][35], one small trial found MCT added to carbohydrate slightly faster [33], and 2 weeks of MCT oil did not help runners [34].

**Negative findings.** In trained cyclists, drinking MCT alone during exercise gave a 17-18% lower time-trial work rate and caused intestinal cramping; 85 g of MCT added to carbohydrate had no effect compared with placebo [35]. In endurance-trained cyclists, replacing carbohydrate with MCT slowed a time trial, while adding MCT to carbohydrate made it slightly faster [33]. In trained male runners, 60 g a day of MCT oil for 2 weeks did not improve endurance [34]. In recreational runners, caffeine with extra virgin coconut oil before a 1600 m run did not improve running time [36].

**Synthesis.** MCTs do not appear to improve exercise performance, and large amounts can cause gut cramping that worsens it [35].

### Epilepsy

A ketogenic diet built on MCT oil works as well as the classic ketogenic diet for drug-resistant epilepsy, but it can cause diarrhea, vomiting, bloating and cramps, so health professionals need to manage it carefully [61].

In a case report, a 43-year-old man whose partial epilepsy had not responded to several drugs went from multiple daily seizures to one every four days after adding MCT oil to his normal diet at four tablespoons twice daily [39]. His seizures came back when he stopped for ten days, and higher doses caused diarrhea and flatulence [39]. One case report cannot guide treatment. If you have epilepsy, ask your doctor before using MCT oil, and do not use it to treat seizures without medical supervision [61].

### Skin Health

#### Moisturizing and Atopic Dermatitis

In 34 people with dry skin (xerosis), virgin coconut oil applied to the legs twice a day for 2 weeks worked as well as mineral oil; both improved skin hydration and surface lipids, with no adverse reactions [11].

In 117 children with mild to moderate atopic dermatitis, 8 weeks of topical virgin coconut oil lowered SCORAD scores by 68.23%, against 38.13% with mineral oil, and improved skin barrier measures more [41].

In adults with atopic dermatitis, applying virgin coconut oil twice a day for 4 weeks cleared *Staphylococcus aureus* better than virgin olive oil: 1 of 20 colonised people on coconut oil stayed positive, against 6 of 12 on olive oil [43].

#### Antimicrobial Properties and Acne

In laboratory tests, lauric acid inhibited *Propionibacterium acnes* at concentrations over 15 times lower than benzoyl peroxide, and it reduced the bacteria and swelling on mouse ears [47]. These were laboratory and mouse studies [47].

#### UV Protection

In laboratory tests, the UV absorptivity of oils, coconut oil among them, was at least two orders of magnitude lower than that of commercial UV blockers, too little for significant protection [49]. Coconut oil is not a substitute for sunscreen [49].

### Hair Health

In tests on hair, coconut oil reduced protein loss from both undamaged and damaged hair when used before or after washing; sunflower and mineral oils did not [51]. The authors linked this to lauric acid's ability to penetrate the hair shaft [51].

#### Dandruff

In a 16-week study of 140 Indian women (70 with dandruff), 12 weeks of coconut oil on the scalp increased *Cutibacterium acnes* and *Malassezia globosa* on dandruff scalps, and both were linked to fewer dandruff signs [53].

In laboratory tests, medium-chain triglycerides were toxic to *Malassezia* yeasts [54].

### Oral Health

Oil pulling means swishing oil between the teeth for several minutes. It is a traditional Indian remedy [55].

In 40 dental students, a 10-minute daily coconut oil rinse for seven days lowered plaque more than placebo, though the difference was significant only on the seventh day [55].

In a crossover trial in 29 volunteers, oil pulling with coconut oil slowed plaque regrowth across a 4-day test about as well as 0.2% chlorhexidine mouthwash, with less tooth staining [56].

In 60 teenagers with gingivitis, adding coconut oil pulling for 30 days lowered plaque and gum scores from day 7; there was no control group [57]. A 2016 systematic review of five trials (160 people) found no significant differences in plaque index (3 studies) and gingival index (2 studies) between oil pulling and control groups, and concluded that limited evidence suggests oil pulling may help oral hygiene over short periods [70]. The authors of the teenage study called it a possible adjuvant to oral hygiene [57].

### Fat Malabsorption

MCTs have long been used in people who cannot absorb fat normally [6].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

Trial doses varied by form and goal. These are the amounts used.

### Oral Dosing by Indication

| Indication | Form | Dose | Duration | Result |
|-----------|------|------|----------|:-:|
| Body fat | MCT in margarine | 5 g daily | 12 weeks | More body fat lost than with long-chain fat [15] |
| Cognitive support (MCI) | Ketogenic MCT drink | 30 g daily in two doses | 6 months | Some cognitive tests improved [19][20] |
| Alzheimer's disease | MCT oil | Target 42 g daily; average 25.2 g | 6 months plus open-label extension | 80% stable or improved; small, partly open-label [14] |
| Alzheimer's disease | Virgin coconut oil | 30 mL daily | 24 weeks | No benefit over canola oil [25] |
| Epilepsy (adjunct) | 100% MCT oil | 4 tablespoons twice daily | Ongoing | Very limited (single case report) [39] |

**Gradual dose escalation.** Gut side effects rise with dose [35][39][61]. In the Alzheimer's trial, people took the target dose "or maximum tolerated" [14].

### Topical Dosing

| Indication | Form | Frequency | Duration |
|-----------|------|-----------|----------|
| Atopic dermatitis (adults) | Virgin coconut oil | Twice a day | 4 weeks [43] |
| Atopic dermatitis (children) | Virgin coconut oil | Applied to the skin | 8 weeks [41] |
| Dry skin | Virgin coconut oil | Twice a day | 2 weeks [11] |
| Hair conditioning | Coconut oil | Before or after washing | Laboratory study [51] |

### Cooking and Storage

Coconut oil is high in saturated fat, so it counts toward your saturated fat intake [7]. For cooking, nontropical vegetable oils give lower LDL cholesterol than coconut oil [7].

### How to Choose a Product

The ketone and cognition trials used MCT oils or MCT drinks, not virgin coconut oil [14][19][22]. Virgin coconut oil is the form tested on skin [11][41][43].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Gastrointestinal Effects

Gut upset is the most common side effect. MCT oil can cause diarrhea, vomiting, bloating and cramps [61]. Diarrhea and flatulence limited the dose in an epilepsy case [39], and 85 g of MCT during exercise caused intestinal cramping [35]. In the AC-1202 Alzheimer's trial, adverse events were more common than on placebo, mostly in the gut, mild to moderate and transient [22]. A daily 30 mL dose of virgin coconut oil caused mild diarrhea in some healthy volunteers [60].

### Cardiovascular Concerns — Cholesterol Effects

This is the main safety concern with coconut oil, which is high in saturated fat [7].

**AHA position.** An American Heart Association advisory concluded that replacing saturated fat with unsaturated fats, especially polyunsaturated fats, lowers LDL cholesterol and will lower cardiovascular disease [9].

**Meta-analysis evidence.** In a meta-analysis of 16 articles, coconut oil raised LDL cholesterol by 10.47 mg/dL (95% CI 3.01 to 17.94) and HDL cholesterol by 4.00 mg/dL compared with nontropical vegetable oils [7]. The authors concluded that coconut oil results in higher LDL cholesterol than nontropical vegetable oils [7].

**Dissenting evidence.** Some smaller trials found no LDL rise. In 32 healthy young Thai adults, a twice-daily 15 mL dose of virgin coconut oil for 8 weeks raised HDL cholesterol by 5.72 mg/dL without changing LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol or triglycerides compared with a control [60]. In the Sri Lankan Alzheimer's trial, virgin coconut oil did not change the lipid profile compared with canola oil [25]. These trials were smaller than the pooled analysis, which still shows higher LDL cholesterol with coconut oil [7].

### Interaction with Statin Therapy

In a double-blind trial in 150 people with dyslipidemia on atorvastatin 10 mg a day, adding virgin coconut oil (1000 mg a day) for 8 weeks raised HDL cholesterol more, but LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol and lipoprotein(a) fell more in the group taking atorvastatin alone [66]. The authors judged the add-on beneficial because of HDL and oxidative stress measures [66].

### Insulin Resistance

In a meta-analysis of 11 long-term trials, coconut fat did not help long-term blood sugar control and appeared to raise insulin resistance [29]. If you have diabetes, ask your doctor first.

### Allergic Reactions

Coconut allergy is rare and is not directly linked to nut allergy [68]. A case report describes a child who became allergic to coconut after regular skin and dietary exposure [68]. Anaphylaxis to coconut has been reported, in one case with cross-reactivity to hazelnut, and the authors recommend that people with coconut allergy be checked for tree nut allergies [71]. For trouble breathing or swelling of the face or throat, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK).

### Special Populations

**Liver disease.** MCTs have been considered contraindicated in cirrhosis, ketosis and acidosis [6]. If you have liver disease, ask your doctor before taking MCT oil.

**Infants.** Never use homemade coconut milk as infant formula. A 5-month-old fed only homemade coconut milk formula developed fatty liver (hepatic steatosis), which resolved when it was stopped [72].

## 6. Drug Interactions

One possible interaction follows from trial evidence. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Documented Interactions

| Drug / Drug Class | Interaction | Evidence | Management |
|----------|------------|:-:|----------|
| Statins (e.g., atorvastatin) | Smaller falls in LDL cholesterol, total cholesterol and lipoprotein(a) | One 8-week randomized trial [66] | Avoid high intakes of coconut oil with a statin; if you use coconut oil, have your cholesterol checked |

## 7. Dietary Sources

### Coconut-Based Sources

Whole coconut is rich in dietary fibre, vitamins and minerals [24]. Coconut oil has no fibre and no significant vitamins or minerals [8].

### Nutritional Profile of Coconut Oil

One tablespoon (14 g) of coconut oil provides approximately [8]:
- 120 calories
- 14 g total fat (all from fat)
- ~11.5 g saturated fat
- ~0.8 g monounsaturated fat
- ~0.2 g polyunsaturated fat
- 0 g protein
- 0 g carbohydrates
- 0 g fiber
- Trace amounts of vitamin E (0.01 mg)
- No significant vitamins or minerals

### Fatty Acid Composition of Coconut Oil

Coconut oil contains mostly medium-chain fatty acids, and 45 to 50% of its fatty acids are lauric acid [57].
