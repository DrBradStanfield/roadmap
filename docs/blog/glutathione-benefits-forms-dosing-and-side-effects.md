## Current view (reviewed 23 October 2026)

Brad describes glutathione as a powerful antioxidant that helps regulate oxidative stress and immune function, and notes that its levels decline rapidly from the age of 45 (Brad's current guidance, 31 Jul 2026). Rather than supplementing with vitamin A or E, he favours GlyNAC, the glutathione precursors glycine and N-acetylcysteine (NAC), because it lets the body regulate its own antioxidant levels instead of flooding the system with external antioxidants ([Brad, 3 Feb 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-most-important-antioxidant-study-i-ve-ever-read)). A human placebo-controlled study found that supplementing with glycine and NAC corrected the glutathione deficiency in older adults and improved hand grip strength (Brad's current guidance, 31 Jul 2026). That study was small, and the benefits appeared only in the older adults, not the younger ones, which suggests to Brad that the effects of antioxidants or their precursors vary with age ([Brad, 3 Feb 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-most-important-antioxidant-study-i-ve-ever-read)). His own plan is GlyNAC 1 g from the age of 45 (Brad's current guidance, 19 Feb 2026). He rates it as the item in his stack with the least evidence, very early in the research, and says that his taking a supplement does not mean you should, so check with a healthcare professional first ([Brad, 19 Nov 2024](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/only-supplements-work-2025)).

## Key safety points

- If you take nitroglycerin or another nitrate, ask your doctor before taking NAC or GlyNAC. When the two were given together by drip in a trial, symptomatic low blood pressure was much more common [81].
- If you take a blood thinner, check with your doctor first. In laboratory tests, NAC reduced platelet clumping [82].
- During chemotherapy, take glutathione or NAC only with your oncologist's approval. Cancer cells can use high glutathione to resist treatment [5].
- Intravenous glutathione for skin lightening has little evidence behind it, and the Philippine FDA has warned against it [60][85]. Reported harms include life-threatening skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis), so if a blistering or peeling rash follows an injection, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) [85].
- Inhaled glutathione briefly lowered lung function (FEV1) after each dose in a cystic fibrosis study [47].
- High NAC doses can cause nausea: in a lupus trial, 33% of people taking 4.8 g a day had nausea, which went away [42].
- In trials lasting 4 to 12 weeks, oral glutathione was well tolerated, though two people in one trial had a temporary rise in liver enzymes [61][80]. Its long-term safety has not been established [61].

## 1. Overview

Glutathione (GSH) is a small protein-like molecule built from glycine, cysteine and glutamic acid, and the most abundant non-protein thiol in mammalian tissues and a major defence against oxidative stress [2][3]. It also takes part in redox signalling, the detoxification of foreign chemicals, and the control of cell growth, cell death and immune function [2]. Glutathione neutralises reactive oxygen species directly and is used by glutathione peroxidase enzymes to break down hydrogen peroxide [2][4].

Glutathione exists in a reduced form (GSH), the active antioxidant, and an oxidized form (GSSG, glutathione disulfide), produced when GSH neutralises an oxidant. The enzyme glutathione reductase, which needs NADPH, recycles GSSG back to GSH [2]. A lower ratio of oxidized to reduced glutathione in blood is used as a sign of lower oxidative stress [12].

Beyond its antioxidant role, glutathione is involved in several other processes [2][3][4]:

- **Phase II detoxification:** Glutathione is joined to foreign chemicals and reactive electrophiles, which helps the body remove them [3][6].
- **Immune function:** Lymphocytes work best at a balanced level of glutathione. In healthy people, there is no sign that extra glutathione or cysteine improves resistance to infection or the response to vaccines; people with a cysteine and glutathione deficiency may benefit [7][8].
- **Protein thiol homeostasis:** Glutathione maintains proteins in their reduced (functional) state through S-glutathionylation, a reversible change that regulates protein activity under oxidative stress [2].
- **Amino acid transport:** The gamma-glutamyl cycle uses glutathione to transport amino acids across cell membranes [2][3].
- **Regulation of cell proliferation and apoptosis:** Glutathione helps regulate cell growth and programmed cell death [2][5].

The body makes glutathione in two steps. The first, run by glutamate cysteine ligase (GCL), is the rate-limiting step; the second uses glutathione synthetase [2]. The supply of cysteine is a key limit on synthesis, which is why NAC, a cysteine source, raises glutathione [2][9].

Glutathione defences decline with age. In 122 healthy people aged 19 to 85 years, the plasma glutathione redox state did not become more oxidized prior to 45 years and then oxidized steadily each year, suggesting the glutathione system holds steady until 45 years and then declines rapidly [79]. In a small study, 8 older adults had lower red blood cell glutathione than 8 younger adults, because they made less of it [11]. Poor glutathione synthesis has been linked with diabetes, lung and liver fibrosis, alcoholic liver disease and drug-resistant tumours [2]. Whether low glutathione causes ageing and disease or results from them is still unclear.

The body makes its own glutathione, so you do not need it from food. Glutathione in food and supplements is broken down by gamma-glutamyltransferase in the gut and liver, so dietary glutathione does little to set blood levels [13]. Newer oral forms, such as liposomal and orobuccal tablets, aim to improve absorption [15][16].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

Getting glutathione into the body by mouth is the main problem. In 7 healthy volunteers, a single 3 g oral dose did not raise blood glutathione, cysteine or glutamate [13]. Taking it daily for months does raise levels, as the trials below show [12].

### Reduced L-Glutathione (GSH)

Most oral glutathione trials used the reduced form (GSH). One trial also gave the oxidized form (GSSG) by mouth, at the same dose, with similar effects on the skin [80].

**Bioavailability data:** A single 3 g oral dose did not raise blood glutathione in healthy volunteers [13]. In a 6-month randomized, placebo-controlled trial in 54 non-smoking adults, oral GSH at 250 or 1,000 mg a day raised glutathione in blood at 1, 3 and 6 months [12]. At 6 months, the higher dose raised glutathione 30 to 35% in red blood cells, plasma and lymphocytes and 260% in cheek cells; the lower dose raised blood and red blood cell glutathione 17% and 29% [12]. Levels returned to baseline after a 1-month washout, so the effect lasts only while you keep taking it [12]. A shorter trial in 40 healthy adults, using 500 mg twice daily for 4 weeks, found no change in glutathione status or oxidative stress markers [29].

**Mechanism of action when taken orally:** Some glutathione is broken down in the gut into its amino acids, which the body can use to make new glutathione. In one small study, a single oral dose of 50 mg per kg body weight raised the protein-bound form of glutathione in plasma from 60 to 120 minutes after the dose, though free glutathione did not change [14].

### Liposomal Glutathione

Liposomal formulations enclose glutathione in phospholipid vesicles, aiming to protect it from digestion.

**Evidence:** In a 1-month pilot study, 12 healthy adults took liposomal glutathione at two doses, 500 and 1,000 mg a day [15]. Compared with their own baseline, the largest rises came after 2 weeks: 40% in whole blood, 25% in red blood cells, 28% in plasma and 100% in immune cells [15]. The two doses did not differ, the study was too small to tell them apart, and it had no comparison with regular glutathione [15]. A different approach, a tablet that dissolves in the mouth, raised blood glutathione within 30 to 60 minutes in 15 healthy volunteers [16]. These studies are small and short.

**Practical consideration:** The liposomal pilot did not compare liposomal with regular glutathione, so any advantage is unproven [15].

### S-Acetyl Glutathione (SAG)

S-acetyl glutathione is a derivative in which an acetyl group is attached to the sulfur atom of the cysteine residue, in theory protecting it from breakdown in the gut. Once absorbed, enzymes inside cells remove the acetyl group to release glutathione. Controlled human evidence that SAG raises glutathione more than regular glutathione is limited.

### N-Acetyl Cysteine (NAC)

NAC is not glutathione itself. It is a precursor of cysteine, the amino acid that limits glutathione synthesis, and it raises glutathione levels [9][20]. Its own trials are covered in detail in the [NAC topic page](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/nac-n-acetyl-cysteine-benefits-dosing-and-side-effects).

**Bioavailability:** In one study, NAC taken by mouth was not detectable in blood; it is quickly converted to cysteine [19]. NAC has long been used as the antidote for acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose and as a drug that thins mucus [9][18].

**Evidence for raising glutathione:** NAC at 600 mg a day for five days raised plasma cysteine and raised glutathione in plasma and lung lining fluid 1 to 3 hours after the last dose [19]. In people with HIV and low glutathione, 8 weeks of oral NAC raised whole blood glutathione from 0.88 mM to 0.98 mM, while levels on placebo stayed the same [39]. A review found strong evidence for NAC in acetaminophen overdose and emerging evidence in some psychiatric disorders [20].

NAC is discussed under "Evidence for Benefits" because its clinical evidence is broader than that of glutathione itself. Each result there is a result for NAC, not for glutathione.

### Glycine + NAC (GlyNAC)

GlyNAC combines glycine and NAC, the two precursors that older adults may run short of for glutathione synthesis [11]. Its results belong to the combination, not to glutathione or either ingredient alone.

**Key evidence:** In an open-label pilot trial by Kumar and colleagues, eight older adults took GlyNAC for 24 weeks. It corrected red blood cell glutathione deficiency, oxidative stress and mitochondrial dysfunction, and improved inflammation, insulin resistance, strength, gait speed and cognition; the benefits declined after 12 weeks off GlyNAC [10]. In the later placebo-controlled trial, older adults were randomized to GlyNAC (12 people) or an alanine placebo (12 people) for 16 weeks, and 12 young adults took GlyNAC for 2 weeks [21]. GlyNAC, but not placebo, improved or corrected the older adults' glutathione deficiency, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, inflammation, insulin resistance, physical function, waist circumference and systolic blood pressure [21]. Brad describes this as the study of 36 people in which the benefits appeared only in older adults ([Brad, 3 Feb 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-most-important-antioxidant-study-i-ve-ever-read)).

### Whey Protein

Whey protein is rich in the building blocks of glutathione, and in animal studies whey protein diets raised glutathione in several tissues [23].

**Evidence:** In a double-blind trial in 30 people with HIV, 45 g of whey protein a day for two weeks significantly raised plasma glutathione with one whey formula; the rise with the other formula was not significant [22].

### Other Glutathione-Sparing Compounds

Several nutrients help maintain glutathione levels by reducing its consumption or supporting its recycling:

- **Vitamin C (ascorbic acid):** In 48 adults with low vitamin C, taking 500 or 1,000 mg a day for 13 weeks raised lymphocyte glutathione by 18%; levels fell back after placebo [24]. Dr Brad Stanfield's [MicroVitamin](https://drstanfield.com/products/microvitamin) includes 45 mg of vitamin C as calcium ascorbate, which may contribute to glutathione sparing alongside dietary intake.
- **Alpha-lipoic acid (ALA):** In old rats, lipoic acid restored the activity of GCL, the enzyme that limits glutathione synthesis, which had declined with age [25]. Human data are limited.
- **Selenium:** Selenium is built into selenoproteins, including the glutathione peroxidases, which have antioxidant effects [26]. MicroVitamin provides 27.5 mcg of selenium as selenium glycinate, supporting glutathione peroxidase activity.
- **Sulforaphane (from broccoli sprouts):** Young broccoli sprouts contain 10 to 100 times more glucoraphanin, the precursor of sulforaphane, than mature plants [27]. In a small human trial, broccoli sprout sulforaphane raised the expression of phase II enzymes, including glutathione S-transferases, in nasal cells [28].

### How to Take Glutathione

In the trials above, oral glutathione was taken daily, in one trial as two divided doses [61]. The trials did not compare taking it with or without food.

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Aging and Oxidative Stress

Older adults have lower glutathione and more oxidative damage than younger adults [11][79].

**Glutathione precursors in older adults:** In eight older adults, 2 weeks of the precursors cysteine and glycine raised red blood cell glutathione by 94.6% and lowered oxidative stress, bringing these measures to the level of younger adults [11]. The GlyNAC trials then tested the combination for longer. In the open-label pilot (eight older adults, 24 weeks), GlyNAC corrected glutathione deficiency and mitochondrial dysfunction and improved inflammation, insulin resistance, genomic damage, strength, gait speed, exercise capacity and cognition, and it lowered body fat and waist size; the benefits declined after 12 weeks off GlyNAC [10].

In the placebo-controlled trial (12 older adults on GlyNAC and 12 on placebo for 16 weeks, plus 12 young adults), GlyNAC improved or corrected glutathione deficiency, oxidative stress, mitochondrial function, inflammation, endothelial function, insulin resistance, aging hallmarks, physical function, waist circumference and systolic blood pressure in the older adults, and was safe and well tolerated [21]. The same group ran an open-label trial in eight people with HIV: 12 weeks of GlyNAC improved many of the same measures, and the benefits receded after stopping [30].

### Liver Health and Detoxification

The liver relies on glutathione to detoxify drugs and other chemicals, and loss of mitochondrial glutathione makes liver cells more likely to die [6].

**Acetaminophen toxicity:** NAC is the antidote for acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose; it restores glutathione [18][39]. In a national study of 2,540 people treated with oral NAC, liver damage developed in 6.1% of those at probable risk when NAC started within 10 hours of the overdose, and in 26.4% when it started 10 to 24 hours after [32]. NAC protected people when started within eight hours, whatever their acetaminophen level [32].

**Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD):** In a small trial in people with fatty liver, NAC lowered the liver enzyme ALT after three months more than vitamin C did [33]. In a rat model of fatty liver inflammation, NAC lowered the liver damage score and oxidative stress without changing the amount of fat in the liver [34].

**Alcoholic liver disease:** Alcohol depletes glutathione in liver mitochondria, which makes liver cells more vulnerable to TNF-induced injury [35]. In a trial of 174 people with severe alcoholic hepatitis, intravenous NAC plus prednisolone lowered 1-month mortality compared with prednisolone alone (8% vs 24%, P = 0.006), but 6-month mortality, the main outcome, did not differ significantly (27% vs 38%, P = 0.07) [36].

**Heavy metal detoxification:** Glutathione binds metals through its sulfur group, which helps the body remove them [2][6]. Adding antioxidants to chelation treatment for metal poisoning is under study [37].

### Immune Function

Glutathione levels affect how lymphocytes work, and both too little and too much can change immune responses [7][8].

**HIV/AIDS:** In people with HIV, low glutathione in CD4 T cells was linked with markedly poorer survival 2 to 3 years later [38]. In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in 81 people with HIV, 8 weeks of oral NAC raised whole blood and T cell glutathione, with minimal side effects [39].

**Respiratory infections:** In a double-blind trial in 262 people, most of them elderly, NAC 600 mg twice daily for 6 months reduced the frequency and severity of influenza-like episodes [40]. Infection rates were similar, but only 25% of infected people on NAC developed symptoms, against 79% on placebo [40].

**Sepsis:** In a trial of 60 people in septic shock, an intravenous NAC bolus of 150 mg per kg followed by an infusion raised liver blood flow and improved one marker of liver function [41].

**Autoimmune disease:** In a placebo-controlled trial in 36 people with lupus, NAC at 2.4 or 4.8 g a day lowered disease activity scores and anti-DNA antibody production during a 3-month treatment period; 1.2 g a day had no effect [42].

### Respiratory and Lung Health

Glutathione also helps protect lung tissue from oxidative damage [8].

**COPD:** A Cochrane review of mucolytics (NAC, carbocysteine and others) in chronic bronchitis and COPD included 38 trials and 10,377 people [43]. In 28 of those trials, with 6,723 people, those taking mucolytics may have been more likely to stay free of flare-ups (Peto OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.56 to 1.91), though recent studies showed less benefit than earlier ones [43].

The PANTHEON trial randomized 1,006 Chinese people with moderate to severe COPD to NAC 600 mg twice daily or placebo for 1 year. Flare-ups fell from 1.49 to 1.16 per patient-year (risk ratio 0.78, 95% CI 0.67 to 0.90; p=0.0011) [44].

**Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF):** In the PANTHER-IPF trial, NAC did not slow the decline in lung capacity (FVC) at 60 weeks compared with placebo (-0.18 liters vs -0.19 liters, P=0.77) [45]. A later genetic analysis suggested NAC may help people with the TOLLIP rs3750920 TT genotype (hazard ratio 0.14, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.83) and showed a trend toward harm in those with the CC genotype [46].

**Cystic fibrosis:** In 17 people with cystic fibrosis, inhaled glutathione at 300 or 450 mg three times daily for 14 days raised glutathione in lung fluid three- to fourfold [47]. Lung function (FEV1) dropped briefly after each inhalation but improved overall after 14 days [47].

### Cognitive Function and Neurodegenerative Disease

The brain is exposed to high oxidative stress, and glutathione helps protect it [2][48].

**Alzheimer's disease:** Brain scans using magnetic resonance spectroscopy showed lower glutathione in the hippocampus and frontal cortex in Alzheimer's disease, and the drop tracked with poorer cognitive function [48].

GlyNAC improved cognition in the open-label pilot trial in older adults [10]. That trial had no placebo group, so the cognitive result needs confirming.

**Parkinson's disease:** In brains from people who died with Parkinson's disease, glutathione in the substantia nigra was 40% lower than in controls [49]. In an open-label study of nine people with early, untreated Parkinson's, intravenous glutathione 600 mg twice daily for 30 days reduced disability by 42%, and the effect lasted 2 to 4 months after stopping [50]. A randomized, placebo-controlled pilot of intravenous glutathione 1,400 mg three times a week for 4 weeks found no significant difference in symptom scores [51]. In a placebo-controlled trial in 45 people, intranasal glutathione at 100 mg or 200 mg three times daily for three months was no better than placebo, and one person on the higher dose developed heart muscle disease (cardiomyopathy) [52].

NAC has also been tested. In a study that randomized people with Parkinson's to daily NAC or a waitlist, 3 months of NAC raised dopamine transporter binding by 4.4% to 7.8% on brain scans and improved symptom scores by 12.9% [53]. The control group received no placebo, so these results need confirming.

**General cognitive decline in aging:** In a placebo-controlled trial in probable Alzheimer's disease, NAC was favoured on nearly every measure after 3 and 6 months, but it was significantly better only for some cognitive tasks [54].

### Type 2 Diabetes and Metabolic Health

Poor glutathione synthesis has been linked with diabetes [2].

**Direct glutathione supplementation:** In a trial, 125 people being treated for type 2 diabetes took 500 mg of oral glutathione a day for six months alongside their diabetes medicines, and were compared with people on medicines alone [55]. Blood glutathione rose and a marker of DNA damage fell within three months [55]. Overall, HbA1c held steady; only in a later subgroup analysis of people above 55 years did HbA1c fall and fasting insulin rise [55].

**GlyNAC and insulin resistance:** GlyNAC improved insulin resistance in older adults, in the open-label pilot and in the placebo-controlled trial [10][21].

**Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS):** In a double-blind trial in women with PCOS who had not responded to clomiphene, adding NAC 1.2 g a day raised the ovulation rate (49.3% vs 1.3%) and the pregnancy rate (21.3% vs 0%) compared with placebo [58]. A meta-analysis of eight trials in 910 women found NAC improved ovulation, pregnancy and live birth compared with placebo, but pregnancy and ovulation were less likely with NAC than with metformin [59].

### Skin Health and Lightening

Glutathione is popular as an oral and injected skin-lightening agent in some countries [60].

**Mechanism:** Glutathione inhibits tyrosinase, an enzyme needed to make melanin, and shifts pigment production from dark eumelanin toward lighter phaeomelanin [60].

**Oral supplementation:** In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in Thai medical students, glutathione 500 mg a day in two divided doses for 4 weeks lowered melanin at all six skin sites measured, but the difference from placebo was significant at only two of them [61]. In another trial, healthy women took 250 mg a day of reduced or oxidized glutathione for 12 weeks; melanin tended to be lower than with placebo, and the reduced form lessened wrinkles at some sites [80]. Reviewers say larger and longer placebo-controlled trials with long-term follow-up are needed [60].

**Topical glutathione:** In 30 healthy women aged 30 to 50, a 2% oxidized glutathione (GSSG) lotion applied twice a day for 10 weeks lightened the treated side of the face compared with a placebo lotion on the other side [62].

**Negative evidence:** In an Indonesian randomized trial lasting 12 weeks, people took an oral supplement combining glutathione with zinc, vitamin C and alpha-lipoic acid; skin lightening was slightly greater than with placebo but not statistically significant [63]. There is no good evidence that intravenous glutathione lightens skin [60].

**Safety concerns with IV glutathione for skin lightening:** Harms from intravenous glutathione led the Philippine FDA to issue a public warning against its use for skin lightening [60]. Reported harms include potentially fatal skin reactions, severe abdominal pain, and thyroid and kidney problems [85].

**Skin aging and UV protection:** In the topical study, the oxidized glutathione lotion also improved skin moisture and smoothness and reduced wrinkle formation [62]. Clinical evidence that oral glutathione prevents sun damage to the skin is limited [2].

### Cardiovascular Health

Oxidative stress and poor blood vessel lining (endothelial) function contribute to heart disease [2][5].

**Endothelial function:** The GlyNAC trials found better endothelial function in older adults [10][21]. In 16 people having heart catheterisation, NAC infused into the arteries improved the widening of coronary and leg arteries in response to acetylcholine, a test of endothelial function [65].

**Homocysteine reduction:** In a study of 40 healthy adults, oral NAC at 600 or 1,800 mg a day lowered plasma homocysteine, and the effect appeared larger at the higher dose [66].

**Contrast-induced nephropathy prevention:** NAC has been tested to prevent kidney injury from contrast dye. A meta-analysis of 22 trials with 2,746 patients found that 18 larger, later trials showed no benefit, while 4 early, small, lower-quality trials suggested a large benefit [67]. In the ACT trial of 2,308 patients, NAC 1,200 mg, taken twice before and twice after the procedure, did not prevent kidney injury (12.7% in both groups) [68].

### Fertility

**Male fertility:** In 120 men with unexplained infertility, NAC 600 mg a day for 3 months improved semen volume, motility and viscosity and antioxidant status compared with placebo, but not sperm count or shape [69]. A Cochrane review of antioxidants for male subfertility found they may increase live birth rates, based on low-quality evidence [70].

**Female fertility (IVF):** In a pilot trial in 60 women with PCOS having IVF with sperm injection, NAC at 1,800 mg, taken daily for 6 weeks, reduced immature and abnormal eggs and increased good-quality embryos compared with placebo [71].

### Psychiatric Conditions

NAC has been studied in several psychiatric conditions, possibly through effects on glutathione and on the brain chemical glutamate [72][76].

**Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD):** In people with OCD who had not responded to standard medicine, adding NAC up to 2,400 mg a day for 12 weeks improved symptom scores; 52.6% fully responded, against 15% on placebo [72].

**Depression:** A meta-analysis of five trials with 574 participants found NAC improved depressive symptoms compared with placebo (SMD 0.37, 95% CI 0.19 to 0.55) [73].

**Bipolar disorder:** In 75 people with bipolar disorder, NAC 1 g twice daily over 24 weeks, added to usual treatment, improved depression scores compared with placebo; the gains were lost after stopping [74].

**Schizophrenia:** A meta-analysis of seven trials (220 people on NAC, 220 on placebo) found NAC improved total and negative symptom scores and working memory after 24 weeks [75].

**Substance use disorders:** In 116 cannabis-dependent young people aged 15 to 21 years, NAC 1,200 mg twice daily for 8 weeks more than doubled the odds of a negative urine cannabis test compared with placebo (odds ratio 2.4, 95% CI 1.1 to 5.2) [76].

### Cancer

The relationship between glutathione and cancer is complex. Low glutathione leaves cells open to oxidative damage linked with cancer, while high glutathione in established tumours can help cancer cells resist chemotherapy [5].

**Prevention:** Broccoli sprouts contain compounds that switch on protective phase 2 enzymes, and sprout extracts reduced mammary tumours in rats [27]. No clinical trial has shown that glutathione supplements prevent cancer.

**Treatment resistance:** Tumour cells often raise glutathione and related enzymes, which helps them resist anticancer drugs [5].

**Chemotherapy side effects:** In a small study, NAC injected through the eardrum protected hearing in the treated ear during cisplatin chemotherapy [77].

**Important caution:** Because glutathione can protect cancer cells, do not take glutathione or NAC during active cancer treatment without your oncologist's approval [2][5].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### Direct Glutathione Supplementation

There is no Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for glutathione because the body makes it.

**Oral reduced glutathione:** The 6-month trial used 250 or 1,000 mg a day [12]. The higher dose raised levels more, levels rose after 1, 3 and 6 months, and they fell back after stopping [12].

**Liposomal glutathione:** A 1-month pilot used two doses, 500 and 1,000 mg a day [15].

**S-acetyl glutathione:** No dose has been established by controlled trials.

### NAC Dosing (as Glutathione Precursor)

Doses from the NAC trials in this article (all NAC, not glutathione):

- **Raising glutathione:** 600 mg/day raised airway glutathione [19]
- **Respiratory conditions (COPD):** 600 mg twice daily in the PANTHEON trial [44]
- **Psychiatric conditions:** up to 2,400 mg/day in OCD, 1 g twice daily in bipolar disorder [72][74]
- **Male fertility:** 600 mg/day [69]
- **Influenza-like illness in older adults:** 600 mg twice daily [40]

### GlyNAC Dosing

The GlyNAC trials used weight-based doses of glycine and NAC, taken daily for 16 to 24 weeks [10][21]. Brad's own plan is GlyNAC 1 g from the age of 45 (Brad's current guidance, 19 Feb 2026).

### Timing and Practical Considerations

- **Glutathione:** Trials gave it daily; one split it into two doses [61]
- **NAC:** See the NAC topic page for timing and taste
- **Duration:** Oral glutathione raised blood levels after 1, 3 and 6 months, with the largest rises at 6 months [12]

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Oral Glutathione

In trials lasting 4 to 12 weeks, oral glutathione was well tolerated [61][80]. In a 12-week trial in women, itching, red patches or spots on the skin and tiredness were reported, about as often as with placebo, and there were no serious side effects [80]. Two people had a rise in liver enzymes that promptly returned to normal once they stopped; the authors advise blood tests even for over-the-counter supplements [80].

Long-term safety has not been established [61].

### NAC Safety

Taken by mouth, NAC is generally safe and well tolerated, while the intravenous form has documented risks [20]. Side effects include:

- **Nausea:** In a lupus trial, 33% of people taking 4.8 g a day had nausea, which went away; 2.4 g a day was tolerated by all [42]
- **Adverse events overall:** In the PANTHEON trial, adverse events were about as common on NAC as on placebo (29% vs 26%) [44]
- **Reactions to intravenous NAC:** Given by drip for acetaminophen overdose, NAC can cause reactions that look like allergy, with rash, itching, swelling, wheeze and, rarely, low blood pressure; women and people with asthma or allergies are more susceptible [83]. Tell the staff at once if these start during an infusion.

**Upper limit:** No upper limit has been set for NAC. Most trials above used 600 to 2,400 mg a day.

### Intravenous Glutathione

IV glutathione carries risks that oral glutathione does not. Harms documented in the Philippines include [85]:

- Skin reactions, including potentially fatal Stevens-Johnson syndrome and toxic epidermal necrolysis
- Severe abdominal pain
- Thyroid and kidney problems
- Air embolism or potentially fatal sepsis from unsterile injection or counterfeit products

In the one trial of IV glutathione for skin lightening, liver problems were the main side effect reported, but reviewers doubt this finding because the liver problems were neither qualified nor quantified [85]. These harms followed off-label IV use for skin lightening, and the Philippine FDA has warned the public against it [60][85]. If a blistering or peeling rash appears after an injection, call your local emergency number (111 in NZ, 000 in Australia, 911 in the US, 999 in the UK) [85].

### Theoretical Cancer Concern

High glutathione helps some tumours resist chemotherapy [5]. This has not been shown with oral glutathione at supplement doses, but people having active cancer treatment should ask their oncologist before taking glutathione or NAC [2][5].

### Pregnancy and Lactation

Glutathione supplements have not been tested for safety in pregnancy or breastfeeding. Talk to your doctor before taking glutathione, NAC or GlyNAC if you are pregnant or breastfeeding.

### Kidney Disease

If you have advanced kidney disease, ask your doctor before taking glutathione or NAC.

## 6. Drug Interactions

### NAC-Drug Interactions

| Drug | Interaction | Clinical Significance |
|------|------------|----------------------|
| **Nitroglycerin** | In a trial of intravenous nitroglycerin with intravenous NAC, symptomatic low blood pressure occurred in seven people on the combination and none on nitroglycerin alone [81] | Ask your doctor before combining. |
| **Activated charcoal** | In ten volunteers, charcoal given with oral NAC did not significantly change NAC levels, though the authors advised against combining them until more studies are done [84] | Relevant only in hospital poisoning care. |
| **Chemotherapy agents** | High glutathione can help tumours resist treatment [5] | Avoid during active chemotherapy unless your oncologist approves [2][5]. |
| **Anticoagulants and antiplatelet drugs** | NAC reduced platelet clumping in laboratory tests [82] | Check with your doctor if you take a blood thinner. |

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

### Glutathione-Drug Interactions

Fewer interactions have been documented for oral glutathione than for NAC. The same caution about chemotherapy applies [2][5].

### Interactions with Other Supplements

- **Vitamin C:** Raised lymphocyte glutathione in people with low vitamin C [24].
- **Alpha-lipoic acid:** Restored glutathione synthesis in old rats [25].
- **Selenium:** Needed for the glutathione peroxidases [26]. People with adequate selenium should not take selenium supplements, as extra selenium may make type 2 diabetes more likely [26].

## 7. Dietary Sources

The body makes glutathione, so dietary intake is not essential. Food provides some glutathione directly and supplies the amino acids the body uses to make it.

### Foods Containing Glutathione

A study that measured glutathione in common US foods found that fruits and vegetables have moderate to high amounts, freshly prepared meats are relatively high, and dairy products, cereals and breads are generally low [78]. Frozen foods kept similar amounts to fresh foods, while other processing and preservation removed much of it [78].

### Foods Rich in Glutathione Precursors

Because cysteine supply limits glutathione synthesis, foods with cysteine and other sulfur-containing amino acids supply the raw material [2][9]:

- **High-cysteine protein sources:** Whey protein, eggs, chicken, turkey, pork, beef, fish
- **Sulfur-rich vegetables:** Broccoli, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, kale, cabbage, garlic, onions, shallots
- **Glycine-rich foods (collagen sources):** Bone broth, gelatin, skin-on chicken, pork rinds
- **Glutamic acid sources:** Tomatoes, mushrooms, parmesan cheese, soy sauce, fermented foods

### Practical Notes

- **Processing reduces glutathione:** Fresh and frozen foods keep more glutathione than other processed or preserved foods [78].
- **Alcohol depletes glutathione:** Alcohol reduces glutathione in liver mitochondria [6][35].
- **Cruciferous vegetables:** Broccoli and its sprouts contain sulforaphane precursors that switch on protective enzymes, including glutathione S-transferases [27][28].
- **A food-first approach** focuses on enough protein (for cysteine, glycine and glutamic acid) and plenty of fruit and vegetables.
