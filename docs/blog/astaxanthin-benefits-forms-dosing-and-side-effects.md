## Current view (reviewed 10 November 2026)

For skin, the evidence is a small change in moisture and one small sunburn result, from short trials mostly paid for by the companies selling astaxanthin, and Brad does not think that is worth paying for every month ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)). In a 2017 trial in Japan, 65 women took 6 mg of astaxanthin, 12 mg or a placebo for 16 weeks; skin got worse on placebo but not on astaxanthin, yet when the groups were compared with each other, none of the measurements differed ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)). When the small skin trials were pooled in 2021, about 300 people showed better moisture and elasticity but no significant change in wrinkles, and a 2025 review found too little evidence to recommend astaxanthin for sun-damaged skin ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)). In the US Interventions Testing Program, astaxanthin added to the diet from middle age made male mice live 12% longer at the median, but a 2026 test with about half as much astaxanthin in the food, started at 11 or 16 months, found no meaningful gain in males; the authors give two explanations: the first result may have been chance, or it may have depended on a particular dose and starting age ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)). Brad is also careful about taking too many antioxidants, because they can interfere with the response to exercise, though for a select group, antioxidants used correctly may improve exercise performance ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)). Brad does not take it; he would rather eat food sources of astaxanthin, such as salmon, trout, shrimp and crab ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)). He will look at it again if larger independent trials show a clear benefit (Brad's current guidance, 9 Oct 2026).

## Key safety points

- In lab and animal studies, astaxanthin reduced platelet activity and blood clotting; one found longer bleeding time in animals, another no excessive bleeding [41][42]. If you take warfarin, another anticoagulant or an antiplatelet medicine, check with your doctor first.
- In human liver cells in the lab, astaxanthin switched on enzymes that break down many medicines [53]. This has not been tested in people, but it may interact with some medicines, so ask your pharmacist.
- In mice, starting astaxanthin after a breast tumour was implanted led to the fastest tumour growth [29]. If you have cancer or are having cancer treatment, ask your oncologist first.
- In one trial in adults with type 2 diabetes, 8 mg a day lowered systolic blood pressure and fructosamine [39]. If you take blood pressure or diabetes medicines, tell your doctor.
- Children and teenagers can reach or go over the safe daily intake, so do not give them astaxanthin supplements unless a doctor advises it [35][36]. Safety in pregnancy, breastfeeding and liver or kidney disease has not been established; ask your doctor first.
- Astaxanthin blocked 5-alpha-reductase in a lab study [43]. If you take finasteride, dutasteride or hormone therapy, ask your doctor first.

## 1. Overview

Astaxanthin is a xanthophyll carotenoid made by the microalga *Haematococcus pluvialis*, where it reaches up to 3.8% of dry weight, and by other algae and the yeast *Phaffia rhodozyma* [2]. Salmon is the main food source, and krill oil also contains it [48]. Astaxanthin can also be made synthetically, and synthetic astaxanthin is chemically different from the natural form [5].

Earlier literature reports that astaxanthin is a stronger antioxidant than other carotenoids and vitamin E [37], but antioxidant strength alone does not show that it does anything in people.

Most human trials are small and short, and several were run or paid for by the companies that make the product [17][44][51]. The areas with the most human data are blood lipids, skin, exercise recovery and joint pain. In mice, astaxanthin extended median male lifespan in one Interventions Testing Program cohort [33] but not in a later one [34].

Astaxanthin supplements come as tablets, capsules, soft gels, oils, syrups, creams and powders [2].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Natural vs. Synthetic Astaxanthin

Natural and synthetic astaxanthin differ in their mix of stereoisomers, the mirror-image forms of the molecule [5][49].

**Natural astaxanthin** comes from algae such as *Haematococcus pluvialis* [2]. In supplements and krill oil it is mostly esterified, meaning bound to fatty acids, while salmon flesh holds the unesterified form [48].

**Synthetic astaxanthin** is chemically different from natural astaxanthin [5]. Farmed salmon carry a different isomer mix from wild salmon: men who ate wild salmon had mostly the (3S,3'S) form in their blood (80%), while those who ate farmed salmon had mostly the meso (3R,3'S) form (48%) [49].

### Bioavailability Comparison

In three middle-aged men given astaxanthin as fatty acid esters, no esters appeared in the blood; the esters were broken down during absorption [3]. In a lab model of digestion, more than 95% of the astaxanthin from supplements and krill oil was in the unesterified form by the end [48]. Both forms therefore appear to end up as free astaxanthin before they reach the blood.

In a lab digestion model, the astaxanthin in salmon, supplements and krill oil stayed relatively stable, with more than 80% recovered [48].

### Absorption Optimization

Fat helps the body absorb astaxanthin. When healthy men took one 40 mg dose, three lipid-based formulations gave 1.7 to 3.7 times the bioavailability of a standard supplement [7]. Taking astaxanthin after a meal rather than before it raised blood levels: the area under the curve was 7,219 versus 2,968 microg h/l [8]. Taking it with food that includes some fat may help.

**Mineral interactions:** In a lab digestion model, calcium, magnesium and zinc lowered the bioaccessibility of other carotenoids (lutein, lycopene and beta-carotene), by up to 100% in the case of calcium [10]. In a trial in 25 healthy men, though, 500 or 1000 mg of calcium carbonate taken with a spinach meal did not change carotenoid absorption [50]. Neither study tested astaxanthin.

### Safety of Synthetic Astaxanthin

A 2019 safety review noted that synthetic astaxanthin is chemically different from natural astaxanthin and has had few safety studies in humans or animals [5]. The authors recommended separate studies, including human trials, to set a safe intake for the synthetic form [5]. An acceptable daily intake of 2 mg once proposed by the European Food Safety Authority came from a rat study of synthetic astaxanthin [5]. In 2019 an EFSA panel replaced its 2014 limit of 0.034 mg per kg with 0.2 mg per kg of body weight a day [35][36]. In 2023 EFSA said this 0.2 mg per kg limit applies to both synthetic astaxanthin and astaxanthin from *Haematococcus pluvialis* [54].

## 3. Evidence for Health Benefits

### Exercise Performance and Recovery

Trials of astaxanthin for exercise have mixed results.

**Meta-analyses:** A 2026 meta-analysis of 24 trials found that astaxanthin lowered creatine kinase after exercise (SMD = -0.45, 95% CI: -0.83 to -0.07) but did not improve VO2max, time-trial performance or power output [46]. An earlier 2024 meta-analysis of 11 trials reported better physical performance when astaxanthin was combined with training, mainly at doses of 20 mg or more [62]. Astaxanthin may help markers of recovery, but the newer and larger analysis shows no gain in performance.

**Positive: cycling time trial (Earnest, 2011):** 21 competitive cyclists were randomized to 4 mg/d of astaxanthin or placebo for 28 d. Of the 14 who finished, the astaxanthin group improved its 20 km time trial by 121 s, against 19 s with placebo [20].

**Negative: cycling time trial (Res, 2013):** In 32 trained male cyclists or triathletes, 20 mg of astaxanthin per day for 4 wk did not improve time trial performance [21].

**Positive: muscle damage markers (Djordjevic, 2012):** In 32 elite young male footballers given astaxanthin or placebo for 90 days, the muscle enzymes creatine kinase and aspartate aminotransferase rose less after a 2 hour exercise bout in the astaxanthin group [22].

**Negative: resistance exercise (Bloomer, 2005):** In 20 resistance-trained men, 3 weeks of a daily algal extract that contained 4 mg of astaxanthin and 480 mg of lutein did not change muscle soreness, creatine kinase or muscle performance after eccentric exercise compared with placebo [23].

**Antioxidants and training adaptations:** A 2026 position stand from the International Society of Sports Nutrition says antioxidants may hinder training adaptations at very high doses [45]. It ranked astaxanthin (4-12 mg/day for 4-12 weeks) among the antioxidants with moderate- to high-quality evidence for recovery or performance without interfering with training adaptations [45]. One of its authors is an employee of AstaReal, which sells astaxanthin [45]. In a trial in people, vitamin C and E supplements hampered some cellular adaptations to endurance training [61].

### Triglycerides and HDL Cholesterol

Evidence in humans on blood lipids is mixed.

**Meta-analyses:** A 2020 meta-analysis of 14 trials found that astaxanthin raised HDL cholesterol slightly (WMD: 1.473 mg/dl, 95 % CI: 0.319-2.627) and had no effect on triglycerides, total cholesterol or LDL cholesterol [38]. A letter in the same journal argued that the analysis needed to be redone [38]. A 2022 meta-analysis of 7 trials in adults at risk of metabolic syndrome found a significant drop in LDL cholesterol and marginal effects on total cholesterol and systolic blood pressure [40].

**Blood sugar:** A 2026 meta-analysis of 9 trials in 403 people with prediabetes or type 2 diabetes found lower fasting blood sugar (WMD: -16.126 mg/dl), HbA1c, triglycerides, total and LDL cholesterol, and higher HDL cholesterol [55]. The 2020 meta-analysis, in a broader group, found no effect on fasting blood sugar or HbA1c [38].

**Yoshida et al., 2010:** A placebo-controlled trial randomized 61 non-obese adults aged 25 to 60 years, with fasting triglycerides of 120 to 200 mg/dl, to 0, 6, 12 or 18 mg/day of astaxanthin for 12 weeks. The 12 and 18 mg/day doses lowered triglycerides, the 6 and 12 mg doses raised HDL cholesterol, and LDL cholesterol did not change [13].

**Choi et al., 2011:** In 27 overweight adults in Korea given astaxanthin or placebo for 12 weeks, LDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein B fell from the start of treatment in the astaxanthin group, while no lipid measure changed with placebo [14]. The abstract reports only these changes within each group, not a difference between astaxanthin and placebo [14].

**Synthesis:** The lipid trials are small and their results disagree. Lipid changes are blood markers, and none of these trials measured heart attacks or strokes. The evidence does not support astaxanthin for managing cholesterol.

### Wrinkle Reduction

**Meta-analyses:** A 2021 meta-analysis of randomized trials found that oral astaxanthin improved skin moisture (SMD = 0.53; 95% CI = 0.05, 1.01) and elasticity (SMD = 0.77; 95% CI = 0.19, 1.35), with no significant change in wrinkle depth [44]. Its trials had 16 to 65 participants, and some were run or funded by companies [44]. A 2025 meta-analysis of supplements for sun-damaged skin found too little evidence to recommend astaxanthin [52]. The trials are small, so astaxanthin may help skin moisture and elasticity modestly at best. It is not a replacement for sunscreen. In an earlier view, Brad wrote that about 6 mg a day may improve skin hydration and elasticity, but that the trials were small, with unclear risks of bias ([Brad, 9 Apr 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-anti-aging-supplement-scam-new-evidence)); his October 2026 review judged the skin results too small to be worth paying for ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)).

**Tominaga et al., 2012:** In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in 36 healthy men, crow's feet wrinkles, skin elasticity and water loss through the skin improved after 6 mg of astaxanthin a day for 6 weeks [26]. The first author worked for Fuji Chemical Industry [26].

**Tominaga et al., 2017:** In 65 healthy women given 6 mg or 12 mg of astaxanthin or a placebo for 16 weeks, wrinkle measures and skin moisture worsened in the placebo group but did not change significantly in the astaxanthin groups [51]. The study ran from August to December, when UV and dryness worsen skin, and Fuji Chemical Industries sponsored it [51]. In a post hoc analysis of women with higher starting skin moisture, one elasticity measure (R2) was better on the high dose than on placebo at week 8 [51].

### Skin Protection and UV Defense

**Ito et al., 2018:** In a 10-week placebo-controlled trial in 23 healthy Japanese adults, 4 mg of astaxanthin a day raised the minimal erythema dose, the UV dose needed to redden the skin, after 9 weeks [25]. The astaxanthin group also lost less skin moisture in irradiated skin and rated rough skin and texture as improved [25]. Of the 22 people analysed, none had adverse events linked to astaxanthin [25]. The authors work for FUJIFILM, which sponsored and funded the study [25].

In cultured human skin cells, an algal astaxanthin extract and synthetic astaxanthin prevented DNA damage from UVA light [24].

### Memory and Thinking

Adults aged 45 to 64 took 8 mg of astaxanthin a day (28 people) or a placebo (26 people) for 8 weeks; blood astaxanthin rose, but memory and thinking test results did not differ between the groups, though a subgroup younger than 55 did better on one recall test [56]. Two of the authors worked for the company that made the extract [56]. In 96 middle-aged and older adults with age-related forgetfulness, 6 or 12 mg a day for 12 weeks improved some test scores, but the authors said the sample was too small to show a difference from placebo [57]. A 2020 review found too few studies to say whether astaxanthin helps thinking [58], and a 2024 meta-analysis found only a marginal effect on accuracy and none on reaction time [62].

### Male Fertility

A 2026 meta-analysis found that astaxanthin improved sperm measures in animals, but in its three human trials, no semen measure improved significantly compared with placebo [47]. In an earlier trial in 30 infertile men, 16 mg a day for 3 months improved sperm speed and partners' pregnancy rates, but only 11 men took astaxanthin and the authors said a larger trial was needed [60].

### Joint Health (Osteoarthritis)

**Hill et al., 2023 (combination product):** A 12-week randomized, double-blind trial in 100 people with mild knee or hip osteoarthritis tested FlexPro MD, a combination of krill oil, astaxanthin and hyaluronic acid, against placebo [17]. Among the 75 who completed the trial as planned, joint pain fell more with FlexPro MD than with placebo (20.8 vs 10.6 mm on a visual analogue scale) [17]. Side effects were reported by 16% of people on placebo and 4% on FlexPro MD [17]. A 2024 correction found no statistically significant difference between the groups in K-WOMAC pain scores at week 12, with the p-value corrected from 0.02635 to 0.2635; the K-WOMAC physical function score did differ [17]. NOVAREX, which made the capsules, funded the trial [17]. Two of the authors work for NOVAREX, and two for Valensa International, which supplied the FlexPro MD ingredients [17].

**Limitations:** The combination product design (astaxanthin + krill oil + hyaluronic acid) makes it impossible to determine which component(s) drove the benefit.

### Eye Health and Visual Acuity

**Sekikawa et al., 2023:** In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in 60 healthy adults, 9 mg/day of astaxanthin for 6 weeks better preserved corrected sight in the dominant eye after screen work in people aged 40 or older [27]. People younger than 40 saw no difference, and functional visual acuity and pupil constriction did not differ between groups [27]. BGG Japan sponsored the trial and paid all its costs, and two of the authors are its employees [27].

**Synthesis:** The eye evidence is thin. The one trial found a benefit only in the dominant eye of people aged 40 or older.

### Fertility in Women

In women with polycystic ovary syndrome having IVF, 8 mg a day for 40 days raised antioxidant genes in the cells around their eggs and improved egg and embryo quality scores, but pregnancy rates did not differ from placebo [59].

### Anti-Inflammatory Effects

**Carpal tunnel syndrome:** In a trial of 63 people with carpal tunnel syndrome, 4 mg of astaxanthin plus a wrist splint worked no better than placebo plus a splint [16].

In lab studies of brain immune cells, astaxanthin blocked the production of inflammatory signals, including COX-2 [15].

### Lifespan in Mice

In the US Interventions Testing Program, astaxanthin started at 12 months extended median male mouse lifespan by 12%; female lifespan was not significantly affected [33]. The target was 4,000 parts per million in the diet, but the diets held an average of 1,840 parts per million, 46% of the target [33]. The astaxanthin was a synthetic form in a water-dispersible beadlet from Cardax, a company run by two of the authors [33]. In a 2026 test, 880 parts per million started at 11 or 16 months did not extend lifespan [34]. In females, astaxanthin was linked to shorter lifespan when data from all three sites were pooled, but not when the site with unusually long-lived control females was left out [34].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### General Dosing Guidance

No best dose of astaxanthin has been set. Recommended or approved doses vary by country, between 2 and 24 mg a day [5]. Trials have used these doses:

| Indication | Dose Used in Studies | Duration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exercise performance / endurance | 4 to 20 mg/day | 3 weeks to 90 days | Mixed results; recovery markers improved more than performance [20][21][22][23][46] |
| Skin protection / UV defense | 4 mg/day | 9 weeks | One small trial [25] |
| Skin moisture, elasticity and wrinkles | 6 mg/day | 6 weeks | Wrinkles and elasticity improved in men [26] |
| Eye fatigue / visual acuity | 9 mg/day | 6 weeks | Dominant eye only, in people 40 or older [27] |
| Triglycerides and HDL | 6, 12 or 18 mg/day | 12 weeks | Results differ between trials [13][38] |
| Joint health (combination product) | Part of a capsule that also holds krill oil and hyaluronic acid | 12 weeks | Cannot isolate astaxanthin's contribution [17] |

### How to Take Astaxanthin

- **With fat:** Fat improves absorption, so take astaxanthin with or after a meal that includes some fat [7][8].
- **Mineral supplements:** Lab work suggests high doses of calcium, magnesium and zinc may reduce carotenoid absorption, but a human trial with calcium found no effect [10][50].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Cancer Therapy Considerations

If you are having cancer treatment, check with your oncologist before taking any antioxidant supplement, including astaxanthin.

In mice fed astaxanthin before breast tumour cells were implanted, tumours appeared later and grew less. Mice that started astaxanthin after the tumour cells were implanted had the fastest tumour growth [29]. The authors suggested astaxanthin may be contraindicated after a tumour has started [29]. This was in mice, not people.

### Long-Term Safety

Most trials lasted weeks to months, so long-term safety is not established. Safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding has not been studied; if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, check with your doctor before taking it. Safety in people with liver or kidney disease has not been established either; if you have either, ask your doctor first.

### General Safety Profile

A 2019 review of 87 human studies found no safety concerns with natural astaxanthin, 35 of them at doses of 12 mg a day or more [5].

The European Food Safety Authority concluded in 2020 that 8 mg a day from supplements, on top of astaxanthin from fish and shellfish, is safe for adults [35]. At that intake, adolescents aged 14 to under 18 years reach the acceptable daily intake, and younger children and infants go over it [35]. In 2025 it advised that children and adolescents should not take astaxanthin supplements on the same day as other foods with added astaxanthin [36].

In a trial in 35 healthy adults, 6 mg a day for 8 weeks caused no clinically important changes in blood pressure, blood chemistry or blood counts [37].

### Allergen Considerations

Most astaxanthin supplements are made from algae, yeast or synthesis [2][5], but krill oil, which also contains astaxanthin, comes from a crustacean [48]. People with a shellfish allergy should check whether a product contains krill, and anyone with an allergy should read the label.

### Hormonal Considerations (Gynecomastia Concern)

In a lab study, astaxanthin blocked 98% of 5-alpha-reductase activity at 300 microg/mL [43]. This enzyme turns testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT). Drugs that block it raise testosterone, and gynecomastia (breast enlargement in men) has been reported when the extra testosterone is converted to estradiol [28]. Astaxanthin and saw palmetto have also been reported to block aromatase, the enzyme that makes estradiol, in lab studies [28].

In an open-label study, 42 healthy men aged 37 to 70 years took 800 mg/day or 2000 mg/day of Alphastat, a product that combines astaxanthin with saw palmetto, for fourteen days [28]. Both groups had higher testosterone and lower DHT; estradiol did not rise, and fell at the higher dose (2,000 mg a day) [28]. There was no placebo group, and the result cannot be credited to astaxanthin alone. Gynecomastia is a theoretical concern here: no study on this page reported breast enlargement with astaxanthin, and in this human study estradiol fell rather than rose [28].

### Reported Side Effects

- **Trials:** In the FlexPro MD trial, side effects were reported by 16% of people on placebo and 4% on the combination, most often gut symptoms [17].
- **Red stools:** In a study of healthy adults given a single 48 mg dose, red-coloured stools were the only adverse event, and blood tests showed no abnormalities [8]. Red stools can be mistaken for blood, so if you notice them, tell your doctor you take astaxanthin.

## 6. Drug Interactions

Drug interactions with astaxanthin have not been established in human studies. If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

- **Anticoagulants/antiplatelets:** In rats and mice and in human platelets in the lab, astaxanthin reduced platelet clumping and blood clotting, without excessive bleeding in one thrombosis model [41]. In another study, astaxanthin by mouth lengthened tail bleeding time in animals [42]. Neither study was in people. If you take warfarin, another anticoagulant or an antiplatelet medicine such as aspirin or clopidogrel, check with your doctor first.
- **Medicines broken down by the liver:** In human liver cells in the lab, astaxanthin switched on CYP3A4 and CYP2B6, enzymes that break down many medicines [53]. This is lab evidence only and has not been tested in people. Astaxanthin may interact with some medicines; ask your pharmacist.
- **Cancer chemotherapy and radiation therapy:** See Cancer Therapy Considerations above [29]. Ask your oncologist before using astaxanthin.
- **Blood pressure and diabetes medicines:** In 44 adults with type 2 diabetes, 8 mg of astaxanthin a day for 8 weeks lowered systolic blood pressure and fructosamine [39]. A 2020 meta-analysis found no effect on blood pressure or blood glucose [38]. If you take these medicines, tell your doctor.
- **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (finasteride, dutasteride):** Astaxanthin blocked 5-alpha-reductase in a lab study [43]. Combining it with these drugs has not been studied.
- **Hormone therapies:** Because of these lab effects on testosterone pathways [28][43], ask your doctor before combining astaxanthin with testosterone therapy or other hormone treatments.
- **Mineral supplements:** High doses of calcium, magnesium and zinc reduced carotenoid uptake in a lab model, but not in a human calcium trial [10][50].

## 7. Dietary Sources

Salmon is the main food source of astaxanthin [48]. Brad notes that trout, shrimp and crab also contain it ([Brad, 24 Oct 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/the-sulforaphane-and-astaxanthin-story-is-changing)).

### Astaxanthin Content in Common Foods

| Food | Astaxanthin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wild salmon | About 10 times more than farmed salmon in one analysis [48] | Mostly the (3S,3'S) form [49] |
| Farmed salmon | Lower than wild salmon [48] | Mostly the meso form [49] |
| Krill oil | Present, mostly as esters [48] | Sold as a supplement |

### Effects of Cooking and Processing

In one lab study, common cooking styles cut the astaxanthin in wild salmon by 48-57% and in farmed salmon by 35-47% [48]. A 2025 study found that cooking did not change the main natural form of astaxanthin in salmon, but levels were lower in canned salmon [31].

### Practical Implications

Two servings of farmed salmon a week for five weeks, as part of a Mediterranean diet, raised blood levels of the main natural form of astaxanthin [31]. Salmon also provides the marine fats EPA and DHA, with more in wild than in farmed salmon [31].

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Is natural or synthetic astaxanthin better?**
Most human trials used natural astaxanthin from algae. Synthetic astaxanthin is chemically different and has had few safety studies, so a 2019 safety review called for separate trials of it [5].

**Can astaxanthin replace sunscreen?**
No. In one small trial, 4 mg a day modestly raised the UV dose needed to redden the skin [25]. That effect is much smaller and less reliable than sunscreen.

**Should I take astaxanthin for exercise performance?**
A 2026 meta-analysis found it lowered a muscle damage marker after exercise but did not improve performance [46].

**Does astaxanthin lower cholesterol?**
One trial found 12 and 18 mg/day lowered triglycerides and 6 and 12 mg raised HDL [13], but a 2020 meta-analysis found no effect on triglycerides or LDL [38]. The evidence does not support astaxanthin for cholesterol.

**Is astaxanthin safe long-term?**
Short-term trials found no safety concerns with natural astaxanthin [5]. Long-term safety is not established.

**Does astaxanthin affect testosterone?**
Astaxanthin blocked 5-alpha-reductase in a lab study [43]. A study of an astaxanthin and saw palmetto combination raised testosterone and lowered DHT, but it had no placebo group [28]. The effect of astaxanthin alone in people is unknown.
