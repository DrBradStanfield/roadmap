## Current view (reviewed 22 October 2026)

Brad sees compelling evidence that melatonin helps sleep: an umbrella review of twelve papers found a statistically significant improvement in how quickly people fall asleep ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/surprising-heart-results-from-this-huge-melatonin-study)). He calls the 2025 study that linked long-term melatonin use to an 89% higher risk of heart failure preliminary and flawed: it was an unreviewed conference abstract, people taking melatonin bought over the counter could have ended up in its comparison group, and it shows association, not causation ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/surprising-heart-results-from-this-huge-melatonin-study)). A 2026 meta-analysis of 63 randomized controlled trials found that melatonin lowered blood pressure, fasting glucose, LDL cholesterol and markers of inflammation, but most trials were short, so long-term effects remain unsettled ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/surprising-heart-results-from-this-huge-melatonin-study)). Because long-term safety data on high doses are lacking, Brad would not take more than 1 mg a night; he takes 0.8 mg of sustained-release melatonin, a fraction of the 3 to 5 mg in most over-the-counter products, though his taking it does not mean you need to ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/surprising-heart-results-from-this-huge-melatonin-study)). He suggests taking it about 2 to 4 hours before you want to fall asleep, because melatonin is a hormone that sets the body clock rather than a sedative, and buying from a reputable maker, because a 2023 study found gummies held 74% to 347% of the labelled amount ([Brad, 18 Mar 2026](https://drstanfield.com/blogs/articles/surprising-heart-results-from-this-huge-melatonin-study)). Melatonin may interact with SSRIs, sedatives, blood thinners and immunosuppressants, so talk to your doctor first if you take any of these (Brad's current guidance, 14 Oct 2026).

## Key safety points

- If you take a blood thinner, NIH advises using melatonin only under medical supervision, as it does for epilepsy [13]. A review linked melatonin to bleeding risk in people on anticoagulants [137].
- In 5 healthy men, the antidepressant fluvoxamine raised exposure to a melatonin dose 17-fold [126]. If you take fluvoxamine, check with your doctor or pharmacist before using melatonin.
- Taken with the sleeping pill zolpidem, melatonin made zolpidem's effects on thinking and driving tests worse [86]. Take care combining it with sedatives.
- In people with type 2 diabetes, melatonin increased swings in blood sugar, and the authors advise caution [111].
- In people whose blood pressure was controlled with nifedipine, evening melatonin raised blood pressure [110]. If you take blood pressure medicine, check with your doctor first.
- Safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding has not been studied. Talk to a doctor before giving melatonin to a child, and store it out of children's reach [13].
- Melatonin may stay active longer in older people and cause daytime drowsiness [13]. In older adults, a 3 mg dose affected balance an hour later [100].
- Reported side effects are usually mild: headache, dizziness, nausea and sleepiness [13].

## 1. Overview

Melatonin is a hormone that the brain makes in response to darkness, and it helps time the body's 24-hour clock and sleep [13]. The pineal gland secretes it [3], and it also synchronises clocks in organs such as the liver, gut and heart [2]. Several tissues besides the pineal gland make it too [27].

Beyond the sleep-wake cycle, melatonin acts as an antioxidant that neutralises free radicals [2]. It also acts on the immune system, as a stimulant at rest and as an anti-inflammatory when immune responses run high [6].

Light at night lowers melatonin [13]. In healthy young adults, room light before bedtime suppressed melatonin and shortened its nightly duration by about 90 min [8]. Beta-blockers suppress the body's night-time melatonin [10], and caffeinated coffee lowered the next night's output of melatonin's urinary breakdown product in a small study [11]. In a randomised trial of adults aged 60 or older, cataract surgery raised melatonin output 3 months later; clear and yellow lens implants did not differ significantly [12].

Most melatonin supplements are made synthetically, though some come from animals or microorganisms [13]. In the United States melatonin is sold as a dietary supplement and regulated less strictly than a drug; in several other countries it is a prescription medicine [13].

Supplements seem to leave natural secretion intact. In night shift workers, 0.5 mg at bedtime for seven days left the amplitude of their own melatonin rhythm unchanged [14]. In an open-label study, 244 adults with primary insomnia took a 2 mg prolonged-release tablet nightly for 6 to 12 months; stopping brought no rebound insomnia or withdrawal symptoms, and their pineal output was not suppressed [15].

## 2. Forms and Bioavailability

### Oral Formulations

Melatonin is sold as tablets, capsules, liquids and gummies [13]. Surge-sustained products combine an immediate-release dose with a controlled-release dose to mimic the body's own overnight profile [101].

### Absorption and First-Pass Metabolism

The liver breaks melatonin down almost entirely through the enzyme CYP1A2 [32]. In healthy volunteers, about 15% of 2 mg and 4 mg oral tablets reached the bloodstream [18]; in another small study of a 10 mg oral dose, bioavailability was only 3% [19]. A systematic review of 22 studies found oral bioavailability ranged from 9 to 33%, averaging about 15%, with a peak about 50 min after an immediate-release dose and a half-life of 45 min [127]. Age, caffeine, smoking, oral contraceptives, food and fluvoxamine all changed how the body handled melatonin [127].

### Synthetic vs. Plant-Derived Melatonin

Plant-based melatonin ("phytomelatonin") comes from sources such as alfalfa, chlorella and rice [20]. In laboratory tests, a phytomelatonin complex inhibited the inflammatory enzyme COX-2 about 6.5 times more strongly than synthetic melatonin, which the authors put down to other plant compounds in the extract [20]. These were enzyme and cell tests, not studies in people.

### Topical Melatonin

In a crossover trial in 63 undergraduates, a melatonin sleep lotion raised salivary melatonin up to about 1,000-fold compared with a placebo lotion. Sleep quality improved in a subgroup of 18 poor sleepers but not in the group as a whole [21]. The authors warn that the undisclosed dose in such lotions can be high and well absorbed through the skin [21].

## 3. Evidence for Benefits

### Insomnia and General Sleep Improvement in Adults

The evidence for melatonin in chronic insomnia is modest. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine suggests clinicians not use melatonin for trouble falling or staying asleep, a weak recommendation [22], and the American College of Physicians strongly recommends cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) as the first treatment [13]. An umbrella review found that, across twelve papers on melatonin, it gave statistically significant improvements in time to fall asleep and total sleep time, but reviewers disagreed on whether these gains are clinically meaningful, and the papers ranged from moderate to critically low in quality [134].

Trials in specific groups give mixed results:

**Primary insomnia:** In 97 middle-aged adults with primary insomnia, 3 mg of fast-release melatonin 1 hour before bedtime for 4 weeks cut early-morning waking by 30.63 min and lowered the share of N2 sleep, measured overnight. It did not change time to fall asleep, sleep efficiency, waking during the night or self-rated sleep [23].

**REM sleep behavior disorder:** In a 4-week pilot trial of 30 people, 2 mg or 6 mg of prolonged-release melatonin 30 min before bedtime did not improve symptoms or sleep quality compared with placebo [25].

**Parkinson's disease:** In a trial of 18 people with Parkinson's disease, 3 mg of melatonin 1 hour before bedtime for four weeks improved self-rated sleep quality, but overnight sleep recordings and motor symptoms did not change [28]. A review suggests melatonin may help non-motor symptoms such as sleep in Parkinson's disease [27].

**Tinnitus:** In a trial of 120 people, tinnitus perception fell by 40% with 3 mg of melatonin daily for 1 month, by 81% with melatonin plus the drug sulpiride, and by 22% with placebo [29].

**Glaucoma:** In a crossover trial, 64 adults with advanced glaucoma took 5 mg a day or placebo for 30 days each; neither questionnaire scores nor wrist-monitor sleep measures differed between the two phases [31].

**Melatonin tolerance:** In three patients with intellectual disability whose good response faded within weeks, melatonin levels stayed high 2 and 4 hours after a dose. Sleep problems went away once the dose was cut considerably. The authors linked this to slow breakdown by CYP1A2 [32].

### Delayed Sleep Phase Syndrome (DSPS)

People with DSPS (also called delayed sleep-wake phase disorder) find it hard to get to sleep at usual times or to wake in the morning, often not sleeping before 2 to 6 a.m. [13]. In 2015, the American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommended melatonin for it, though the recommendation was weak and it was uncertain whether benefits outweigh harms [13].

In a trial of 116 people with DSPS, 0.5 mg of fast-release melatonin 1 hour before the desired bedtime for 4 weeks, combined with a set bedtime in both groups, brought sleep onset 34 min earlier than placebo. Sleep disturbance, insomnia severity and functional disability also fell. In a subset, the timing of the body's own melatonin did not differ between groups after treatment [33].

A 2016 review of 2 studies (52 people) found melatonin cut the time to fall asleep by about 22 minutes in DSPS [13].

### Jet Lag

Four studies of 142 travelers showed melatonin may reduce overall jet lag symptoms after eastward flights, and a study of 234 travelers found low-quality evidence of better sleep quality. Two studies of 90 travelers showed benefit after westward flights [13].

### Shift Work

Evidence is small and inconclusive [13]. A Cochrane review found low-quality evidence that 1 to 10 mg of melatonin after a night shift may lengthen daytime sleep by about 24 minutes (seven trials, 263 participants), with no change in time to fall asleep [35]. Another review of 8 studies (300 participants) was inconclusive and made no recommendations [13].

In a pilot crossover trial in 25 doctors and nurses, 6 mg of slow-release melatonin before sleep after each night shift improved a concentration task, with negligible side effects, though melatonin levels stayed raised for longer [36]. In 27 overweight female nurses, 3 mg of melatonin on nights they slept, for 12 weeks, reduced circadian misalignment by about 20% and, in early chronotypes, reduced body weight, BMI, and waist and hip circumference [37].

### Sleep in Children and Adolescents

NIH advises parents to speak with a health care provider before giving a child melatonin, because much is unknown about the right dose, long-term effects and whether benefits outweigh risks [13].

A 2019 review of 18 studies (n=1,021 children) found melatonin was better than placebo for time to fall asleep and total sleep. Results by condition [13]:
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD):** Fell asleep 37 minutes earlier, slept 48 minutes longer
- **ADHD:** Fell asleep 20 minutes earlier, slept 33 minutes longer
- **Atopic dermatitis:** Fell asleep 6.8 minutes earlier, slept 35 minutes longer
- **Chronic sleep-onset insomnia:** Fell asleep 24 minutes earlier, slept 25 minutes longer

**Children with late sleep onset:** In 84 children (mean age 10.0 years) whose own melatonin rose late in the evening, a 3- to 4-week course shortened sleep latency and lengthened sleep, but waking after sleep onset increased [39].

**Children with ASD:** In a 13-week trial of 125 children and adolescents with ASD or Smith-Magenis syndrome, prolonged-release melatonin (2 mg, raised to 5 mg) lengthened sleep by 32.43 minutes and shortened sleep latency by 25.30 minutes compared with placebo; somnolence was more common with melatonin [40]. In a follow-up of 80 participants given 2 mg, 5 mg or 10 mg nightly for up to 104 weeks, fatigue (6.3%), somnolence (6.3%) and mood swings (4.2%) led the list of treatment-related adverse events, and growth and puberty stayed within normal ranges [41].

In children with ASD given 1 mg (n = 65), 4 mg (n = 65) or placebo (n = 66) before bedtime, sleep onset latency shortened by 22.0, 28.0 and 5.0 min respectively [42].

In 62 children with ASD, melatonin, cycling and the two combined each improved sleep efficiency, sleep onset latency and sleep duration, with no difference between them [43].

**Children with ADHD:** In 105 children with ADHD and chronic sleep onset insomnia, 3 or 6 mg of melatonin for 4 weeks brought sleep onset forward by 26.9 min (placebo delayed it by 10.5 min) and lengthened total sleep, but did not change behavior, cognition or quality of life [98]. A review of 19 trials found melatonin helped children fall asleep (median 28 minutes sooner) and sleep longer (median 33 minutes), with the largest gains in autism and other neurodevelopmental disorders [44].

### Perioperative Use (Anxiety, Sedation, and Recovery)

**Preoperative anxiety:** A Cochrane review of 27 trials (2,319 participants) found melatonin probably reduces anxiety before surgery compared with placebo, and may work about as well as benzodiazepines. Some studies reported nausea, sleepiness, dizziness and headache, with no serious adverse events [45]. An NIH-cited 2015 review of 12 studies (n=774) found strong evidence that melatonin reduces anxiety before surgery, possibly as well as midazolam [13].

**ICU sleep quality:** In 203 ICU patients, 10 mg on up to seven nights in a row raised sleep quality scores. Very good sleep was reported by 45.8% versus 34.4% with placebo (risk ratio 1.33, 95% CI 0.94 to 1.89), and very poor sleep by 3.1% versus 14.6% (risk ratio 0.21, 95% CI 0.06 to 0.71). Melatonin did not reduce sedative or pain medicine use, pain, anxiety or delirium [47].

**After surgery:** In 80 patients after rotator cuff repair, 5 mg of melatonin 1 hour before bed for 6 weeks improved sleep quality at 6 weeks compared with sleep-hygiene advice alone, though one shoulder score (SANE) was worse at 6 weeks, and there was no placebo group [48]. Shoulder function was better and narcotic use lower months later [48].

**Post-operative delirium:** In 210 adults having heart surgery, a 7-day course of 3 mg nightly, begun 2 days beforehand, left delirium unchanged, at 21.4% versus 20.2% with placebo, and did not shorten it or make it milder [51].

**Post-stroke delirium:** In a propensity-matched (non-randomised) comparison, 2 mg of melatonin at night started within 24 hours of an ischemic stroke was linked to less delirium: 25.6% versus 36.6% (odds ratio 0.597, 95% CI 0.372 to 0.958) [52].

### Headache and Migraine

**Migraine:** In a placebo-controlled trial of 178 people, 3 mg of melatonin for 3 months cut migraine days by 2.7 a month, against 2.2 with 25 mg of amitriptyline and 1.1 with placebo [53]. Melatonin was better tolerated than amitriptyline, and people taking it lost weight while amitriptyline users gained [53].

### Menopause and Women's Health

**Postmenopause -- symptom relief:** In 60 postmenopausal women aged 51 to 64, a year of melatonin, 3 mg each morning plus 5 mg at night, lowered the Kupperman Index of climacteric symptoms from 29.1 to 19.7 points, against 28.4 to 25.6 with placebo [57].

**Climacteric -- sleep quality at low dose:** In 46 nurses working fixed shifts, 0.3 mg of melatonin on nights off reduced climacteric symptoms by 15.8% compared with placebo and improved sleep quality on days off by 35.33%. It did not change total sleep time or reproductive hormones [58].

**Nocturia:** In 60 women aged over 55 years with nocturia, 2 mg of melatonin a day for 2 weeks reduced night-time urination by a median of 1.0 episode a night (0.0 with placebo) and lengthened the first stretch of unbroken sleep by a median of 1.0 h [59].

**Bone density in postmenopausal women:** In 81 postmenopausal women with osteopenia, a year of nightly melatonin (1 mg or 3 mg) raised femoral neck bone density by 2.3% at 3 mg compared with placebo. At 3 mg, trabecular thickness in the tibia rose by 2.2% and volumetric spine density by 3.6%, and 24-hour urinary calcium fell by 12.2% [60]. An observational study has linked prescribed melatonin with fractures (see Safety section).

**Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS):** In 56 women with PCOS, taking 5 mg twice a day for 12 weeks lowered hirsutism scores, total testosterone, hs-CRP and malondialdehyde compared with placebo [61].

**Endometriosis pain:** Mixed results. In 40 women with endometriosis, 10 mg of melatonin daily for 8 weeks cut daily pain scores by 39.80% and menstrual pain by 38.01% compared with placebo [63]. In a later trial, 40 women with endometriosis took 20 mg at bedtime or placebo for two menstrual cycles, and pain did not differ significantly, statistically or clinically [62].

### Cancer Treatment Support

Studies of melatonin for cancer symptoms or treatment side effects have been small, with mixed results, and NIH advises people with cancer to talk with their care team before using it [13].

**Breast cancer:** In 54 women randomised after breast cancer surgery, 6 mg of melatonin for 3 months raised sleep efficiency by 4.28% and total sleep period by 37.0 min compared with placebo, without affecting cognitive function; dropouts were 1 with melatonin and 10 with placebo [66]. In 79 women with early breast cancer, 20 mg of melatonin during radiotherapy did not prevent fatigue or improve other symptoms, and the trial stopped early [67].

### Multiple Sclerosis

Mixed results. In 30 people with relapsing MS who took either 3 mg or 5 mg a day for 12 months (no placebo group), fatigue, MS impact and sleep quality scores did not change [69]. In a placebo-controlled trial of 27 people with MS, 3 mg a night for 12 weeks improved sleep quality and total sleep time as well as heart rate variability and oxidative stress markers [70]. In a pilot study of 14 adults with relapsing-remitting MS, a single 6 mg night-time dose improved next-morning balance and raised sleep questionnaire scores by 32.21% compared with placebo [71].

### Gastrointestinal Conditions

**Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS):** Several small studies suggest 3 mg of melatonin at bedtime may ease IBS symptoms without improving sleep. In a crossover trial in 17 women with IBS, symptom scores improved further on melatonin than on placebo, 88% versus 47% responded, and sleep scores did not change [72]. In 18 patients, overall IBS scores improved by 45% versus 16.66% and quality of life by 43.63% versus 14.64% [73]. In 40 patients with sleep problems, melatonin lowered mean abdominal pain score more than placebo over two weeks (2.35 v 0.70), while sleep did not change [74].

**Gastric ulcers:** In people with ulcers and H. pylori infection, all ulcers had healed by day 21 when 5 mg of melatonin twice daily was added to omeprazole, against 3 of 7 with omeprazole plus placebo in each ulcer group [77].

**GERD:** Preliminary studies suggest melatonin may ease GERD symptoms alongside a proton-pump inhibitor. In 72 people who completed a trial, 3 mg of sublingual melatonin plus omeprazole reduced heartburn, epigastric pain and symptom scores more than omeprazole plus placebo [78]. In a single-blind trial, every one of 176 people given a combined supplement (melatonin plus L-tryptophan, vitamin B6, folic acid, vitamin B12, methionine and betaine) had their symptoms regress after 40 days, against 65.7% of those taking omeprazole; the result cannot be credited to melatonin alone [79].

### Skin Conditions

**Atopic dermatitis:** In a crossover trial in 48 children and adolescents, 3 mg of melatonin a day for 4 weeks lowered the SCORAD severity index by 9.1 points and shortened sleep-onset latency by 21.4 minutes compared with placebo, with no adverse events [82].

### Pain Conditions

**Neuropathic pain:** In a crossover trial of 30 adults with nerve pain, melatonin at the highest tolerated dose (mean 11.9 mg a day) for 4 weeks left mean daily pain no lower than on placebo (4.1 versus 4.2) [85].

### Beta-Blocker-Induced Sleep Disturbance

Beta-blockers suppress the body's night-time melatonin. In 16 people taking atenolol or metoprolol for high blood pressure, 2.5 mg nightly for three weeks added 36 min of sleep and cut the time to reach stage 2 sleep by 14 min compared with placebo, with no rebound on stopping [10].

### Cognitive Function and Dementia

In healthy adults aged 55 and older, a 2 mg prolonged-release dose did not impair thinking, memory or driving tests [86]. In a meta-analysis including 9 Alzheimer's trials, those with the disease treated for more than 12 weeks improved their MMSE scores (mean difference 1.82), including in mild disease, while daytime melatonin lowered accuracy on some tasks in healthy people [87]. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends against melatonin for people with dementia [13].

### Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)

In US health records of 121,523 people aged 50 or older, melatonin use was linked to a lower risk of developing AMD (RR 0.42, 95% CI 0.28 to 0.62) and, in people who already had dry AMD, of progression to wet AMD (RR 0.44, 95% CI 0.34 to 0.56) [89]. This is an observational association and does not prove causation. In a study without a placebo group, 55 people with AMD given 3 mg nightly were followed for more than 6 months, and visual acuity stayed stable [90].

### COVID-19

NIH considers it too soon to say whether melatonin helps prevent or treat COVID-19 [13]. In a pilot trial of 98 adults with mild to moderate COVID-19, 10 mg of melatonin daily for 14 days led to faster symptom resolution and quality-of-life recovery than placebo, though the authors were unsure the benefit was clinically meaningful [92].

## 4. Recommended Dosing

### General Principles

Especially at doses above what the body makes, there is too little information to judge melatonin's overall safety, and long-term data are lacking [13]. In older adults, even a 0.4 mg surge-sustained dose produced peak levels well above normal levels for their age [101]. Brad points out that the body makes about 10 to 80 micrograms a night and only about 15% of an oral dose is absorbed, so 5 mg delivers 10 times what the body makes or more; he would not take more than 1 mg a night [135].

| IBS symptoms | 3 mg [74] | Bedtime | 2 weeks |
| Beta-blocker sleep disturbance | 2.5 mg [10] | Nightly | 3 weeks |

### How and When to Take

Brad suggests taking melatonin about 2 to 4 hours before you want to fall asleep, because it is a hormone that shifts the body clock over hours rather than a sedative [135]. In the delayed sleep phase trial, people took it 1 hour before their desired bedtime [33].

## 5. Safety and Side Effects

### Common Side Effects

Short-term use appears safe for most people, but long-term safety is unclear [13]. A 2015 review found only mild side effects in short-term studies of adults, surgical patients and critically ill patients, including headache, dizziness, nausea and sleepiness [13]. In children, reported side effects have usually been mild: drowsiness, more bedwetting or evening urination, headache, dizziness and agitation [13]. There may be a risk of allergic reactions [13]. A single case report linked melatonin to Crohn's disease symptoms [99].

### Balance and Driving

Melatonin may stay active longer in older people and cause daytime drowsiness [13]. In 34 older adults, a single 3 mg dose increased body sway one hour later, though it did not affect a thinking task [100]. Until you know how it affects you, avoid driving or using machinery soon after taking it.

Higher-dose sustained products may keep melatonin raised beyond the usual sleep period. In older adults, a 4.0 mg surge-sustained dose kept levels above 50 pg/mL for an average of 10 hours [101].

### Interactions with Sleep Medications

In healthy adults aged 55 and older, a 2 mg prolonged-release dose taken with 10 mg of zolpidem (Ambien) worsened zolpidem's impairment of thinking and driving tests 1 and 4 hours later; the authors point to an interaction between melatonin and drugs of this class (GABA-A modulators) [86]. In a trial of 34 people taking benzodiazepines, 14 of 18 given 2 mg of controlled-release melatonin stopped their benzodiazepine, against 4 of 16 given placebo [103].

### Fracture Risk

In UK primary care records, people aged 45 and older prescribed melatonin had a higher fracture risk than matched controls (hazard ratio 1.90, 95% CI 1.41 to 2.57), still raised after adjustment (hazard ratio 1.44, 95% CI 1.01 to 2.04). Only those with three or more prescriptions had raised risk [105]. This is an observational association.

### Cardiovascular Concerns

An analysis of 5 years of health records from over 130,000 adults with insomnia, presented at the American Heart Association meeting in 2025, linked a year or more of prescribed melatonin to heart failure (HR 1.89, 95% CI 1.78 to 2.00), heart failure hospitalisation (HR 3.44, 95% CI 3.32 to 3.56) and death from any cause (HR 2.09, 95% CI 1.99 to 2.18) [107][136]. It was a conference abstract, not a peer-reviewed paper, and it was observational. Brad notes that people who bought melatonin over the counter could have landed in the comparison group, and that insomnia itself is linked to heart disease [135]. A 2026 meta-analysis of 63 randomized controlled trials with over 3,000 participants found melatonin reduced blood pressure, fasting blood glucose, LDL cholesterol and markers of inflammation, though most trials lasted only weeks [135].

A report of two patients with structurally normal hearts described frequent premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) while on melatonin that stopped after melatonin was discontinued [108].

### Blood Pressure Effects

In healthy men, 1 mg of melatonin lowered blood pressure within 90 minutes [109]. In 47 people whose high blood pressure was controlled with nifedipine GITS, a 5 mg evening dose for 4 weeks pushed 24-hour systolic pressure up 6.5 mmHg and diastolic up 4.9 mmHg [110]. If you take blood pressure medicine, check with your doctor first.

### Blood Sugar and Diabetes

Blood sugar control may suffer. In a crossover trial, 30 patients with type 2 diabetes took 3 mg nightly for 7 days; glucose swings around breakfast grew compared with placebo, and the authors advise caution [111]. In 21 healthy women, a 5 mg dose before a glucose drink worsened glucose tolerance, mainly through lower insulin release at 9 AM and lower insulin sensitivity at 9 PM [112]. If you have diabetes or take diabetes medicines, talk to your doctor first.

### Hormonal Effects

Because melatonin is a hormone, it is possible that supplements could affect hormonal development in children, including puberty, menstrual cycles and prolactin, but this is uncertain [13]. In 29 people given 6 mg of melatonin, blood levels were 4- to 5-fold higher in oral contraceptive users, because the pill slows melatonin's breakdown by CYP1A2 [113].

### Restless Legs Syndrome

Leg movements may get worse. In 8 people with RLS, melatonin increased periodic leg movements on a standard immobilization test [115].

### Aggression

In 63 healthy men, those given melatonin chose high punishments for an opponent more often than those given placebo in a laboratory aggression task [116].

### Mental Health

In a 1976 crossover study in six people with moderate to severe depression and two with Huntington's chorea, melatonin given in varying doses worsened low mood and caused loss of sleep and weight [117].

### Seizures

Evidence is limited. A 1998 report described pro-convulsant effects of oral melatonin in children with neurological disabilities [120]. NIH advises people with epilepsy to take melatonin only under medical supervision [13].

### Asthma

In 22 women with mild to moderate asthma, a 4-week course of 3 mg improved sleep quality without changing asthma symptoms, reliever use or peak flow; the authors say longer studies are needed before melatonin can be recommended in asthma [121].

### Pregnancy and Lactation

Safety in pregnant or breastfeeding women has not been studied [13]. In a trial of 189 women having labor induced, 10 mg of melatonin every 6 hours (up to four doses) did not reduce cesarean births, and more babies in the melatonin group needed special care nursery admission, mainly to monitor for low blood sugar (18.5% vs. 8.1%) [123].

### Children -- Poison Control Concerns

Reports to US poison control centers of melatonin ingestion by people 19 and younger rose 530% from 2012 to 2021; five children needed mechanical ventilation and two died [124]. Most of those who took melatonin (82.8%) had no symptoms, and the data did not show whether the two deaths were caused by melatonin [13]. NIH advises keeping melatonin out of children's reach and sight [13].

### Label Accuracy Concerns

A 2023 study found 22 of 25 melatonin gummy products were inaccurately labeled, with melatonin ranging from 74% to 347% of the labeled amount; most held 1.2 to 1.7 times more than stated [13]. In an earlier study, 26% of supplements contained serotonin, a hormone that can have harmful effects at relatively low levels [13]. To check independent testing of a product, you can look it up at consumerlab.com or labdoor.com.

## 6. Drug Interactions

If you take prescription medicines, check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting.

| Medication | Interaction | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| **Blood thinners (such as warfarin)** | NIH advises medical supervision [13]; linked to bleeding risk in people on anticoagulants [137] | In healthy men, a single 3 mg dose blunted a rise in the clotting marker D-dimer after stress [128] |

## 7. Dietary Sources

Melatonin occurs naturally in many foods, but in tiny amounts compared with supplements. In 8,320 university graduates, mean dietary melatonin intake was 25554.7 ng a day [130].

### Food Sources

Among animal foods, eggs and fish contain more melatonin; among plant foods, nuts contain the most, and some mushrooms, cereals and germinated legumes or seeds are also sources [132]. Blood melatonin rises after eating melatonin-containing food [132].
