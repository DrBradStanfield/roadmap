## Part 1: Jeff Nippard -- Key Recommendations & Cited Studies

### Source: "How To Train Like A Minimalist (More Gains In Less Time)"

#### Training Recommendations from Transcript

**Volume (sets per muscle per week):**
- ~10 sets/muscle/week is a good target for most people; more advanced trainees may benefit from up to 20 sets for some body parts
- But 1-4 sets/muscle/week still yields ~64% of maximum gains (from Schoenfeld et al. 2017 meta-analysis of 15 studies)
- 5-9 sets/muscle/week yields ~84% of maximum gains
- The dose-response curve has steep initial gains that flatten quickly -- diminishing returns above ~10 sets

**Strength:**
- Just 1 heavy set done 1-3x/week is enough for significant strength gains
- 2017 meta-analysis: 81% of strength gains came from just 1-4 sets per exercise per week
- Most important factor for strength is exposure to heavy loads (1-5 rep zone)

**Health / Mortality:**
- Cites 2022 systematic review (16 studies on mortality, CVD, cancer, diabetes): just 30-60 min/week of lifting was enough for "maximum risk reduction"
- Maximum health benefits achievable with 0.5-1 hr of lifting per week

**Frequency:**
- Split those ~10 sets across at least 2 workouts per muscle per week
- Frequency itself is secondary to total volume -- when volume is equated, frequency matters less

**Intensity (effort level):**
- Lower volume must be compensated with higher intensity
- On minimalist routines: most sets should go to failure (RPE 10) or within 1 rep of failure (RPE 9)

**Minimalist strategies:**
1. Push harder (higher RPE to compensate for lower volume)
2. Use drop sets to increase effective volume without adding time
3. Use "sensible supersets" (antagonist muscles, not same-muscle supersets -- citing 2020 study showing same-muscle supersets decrease performance)
4. Gradually reduce rest periods over time (start ~2 min, reduce by 15s/week to ~1 min)
5. Prioritize machine exercises to reduce warm-up set requirements
6. General warm-up: 3-5 min treadmill + dynamic stretches

**Maintenance:**
- Bickel et al. study: subjects maintained muscle mass for 8 months on 1/9 of baseline volume

**Researchers/sources cited in transcript:**
- Brad Schoenfeld (textbook: Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy; drop sets)
- 2017 meta-analysis (Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger -- volume dose-response)
- 2022 systematic review (likely Momma et al. or Shailendra et al. -- mortality/health)
- 2020 study on supersets (same-muscle vs antagonist)
- Bickel et al. (maintenance volume study)
- Dorian Yates, Jeff Alberts, Martin Berkhan (real-world minimalist examples)

#### Other Jeff Nippard Content (from web research, no transcript obtained)

**"How Many Sets Do You Need" (blog/video):**
- Beginner (<12 months): start low
- Intermediate (1-4 years): 8-15 sets/muscle/week optimal
- Advanced (4+ years): 12-20 sets/muscle/week for continued progress
- For maximizing natural potential: 15-25 sets/muscle/week may be beneficial

**"Junk Volume" (blog/video, Sep 2023):**
- Junk volume = training that takes time/energy but produces no muscle or strength benefit
- Three categories of junk volume (excessive volume being most common)
- Quality > quantity; each set must be sufficiently stimulating

**100-Day Low Volume Experiment (2025):**
- Dropped from usual 3-4 sets/exercise to just 1-2 sets per exercise, all to absolute failure
- Training split: upper/lower/rest/upper/lower/arms+delts/rest
- Average volume: ~6 sets/muscle/week (well below typical 10-20 recommendation)
- Results: maintained strength and muscle, improved body composition, hit new PRs on several lifts
- Key compensator: ultra-high intensity (every set to complete muscular failure)
- Conclusion: volume can be cut dramatically if intensity is maximized

**"The New Science of Muscle" (Men's Health, Jan/Feb 2024):**
- Featured alongside Mike Israetel PhD and JP Gallardo
- Volume guideline: 10-12 hard sets per muscle group weekly
- Rest periods: 2-3 minutes between sets (longer than conventional 30s-1min)
- Eccentric phase: 2-3 seconds lowering
- Incorporate machines 1-2x/week for safer failure training

## Part 2: Brad Schoenfeld -- Key Publications

### 2A. Foundational Mechanism Paper

**Schoenfeld BJ.** The mechanisms of muscle hypertrophy and their application to resistance training. *J Strength Cond Res.* 2010;24(10):2857-2872. DOI: [10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181e840f3](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181e840f3). PMID: 20847704.

**Key findings:**
- Three primary pathways of exercise-induced muscle growth: (1) mechanical tension, (2) muscle damage, (3) metabolic stress
- Bodybuilders use moderate loads + short rest (high metabolic stress); powerlifters use high loads + long rest (high mechanical tension) -- both produce impressive muscularity
- Many factors mediate hypertrophy; multiple effective pathways exist rather than a single optimal method

---

### 2B. Volume Dose-Response (Landmark Meta-Analysis)

**Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW.** Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *J Sports Sci.* 2017;35(11):1073-1082. DOI: [10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197). PMID: 27433992.

**Key findings:**
- 34 treatment groups from 15 studies
- Each additional set associated with ES increase of 0.023 (percentage gain increase of 0.37%)
- ES difference between higher vs lower volumes: 0.241 (3.9% percentage gain difference)
- **Conclusion: Graded dose-response relationship -- more volume = more hypertrophy**

---

### 2C. Volume Enhances Hypertrophy (RCT)

**Schoenfeld BJ, Contreras B, Krieger J, Grgic J, Delcastillo K, Belliard R, Alto A.** Resistance training volume enhances muscle hypertrophy but not strength in trained men. *Med Sci Sports Exerc.* 2019;51(1):94-103. DOI: [10.1249/MSS.0000000000001764](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000001764). PMID: 30153194.

**Key findings:**
- 34 resistance-trained men randomized to 1, 3, or 5 sets per exercise (3 sessions/week, 8 weeks)
- All groups gained similar strength (no significant between-group differences)
- Higher-volume groups showed significantly greater hypertrophy in elbow flexors, mid-thigh, lateral thigh
- **Muscle hypertrophy follows a dose-response; strength gains require only minimal volume**

---

### 2D. Training Frequency for Hypertrophy

**Schoenfeld BJ, Ogborn D, Krieger JW.** Effects of resistance training frequency on measures of muscle hypertrophy: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Sports Med.* 2016;46(11):1689-1697. DOI: [10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8). PMID: 27102172.

**Key findings:**
- 10 studies, volume-equated comparisons
- Training 2x/week produced superior hypertrophy vs 1x/week (ES 0.49 vs 0.30)
- **Major muscle groups should be trained at least 2x/week**
- Insufficient evidence to determine if 3x/week > 2x/week

---

### 2E. Frequency Update (When Volume Is Equated)

**Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Krieger J.** How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis of studies examining the effects of resistance training frequency. *J Sports Sci.* 2019;37(11):1286-1295. DOI: [10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906). PMID: 30558493.

**Key findings:**
- 25 studies
- **When volume is equated, frequency does NOT significantly impact hypertrophy**
- Modest advantages for higher frequencies only in studies that didn't control for total volume
- Individuals can select frequency based on preference, provided total volume is constant

---

### 2F. Low vs High Load (Rep Range) Meta-Analysis

**Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Ogborn D, Krieger JW.** Strength and hypertrophy adaptations between low- vs. high-load resistance training: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *J Strength Cond Res.* 2017;31(12):3508-3523. DOI: [10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200](https://doi.org/10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200). PMID: 28834797.

**Key findings:**
- 21 studies comparing <60% 1RM vs >60% 1RM, all sets to failure
- **Muscle hypertrophy was similar between low-load and high-load conditions** (trivial ES difference: 0.03)
- Strength gains (1RM) significantly favored high-load training
- Isometric strength: no difference between conditions
- **Practical: Muscle can be built across a wide range of loads; heavy loads optimize 1RM strength specifically**

---

### 2G. Repetition Continuum Re-Examination

**Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J, Van Every DW, Plotkin DL.** Loading recommendations for muscle strength, hypertrophy, and local endurance: A re-examination of the repetition continuum. *Sports (Basel).* 2021;9(2):32. DOI: [10.3390/sports9020032](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports9020032). PMID: 33671664.

**Key findings:**
- Challenges traditional "rep continuum" model (heavy = strength, moderate = hypertrophy, light = endurance)
- Muscular adaptations can be obtained across a wide spectrum of loading zones
- Greater flexibility in program design than previously assumed
- Similar hypertrophy across rep ranges when sets are taken close to failure

---

### 2H. Training to Failure

**Grgic J, Schoenfeld BJ, Orazem J, Sabol F.** Effects of resistance training performed to repetition failure or non-failure on muscular strength and hypertrophy: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *J Sport Health Sci.* 2022;11(2):202-211. DOI: [10.1016/j.jshs.2021.01.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jshs.2021.01.007). PMID: 33497853.

**Key findings:**
- 15 studies in young adults
- No significant difference between failure vs non-failure for strength (ES = -0.09) or hypertrophy (ES = 0.22)
- When volume was NOT equated, non-failure training showed superior strength outcomes
- In resistance-trained individuals: modest hypertrophy advantage with failure training (ES = 0.15)
- **Training to failure is not required for gains, but does not harm them either**

---

### 2I. Range of Motion

**Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J.** Effects of range of motion on muscle development during resistance training interventions: A systematic review. *SAGE Open Med.* 2020;8:2050312120901559. DOI: [10.1177/2050312120901559](https://doi.org/10.1177/2050312120901559). PMID: 32030125.

**Key findings:**
- 6 studies, 135 participants
- Lower body: benefit to training with full ROM (3 of 4 studies favored full ROM)
- Upper body: evidence less conclusive
- Generally: full ROM appears advantageous, especially for lower body

---

### 2J. Lengthened Partials (2025)

**Wolf M et al. (including Schoenfeld BJ).** Lengthened partial repetitions elicit similar muscular adaptations as full range of motion repetitions during resistance training in trained individuals. *PeerJ.* 2025;e18904. DOI: [10.7717/peerj.18904](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.18904). PMID: 39959841.

**Key findings:**
- In trained individuals, lengthened partial ROM produced similar muscle growth to full ROM
- Training in the lengthened (stretched) position appears particularly effective for hypertrophy

---

### 2K. IUSCA Position Stand (Comprehensive Guidelines)

**Schoenfeld BJ, Fisher J, Grgic J, Haun C, Helms E, Phillips S, Steele J, Vigotsky A.** Resistance training recommendations to maximize muscle hypertrophy in an athletic population: Position stand of the IUSCA. *Int J Strength Cond.* 2021;1(1). DOI: [10.47206/ijsc.v1i1.81](https://doi.org/10.47206/ijsc.v1i1.81).

**Key recommendations from position stand:**
- Synthesizes the full body of hypertrophy research into practical guidelines
- Covers: volume, frequency, load/rep ranges, rest periods, exercise selection, tempo, proximity to failure
- The definitive "summary document" of Schoenfeld's research program

### 2L. Evidence-Based Volume Guidelines

**Schoenfeld BJ, Grgic J.** Evidence-based guidelines for resistance training volume to maximize muscle hypertrophy. *Strength Cond J.* 2018;40(4):107-112. DOI: [10.1519/SSC.0000000000000363](https://doi.org/10.1519/SSC.0000000000000363).

**Key recommendations:**
- Practical translation of the 2017 meta-analysis findings
- Tiered volume recommendations based on training experience
- Provides actionable prescription for coaches and trainees

## Part 3: Updated Dose-Response Meta-Regression (2026)

**Pelland JC, Remmert JF, Robinson ZP, Hinson SR, Zourdos MC.** The resistance training dose response: Meta-regressions exploring the effects of weekly volume and frequency on muscle hypertrophy and strength gains. *Sports Med.* 2026;56(2):481-505. DOI: [10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w). PMID: 41343037.

**Key findings:**
- 67 studies, 2,058 participants (79.1% male, avg age 25.2 years)
- **Volume:** Gains in both muscle size and strength increase with volume, but both show diminishing returns; strength shows more pronounced diminishing returns than hypertrophy
- **Frequency:** For hypertrophy, frequency effects are uncertain. For strength, gains increase with increasing frequency (with diminishing returns)
- Innovation: "fractional" method (0.5 weighting for indirect sets) provided superior predictive accuracy

## Part 4: Resistance Training & Health Outcomes (Beyond Hypertrophy)

### 4A. Mortality Risk

**Shailendra P, Baldock KL, Li LSK, Bennie JA, Boyle T.** Resistance training and mortality risk: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Am J Prev Med.* 2022;63(2):277-285. DOI: [10.1016/j.amepre.2022.03.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amepre.2022.03.020). PMID: 35599175.

**Key findings:**
- 10 studies
- Any resistance training reduced all-cause mortality risk by **15%**
- CVD mortality reduced by **19%**
- Cancer mortality reduced by **14%**
- Dose-response: optimal at ~60 min/week (maximum risk reduction of **27%**)
- Mortality risk reductions diminished at higher volumes (nonlinear relationship)

---

### 4B. Muscle-Strengthening Activities & NCD Risk (J-Shaped Dose-Response)

**Momma H, Kawakami R, Honda T, Sawada SS.** Muscle-strengthening activities are associated with lower risk and mortality in major non-communicable diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies. *Br J Sports Med.* 2022;56(13):755-763. DOI: [10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061](https://doi.org/10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061). PMID: 35228201.

**Key findings:**
- **J-shaped association** for all-cause mortality, CVD, total cancer: benefits peak at ~30-60 min/week (10-20% risk reduction); higher volumes show unclear additional benefit
- **L-shaped association** for diabetes: continuous improvement up to 60 min/week with no plateau
- 10-17% lower risk across all-cause mortality, CVD, cancer, lung cancer vs no activity
- **Critical finding: More is not always better for mortality reduction -- the sweet spot is ~30-60 min/week**

---

### 4C. Muscular Strength & All-Cause Mortality

**Garcia-Hermoso A, Cavero-Redondo I, Ramirez-Velez R, Ruiz JR, Ortega FB, Lee DC, Martinez-Vizcaino V.** Muscular strength as a predictor of all-cause mortality in an apparently healthy population: A systematic review and meta-analysis of data from approximately 2 million men and women. *Arch Phys Med Rehabil.* 2018;99(10):2100-2113.e5. DOI: [10.1016/j.apmr.2018.01.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apmr.2018.01.008). PMID: 29425700.

**Key findings:**
- 38 studies, ~1.9 million participants, 63,087 deaths
- Higher handgrip strength: **31% reduced all-cause mortality** (HR=0.69; 95% CI 0.64-0.74)
- Higher knee extension strength: **14% lower mortality**
- Association holds regardless of age and follow-up period
- Women showed stronger associations than men

---

### 4D. Bone Mineral Density in Older Adults

**Massini DA et al.** The effect of resistance training on bone mineral density in older adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Healthcare (Basel).* 2022;10(6):1129. DOI: [10.3390/healthcare10061129](https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare10061129). PMID: 35742181.

**Key findings:**
- 7 studies, 370 older adults
- Positive effect on BMD at hip (+0.64%) and spine (+0.62%)
- Typical protocol: 4-5 exercises (upper + lower), 2-3 sets, 8-12 reps to failure at 70-90% 1RM, 3x/week, 12-52 weeks
- Preventive effect against bone frailty evident beyond 12 weeks of RT

---

### 4E. Resistance Training & Type 2 Diabetes (HbA1c)

**Jansson AK, Chan LX, Lubans DR, Duncan MJ, Plotnikoff RC.** Effect of resistance training on HbA1c in adults with type 2 diabetes mellitus and the moderating effect of changes in muscular strength: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *BMJ Open Diabetes Res Care.* 2022;10(2):e002595. DOI: [10.1136/bmjdrc-2021-002595](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjdrc-2021-002595). PMID: 35273011.

**Key findings:**
- 20 RCTs (n=1,172)
- RT significantly reduced HbA1c vs controls: weighted mean difference **-0.39%** (95% CI -0.60 to -0.18, p<0.001)
- Larger strength gains predicted greater HbA1c reductions (beta=-0.99)
- RT comparable to aerobic training for HbA1c reduction (p=0.42)
- Neither intervention length nor risk of bias influenced effectiveness

---

### 4F. Cognitive Function in Older Adults

**Coelho-Junior HJ et al.** Resistance training improves cognitive function in older adults with different cognitive status: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *Aging Ment Health.* 2022;26(2):213-224. DOI: [10.1080/13607863.2020.1857691](https://doi.org/10.1080/13607863.2020.1857691). PMID: 33325273.

**Key findings:**
- 18 studies across cognitively healthy and cognitively impaired older adults
- RT significantly improved overall cognitive function (SMD = 0.40)
- Working memory (SMD = 0.44), verbal learning/memory (MD = 3.01), spatial memory span (SMD = 0.63) all improved
- Recommended protocol: 12 weeks, 2-3x/week, 45 min/session

---

### 4G. Depression & Anxiety

- Resistance training significantly alleviates depressive symptoms (large ES ~-0.94 from 29 RCTs)
- In older adults (>=60 years): mental health SMD 0.44, depression SMD -1.13, anxiety SMD -1.33
- RT is at least as effective as many other lifestyle/psychosocial interventions for depression

---

### 4H. Protein Requirements (Stuart Phillips, McMaster University)

- Daily protein intake ~1.6 g/kg/day for maximizing muscle protein synthesis
- Per-meal dose: ~0.4 g/kg for optimal stimulation
- Key supplements to augment RT in older adults: leucine, omega-3 PUFAs, creatine
- Resistance training + adequate protein is the most potent non-pharmacological strategy against sarcopenia

## Part 5: Synthesis -- Beyond WHO/PAG Guidelines

### What the standard guidelines say:
- WHO/US Physical Activity Guidelines: >=2 days/week of muscle-strengthening activities, 150-300 min/week moderate aerobic
- These are **minimum recommendations** with limited dose-response specificity for resistance training

### What this research adds (specific prescriptions):

| Variable | Evidence-Based Recommendation | Source |
|----------|------------------------------|--------|
| **Weekly volume** | 10+ sets/muscle/week for near-optimal hypertrophy; 1-4 sets/week still yields ~64% of max gains | Schoenfeld 2017 meta-analysis |
| **Minimum effective dose** | 1 set/exercise 1-3x/week sufficient for strength gains; 30-60 min/week sufficient for mortality reduction | Nippard transcript; Momma 2022 |
| **Frequency** | >=2x/week per muscle group; when volume equated, frequency is flexible | Schoenfeld 2016, 2019 |
| **Load (rep range)** | Hypertrophy occurs across all rep ranges (low to high load) when sets approach failure; heavy loads (>60% 1RM) optimize 1RM strength | Schoenfeld 2017, 2021 |
| **Proximity to failure** | Not required to reach failure, but should be within 1-3 reps of failure for optimal stimulus; trained individuals may benefit slightly from failure training | Grgic 2022 |
| **Range of motion** | Full ROM preferred, especially lower body; lengthened partials appear equally effective to full ROM in trained individuals | Schoenfeld 2020; Wolf 2025 |
| **Rest periods** | 2-3 minutes between sets for hypertrophy optimization; can be shortened over time as cardiovascular fitness improves | Nippard; Schoenfeld guidelines |
| **Health dose-response** | J-shaped: max mortality benefit at ~30-60 min/week RT; diminishing/unclear benefit beyond that | Momma 2022; Shailendra 2022 |
| **Mortality reduction** | Any RT: 15% all-cause, 19% CVD, 14% cancer mortality reduction; muscular strength independently predicts 31% lower mortality | Shailendra 2022; Garcia-Hermoso 2018 |
| **HbA1c (T2DM)** | RT reduces HbA1c by ~0.39%; comparable to aerobic training; strength gains moderate the effect | Jansson 2022 |
| **Bone density** | RT improves BMD at hip (+0.64%) and spine (+0.62%) in older adults; protocol: 2-3 sets, 8-12 reps, 70-90% 1RM, 3x/week | Massini 2022 |
| **Cognitive function** | RT improves cognitive function (SMD 0.40); 12 weeks, 2-3x/week, 45 min/session | Coelho-Junior 2022 |
| **Mental health** | Large effect on depression (ES ~-0.94); moderate-large effect on anxiety | Multiple meta-analyses |
| **Protein** | ~1.6 g/kg/day; ~0.4 g/kg per meal | Phillips (McMaster) |
| **Maintenance** | Can maintain muscle on as little as 1/9 of building volume | Bickel et al. |

### Key Takeaway for Clinical Exercise Guideline

The evidence supports resistance training as a **cornerstone health intervention** with benefits for mortality, CVD, cancer, diabetes, bone density, cognitive function, and mental health -- most of which accrue at relatively modest doses (30-60 min/week, 2+ sessions). The dose-response for hypertrophy is graded (more volume = more growth), but the dose-response for health outcomes is J-shaped or L-shaped, meaning **most health benefits are captured in the first ~60 min/week** of resistance training. This is substantially more specific than the generic "2+ days/week" WHO/PAG recommendation and provides actionable prescription data for clinical guidelines.
