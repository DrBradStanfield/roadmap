## Decisions for you

House safety lines (ruling 11, beyond the source):
1. Sudden vision loss: severe eye pain with blurred vision, halos, headache, nausea or vomiting (possible acute angle-closure glaucoma) means call 111.
2. Shingles: confusion or marked drowsiness, a severe headache with a stiff neck, new one-sided weakness, or being very unwell means call 111 (disseminated disease).
3. COVID-19: breathlessness at rest, chest pain, confusion or marked drowsiness means call 111.
4. Transient vision loss in one eye: vision that has not come back, or face, arm, leg or speech symptoms, means call 111 (possible stroke). Vision that recovered fully means see a doctor straight away (your doctor today, or urgent care or the emergency department after hours), because stroke risk is highest in the first 2 days. Giant cell arteritis "needs prompt treatment to protect your sight".
5. Hyperthyroidism: a swollen face with swollen neck and chest veins (goitre pressing on a large vein) means call 111.

Safety corrections where the source is wrong or outdated:
6. Calcium: the source says iron, tetracyclines and bisphosphonates "reduce calcium absorption". The entry says calcium reduces how well those medicines are absorbed.
7. Abnormal LFTs: removed the source's "AST is usually higher than ALT for NASH". Standard teaching is ALT above AST, except in advanced fibrosis.
8. Cirrhosis: the source lists paracetamol as harmful with no dose. The entry says "in high doses" and that paracetamol at the lower dose the doctor advises is usually the preferred pain reliever, while NSAIDs are best avoided.
9. Depression: the source's PHQ-9 row "more than 20" leaves a score of 20 unclassified. The entry uses the standard 20 to 27 for severe.
10. Currency check: co-trimoxazole from 8 weeks of age, not 6 (data sheet, July 2025). SSRI bleeding risk with blood thinners. Carbimazole liver warning. Paxlovid interactions. Remdesivir not usually used in the first trimester. Reandron contraindications, and observation after the injection.

Urgency choices:
11. Knee injuries: "any knee fracture" becomes a suspected fracture (you cannot take four steps) and a knee that stays locked: both mean call 111. Swelling alone means see your doctor the same day for an X-ray.
12. Heart murmurs: rheumatic fever signs and possible endocarditis mean see your doctor the same day (ruling 12). Fainting and new chest pain mean call 111.
13. Cirrhosis and abnormal LFTs: new jaundice means see your doctor the same day (ruling 12). With confusion, drowsiness, vomiting blood or black stools, call 111.

Kept, for your view:
14. Weight concerns (the most-asked page): the new source no longer describes semaglutide or tirzepatide, so their rows came out. Restore neutral facts from the Medsafe data sheets (recommended), or leave them out?
15. Cirrhosis: carvedilol for varices stays (specialist standard of care, flagged off-label), although the data sheet contraindicates it in liver dysfunction.
16. Metabolic syndrome: restored the IDF rule that treatment for raised triglycerides, blood pressure or glucose counts (Codex R4). The source counts treatment only for low HDL.
17. Bone density scan: the September scrape looks older than April's (next review overdue since November 2025). Several other sources are past their review dates (metabolic syndrome 2018, calcium 2017).
