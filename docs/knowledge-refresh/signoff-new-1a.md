## Decisions for you

New standing rule:
1. Ruling 12: HealthPathways asks for "acute" specialist assessment even for slow signs. These include hoarseness for 4 weeks, weight loss and anaemia on a blood test. Emergency symptoms you feel now keep "call 111 or go to the emergency department". Slow or background signs get "see your doctor the same day", and the doctor arranges the acute assessment. Without this rule, those pages would send people to 111 for weeks-old hoarseness.

House safety lines (ruling 11, beyond the source):
2. Wound care: spreading infection with fever, shivering, a racing heart, or pain far worse than the wound looks (possible necrotising fasciitis) means call 111.
3. Stridor (noisy, high-pitched breathing) means call 111, in dysphagia and hoarse voice.
4. Anxiety: "If you are thinking about suicide, see your doctor the same day, or call or text 1737 (free, any time). If you have a plan or feel you might act on it, call 111 or go to the emergency department."
5. Epilepsy: a seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or seizures one after another without recovery, means call 111.
6. Cognitive impairment: sudden confusion means see your doctor the same day, or call 111 if the person is very drowsy or hard to wake.
7. Abortion: heavy bleeding (2 or more pads an hour for 2 hours) means contact the provider straight away, or call 111 if you feel faint.

Safety corrections where the source is outdated or unsafe:
8. Analgesia in children: the source still gives a reduced tramadol dose after tonsillectomy. The entry instead says tramadol must not be used after tonsillectomy or adenoidectomy in anyone under 18, which is Medsafe's contraindication.
9. Anxiety: the source says escitalopram does not appear to carry QT risk. The entry now says citalopram and escitalopram can both affect heart rhythm (MHRA: dose-dependent QT prolongation).
10. Dyspepsia: the source's lifestyle advice for pregnancy included weight loss and limiting alcohol. In pregnancy the entry gives no weight-loss advice and says to avoid alcohol.

Units and doses:
11. IBD: the source prints "Hb less than 105" and "CRP more than 30" with no units. The entry adds g/L and mg/L, the NZ reporting units, because these numbers trigger a 111 line.
12. IBD: the faecal calprotectin cut-off is printed as "less than 50 micrograms per L", as the source has it. Labs report micrograms per g, and our calprotectin page uses micrograms per g. Change it to "per g"? I recommend yes. The source looks wrong, but the change is yours to make.
13. Antenatal: the calcium dose reads "two tablets a day, each giving 500 mg of elemental calcium (1.25 g calcium carbonate)". The source names the brand (Calci-Tab) without the elemental dose.

Safety corrections from Codex round 2 (accepted, not yet applied):
14. IBD: after infliximab in pregnancy, no live vaccines for the baby for 12 months (Medsafe data sheet), not the source's 6 months.
15. Anxiety: for people over 65, the maximum is citalopram 20 mg and escitalopram 10 mg a day (Medsafe data sheets). The source table gives only the adult maxima.
16. IBD: the first surveillance colonoscopy comes 8 years after diagnosis, per the 2025 NZSG guidance the source links. The source text still says 10.
