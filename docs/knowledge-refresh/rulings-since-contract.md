# Rulings since the contract (orchestrator, 2026-09-29 to 2026-10-08)

These override the contracts where they differ.

1. Report shape. Each `changed_tokens` entry is `{body_line, raw_quote, notes?}`. No `token` field: the checker works out the numbers itself. One entry per body line that carries a new or changed number. `body_line` is the exact current text of that body line. `raw_quote` is verbatim source text (copied, never retyped) of at least 6 words or 30 characters that holds the same numbers.
2. Urgency follows the source's own grouping. Never invent tiers. Where the source marks a presentation for acute assessment, admission or as life-threatening, say "call 111 or go to the emergency department". Where it says urgent or same-day review, say "see your doctor the same day". Where it groups several red flags under one action, keep them under one action. "111" in "call 111" needs no report entry.
3. Comparator words follow the source. If the source says "less than 4 mmol/L", the body says "less than", not "below". "Over 3 to 6 months" (a time span) stays as the source writes it.
4. Funding criteria never appear (Special Authority, eligibility rules, PHARMAC conditions). A one-word status ("funded") may stay.
5. Brands only where the source uses the brand as the drug's name, plus a supply-change notice that alters a dose. Remove expired supply notices.
6. Red flags for back pain (new bladder or bowel control problems, saddle numbness, new limb weakness) appear only where the source lists them, and only the ones it lists. Where they appear, contract rule 11 still applies: they get "call 111 or go to the emergency department", as a safety rule even when the source attaches no action.
7. Your old report from 2026-09-29 survives at ~/.codex-review/diff-reports/<batch>/<handle>.json. Its entries are sentences and quotes that were already checked against the same raw, and its notes record rulings for your entry (for example a reverted tier, a removed sentence, a FOR BRAD item). Start from it. Re-check every quote against the raw yourself; keep its rulings unless the raw contradicts them.
8. References: a number in a sentence that cites [n], where n is a PubMed record, must be quoted from that PMID's abstract (prefix "EXTRA: ", file listed in extra_raw with its sha256). Safety cautions that rested only on Grokipedia stay out; list them in notes under SAFETY.
9. Writers never commit and never touch index.json. Write only your own entry and your own report.
10. Never reword, spell out ("forty-five"), abbreviate or delete text to get past the checker, and never drop bibliographic detail (volume, pages, year). If a number in the body matches the source but the checker cannot see it, that is a checker defect: leave the text as it should read and report the failing line.
11. House safety lines (orchestrator 2026-10-08, from the pilot Brad signed). These lines stand even though the source gives a lower action: on SGLT2 inhibitors, blood ketones over 1.5 mmol/L, or confusion, marked drowsiness or struggling to breathe, mean "call 111 or go to the emergency department". Where hypoglycaemia is described: "If someone with diabetes is unconscious, having a seizure, or cannot swallow safely, call 111. Do not give food or drink by mouth." Reports note them as HOUSE RULE. A lab result that the source sends for acute assessment gets the ruling 2 wording too: patients see results in portals before the doctor calls (Codex R1, batch 2).
12. Acute requests for slow signs (orchestrator 2026-10-12, new-1a review). HealthPathways files some slow or background signs under "request acute assessment". Emergency symptoms the reader feels now keep "call 111 or go to the emergency department". A sign that has lasted weeks, weight loss, or a blood-test finding other than a dangerous lab value (for example corrected calcium less than 2 mmol/L) gets "see your doctor the same day"; the doctor arranges the acute assessment. Ruling 11 house lines added the same day: spreading infection with fever, shivering, a racing heart, or pain far worse than the wound looks; stridor; suicide (1737, and 111 with a plan); seizures over 5 minutes or back to back; heavy bleeding after an abortion. Added for new-1b: non-fatal strangulation (see a doctor the same day even if you feel fine; 111 for trouble breathing or swallowing, a changed voice, or new weakness or numbness). Added for new-1b review: danger now (111, ask for the police); kidney infection signs with a UTI (same day; 111 if very unwell, confused or faint); clot symptoms on clot-risk pages (painful swollen leg same day; sudden breathlessness, chest pain or coughing up blood 111); a bleeding varicose vein (lie down, raise the leg, press firmly; 111 if it does not stop).
13. Currency check (Brad 2026-10-14). HealthPathways pages lag Medsafe data sheets and current guidance. Each batch gets a currency check before review: a Sonnet agent compares every dose, maximum, contraindication and drug warning with the current Medsafe data sheet (and NZF where a data sheet is silent) and lists differences, editing nothing. The orchestrator rules each one; an Opus worker applies accepted ones as SAFETY CORRECTION notes in the report. Brad also ruled: faecal calprotectin cut-off is micrograms per g.
