## Decisions for you

House safety lines (ruling 11, beyond the source):
1. Physical assault: "If you are in danger now, call 111 and ask for the police." If someone tried to strangle you, see a doctor the same day even if you feel fine. Call 111 for trouble breathing or swallowing, a changed voice, or new weakness or numbness. Suicide gets the 1737 line.
2. UTI: fever, shivering, pain in the side or back, or vomiting means see your doctor the same day. Call 111 if you are very unwell, confused or faint.
3. Thrombotic disorders: a painful, swollen leg means see your doctor the same day. Sudden breathlessness, chest pain or coughing up blood means call 111.
4. Varicose veins: for a bleeding vein, lie down, raise the leg and press firmly. Call 111 if the bleeding does not stop.
5. Stridor means call 111 (motor neurone disease, which lists it only as an exam finding).

Safety corrections where the source is outdated:
6. Thrombotic disorders: the source says it is "reasonable to continue" the combined pill after a factor V Leiden finding. The entry says to talk to your doctor, because combined pills, patches and rings are usually not recommended (UKMEC category 4).
7. Testosterone: "long-term risks largely unknown" is replaced with the 2023 TRAVERSE result: no rise in heart attacks or strokes over about 2 years, but more atrial fibrillation, kidney injury and blood clots in the lungs.
8. UTI in men: the source says men take the same short course as women. The entry says men usually need a longer course and the doctor decides how long.
9. Motor neurone disease: quinine for cramps now carries a caution: rare but serious blood problems, and very dangerous in overdose, so keep it away from children.
10. Glue ear: steroid nasal sprays are not recommended for glue ear on its own (NICE). The source said "safe at any age".
11. Osteoporosis: the source presents a break after 5 years of tablets as routine. The entry has the doctor reassess first, with a break only if risk is now lower. Zoledronic acid keeps the source's "no drug holiday" and gains the data sheet's kidney limit (creatinine clearance less than 35).
12. Catheter: doxazosin starts at 1 mg (NZ data sheet), not the source's 2 mg. A catheter in place for more than 7 days may be replaced when treating a UTI, without delaying antibiotics (NICE). The source said never to change it.
13. Menopause: on an aromatase inhibitor, vaginal oestrogen is used only with the breast cancer specialist, after non-hormonal options have not helped. The source reassured that there was no evidence of harm.

Kept against an older data sheet (currency check):
14. UTI: nitrofurantoin is not used below creatinine clearance 30, following MHRA 2014 and NZ guidance. The Macrobid data sheet still says 60. Keep?

Urgency choices:
15. Red eye and eye trauma: sudden vision loss, penetrating or chemical injury, severe pain with marked light sensitivity, and hyphaema mean call 111. A chemical injury gets "rinse straight away" first. Slow signs get same-day review (ruling 12).
16. Skin infections in children: a weakened immune system means see your doctor the same day, not 111. Swelling of the eyelids or around the eye means call 111.
17. Iron deficiency: visible active bleeding (vomiting blood, black stools, heavy rectal bleeding) means call 111.
18. Intrauterine contraception: a possible pregnancy with an IUD means see your doctor the same day, because of the risk of an ectopic pregnancy.
