## Decisions for you

Vitamin K
1. Warfarin: MK-7 can interfere at 10 mcg a day (ConsumerLab; Theuwissen 2013 supports it); the Schurgers 2007 abstract says 50 mcg or more. The article keeps the cautious 10 mcg.
2. Five cautions stay out (they rested only on Grokipedia or an unresolved record): St John's wort, IV vitamin K reactions, the 81-fold newborn-bleeding reduction, 20% late-bleeding mortality, oral newborn prophylaxis.

Lithium
3. Interaction warnings now come from the FDA label: diuretics, ACE inhibitors and ARBs, NSAIDs, pregnancy (Ebstein's anomaly), with "this list is not complete". Confirm the wording suits a supplement article.
4. Long-COVID trial doses (10 to 15 mg/d, 40 to 45 mg/d in follow-up) are called "likely elemental lithium" as an inference from the serum levels; the abstract does not say elemental.
5. "Don't add a supplement to prescription lithium without your doctor" is an inference, not a sourced line. Keep?
6. Leyhe 2009: the abstract links the BDNF rise to better cognition in a subset; ConsumerLab says it was not. The article states both.
7. Five sections removed (their only source was Grokipedia): neurotransmitter modulation, anti-inflammatory effects, BDNF and synaptogenesis, traumatic brain injury, mechanisms of ageing.

Magnesium

9. The frozen summary still lists cramps and anxiety as benefits; a corrected summary is proposed in the report (held for the paired router arm).
10. Reference [7] undercuts "glycinate is preferred for GI tolerance". Soften or keep?

Taurine
11. Heart failure evidence now matches the abstract (14 patients, no dose).
12. Dog whole-blood threshold reads "250 nmol/L" as ConsumerLab prints it; the usual unit may be nmol/mL. The cited abstract is silent.
13. Cautions out (Grokipedia-only): leukaemia, bipolar disorder with lithium, kidney disease, caffeine, doses above 6 g a day.

Creatine

18. Jager: "nearly 99% taken up by muscle or excreted in urine", worded as uptake, not bioavailability.
